Volume by Cross Section S(x)

Slice it like bread

Cut a solid with planes perpendicular to the $x$-axis. The slice at $x$ has area $S(x)$. A very thin slice of thickness $dx$ has volume about $S(x)\,dx$.

Adding all the slices gives the volume. So if you can write the cross-section area as a function of $x$, the volume is a single integral.

Solid between the planes $x = a$ and $x = b$

$$V = \int_a^b S(x)\,dx$$

$S(x)$: area of the cross-section by the plane through $x$, perpendicular to the $x$-axis.

  1. A pyramid or cone of height $h$ with base area $A$: put the apex at $x = 0$ and the base at $x = h$.
  2. The slice at $x$ is the base scaled by $\dfrac{x}{h}$, so $S(x) = A\left(\dfrac{x}{h}\right)^2$.
  3. $V = \displaystyle\int_0^h \frac{A}{h^2}x^2\,dx = \frac{A}{h^2} \cdot \frac{h^3}{3} = \frac{1}{3}Ah$, the familiar formula.

Side view with the slice at $x$ (teal); its cross-section on the right

Example 1

A solid has the disk $x^2 + y^2 \le r^2$ as its base. Every cross-section perpendicular to the $x$-axis is a square. Find its volume.

Show solution
  1. At $x$ the chord has length $2\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$, so $S(x) = 4(r^2 - x^2)$.
  2. $V = \displaystyle\int_{-r}^{r} 4(r^2 - x^2)\,dx = 8\int_0^r (r^2 - x^2)\,dx = 8 \cdot \frac{2r^3}{3}$

$\dfrac{16}{3}r^3$

Example 2

Using cross-sections, derive the volume of a sphere of radius $r$.

Show solution
  1. The slice at distance $x$ from the centre is a disk of radius $\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$: $S(x) = \pi(r^2 - x^2)$.
  2. $V = \displaystyle\int_{-r}^{r} \pi(r^2 - x^2)\,dx = 2\pi\left(r^3 - \frac{r^3}{3}\right)$

$\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$

Example 3

A solid lies between $x = 0$ and $x = \pi$. Its cross-section at $x$ is an equilateral triangle of side $\sqrt{\sin x}$. Find its volume.

Show solution
  1. Area of an equilateral triangle of side $s$: $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2$, so $S(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\sin x$.
  2. $V = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \sin x\,dx = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 2$

$\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

Example 4

A cylinder of radius $r$ stands on the $xy$-plane. It is cut by a plane through a diameter of the base, at $45°$ to the base. Find the volume of the wedge.

Show solution
  1. Let the diameter lie on the $y$-axis and slice perpendicular to the $x$-axis ($0 \le x \le r$). The slice is a rectangle, width $2\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$ and height $x$ (since $\tan 45° = 1$).
  2. $V = \displaystyle\int_0^r 2x\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}\,dx$. Put $u = r^2 - x^2$: $\displaystyle\int_0^{r^2} \sqrt{u}\,du = \frac{2}{3}r^3$.

$\dfrac{2}{3}r^3$

The slices must be perpendicular to the axis you integrate along, and $S(x)$ must be written using $x$ only. Choosing the slice direction is part of the problem; some directions give much simpler $S(x)$.
Draw a single slice at a general $x$, label its dimensions in terms of $x$, and find its area. After that the problem is just an integral.

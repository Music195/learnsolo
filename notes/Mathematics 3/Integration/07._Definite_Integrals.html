Definite Integrals

A number: the rise of the antiderivative

The definite integral $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is a number: $F(b) - F(a)$, for any antiderivative $F$. The constant $C$ cancels in the subtraction, so you can leave it out.

Geometrically, it is the signed area between $y = f(x)$ and the $x$-axis from $a$ to $b$. Parts above the axis count as positive and parts below as negative.

$F'(x) = f(x)$

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b = F(b) - F(a)$$

$a$: lower limit, $b$: upper limit. The letter used for the variable doesn't matter: $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(t)\,dt$.

  1. Let $S(x)$ be the area under $f$ from $a$ to $x$ (with $f \ge 0$). Widening by a thin strip adds $S(x + h) - S(x) \approx f(x)h$.
  2. So $S'(x) = f(x)$: $S$ is an antiderivative of $f$, and $S(x) = F(x) + C$.
  3. $S(a) = 0$ gives $C = -F(a)$. Then the area up to $b$ is $S(b) = F(b) - F(a)$.

Shaded signed area of $f$ (blue) = rise of $F$ (orange) from $a$ to $b$

Example 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^2 (3x^2 - 2x)\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[x^3 - x^2\Big]_1^2 = (8 - 4) - (1 - 1)$

$4$

Example 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^e \frac{1}{x}\,dx$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin x\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[\log x\Big]_1^e = 1 - 0$
  2. $\Big[-\cos x\Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = 0 - (-1)$

$1$ and $1$

Example 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{2x}\,dx$ and $\displaystyle\int_1^4 \sqrt{x}\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[\dfrac{e^{2x}}{2}\Big]_0^1 = \dfrac{e^2 - 1}{2}$
  2. $\Big[\dfrac{2}{3}x^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big]_1^4 = \dfrac{2}{3}(8 - 1)$

$\dfrac{e^2 - 1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{14}{3}$

Example 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \cos x\,dx$, and explain the answer with the graph.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[\sin x\Big]_0^{\pi} = 0 - 0 = 0$
  2. On $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$, $\cos x$ is above the axis. On $\left[\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right]$ it is below, with the same area by symmetry. The two parts cancel.

$0$ (signed area, not total area)

A definite integral is a signed area. If $f$ goes below the axis, $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx$ is not the geometric area. Areas are handled in note 15.
Write the bracket $\Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b$ first, then substitute. Put $F(a)$ inside brackets so its minus sign is applied to every term.
  • $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$; no $+ C$ needed.
  • It is the signed area: above the axis positive, below negative.
  • The area function $S(x)$ has $S'(x) = f(x)$.

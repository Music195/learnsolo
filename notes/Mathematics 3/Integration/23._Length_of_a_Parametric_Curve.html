Length of a Parametric Curve

Many short straight pieces

Mark many points along the curve and join them with straight segments. The total length of the segments is close to the length of the curve. The more points you use, the closer it gets.

Each segment's length comes from Pythagoras. In the limit, the sum becomes the integral of $\sqrt{(dx/dt)^2 + (dy/dt)^2}$, the same integrand as the distance travelled (note 22).

Curve $x = f(t)$, $y = g(t)$, $a \le t \le b$

$$L = \int_a^b \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt = \int_a^b \sqrt{f'(t)^2 + g'(t)^2}\,dt$$

This gives the length when the curve is traced once, without going back over itself.

  1. Split $[a, b]$ into $n$ equal steps $\Delta t$. The chord between neighbouring points has length $\sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$.
  2. By the mean value theorem, $\Delta x = f'(s)\Delta t$ and $\Delta y = g'(u)\Delta t$ for some $s$, $u$ in that step. So the chord is $\approx \sqrt{f'^2 + g'^2}\,\Delta t$.
  3. The sum of the chords is then a Riemann sum (note 13), and it tends to the integral as $n \to \infty$.

$n$ straight pieces (orange) along the curve (blue)

Example 1

Find the length of $x = t^2$, $y = \dfrac{2}{3}t^3$ for $0 \le t \le 1$.

Show solution
  1. $x' = 2t$, $y' = 2t^2$: $\sqrt{4t^2 + 4t^4} = 2t\sqrt{1 + t^2}$ for $t \ge 0$.
  2. Put $u = 1 + t^2$, $du = 2t\,dt$: $\displaystyle\int_1^2 \sqrt{u}\,du = \frac{2}{3}(2\sqrt{2} - 1)$.

$\dfrac{2}{3}(2\sqrt{2} - 1)$

Example 2

Find the total length of the astroid $x = a\cos^3 t$, $y = a\sin^3 t$ ($a > 0$).

Show solution
  1. $x' = -3a\cos^2 t\sin t$, $y' = 3a\sin^2 t\cos t$.
  2. $x'^2 + y'^2 = 9a^2\sin^2 t\cos^2 t(\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t)$, so the speed is $3a|\sin t\cos t|$.
  3. By symmetry, use 4 times the first quarter: $4\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} 3a\sin t\cos t\,dt = 12a \cdot \frac{1}{2}$.

$6a$

Example 3

Find the length of $x = 3t - t^3$, $y = 3t^2$ for $0 \le t \le 1$.

Show solution
  1. $x'^2 + y'^2 = 9(1 - t^2)^2 + 36t^2 = 9(1 + 2t^2 + t^4) = 9(1 + t^2)^2$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^1 3(1 + t^2)\,dt = 3\left(1 + \frac{1}{3}\right)$

$4$

$\sqrt{A^2} = |A|$, not $A$. Over the whole astroid, $\sin t\cos t$ changes sign. Without the absolute value, $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} 3a\sin t\cos t\,dt = 0$, which is clearly wrong. Use symmetry or split the interval.
In exam problems, $x'^2 + y'^2$ is almost always a perfect square, as in Example 3, or a multiple of $\sin^2 + \cos^2$. If it isn't, check your derivatives.
  • $L = \int_a^b \sqrt{x'^2 + y'^2}\,dt$.
  • It is the limit of the lengths of inscribed polygons.
  • Use symmetry and $|\ |$ correctly when taking the root.

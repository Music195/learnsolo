Distance Traveled On a Number Line

Integrate velocity for the change of position, speed for the distance

A point P moves on a number line with velocity $v(t)$. Integrating $v$ gives the change in position (displacement). Backward motion counts as negative, so it can cancel forward motion.

The distance travelled counts every bit of motion as positive. So integrate the speed $|v(t)|$, splitting at the times where $v$ changes sign.

Velocity $v(t)$, starting position $x(a)$

$$x(b) = x(a) + \int_a^b v(t)\,dt$$
$$\text{distance} = \int_a^b |v(t)|\,dt$$
  1. $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = v$, so $x(t)$ is an antiderivative of $v$, and $\displaystyle\int_a^b v\,dt = x(b) - x(a)$.
  2. On a time interval where $v \ge 0$, P moves right, and the distance equals the displacement $\int v\,dt$.
  3. Where $v \le 0$, P moves left a distance $-\int v\,dt$. Adding the pieces gives $\int |v|\,dt$.

Velocity graph: blue moves forward, red moves back

Example 1

P starts at $x = 1$ with $v = t^2 - 4t + 3$. Find its position at $t = 4$ and the distance it travels from $t = 0$ to $t = 4$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_0^4 v\,dt = \Big[\frac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 3t\Big]_0^4 = \frac{64}{3} - 32 + 12 = \frac{4}{3}$, so $x(4) = 1 + \dfrac{4}{3}$.
  2. $v = (t - 1)(t - 3)$: positive on $[0, 1]$ and $[3, 4]$, negative on $[1, 3]$.
  3. With $F(t) = \dfrac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 3t$: $F(0) = 0$, $F(1) = \dfrac{4}{3}$, $F(3) = 0$, $F(4) = \dfrac{4}{3}$. Distance $= \dfrac{4}{3} + \dfrac{4}{3} + \dfrac{4}{3}$.

Position $\dfrac{7}{3}$; distance $4$

Example 2

P moves with $v = 2 - t$. Find the distance travelled from $t = 0$ to $t = 4$.

Show solution
  1. $v \ge 0$ on $[0, 2]$ and $v \le 0$ on $[2, 4]$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (2 - t)\,dt - \int_2^4 (2 - t)\,dt = 2 - (-2)$

$4$ (the displacement is $0$: P comes back to its start)

Example 3

P moves with $v = 3(t - 1)^2$. Find the distance travelled from $t = 0$ to $t = 2$.

Show solution
  1. $v \ge 0$ for all $t$ ($v = 0$ only at the instant $t = 1$), so P never moves backward.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^2 3(t - 1)^2\,dt = \Big[(t - 1)^3\Big]_0^2 = 1 - (-1)$

$2$ (here distance = displacement)

Example 4

A car brakes with $v = 20 - 4t$ m/s until it stops. How far does it travel while braking?

Show solution
  1. It stops when $v = 0$: $t = 5$ s.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^5 (20 - 4t)\,dt = 100 - 50$

$50$ m

Split only where $v$ actually changes sign. In Example 3, $v = 0$ at $t = 1$ but P doesn't turn back, because $v \ge 0$ on both sides. Check the sign of $v$ on each piece instead of assuming it.
On the $v$–$t$ graph, the area above the axis is forward motion and the area below is backward motion. Displacement = above − below. Distance = above + below.
  • $x(b) = x(a) + \int_a^b v\,dt$.
  • Distance $= \int_a^b |v|\,dt$: split where $v$ changes sign.
  • Distance ≥ |displacement|, with equality when P never turns back.

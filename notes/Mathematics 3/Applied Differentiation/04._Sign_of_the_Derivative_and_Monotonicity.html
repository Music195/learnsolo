Sign of the Derivative and Monotonicity

Positive slope means going up

Where $f'(x) > 0$ every tangent slopes upward, so the graph rises (the function is increasing). Where $f'(x) < 0$ it falls (decreasing).

To see the whole picture, find where $f'(x) = 0$, check the sign of $f'$ in between, and record it in an increase/decrease table (増減表).

On an interval

$$f'(x) > 0 \Rightarrow f \text{ increasing}, \qquad f'(x) < 0 \Rightarrow f \text{ decreasing}, \qquad f'(x) = 0 \text{ everywhere} \Rightarrow f \text{ constant}$$

The converse can fail: $x^3$ is increasing everywhere although $f'(0) = 0$.

  1. Take any $x_1 < x_2$ in the interval. By the mean value theorem, $f(x_2) - f(x_1) = f'(c)(x_2 - x_1)$ with $x_1 < c < x_2$.
  2. If $f' > 0$ throughout, then $f'(c) > 0$ and $x_2 - x_1 > 0$, so $f(x_2) > f(x_1)$: increasing.
  3. The same argument with $f' < 0$ or $f' = 0$ gives decreasing or constant.

Example 1

Find where $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^2$ is increasing and decreasing.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 8x = 4x(x + \sqrt{2})(x - \sqrt{2})$, zero at $x = -\sqrt{2}, 0, \sqrt{2}$.
  2. Signs of $f'$ from left to right: $-$, $+$, $-$, $+$ (test e.g. $x = -2, -1, 1, 2$).
  3. Table: $f$ falls to $-4$ at $x = -\sqrt{2}$, rises to $0$ at $x = 0$, falls to $-4$ at $x = \sqrt{2}$, then rises.

Decreasing on $x \le -\sqrt{2}$ and $0 \le x \le \sqrt{2}$; increasing on $-\sqrt{2} \le x \le 0$ and $x \ge \sqrt{2}$

Example 2

Find where $f(x) = x - 2\sin x$ ($0 \le x \le 2\pi$) is increasing and decreasing.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 1 - 2\cos x = 0 \Rightarrow \cos x = \dfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$.
  2. $f' < 0$ when $\cos x > \dfrac{1}{2}$ (near $0$ and $2\pi$); $f' > 0$ in between.

Decreasing on $0 \le x \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{3} \le x \le 2\pi$; increasing on $\dfrac{\pi}{3} \le x \le \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$

Example 3

Show that $e^x \ge 1 + x$ for all real $x$.

Show solution
  1. Let $g(x) = e^x - 1 - x$. Then $g'(x) = e^x - 1$: negative for $x < 0$, zero at $0$, positive for $x > 0$.
  2. So $g$ decreases to $g(0) = 0$ and then increases: its smallest value is $0$.

$g(x) \ge 0$, i.e. $e^x \ge 1 + x$ (equality only at $x = 0$)

Example 4

For which $a$ is $f(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + 3x$ increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$?

Show solution
  1. Need $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 2ax + 3 \ge 0$ for all $x$, i.e. its discriminant is not positive.
  2. $\dfrac{D}{4} = a^2 - 9 \le 0$

$-3 \le a \le 3$

$f'(a) = 0$ does not mean the direction changes at $a$. Always check the sign on both sides. For $x^3$, $f' \ge 0$ on both sides of $0$, so it keeps rising.
Factor $f'(x)$ fully. Then each factor's sign is easy to read, and the sign of $f'$ only changes at the roots of odd multiplicity. Parts that are always positive, like $e^x$ or $x^2 + 1$, can be ignored when reading the sign.

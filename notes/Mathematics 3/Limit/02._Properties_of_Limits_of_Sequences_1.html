Properties of Limits of Sequences 1

Limits follow the ordinary arithmetic

If $a_n \to \alpha$ and $b_n \to \beta$ (both finite), then adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing the sequences does the same thing to the limits. This lets you split a complicated limit into easy pieces.

If $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = \alpha$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} b_n = \beta$

$$\lim (k a_n + l b_n) = k\alpha + l\beta, \qquad \lim a_n b_n = \alpha\beta, \qquad \lim \frac{a_n}{b_n} = \frac{\alpha}{\beta} \;(\beta \ne 0)$$

$k$, $l$: constants. All limits are as $n \to \infty$.

  1. To find $\displaystyle\lim \frac{n^2 + 1}{2n^2 - n}$, first rewrite it as $\dfrac{1 + \frac{1}{n^2}}{2 - \frac{1}{n}}$.
  2. Top: $1 + \dfrac{1}{n^2} \to 1 + 0 = 1$ (sum rule). Bottom: $2 - \dfrac{1}{n} \to 2$, which is not $0$.
  3. Quotient rule: the limit is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Before rewriting, top and bottom both went to $\infty$, so the rules could not be used.

$a_n = 2 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ and $b_n = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2n}$

Example 1

If $a_n \to 3$ and $b_n \to -2$, find $\lim (2a_n + 3b_n)$ and $\lim \dfrac{a_n^2}{b_n}$.

Show solution
  1. $2 \cdot 3 + 3 \cdot (-2) = 0$
  2. $\dfrac{3^2}{-2} = -\dfrac{9}{2}$ (allowed because $\beta = -2 \ne 0$)

$0$ and $-\dfrac{9}{2}$

Example 2

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(2n + 1)(n - 3)}{n^2 + 4}$.

Show solution
  1. Divide top and bottom by $n^2$: $\dfrac{\left(2 + \frac{1}{n}\right)\left(1 - \frac{3}{n}\right)}{1 + \frac{4}{n^2}}$.
  2. Product and quotient rules: $\dfrac{2 \cdot 1}{1}$.

$2$

Example 3

If $a_n \to 1$ and $b_n \to 1$, what is $\lim \dfrac{a_n + b_n}{a_n - b_n}$?

Show solution
  1. The bottom goes to $1 - 1 = 0$, so the quotient rule can't be used. The answer depends on the actual sequences.
  2. With $a_n = 1 + \dfrac{1}{n}$, $b_n = 1 + \dfrac{2}{n}$: $\dfrac{2 + \frac{3}{n}}{-\frac{1}{n}} = -(2n + 3) \to -\infty$.
  3. With $a_n = 1 + \dfrac{2}{n}$, $b_n = 1 + \dfrac{1}{n}$ it goes to $+\infty$ instead.

It can't be found from $\alpha$ and $\beta$ alone

The rules need finite limits (and $\beta \ne 0$ for division). Writing $\lim (n^2 - n) = \lim n^2 - \lim n = \infty - \infty$ is not allowed: rewrite as $n^2\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right) \to \infty$.
Usual order: rewrite (divide by the top power, factor, rationalise) until every piece has a finite limit, then apply the rules.
  • $\lim (ka_n + lb_n) = k\alpha + l\beta$, $\;\lim a_nb_n = \alpha\beta$, $\;\lim \dfrac{a_n}{b_n} = \dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}$ ($\beta \ne 0$).
  • Only for convergent sequences.
  • If the denominator's limit is $0$, look at the actual sequences.

Properties of Limits of Functions

What $f(x)$ approaches, not what $f(a)$ is

$\displaystyle\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \alpha$ means: when $x$ gets close to $a$ (but $x \ne a$), $f(x)$ gets close to $\alpha$. $f(a)$ itself doesn't matter. It may not even exist.

Approaching from the right ($x \to a + 0$) and from the left ($x \to a - 0$) can give different answers. The limit exists only when both sides agree.

If $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \alpha$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \beta$

$$\lim \{kf(x) + lg(x)\} = k\alpha + l\beta, \qquad \lim f(x)g(x) = \alpha\beta, \qquad \lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{\alpha}{\beta} \;(\beta \ne 0)$$

The same rules hold for $x \to \infty$ and $x \to -\infty$. The order rule and the squeeze theorem carry over from sequences too.

One-sided limits

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \alpha \iff \lim_{x \to a + 0} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a - 0} f(x) = \alpha$$
  1. $\sin\dfrac{1}{x}$ wobbles faster and faster near $0$ and has no limit there.
  2. But $\left|\sin\dfrac{1}{x}\right| \le 1$, so $-|x| \le x\sin\dfrac{1}{x} \le |x|$ for $x \ne 0$.
  3. $\pm|x| \to 0$ as $x \to 0$, so by the squeeze theorem $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} x\sin\frac{1}{x} = 0$.

Approach $x = a$ from both sides

Example 1

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^3 - 8}{x - 2}$.

Show solution
  1. Substituting gives $\dfrac{0}{0}$, so factor: $x^3 - 8 = (x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4)$.
  2. For $x \ne 2$ the fraction is $x^2 + 2x + 4$, and this goes to $4 + 4 + 4$.

$12$

Example 2

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x + 1} - 1}{x}$.

Show solution
  1. Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{x + 1} + 1$: $\dfrac{(x + 1) - 1}{x(\sqrt{x + 1} + 1)} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + 1}$.
  2. This goes to $\dfrac{1}{1 + 1}$.

$\dfrac{1}{2}$

Example 3

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0 + 0} \frac{1}{x}$, $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0 - 0} \frac{1}{x}$. Does $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x}$ exist?

Show solution
  1. From the right $x$ is a small positive number, so $\dfrac{1}{x} \to \infty$. From the left, $\dfrac{1}{x} \to -\infty$.
  2. The two sides disagree.

$\infty$ and $-\infty$; the limit as $x \to 0$ does not exist

Example 4

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(\sqrt{x^2 + x} - x\right)$.

Show solution
  1. "$\infty - \infty$": multiply by $\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x$ to get $\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x}$.
  2. Divide top and bottom by $x$ ($> 0$): $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x}} + 1} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$\dfrac{1}{2}$

Example 5

Find constants $a$, $b$ with $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 + ax + b}{x - 2} = 5$.

Show solution
  1. The bottom goes to $0$ and the limit is finite, so the top must go to $0$ too: $4 + 2a + b = 0$, i.e. $b = -2a - 4$.
  2. Then $x^2 + ax - 2a - 4 = (x - 2)(x + a + 2)$, so the fraction is $x + a + 2 \to a + 4$.
  3. $a + 4 = 5$.

$a = 1$, $b = -6$

For $x \to -\infty$, put $x = -t$ with $t \to \infty$ before pulling $x$ out of a root. $\sqrt{x^2} = |x| = -x$ when $x < 0$, not $x$.
For $\dfrac{0}{0}$: factor and cancel, or rationalise. For $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$: divide by the top power. For $\infty - \infty$: rationalise or factor out the top power. If the bottom goes to $0$ but the limit is finite, the top must go to $0$ as well (Example 5).
  • The limit is about values near $a$, never at $a$.
  • It exists only if the right- and left-hand limits agree.
  • Sum, product and quotient rules plus the squeeze theorem, as for sequences.

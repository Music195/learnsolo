Position Vector of Centroid

The average of the three corners

A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. The three medians meet at the centroid (重心) G, which divides each median in $2 : 1$ from the vertex.

With position vectors, G is simply the average of the three vertices: $\vec{g} = \dfrac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}}{3}$. In coordinates, average the $x$'s and average the $y$'s.

Triangle ABC with A$(\vec{a})$, B$(\vec{b})$, C$(\vec{c})$

$$\vec{g} = \frac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}}{3}, \qquad \overrightarrow{GA} + \overrightarrow{GB} + \overrightarrow{GC} = \vec{0}$$

Coordinates: G$\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3}\right)$.

  1. The midpoint of BC is D$\left(\dfrac{\vec{b} + \vec{c}}{2}\right)$.
  2. G divides AD internally in $2 : 1$: $\vec{g} = \dfrac{1 \cdot \vec{a} + 2 \cdot \dfrac{\vec{b} + \vec{c}}{2}}{3} = \dfrac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}}{3}$.
  3. This is symmetric in $\vec{a}$, $\vec{b}$, $\vec{c}$, so the same point lies on the other two medians: all three meet at G.

Drag A, B and C: the three medians always meet at G

Example 1

Find the centroid of the triangle with vertices A$(2, 1)$, B$(4, 5)$, C$(6, 3)$, and of A$(-1, 2)$, B$(3, -1)$, C$(1, 4)$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1}{3}(12, 9)$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}(3, 5)$.

$(4, 3)$ and $\left(1, \dfrac{5}{3}\right)$

Example 2

A$(1, 2)$, B$(5, 0)$ and the centroid is G$(2, 2)$. Find C.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{c} = 3\vec{g} - \vec{a} - \vec{b} = (6, 6) - (1, 2) - (5, 0)$.

C$(0, 4)$

Example 3

Show that $\overrightarrow{GA} + \overrightarrow{GB} + \overrightarrow{GC} = \vec{0}$.

Show solution
  1. $(\vec{a} - \vec{g}) + (\vec{b} - \vec{g}) + (\vec{c} - \vec{g}) = (\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}) - 3\vec{g}$.
  2. $3\vec{g} = \vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}$, so this is $\vec{0}$.

$\vec{0}$

Example 4

D, E, F are the midpoints of BC, CA, AB. Show that triangle DEF has the same centroid as triangle ABC.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{d} + \vec{e} + \vec{f} = \dfrac{\vec{b} + \vec{c}}{2} + \dfrac{\vec{c} + \vec{a}}{2} + \dfrac{\vec{a} + \vec{b}}{2} = \vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}$.
  2. Divide by $3$: both centroids are $\dfrac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}}{3}$.

Same centroid G

G divides a median in $2 : 1$ from the vertex: AG is twice GD, not the other way round.
To find a missing vertex from the centroid, rearrange $3\vec{g} = \vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}$ (Example 2). No need to set up unknown coordinates.
  • $\vec{g} = \dfrac{\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c}}{3}$.
  • G divides each median $2 : 1$ from the vertex.
  • $\overrightarrow{GA} + \overrightarrow{GB} + \overrightarrow{GC} = \vec{0}$.

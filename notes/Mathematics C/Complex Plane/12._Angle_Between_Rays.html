Angle Between Rays

Divide the two arrows

From the vertex A, the arrows to B and to C are $\beta - \alpha$ and $\gamma - \alpha$. Dividing one by the other subtracts their arguments, so the quotient's argument is the angle from AB round to AC. Its modulus is the ratio of the lengths, $\dfrac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}}$.

So a single complex number, $\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}$, describes the whole shape at A: both the angle and the ratio of the sides.

Three different points A($\alpha$), B($\beta$), C($\gamma$)

$$\text{angle from AB to AC} = \arg\frac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}, \qquad \left|\frac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}\right| = \frac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}}$$

Taking the argument in $-\pi < \theta \le \pi$: positive means anticlockwise from AB to AC. The size of the (unsigned) angle BAC is $|\theta|$.

  1. $\arg(\beta - \alpha)$ is the direction of the arrow AB, and $\arg(\gamma - \alpha)$ is the direction of AC.
  2. By note 06, $\arg\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} = \arg(\gamma - \alpha) - \arg(\beta - \alpha)$, the turn from AB to AC.
  3. The modulus is $\dfrac{|\gamma - \alpha|}{|\beta - \alpha|} = \dfrac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}}$.

Example 1

A($1 + i$), B($3 + 2i$), C($2 + 4i$). Find $\angle$BAC and $\dfrac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} = \dfrac{1 + 3i}{2 + i} = \dfrac{(1 + 3i)(2 - i)}{5} = \dfrac{5 + 5i}{5} = 1 + i$.
  2. $1 + i = \sqrt{2}\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$.

$\angle\text{BAC} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}} = \sqrt{2}$

Example 2

B($1 + i$), C($(1 - \sqrt{3}) + (1 + \sqrt{3})i$). Find $\angle$BOC and $\dfrac{\text{OC}}{\text{OB}}$.

Show solution
  1. The vertex is O ($\alpha = 0$): $\dfrac{\gamma}{\beta} = \dfrac{\{(1 - \sqrt{3}) + (1 + \sqrt{3})i\}(1 - i)}{2} = \dfrac{2 + 2\sqrt{3}i}{2}$.
  2. $1 + \sqrt{3}i = 2\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$.

$\angle\text{BOC} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{\text{OC}}{\text{OB}} = 2$

Example 3

$\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} = \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{3}i}{2}$. What shape is triangle ABC?

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1 + \sqrt{3}i}{2} = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}$: modulus $1$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
  2. So AB = AC and $\angle$BAC $= 60°$.

An equilateral triangle

Example 4

$\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} = 1 + i$. Find the angles of triangle ABC.

Show solution
  1. $1 + i = \sqrt{2}\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$: $\angle A = 45°$ and AC $= \sqrt{2}\,$AB.
  2. With AB $= 1$ and AC $= \sqrt{2}$ at $45°$, the cosine rule gives BC $= 1$. So the triangle is isosceles with BA = BC.

$\angle A = 45°$, $\angle C = 45°$, $\angle B = 90°$

$\arg\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}$ is the angle from AB to AC. Swapping B and C changes its sign. The vertex $\alpha$ must be subtracted in both the top and the bottom.
Write the quotient in polar form, $r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$. Then $\theta$ is the angle at A and $r = \dfrac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}}$. Together they usually pin down the triangle's shape.
  • Angle from AB to AC: $\arg\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}$.
  • Its modulus is $\dfrac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}}$.
  • $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}$: equilateral; $\pm i$: right isosceles at A.

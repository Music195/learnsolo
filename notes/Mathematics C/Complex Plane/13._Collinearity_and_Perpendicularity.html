Collinearity and Perpendicularity

Real ratio: same line. Purely imaginary ratio: right angle.

From note 12, the argument of $w = \dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}$ is the angle from AB to AC. If that angle is $0$ or $\pi$, the three points lie on one line, and $w$ is real.

If the angle is $\pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, then AB ⊥ AC, and $w$ is purely imaginary. Both tests reduce a geometry question to checking the real or imaginary part of one number.

Three different points A($\alpha$), B($\beta$), C($\gamma$)

$$\text{A, B, C on one line} \iff \frac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} \text{ is real}$$
$$\text{AB} \perp \text{AC} \iff \frac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} \text{ is purely imaginary}$$

Test with conjugates if useful: $w$ real $\iff \overline{w} = w$; $\;w$ purely imaginary $\iff \overline{w} = -w$, $w \ne 0$ (note 03).

  1. $w = \dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} \ne 0$ has argument $\theta$, the angle from AB to AC.
  2. On one line: $\theta = 0$ or $\pi$, i.e. $w = r(\pm 1)$, a nonzero real number.
  3. Perpendicular: $\theta = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, i.e. $w = r(\pm i)$, a purely imaginary number.

Example 1

A($1 + i$), B($3 + 2i$), C($x + 4i$) lie on one line. Find the real number $x$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} = \dfrac{(x - 1) + 3i}{2 + i} = \dfrac{\{(x - 1) + 3i\}(2 - i)}{5} = \dfrac{(2x + 1) + (7 - x)i}{5}$.
  2. Real: $7 - x = 0$.

$x = 7$

Example 2

For the same A and B, find $x$ so that AB ⊥ AC.

Show solution
  1. Purely imaginary: real part $2x + 1 = 0$ (and then the imaginary part is $\dfrac{15}{2} \ne 0$).

$x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

Example 3

Are A($1 + i$), B($3 + i$), C($2 + 3i$) on one line? Is the angle at A a right angle?

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} = \dfrac{1 + 2i}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} + i$: neither real nor purely imaginary.
  2. So they are not on one line, and the angle at A is not $90°$. In fact AC = BC $= \sqrt{5}$: the triangle is isosceles, not right-angled.

Example 4

$z \ne 0$ and $z \ne 1$. Show that if $\dfrac{z - 1}{z}$ is purely imaginary, then $z$ lies on the circle with diameter joining $0$ and $1$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{z - 1}{z - 0}$ is purely imaginary, so at the point $z$ the lines to $0$ and to $1$ are perpendicular.
  2. A right angle subtending the segment from $0$ to $1$ means $z$ is on the circle with that diameter (Thales' theorem).

$z$ is on the circle $\left|z - \dfrac{1}{2}\right| = \dfrac{1}{2}$, excluding $0$ and $1$

"Purely imaginary" means real part $0$ and imaginary part not $0$. If both are $0$, then C = A, and there is no angle. Check this when you solve for a parameter.
Write the ratio as $a + bi$. Then collinearity is the single equation $b = 0$, and perpendicularity is $a = 0$ with $b \ne 0$.
  • $\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}$ real $\iff$ A, B, C on one line.
  • $\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha}$ purely imaginary $\iff$ AB ⊥ AC.
  • Both come from the argument of the ratio (note 12).

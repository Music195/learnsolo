Logarithm Properties

Exponent laws, seen through logs

Multiplying powers adds exponents, and logs are exponents. So the log of a product is the sum of the logs, the log of a quotient is the difference, and the log of a power brings the power down in front.

Rules ($a > 0$, $a \ne 1$, $M, N > 0$, $k$ real)

$$\log_a MN = \log_a M + \log_a N, \qquad \log_a \frac{M}{N} = \log_a M - \log_a N, \qquad \log_a M^k = k\log_a M$$
  1. Let $p = \log_a M$, $q = \log_a N$, so $M = a^p$, $N = a^q$.
  2. $MN = a^p a^q = a^{p+q}$, so $\log_a MN = p + q$.
  3. Similarly $\dfrac{M}{N} = a^{p-q}$ and $M^k = a^{kp}$ give the other two rules.

Check the rules with numbers (base $a$)

Example 1

Calculate $\log_6 4 + \log_6 9$.

Show solution
  1. $\log_6 (4 \times 9) = \log_6 36$

$2$

Example 2

Calculate $2\log_3 6 - \log_3 4$.

Show solution
  1. $\log_3 6^2 - \log_3 4 = \log_3 \dfrac{36}{4} = \log_3 9$

$2$

Example 3

Calculate $\log_2 \sqrt{12} - \dfrac{1}{2}\log_2 3$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1}{2}\log_2 12 - \dfrac{1}{2}\log_2 3 = \dfrac{1}{2}\log_2 4$

$1$

Example 4

With $\log_{10} 2 = a$ and $\log_{10} 3 = b$, write $\log_{10} 18$ and $\log_{10} 5$ in terms of $a, b$.

Show solution
  1. $18 = 2 \cdot 3^2$: $\log_{10} 18 = a + 2b$.
  2. $5 = \dfrac{10}{2}$: $\log_{10} 5 = 1 - a$.

$a + 2b$; $\;1 - a$

There is no rule for a sum: $\log_a (M + N) \ne \log_a M + \log_a N$. Also $(\log_a M)^2 \ne 2\log_a M$.
Break numbers into primes ($18 = 2 \cdot 3^2$) and use the rules to write everything with $\log 2$, $\log 3$, … This is how log tables are used.
  • Product → sum, quotient → difference, power → multiply.
  • They come from the exponent laws.
  • No rule for $\log(M + N)$.

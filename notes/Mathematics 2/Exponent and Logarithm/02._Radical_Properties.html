Radical Properties

$\sqrt[n]{a}$: the number whose $n$th power is $a$

A number $x$ with $x^n = a$ is an $n$th root of $a$. Among the real ones, $\sqrt[n]{a}$ means: the positive one when $a > 0$, and (for odd $n$) the negative one when $a < 0$.

Example: $(-2)^3 = -8$, so $\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2$. But $x^4 = -16$ has no real solution, because a 4th power is never negative.

Properties ($a, b > 0$; $m, n, p$ positive whole numbers)

$$\sqrt[n]{a}\sqrt[n]{b} = \sqrt[n]{ab}, \qquad \frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}} = \sqrt[n]{\frac{a}{b}}, \qquad \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$$
$$\sqrt[m]{\sqrt[n]{a}} = \sqrt[mn]{a}, \qquad \sqrt[np]{a^{mp}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$$

All of these follow from $\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{\frac{1}{n}}$ and the exponent laws (note 01).

Real solutions of $x^n = a$

Example 1

Find $\sqrt[3]{64}$, $\;\sqrt[4]{81}$ and $\sqrt[3]{-125}$.

Show solution
  1. $4^3 = 64$, $\;3^4 = 81$, $\;(-5)^3 = -125$.

$4$, $\;3$, $\;-5$

Example 2

Calculate $\sqrt[3]{4} \times \sqrt[3]{16}$ and $\sqrt[3]{54} - \sqrt[3]{16}$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$.
  2. $\sqrt[3]{27 \cdot 2} - \sqrt[3]{8 \cdot 2} = 3\sqrt[3]{2} - 2\sqrt[3]{2}$.

$4$, $\;\sqrt[3]{2}$

Example 3

Simplify $\sqrt[6]{8}$ and $\sqrt{\sqrt[3]{64}}$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt[6]{2^3} = 2^{\frac{3}{6}} = 2^{\frac{1}{2}}$.
  2. $\sqrt[6]{64} = \sqrt[6]{2^6}$.

$\sqrt{2}$, $\;2$

$\sqrt[4]{-16}$ is not a real number. Odd roots of negatives are fine ($\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2$), even roots are not.
To simplify $\sqrt[3]{n}$, look for cube factors (8, 27, 64, 125, …) and take their cube roots outside, just as you take square factors out of $\sqrt{\ }$.
  • $\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{\frac{1}{n}}$.
  • Odd $n$: exactly one real $n$th root. Even $n$: two ($\pm$) if $a > 0$, none if $a < 0$.
  • Combine roots by converting to exponents.

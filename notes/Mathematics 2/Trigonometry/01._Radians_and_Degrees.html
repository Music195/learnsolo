Radians and Degrees

Measure an angle by the arc it cuts off

On a circle of radius $1$, the angle whose arc has length $1$ is called 1 radian. A full turn has arc length $2\pi$, so a full turn is $2\pi$ radians $= 360°$.

A general angle can be any size: anticlockwise turns are positive, clockwise turns negative, and adding a full turn $2\pi$ gives the same direction.

Converting

$$180° = \pi \text{ rad}, \qquad 1° = \frac{\pi}{180} \text{ rad}, \qquad 1 \text{ rad} = \frac{180°}{\pi} \approx 57.3°$$

General angle: $\theta + 2n\pi$ ($n$ an integer) all point in the same direction. "rad" is usually not written.

  1. The circumference of a circle of radius $1$ is $2\pi$, so a full turn ($360°$) has arc length $2\pi$, i.e. it is $2\pi$ radians.
  2. Halving: $180° = \pi$. Dividing by $180$: $1° = \dfrac{\pi}{180}$.

Example 1

Convert $120°$ and $-45°$ to radians, and $\dfrac{5}{4}\pi$ to degrees.

Show solution
  1. $120 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{2}{3}\pi$; $\;-45 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
  2. $\dfrac{5}{4} \times 180° = 225°$.

$\dfrac{2}{3}\pi$, $\;-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\;225°$

Example 2

Write $\dfrac{19}{6}\pi$ as $\alpha + 2n\pi$ with $0 \le \alpha < 2\pi$. Which quadrant is it in?

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{19}{6}\pi = \dfrac{7}{6}\pi + 2\pi$.
  2. $\pi < \dfrac{7}{6}\pi < \dfrac{3}{2}\pi$: third quadrant.

$\dfrac{7}{6}\pi + 2\pi$; third quadrant

Example 3

Which of $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{5}{3}\pi$, $\dfrac{11}{3}\pi$ point in the same direction?

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{5}{3}\pi = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2\pi$ and $\dfrac{11}{3}\pi = -\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 4\pi$.

All three

$\theta$ without a degree sign means radians: $\sin 1$ is the sine of $1$ radian ($\approx 57.3°$), not of $1°$.
Learn the common ones: $30° = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $45° = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $60° = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $90° = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
  • $\pi = 180°$.
  • Degrees → radians: $\times \dfrac{\pi}{180}$; radians → degrees: $\times \dfrac{180}{\pi}$.
  • $\theta$ and $\theta + 2n\pi$ point the same way.

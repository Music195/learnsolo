Probability in Repeated Trials (Binomial)

Exactly $r$ successes in $n$ tries

Repeat the same trial $n$ times, independently. Each time, "success" has probability $p$ and "failure" has probability $1 - p$.

One particular order, such as SSF (success, success, failure), has probability $p \cdot p \cdot (1-p)$. Every order with 2 successes has the same probability, and there are ${}_3\mathrm{C}_2 = 3$ such orders (SSF, SFS, FSS). So multiply the probability of one order by the number of orders.

Repeated trials

$$P(\text{exactly } r \text{ successes}) = {}_n\mathrm{C}_r \; p^r \, (1-p)^{n-r}$$

${}_n\mathrm{C}_r$: which $r$ of the $n$ tries are successes. $\;p^r$: those $r$ succeed. $\;(1-p)^{n-r}$: the rest fail.

The formula needs the same $p$ every time and independent tries (dice, coins, drawing with replacement). It does not apply to drawing without replacement.

All the probabilities for $r = 0, 1, \dots, n$

Example 1

A fair coin is tossed 5 times. Find the probability of exactly 3 heads.

Show solution
  1. $n = 5$, $r = 3$, $p = \dfrac{1}{2}$
  2. ${}_5\mathrm{C}_3 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 10 \times \dfrac{1}{32}$

$\dfrac{5}{16}$

Example 2

A die is rolled 4 times. Find the probability that a number $\le 2$ appears exactly twice.

Show solution
  1. Success = 1 or 2, so $p = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$
  2. ${}_4\mathrm{C}_2 \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2 = 6 \times \dfrac{1}{9} \times \dfrac{4}{9}$

$\dfrac{24}{81} = \dfrac{8}{27}$

Example 3

A die is rolled 5 times. Find the probability that a 6 appears at least 4 times.

Show solution
  1. "At least 4" = exactly 4 or exactly 5 (mutually exclusive, so add)
  2. Exactly 4: ${}_5\mathrm{C}_4 \left(\dfrac{1}{6}\right)^4 \dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{25}{7776}$
  3. Exactly 5: $\left(\dfrac{1}{6}\right)^5 = \dfrac{1}{7776}$

$\dfrac{26}{7776} = \dfrac{13}{3888}$

Example 4

40% of people prefer brand A. If 12 people are asked, find the probability that exactly 5 prefer brand A.

Show solution
  1. $n = 12$, $r = 5$, $p = 0.4$
  2. ${}_{12}\mathrm{C}_5 = 792$, $\;0.4^5 = 0.01024$, $\;0.6^7 \approx 0.02799$
  3. $792 \times 0.01024 \times 0.02799$

$\approx 0.227$

For "at least once", don't add many cases. Use the complement: $1 - (1-p)^n$.
  • Exactly $r$ successes in $n$ independent tries: ${}_n\mathrm{C}_r\, p^r (1-p)^{n-r}$.
  • ${}_n\mathrm{C}_r$ counts the orders; each order has the same probability.
  • "At least $k$": add the exact cases, or use the complement if that is shorter.

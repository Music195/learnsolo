Counting Permutations

Arranging in order

A permutation is an arrangement where order matters: ABC and BAC are different.

Arranging 3 of the 4 letters A, B, C, D: 4 choices for the 1st place, then 3 left for the 2nd, then 2 for the 3rd. By the multiplication rule there are $4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24$ arrangements.

Arrange $r$ of $n$ different things

$${}_n\mathrm{P}_r = \underbrace{n(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-r+1)}_{r \text{ factors}} = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$$

Start at $n$ and multiply $r$ numbers, counting down.

Arrange all $n$

$${}_n\mathrm{P}_n = n! = n(n-1)\cdots 2 \cdot 1$$

By definition $0! = 1$, so that ${}_n\mathrm{P}_n = \dfrac{n!}{0!}$ still works.

See every arrangement

Example 1

From 10 candidates, choose a president, a vice-president and a secretary. How many ways?

Show solution
  1. The three roles are different, so order matters.
  2. ${}_{10}\mathrm{P}_3 = 10 \times 9 \times 8$

$720$

Example 2

How many 3-digit even numbers can be made from the digits $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ without repeating a digit?

Show solution
  1. Fill the restricted place first. Units digit: $2$ or $4$, so 2 choices
  2. The other two places use 2 of the remaining 4 digits: ${}_4\mathrm{P}_2 = 12$

$2 \times 12 = 24$

Example 3

5 people stand in a row. In how many arrangements are A and B next to each other?

Show solution
  1. Tie A and B together as one block. Now arrange 4 items (the block + 3 people): $4! = 24$
  2. Inside the block, A and B can be AB or BA: $2! = 2$

$24 \times 2 = 48$

With a condition, place the restricted items first (Example 2), or tie items that must be together into one block (Example 3). For "not next to each other", subtract: total − together.
Permutations are for different items where order matters. If order doesn't matter (choosing a team), use combinations; if items repeat (letters of "BANANA"), see the note on identical items.
  • ${}_n\mathrm{P}_r = n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1) = \dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!}$; $\;{}_n\mathrm{P}_n = n!$; $\;0! = 1$.
  • Place restricted items first; tie "together" items into a block.
  • "Not together" = total − together.

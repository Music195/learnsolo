Probability of Event A

Favourable out of possible

List every possible result of an experiment (the sample space $U$). If every result is equally likely, the probability of an event $A$ is the fraction of results that are in $A$.

Rolling a die, $U = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$. For "an even number", $A = \{2, 4, 6\}$, so $P(A) = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Probability (equally likely outcomes)

$$P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(U)} = \frac{\text{number of outcomes in } A}{\text{number of all outcomes}}$$

Always $0 \le P(A) \le 1$. $\;P(\varnothing) = 0$ (impossible), $\;P(U) = 1$ (certain).

Treat identical things as different when listing outcomes. With two dice, "sum 2" and "sum 7" are not equally likely: sum 2 happens only as $(1, 1)$, sum 7 happens 6 ways. Use the 36 ordered pairs, not the 11 sums.

Roll two dice many times

Example 1

Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the sum is 8.

Show solution
  1. All outcomes: $6 \times 6 = 36$ ordered pairs
  2. Sum 8: $(2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2)$, so 5 outcomes

$P = \dfrac{5}{36}$

Example 2

A bag has 5 red and 3 white balls. Two balls are taken out together. Find the probability that both are red.

Show solution
  1. Treat all 8 balls as different. All ways to take 2: ${}_8\mathrm{C}_2 = 28$
  2. Ways to take 2 red: ${}_5\mathrm{C}_2 = 10$

$P = \dfrac{10}{28} = \dfrac{5}{14}$

Example 3

Three coins are tossed. Find the probability of exactly 2 heads.

Show solution
  1. All outcomes: $2^3 = 8$ (HHH, HHT, …)
  2. Exactly 2 heads: HHT, HTH, THH, so 3 outcomes

$P = \dfrac{3}{8}$

Probability problems are usually counting problems in disguise: count $n(U)$ and $n(A)$ with the same method (both ordered, or both unordered) and divide.
  • $P(A) = \dfrac{n(A)}{n(U)}$ when all outcomes are equally likely.
  • $0 \le P(A) \le 1$.
  • Treat identical dice, coins and balls as different so outcomes are equally likely.
  • Count the top and bottom the same way (both ordered or both unordered).

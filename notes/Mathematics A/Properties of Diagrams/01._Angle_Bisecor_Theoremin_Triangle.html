Angle Bisector Theorem in a Triangle

The bisector splits the opposite side in the ratio of the sides

Draw the line that cuts $\angle A$ of $\triangle ABC$ exactly in half. It meets $BC$ at a point $D$. $D$ is closer to the end of the shorter side: the two pieces of $BC$ are in the same ratio as $AB$ and $AC$.

Angle bisector theorem

$$BD : DC = AB : AC$$

$AD$ bisects $\angle A$, $D$ on $BC$. So $BD = BC \times \dfrac{AB}{AB + AC}$.

  1. Through $C$ draw a line parallel to $AD$. It meets line $BA$ (extended past $A$) at $E$.
  2. $\angle ACE = \angle CAD$ (alternate angles) and $\angle AEC = \angle BAD$ (corresponding angles).
  3. $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$, so $\angle ACE = \angle AEC$: $\triangle ACE$ is isosceles and $AE = AC$.
  4. Since $AD \parallel EC$, the line $AD$ cuts $BE$ and $BC$ in the same ratio: $BD : DC = BA : AE = AB : AC$.

Example 1

In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 8$, $AC = 6$, $BC = 7$. The bisector of $\angle A$ meets $BC$ at $D$. Find $BD$ and $DC$.

Show solution
  1. $BD : DC = 8 : 6 = 4 : 3$
  2. $BD = 7 \times \dfrac{4}{7} = 4$, $\;DC = 7 \times \dfrac{3}{7} = 3$

$BD = 4$, $DC = 3$

Example 2

In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 5$, $AC = 4$, $BC = 6$. The bisector of $\angle A$ meets $BC$ at $D$. Find $BD$.

Show solution
  1. $BD : DC = 5 : 4$, so $BD = 6 \times \dfrac{5}{9}$

$BD = \dfrac{10}{3}$

Example 3

In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 6$, $BC = 5$, $CA = 4$. The bisector of $\angle A$ meets $BC$ at $D$, and $I$ is the incenter. Find $AI : ID$.

Show solution
  1. $BD : DC = 6 : 4$, so $BD = 5 \times \dfrac{6}{10} = 3$
  2. $I$ lies on $AD$ and $BI$ bisects $\angle B$. In $\triangle ABD$, use the theorem again: $AI : ID = BA : BD = 6 : 3$

$AI : ID = 2 : 1$

Match the pieces to the sides that touch them: $BD$ goes with $AB$ (both touch $B$), $DC$ goes with $AC$ (both touch $C$).

Law of Sines

Each side over the sine of its opposite angle

In any triangle $ABC$ write $a = BC$, $b = CA$, $c = AB$ (each side is named after the angle opposite it). Divide each side by the sine of its opposite angle: you get the same number every time, and that number is the diameter of the circumcircle.

Law of sines

$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} = 2R$$

$R$: radius of the circumcircle. Also $a : b : c = \sin A : \sin B : \sin C$.

  1. Let $O$ be the circumcentre and draw the diameter $BD$ (length $2R$).
  2. If $A$ is acute, $D$ and $A$ are on the same arc $BC$, so $\angle BDC = \angle A$ (inscribed angles).
  3. $BD$ is a diameter, so $\angle BCD = 90°$. In right triangle $BCD$: $BC = BD \sin\angle BDC$, i.e. $a = 2R\sin A$.
  4. If $A$ is obtuse, $\angle BDC = 180° - A$ and $\sin(180° - A) = \sin A$; if $A = 90°$, $a = 2R$ directly. Same result each time.

Example 1

In $\triangle ABC$, $A = 45°$, $B = 60°$, $a = 10$. Find $b$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{10}{\sin 45°} = \dfrac{b}{\sin 60°}$
  2. $b = 10 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}/2}{\sqrt{2}/2} = \dfrac{10\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}$

$b = 5\sqrt{6}$

Example 2

In $\triangle ABC$, $a = 6$ and $A = 30°$. Find the circumradius $R$.

Show solution
  1. $2R = \dfrac{6}{\sin 30°} = 12$

$R = 6$

Example 3

In $\triangle ABC$, $b = \sqrt{2}$, $c = \sqrt{3}$, $B = 45°$. Find $C$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin C = \dfrac{c \sin B}{b} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
  2. $C = 60°$ or $120°$. Both work, because $45° + 120° < 180°$.

$C = 60°$ or $C = 120°$ (two different triangles)

$\sin C = k$ can give an acute and an obtuse angle. Check each one: it is only valid if the angles still add to less than $180°$.
Use the law of sines when you know a side and its opposite angle, or when the circumradius is involved. With two sides and the angle between them, use the law of cosines (note 07).
  • $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C} = 2R$.
  • $a : b : c = \sin A : \sin B : \sin C$.
  • Finding an angle from its sine may give two answers.

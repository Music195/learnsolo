Average (Mean) of Data

Share the total equally

The mean $\overline{x}$ is what each value would be if the total were shared out equally: add everything up and divide by how many values there are.

It is also the balance point: put the values as weights on a ruler and it balances at $\overline{x}$.

Mean

$$\overline{x} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_n}{n}$$

$n$: number of values.

Mean from a frequency table

$$\overline{x} = \frac{x_1 f_1 + x_2 f_2 + \cdots + x_k f_k}{N}$$

Value $x_i$ appears $f_i$ times; $N = f_1 + \cdots + f_k$ is the total count.

Test scores: 85, 92, 78, 96, 88. Find the mean.

Show solution
  1. Total $= 439$, $n = 5$

$\overline{x} = 87.8$

Example 2

Study hours: 2 h (5 students), 4 h (8), 6 h (6), 8 h (3). Find the mean.

Show solution
  1. $2 \cdot 5 + 4 \cdot 8 + 6 \cdot 6 + 8 \cdot 3 = 10 + 32 + 36 + 24 = 102$
  2. $N = 5 + 8 + 6 + 3 = 22$

$\overline{x} = \dfrac{102}{22} \approx 4.64$ hours

Example 3

The mean of 8 numbers is 25. One number is removed and the mean of the rest is 22. Find the removed number.

Show solution
  1. Total before: $8 \times 25 = 200$. Total after: $7 \times 22 = 154$.

$200 - 154 = 46$

Provisional mean (仮平均): pick a convenient value $a$ near the middle, average the differences $x - a$, then add $a$ back. For 498, 503, 501, 497, 506 with $a = 500$: differences $-2, 3, 1, -3, 6$ average to $1$, so $\overline{x} = 501$.
One extreme value can drag the mean a long way. For 2, 4, 6, 8, 100 the mean is 24, though most values are under 10. Compare with the median (note 02).
  • $\overline{x} = \dfrac{\text{total}}{n}$; with frequencies, $\dfrac{\sum x_i f_i}{N}$.
  • Total $= n \times \overline{x}$: handy for "removed / added value" problems.
  • The differences from the mean always add to 0.

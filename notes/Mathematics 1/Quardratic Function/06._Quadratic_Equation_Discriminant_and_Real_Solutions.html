Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions

The part under the root decides how many solutions

In the quadratic formula, $\pm\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$ is what splits the answer into two. If that number is positive there are two solutions; if it is $0$ the two collapse into one; if it is negative there is no real square root, so there are no real solutions.

Discriminant of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$

$$D = b^2 - 4ac$$

$D > 0$: two different real solutions. $\;D = 0$: one repeated solution (重解) $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$. $\;D < 0$: no real solutions. With $b = 2b'$ you may use $\dfrac{D}{4} = b'^2 - ac$ instead.

Change $c$ and watch $D$

Example 1

How many real solutions does each equation have? (i) $2x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0$ (ii) $4x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$ (iii) $x^2 - 2x + 3 = 0$

Show solution
  1. (i) $D = 25 - 16 = 9 > 0$
  2. (ii) $D/4 = 4 - 4 = 0$
  3. (iii) $D/4 = 1 - 3 = -2 < 0$

(i) 2   (ii) 1   (iii) 0

Example 2

Find $k$ so that $x^2 + 2kx + k + 2 = 0$ has a repeated solution, and find that solution.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{D}{4} = k^2 - (k + 2) = 0 \Rightarrow (k - 2)(k + 1) = 0$.
  2. Repeated solution $x = -\dfrac{2k}{2} = -k$.

$k = 2$: $x = -2$; $\;k = -1$: $x = 1$

Example 3

Find the range of $m$ for which $x^2 - 4x + m = 0$ has real solutions.

Show solution
  1. "Has real solutions" means $D \ge 0$ (one or two): $\dfrac{D}{4} = 4 - m \ge 0$.

$m \le 4$

"Has real solutions" is $D \ge 0$; "has two different real solutions" is $D > 0$. Read the wording carefully.
$D$ tells you how many times the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ meets the $x$-axis: 2, 1 (touching), or 0. See note 07.
  • $D = b^2 - 4ac$ (or $D/4 = b'^2 - ac$).
  • $D > 0$: 2 solutions, $D = 0$: 1, $D < 0$: none.
  • Conditions on a parameter: set up $D = 0$, $D > 0$ or $D \ge 0$ and solve.

Nuclear Reactions and Energy

In a nuclear reaction the totals of $A$ and $Z$ stay the same; the rest energy that disappears ($\Delta mc^2$) becomes kinetic energy

Balancing. In a chemical equation the atoms stay the same and you balance their numbers. In a nuclear reaction the elements themselves change, so balance the protons and neutrons instead:

  • total mass number $A$ before = total after;
  • total atomic number $Z$ before = total after.

Useful symbols: neutron ${}^1_0n$, proton ${}^1_1\mathrm{H}$, α particle ${}^4_2\mathrm{He}$. An electron appearing in a nuclear equation means β decay ($A$ same, $Z + 1$); it is not like an ion equation in chemistry.

Chain reactions. When ${}^{235}\mathrm{U}$ absorbs a neutron it can split (fission) and give out about 3 neutrons. Each of those can split another uranium nucleus, and so on: a chain reaction.

Energy. Every mass has a rest energy $mc^2$. Normally it never changes form, but in a nuclear reaction some of it turns into kinetic energy:

$$\text{rest energy} + \text{kinetic energy} = \text{constant}$$

That form is hard to use, because the separate energies are rarely given. In almost every reaction the rest energy goes down and the kinetic energy goes up, so use the more practical form:

$$\text{decrease in rest energy} = \text{increase in kinetic energy}$$

Find the decrease from the mass difference, $\Delta m \times 931$ MeV per u. If the reactants hit head-on with equal and opposite momenta (total momentum zero), the two products fly apart with equal and opposite momenta too. Then their speeds and their kinetic energies are both in the inverse ratio of their masses.

Energy released and how two products share it

$$Q = \Delta m\,c^2 = (m_{\text{before}} - m_{\text{after}})c^2, \qquad K_{\text{after}} = K_{\text{before}} + Q, \qquad \frac{K_1}{K_2} = \frac{m_2}{m_1}\ (\text{total momentum } 0)$$

$Q$: energy released (931 MeV per u of mass lost). $K$: kinetic energies. $m_1$, $m_2$: masses of the two products (approximately their mass numbers).

  1. No outside force acts, so momentum is conserved. If the total is 0 before, then after: $m_1v_1 = m_2v_2$ (opposite directions). So $\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{m_2}{m_1}$.
  2. Kinetic energy in terms of momentum: $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$. Both products have the same $p$, so $\dfrac{K_1}{K_2} = \dfrac{m_2}{m_1}$.
  3. So the lighter product takes the larger share. Split the total kinetic energy in the ratio $m_2 : m_1$.

Two fusion reactions. Watch the products share the released energy (red: protons, grey: neutrons)

Example 1

Complete: ${}^2_1\mathrm{H} + {}^2_1\mathrm{H} \to {}^3_1\mathrm{H} + \square$.

Show solution
  1. $A$: $2 + 2 = 4$, so the missing part has $A = 4 - 3 = 1$.
  2. $Z$: $1 + 1 = 2$, so it has $Z = 2 - 1 = 1$: hydrogen.

${}^1_1\mathrm{H}$ (a proton)

Example 2

Complete: ${}^4_2\mathrm{He} + {}^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N} \to \square + {}^1_1\mathrm{H}$.

Show solution
  1. $A$: $4 + 14 = 18$, so $18 - 1 = 17$.
  2. $Z$: $2 + 7 = 9$, so $9 - 1 = 8$: oxygen.

${}^{17}_{8}\mathrm{O}$

Example 3

Complete: ${}^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C} \to \square + e^-$.

Show solution
  1. An electron from a nucleus means β decay: $A$ stays 14, $Z$ goes up by 1 to 7.

${}^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}$

Example 4

Complete the fission: ${}^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U} + {}^1_0n \to {}^{93}_{38}\mathrm{Sr} + {}^{140}_{54}\mathrm{Xe} + x\,{}^1_0n$. What is $x$, and why does this allow a chain reaction?

Show solution
  1. $A$: $235 + 1 = 236 = 93 + 140 + x$, so $x = 3$. ($Z$: $92 = 38 + 54$ ✓; neutrons have $Z = 0$.)
  2. One neutron goes in and three come out; each can split another uranium nucleus, so the reaction can keep itself going.

$x = 3$

Example 5

Two deuterons, each with 2.0 MeV of kinetic energy, collide head-on: ${}^2_1\mathrm{H} + {}^2_1\mathrm{H} \to {}^3_2\mathrm{He} + {}^1_0n$. Masses: ${}^2\mathrm{H}$ 2.0136 u, ${}^3\mathrm{He}$ 3.0150 u, $n$ 1.0087 u (1 u ↔ 931 MeV). Find the energy released, and the kinetic energy of each product. (Take the masses of the products as 3 : 1.)

Show solution
  1. $\Delta m = 2 \times 2.0136 - (3.0150 + 1.0087) = 4.0272 - 4.0237 = 0.0035$ u.
  2. $Q = 931 \times 0.0035 = 3.3$ MeV of rest energy turns into kinetic energy.
  3. Total kinetic energy afterwards: $2.0 + 2.0 + 3.26 = 7.3$ MeV.
  4. Head-on with equal energies and masses, the total momentum is 0, so the energies split in the inverse mass ratio, He : n = 1 : 3. He: $7.26 \times \tfrac14 = 1.8$ MeV; n: $7.26 \times \tfrac34 = 5.4$ MeV.

3.3 MeV released; ³He 1.8 MeV, neutron 5.4 MeV

The kinetic energy after the reaction is the released energy plus any kinetic energy the reactants already had. Leaving out the starting 2.0 + 2.0 MeV in Example 5 is a common slip.
"Head-on with equal speeds" or "starting at rest" means total momentum 0. Then split the kinetic energy in the inverse ratio of the masses, using the mass numbers.
  • Balance $A$ and $Z$ separately.
  • Decrease in rest energy ($\Delta m \times 931$ MeV) = increase in kinetic energy.
  • Total momentum 0: kinetic energies in the inverse ratio of masses.

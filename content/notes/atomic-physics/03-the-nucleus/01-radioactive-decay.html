Radioactive Decay

Unstable nuclei give out α (a helium nucleus), β (an electron) or γ (light); each changes the mass number and atomic number in its own way

Writing a nucleus. ${}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}$: X is the element's symbol, $Z$ the atomic number (number of protons) and $A$ the mass number (protons + neutrons).

Some nuclei are unstable and change by themselves, giving out radiation. The three kinds were named before anyone knew what they were; now we do:

  • α decay: an α particle is a helium nucleus ${}^4_2\mathrm{He}$ (2 protons + 2 neutrons). The nucleus loses them: $A$ drops by 4, $Z$ by 2. α rays carry positive charge. $${}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \to {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}\mathrm{Y} + {}^4_2\mathrm{He}$$
  • β decay: a β particle is an electron. A nucleus has no electrons, so where does it come from? A neutron turns into a proton and an electron, and the electron is thrown out. One more proton, one less neutron: $Z$ goes up by 1, $A$ stays the same. β rays carry negative charge. $${}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \to {}^{A}_{Z+1}\mathrm{Y} + e^-$$
  • γ decay: γ rays are electromagnetic waves (very short-wavelength light), with no charge. Nothing leaves the nucleus but energy: it stays the same element with the same $A$ and $Z$, in a lower energy state.

In a field. α and β are charged, so an electric field bends them (α towards the negative plate, β towards the positive plate) and a magnetic field turns them in circles (the Lorentz force) in opposite directions. γ has no charge and goes straight on in both.

What each decay does to $A$ and $Z$

$$\alpha:\ A - 4,\ Z - 2 \qquad \beta:\ A \text{ same},\ Z + 1 \qquad \gamma:\ \text{no change}$$

$A$: mass number (protons + neutrons). $Z$: atomic number (protons). α = ${}^4_2\mathrm{He}$ nucleus (charge $+2e$), β = electron (charge $-e$), γ = electromagnetic wave (no charge).

  1. Only α decay changes $A$, by 4 each time. So in a chain, number of α decays = (drop in $A$) ÷ 4.
  2. Each α lowers $Z$ by 2 and each β raises it by 1. So $Z_{\text{end}} = Z_{\text{start}} - 2(\text{number of }\alpha) + (\text{number of }\beta)$.
  3. Find the α's from $A$ first, then the β's from $Z$. (γ decays don't count: they change neither.)

Example 1

Radium ${}^{226}_{88}\mathrm{Ra}$ undergoes α decay. What nucleus is left?

Show solution
  1. $A$: $226 - 4 = 222$. $Z$: $88 - 2 = 86$, which is radon.

${}^{222}_{86}\mathrm{Rn}$

Example 2

Carbon-14 (${}^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C}$) undergoes β decay. What nucleus is formed?

Show solution
  1. $A$ stays 14. $Z$: $6 + 1 = 7$, which is nitrogen.

${}^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}$

Example 3

${}^{238}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ becomes ${}^{206}_{82}\mathrm{Pb}$ after a series of decays. How many α decays and β decays happen?

Show solution
  1. $A$ drops by $238 - 206 = 32$, so there are $32 / 4 = 8$ α decays.
  2. 8 α decays alone would give $Z = 92 - 16 = 76$. The end is 82, so $82 - 76 = 6$ β decays.

8 α decays and 6 β decays

Example 4

α, β and γ rays move to the right into a uniform magnetic field pointing out of the page. Which way does each turn?

Show solution
  1. For a positive charge, the force is along $\vec v \times \vec B$: right × out of the page = down the page. So α curves downwards (in a circle).
  2. β is negative: the force is the other way, so it curves upwards (more tightly, being much lighter).
  3. γ has no charge: straight on.

α turns down, β turns up, γ goes straight

Example 5

${}^{232}_{90}\mathrm{Th}$ undergoes one α decay and then two β decays. What is the final nucleus?

Show solution
  1. α: ${}^{228}_{88}\mathrm{Ra}$. β: ${}^{228}_{89}\mathrm{Ac}$. β: ${}^{228}_{90}\mathrm{Th}$.
  2. It is thorium again, but a lighter isotope.

${}^{228}_{90}\mathrm{Th}$

β decay does not mean an orbiting electron leaves. The electron is made in the nucleus when a neutron turns into a proton, which is why $Z$ goes up by 1.
In decay-chain questions, count α decays from the mass number first; β decays then follow from the atomic number.
  • α = ${}^4_2\mathrm{He}$: $A - 4$, $Z - 2$, positive.
  • β = electron (neutron → proton + electron): $Z + 1$, $A$ same, negative.
  • γ = electromagnetic wave: no change, no charge, not deflected.

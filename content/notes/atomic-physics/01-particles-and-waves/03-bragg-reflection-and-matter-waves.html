Bragg Reflection and Matter Waves

Waves reflected by layers of atoms reinforce when $2d\sin\theta = n\lambda$; electrons do this too, with wavelength $\lambda = h/(mv)$

In a crystal the atoms sit in neat rows, so they form flat layers called atomic planes, a distance $d$ apart. X-rays shone on a crystal are reflected a little by every plane. The reflections from different planes interfere, and in certain directions they reinforce: this is Bragg reflection.

Two points to watch:

  • the angle $\theta$ is measured from the plane (the surface), not from the normal as in the Waves course;
  • the ray reflected by the lower plane travels an extra $d\sin\theta$ going down and $d\sin\theta$ coming up: an extra $2d\sin\theta$ in total.

The surprise came when electrons were fired at crystals instead of X-rays: they showed the same strong reflections. Interference is a wave property, so electrons, the classic particle, also behave as waves.

That fits a pattern. Light (a wave) turned out to act as particles in the photoelectric and Compton effects. Physics looks for rules that hold for everything, so if waves can act as particles, particles should act as waves. De Broglie gave the wavelength of such a matter wave: take the photon rule $p = h/\lambda$ and use the particle's momentum $mv$. In exams this is almost always about electrons.

Bragg condition and de Broglie wavelength

$$2d\sin\theta = n\lambda \ (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots), \qquad \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}$$

$d$: spacing of the atomic planes (m). $\theta$: angle between the ray and the plane. $m$, $v$: mass and speed of the particle. $V$: voltage that accelerated an electron from rest ($\tfrac12mv^2 = eV$).

  1. Two parallel rays meet the crystal at angle $\theta$: one reflects at the top plane, the other at the plane below, a distance $d$ deeper.
  2. Drop perpendiculars from the top reflection point onto the lower ray. The two pieces of the lower ray between them are each $d\sin\theta$ long (the angle at the lower point is $\theta$, the hypotenuse is $d$).
  3. Everything else is the same for both rays, so the extra path is $2d\sin\theta$.
  4. Reinforcement when the extra path is a whole number of wavelengths: $2d\sin\theta = n\lambda$. Every deeper plane adds the same extra path again, so all of them reinforce together.
  5. For an electron: $p = mv$ and $\lambda = h/p$. If it was accelerated from rest by $V$, $\tfrac12mv^2 = eV$ gives $mv = \sqrt{2meV}$.

Rays reflected by two atomic planes ($d = 2.0\times10^{-10}$ m). Right: reflected strength against $\theta$

Example 1

X-rays of wavelength $1.5\times10^{-10}$ m fall on a crystal with plane spacing $2.0\times10^{-10}$ m. At what angle $\theta$ does the first strong reflection ($n = 1$) appear?

Show solution
  1. $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{2d} = \dfrac{1.5}{4.0} = 0.375$.
  2. $\theta = 22°$.

About 22°

Example 2

The first strong reflection of X-rays of wavelength $1.5\times10^{-10}$ m is found at $\theta = 30°$. Find the plane spacing.

Show solution
  1. $d = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\sin\theta} = \dfrac{1.5\times10^{-10}}{2 \times 0.5}$.

$1.5\times10^{-10}$ m

Example 3

Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at $1.0\times10^6$ m/s. ($h = 6.6\times10^{-34}$ J·s, $m = 9.1\times10^{-31}$ kg)

Show solution
  1. $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv} = \dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}}{9.1\times10^{-31} \times 1.0\times10^6}$.

$7.3\times10^{-10}$ m

Example 4

An electron starts from rest and is accelerated through 150 V. Find its wavelength. ($e = 1.6\times10^{-19}$ C)

Show solution
  1. $mv = \sqrt{2meV} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.1\times10^{-31} \times 1.6\times10^{-19} \times 150} = 6.6\times10^{-24}$ kg·m/s.
  2. $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv} = \dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}}{6.6\times10^{-24}} = 1.0\times10^{-10}$ m: about the size of an atom, so crystals diffract it.

$1.0\times10^{-10}$ m

Example 5

The accelerating voltage in Example 4 is made 4 times as large. What happens to the wavelength, and to the angle of the first strong reflection from planes $2.0\times10^{-10}$ m apart?

Show solution
  1. $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt V}$, so 4 times the voltage halves the wavelength: $0.50\times10^{-10}$ m.
  2. Before: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1.0}{4.0} = 0.25$, $\theta = 14.5°$. After: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{0.50}{4.0} = 0.125$, $\theta = 7.2°$.

Wavelength halves; the angle drops from about 14.5° to about 7.2°

In Bragg reflection, $\theta$ is the angle to the atomic plane, not to the normal. If a problem gives the angle to the normal, use $90° - $ that angle.
Don't memorise $\lambda = h/(mv)$ separately: it is the photon's $p = h/\lambda$ turned round, with $p = mv$.
  • Bragg: $2d\sin\theta = n\lambda$, with $\theta$ measured from the plane.
  • Electrons show the same reflections, so particles also act as waves.
  • De Broglie wavelength $\lambda = h/(mv) = h/\sqrt{2meV}$.

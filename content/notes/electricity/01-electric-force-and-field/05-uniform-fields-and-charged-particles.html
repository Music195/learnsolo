Uniform Fields and Charged Particles

Between charged plates the field is the same everywhere, so a particle moves like a falling ball

A charged plane. Spread charge evenly over a large flat sheet. Each bit of charge sends field out in all directions. Next to the sheet, the sideways parts from neighbouring bits cancel, leaving only the part perpendicular to the sheet. The field lines are straight and evenly spaced, and they stay that way however far you go. A field that is the same size and direction everywhere is called a uniform field. Two parallel plates with $+Q$ and $-Q$ make a uniform field between them.

Same maths as gravity. Near the ground, gravity is a uniform field too:

  • gravity: force $mg$; lifting through height $h$ takes work $mgh$, so $U = mgh$;
  • uniform field: force $qE$; moving against the field through distance $d$ takes work $qEd$, so $U = qEd$.

For $q = +1$ C the energy $Ed$ is the potential difference, so $V = Ed$. Also $U = qV$, the same rule as for point charges.

A charged particle between plates feels a constant force $qE$, so it has a constant acceleration $a = qE/m$. It moves exactly like an object in free fall, so all the constant-acceleration formulas work. Gravity also acts, but for an electron or a proton it is millions of times smaller than $qE$, so we ignore it. (A charged steel ball is a different story: there gravity often matters.)

In a uniform field

$$V = Ed \qquad U = qV \qquad a = \frac{qE}{m} \qquad \tfrac12 mv^2 = qV$$

$E$: field (N/C = V/m). $d$: distance measured along the field (m). $V$: potential difference across that distance (V). The last formula gives the speed of a particle that starts at rest and crosses a potential difference $V$.

  1. A particle (charge $q$, mass $m$) starts at rest at one plate and crosses the gap $d$ to the other.
  2. Kinematics: $d = \tfrac12 at^2$ gives $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2md}{qE}}$, and then $v = at = \dfrac{qE}{m}\sqrt{\dfrac{2md}{qE}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2qEd}{m}}$.
  3. Energy: the field does work $qEd = qV$, which becomes kinetic energy: $\tfrac12 mv^2 = qEd$, so again $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2qEd}{m}}$.
  4. Both routes agree. If you only need the speed, energy is faster. If you need the time, use kinematics.

Plates 0.020 m apart. Released from rest (the motion is slowed down)

Example 1

Two parallel plates 0.010 m apart are connected to 200 V. Find the field between them.

Show solution
  1. $E = V/d = 200/0.010$.

$E = 2.0 \times 10^{4}$ V/m (N/C)

Example 2

An electron starts at rest and is accelerated through 100 V. Find its kinetic energy and speed. (electron mass $9.1 \times 10^{-31}$ kg)

Show solution
  1. $\tfrac12 mv^2 = eV = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 100 = 1.6 \times 10^{-17}$ J.
  2. $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-17}}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}}} = \sqrt{3.5 \times 10^{13}}$.

$1.6 \times 10^{-17}$ J; $v \approx 5.9 \times 10^{6}$ m/s

Example 3

A proton is released at the positive plate. The plates are 0.020 m apart at 100 V. Find its acceleration and the time to reach the other plate. (proton mass $1.67 \times 10^{-27}$ kg)

Show solution
  1. $E = 100/0.020 = 5.0 \times 10^{3}$ N/C, so $a = \dfrac{qE}{m} = \dfrac{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 5.0 \times 10^{3}}{1.67 \times 10^{-27}} \approx 4.8 \times 10^{11}$ m/s².
  2. $t = \sqrt{2d/a} = \sqrt{\dfrac{0.040}{4.8 \times 10^{11}}} \approx 2.9 \times 10^{-7}$ s.

$a \approx 4.8 \times 10^{11}$ m/s²; $t \approx 2.9 \times 10^{-7}$ s

Example 4

In Example 3, compare the electric force on the proton with its weight. ($g = 9.8$ m/s²)

Show solution
  1. $qE = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 5.0 \times 10^{3} = 8.0 \times 10^{-16}$ N.
  2. $mg = 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 9.8 \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-26}$ N.
  3. Ratio $\approx 5 \times 10^{10}$: gravity is negligible.

The electric force is about $5 \times 10^{10}$ times the weight

Example 5

A particle (mass $m$, charge $q > 0$) is released from rest at the positive plate of a pair $d$ apart with a uniform field $E$. Find (1) its acceleration, (2) the time to reach the negative plate, (3) its speed there.

Show solution
  1. (1) Equation of motion, taking the field direction as positive: $ma = qE$, so $a = qE/m$. It is constant: this is "free fall".
  2. (2) $d = \tfrac12 at^2$, so $t = \sqrt{2md/(qE)}$.
  3. (3) Energy: $qEd = \tfrac12 mv^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2qEd/m}$ (the same as $v = at$).

$a = \dfrac{qE}{m}$, $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2md}{qE}}$, $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2qEd}{m}}$

$V = Ed$ works only in a uniform field, and $d$ must be measured along the field. Moving parallel to the plates (across the field) does not change the potential.
If a question asks only for a speed, use energy ($\tfrac12 mv^2 = qV$) straight away. Use acceleration and kinematics only when it asks for a time.
  • Parallel plates: uniform field, $V = Ed$, $U = qV$.
  • Charged particle: $a = qE/m$, constant, like free fall.
  • Speed after crossing $V$: $\tfrac12 mv^2 = qV$; ignore gravity for particles.

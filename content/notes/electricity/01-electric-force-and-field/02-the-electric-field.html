The Electric Field

The electric field is the force that +1 C would feel at a point

A field is a space where a force acts. We live in a gravitational field: every object with mass feels gravity wherever it is. In the same way, around charges there is an electric field, and any charge placed there feels a force.

To describe the field at a point, imagine putting +1 C there. The force it would feel (size and direction) is the electric field $\vec E$ at that point. Units: newtons per coulomb (N/C).

  • A charge $q$ feels $q$ times as much: $F = qE$. A positive charge is pushed along $\vec E$.
  • A negative charge is pushed the opposite way. The size is still $|q|E$; don't put a minus sign in the size.

Field of a point charge. Put +1 C at distance $r$ from a charge $Q$. Coulomb's law gives the force on it, which is the field: $E = kQ/r^2$. It points away from a positive $Q$ and towards a negative $Q$.

The field is a vector. With several charges, find each one's field at the point (size from the formula, direction from a sketch), then add the arrows (the parallelogram rule). This is called superposition.

Electric field

$$F = qE \qquad E = k\frac{Q}{r^2}\ \ \text{(point charge)}$$

$E$: electric field (N/C). $F$: force on charge $q$ (N). $Q$: the charge making the field (C). $r$: distance from $Q$ (m). $F = qE$ holds for any field; $E = kQ/r^2$ only for a point charge.

  1. Place a test charge $q$ at distance $r$ from $Q$. Coulomb's law: $F = k\dfrac{Qq}{r^2}$.
  2. The field is the force per coulomb: $E = \dfrac{F}{q}$.
  3. So $E = k\dfrac{Q}{r^2}$. The test charge cancels: the field belongs to $Q$ and the point, not to whatever we place there.

Drag P. Each charge is 1.0 nC, 1.0 m apart (the dashed line)

Example 1

In a field of $3.0 \times 10^{3}$ N/C pointing east, find the force on (a) $+2.0 \times 10^{-6}$ C and (b) an electron.

Show solution
  1. (a) $F = qE = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 3.0 \times 10^{3} = 6.0 \times 10^{-3}$ N, east (along $\vec E$).
  2. (b) $F = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 3.0 \times 10^{3} = 4.8 \times 10^{-16}$ N, west (negative charge: opposite to $\vec E$).

(a) $6.0 \times 10^{-3}$ N east; (b) $4.8 \times 10^{-16}$ N west

Example 2

Find the electric field 0.30 m from a charge of $+4.0 \times 10^{-9}$ C.

Show solution
  1. $E = 9.0 \times 10^{9} \times \dfrac{4.0 \times 10^{-9}}{0.30^2} = \dfrac{36}{0.090}$.

$E = 4.0 \times 10^{2}$ N/C, pointing away from the charge

Example 3

Charges $+4q$ and $+q$ are 0.90 m apart. Where between them is the field zero?

Show solution
  1. Between two positive charges the two fields point in opposite directions, so they can cancel. Let the point be $x$ from $+4q$.
  2. Equal sizes: $\dfrac{k \cdot 4q}{x^2} = \dfrac{kq}{(0.90 - x)^2}$, so $\dfrac{2}{x} = \dfrac{1}{0.90 - x}$ (square roots, all positive).
  3. $1.80 - 2x = x$, so $x = 0.60$ m.

0.60 m from $+4q$ (0.30 m from $+q$)

Example 4

$-Q$ is at $(-a, 0)$ and $+Q$ is at $(a, 0)$. Find the field at A $(0, a)$.

Show solution
  1. Imagine +1 C at A. Both charges are $\sqrt2\,a$ away, so each field has size $\dfrac{kQ}{(\sqrt2 a)^2} = \dfrac{kQ}{2a^2}$.
  2. From $+Q$: away from it, pointing up-left at 45°. From $-Q$: towards it, pointing down-left at 45°.
  3. The up and down parts cancel; the left parts add. Two equal arrows at 90° to each other add to $\sqrt2$ times one: $\sqrt2 \times \dfrac{kQ}{2a^2}$.

$E = \dfrac{\sqrt2\,kQ}{2a^2}$, in the $-x$ direction

Example 5

Same charges as Example 4. Find the field at B $(2a, 0)$.

Show solution
  1. B is $a$ from $+Q$ and $3a$ from $-Q$.
  2. From $+Q$: $\dfrac{kQ}{a^2}$, pointing $+x$ (away). From $-Q$: $\dfrac{kQ}{9a^2}$, pointing $-x$ (towards).
  3. Opposite directions, so subtract: $\dfrac{kQ}{a^2} - \dfrac{kQ}{9a^2} = \dfrac{8kQ}{9a^2}$. The nearer charge wins.

$E = \dfrac{8kQ}{9a^2}$, in the $+x$ direction

$E = kQ/r^2$ gives only the size. Put $|Q|$ in it, never a signed value. Get each direction from a sketch, then add the fields as arrows. Adding the sizes is wrong unless the arrows point the same way.
To find a field at a point, imagine a +1 C charge sitting there. Ask "is it pushed away or pulled in?" for each source charge, and draw the arrows before calculating.
  • $E$ = force on +1 C; $F = qE$ (negative $q$: force opposite to $\vec E$).
  • Point charge: $E = kQ/r^2$, away from +, towards −.
  • Fields add as vectors.

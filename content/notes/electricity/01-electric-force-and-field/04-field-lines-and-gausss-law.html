Field Lines and Gauss's Law

Field lines show the field; the number leaving a closed surface is set by the charge inside

Scatter tiny +1 C charges around a positive charge and let go: they fly straight outwards. Around a negative charge they fly inwards. Their paths, joined up smoothly, are electric field lines. The rules:

  • lines start on positive charges and end on negative charges (or go off to infinity);
  • the tangent to a line at a point gives the direction of $\vec E$ there, so lines never cross or branch;
  • lines are drawn closer together where the field is stronger.

We make the last rule exact by a convention: where the field is $E$, draw $E$ lines through each 1 m² of area facing the field. A light bulb gives a good picture: its rays are dense (bright) close up and spread out (dim) far away. Field lines from a point charge spread out the same way.

Gauss's law. Wrap any closed surface around a charge $Q$. The number of lines coming out of it is $4\pi kQ$. The size and shape of the surface don't matter, only the charge inside. The constant $4\pi k$ is usually written $1/\varepsilon_0$, where $\varepsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space.

The real message is simple: the number of lines is proportional to the charge. Lines that enter and leave again (from charges outside) count zero.

Gauss's law

$$N = 4\pi kQ = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0} \qquad \varepsilon_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi k} \approx 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$$

$N$: number of field lines leaving a closed surface (lines out minus lines in). $Q$: total charge inside (C). Field lines through 1 m² facing the field = $E$.

  1. Surround a point charge $Q$ with a sphere of radius $r$. By symmetry $E$ is the same everywhere on it and points straight out.
  2. Each square metre of the sphere is crossed by $E = kQ/r^2$ lines.
  3. The sphere's area is $4\pi r^2$, so $N = \dfrac{kQ}{r^2} \times 4\pi r^2 = 4\pi kQ$.
  4. $r$ has cancelled: every sphere, small or large, is crossed by the same lines. Lines don't appear or vanish in empty space, so the result holds for any closed surface.

Example 1

How many field lines come out of a charge of $1.0 \times 10^{-9}$ C?

Show solution
  1. $N = 4\pi kQ = 4 \times 3.14 \times 9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 1.0 \times 10^{-9}$.

$N \approx 1.1 \times 10^{2}$ lines (113)

Example 2

$1.0 \times 10^{3}$ field lines leave a sphere of radius 0.20 m around a point charge. Find the field on the sphere and the charge.

Show solution
  1. Lines per square metre: $E = \dfrac{N}{4\pi r^2} = \dfrac{1000}{4\pi \times 0.040} = \dfrac{1000}{0.503} \approx 2.0 \times 10^{3}$ N/C.
  2. $Q = \dfrac{N}{4\pi k} = \dfrac{1000}{1.13 \times 10^{11}} \approx 8.8 \times 10^{-9}$ C.

$E \approx 2.0 \times 10^{3}$ N/C; $Q \approx 8.8 \times 10^{-9}$ C

Example 3

A closed box contains $+3.0 \times 10^{-9}$ C and $-1.0 \times 10^{-9}$ C. How many lines leave the box (out minus in)?

Show solution
  1. Only the total charge inside matters: $Q = +2.0 \times 10^{-9}$ C.
  2. $N = 4\pi \times 9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-9}$.

$N \approx 2.3 \times 10^{2}$ lines

Example 4

A closed surface contains no charge, but a charge sits just outside it. How many lines leave the surface?

Show solution
  1. Lines from the outside charge do cross the surface, but each one that goes in also comes out.
  2. Out minus in $= 0$, as Gauss's law says for $Q_{\text{inside}} = 0$. Check it in the demo by placing the circle where it holds no charge.

Zero (net)

Example 5

For a charge of $1.0 \times 10^{-9}$ C, find the number of lines through 1 m² facing the field at 0.30 m and at 0.60 m. Explain the ratio with Gauss's law.

Show solution
  1. At 0.30 m: $E = 9.0/0.30^2 = 100$ N/C, so 100 lines per m². At 0.60 m: $9.0/0.36 = 25$ lines per m².
  2. The same 113 lines cross both spheres, but the bigger sphere has 4 times the area, so the lines are 4 times sparser.

100 and 25 lines per m² (ratio 4 : 1)

The "number of lines" is a counting convention ($E$ per m²), not real threads. A diagram draws only a few for clarity. Field lines never cross: at a crossing the field would have two directions.
Learn Gauss's law as a sentence: "For any closed surface with charge $Q$ inside, the number of field lines going out through it is $4\pi kQ$." You'll use it in the next note to find the field of a charged plate in two lines.
  • Lines: out of +, into −; tangent = field direction; density = field strength.
  • $E$ = number of lines per m² facing the field.
  • Gauss: $N = 4\pi kQ = Q/\varepsilon_0$ through any closed surface.

Wheatstone Bridge and Potentiometer

When a meter reads zero, its two ends are at the same potential, and that gives a ratio

Wheatstone bridge. Two paths run side by side between the same two points: $R_1$ then $R_3$ on one, $R_2$ then $R_4$ on the other. A "bridge" (a galvanometer, or a resistor $R_5$) joins their midpoints. Adjust a resistor until no current crosses the bridge. Then:

  • no current through the bridge means no potential difference across it, so both midpoints share a colour;
  • so $R_1$ and $R_2$ have equal drops, and so do $R_3$ and $R_4$;
  • this gives $\dfrac{R_1}{R_3} = \dfrac{R_2}{R_4}$, which is easy to remember as the four resistors in the same positions as on the diagram.

Look for this shape even when the diagram isn't drawn as a diamond. A plain rectangle with a cross-link is the same circuit.

Potentiometer. A uniform resistance wire AB carries a steady current $I$ from a driver cell. A cell to be tested is connected, through a galvanometer, between A and a sliding contact C. Slide C until the galvanometer reads zero. Then the test cell has no current, so no voltage is lost inside it: its emf exactly equals the drop along AC, which is proportional to the length AC. Do this with a known cell $E_0$ (balance length $L_0$) and an unknown cell $E$ (balance length $L$): $E/E_0 = L/L_0$.

Balance conditions

$$\frac{R_1}{R_3} = \frac{R_2}{R_4}\ \ (\text{i.e. } R_1R_4 = R_2R_3) \qquad E = \frac{L}{L_0}E_0$$

Bridge: $R_1$, $R_3$ on one path, $R_2$, $R_4$ on the other, no current through the bridge. Potentiometer: $L_0$, $L$ = balance lengths for the known emf $E_0$ and the unknown emf $E$.

  1. Bridge. With no bridge current, $R_1$ and $R_3$ carry the same current $I$; $R_2$ and $R_4$ carry the same current $I'$.
  2. The midpoints are at equal potential, so the drops from the top match: $R_1I = R_2I'$. Same for the drops to the bottom: $R_3I = R_4I'$.
  3. Divide: $\dfrac{R_1}{R_3} = \dfrac{R_2}{R_4}$. The unknown currents cancel.
  4. Potentiometer. Let the wire have resistance $r$ per metre. At balance with no test current, $E_0 = rL_0I$ and $E = rLI$. Divide: $E/E_0 = L/L_0$. Here $r$, $I$ and the test cells' internal resistances all cancel.

12 V cell; R₁ = 10 Ω, R₂ = 20 Ω, R₃ = 30 Ω; galvanometer 10 Ω. Adjust R₄

Example 1

In the demo bridge, find $R_4$ that makes the galvanometer read zero.

Show solution
  1. $R_1R_4 = R_2R_3$: $10R_4 = 20 \times 30$.

$R_4 = 60$ Ω (try it on the slider)

Example 2

With the bridge balanced as in Example 1, find the current drawn from the 12 V cell. (Ignore its internal resistance.)

Show solution
  1. No bridge current, so the bridge can be removed: two separate paths.
  2. Path 1: $10 + 30 = 40$ Ω, so 0.30 A. Path 2: $20 + 60 = 80$ Ω, so 0.15 A.

0.45 A

Example 3

A metre bridge: an unknown resistor $X$ and a 20 Ω resistor are in the two gaps; a 100 cm uniform wire forms the other two arms. Balance is 60 cm from the end next to $X$. Find $X$.

Show solution
  1. The wire's resistance is proportional to length, so the two wire arms are in ratio 60 : 40.
  2. $\dfrac{X}{20} = \dfrac{60}{40}$.

$X = 30$ Ω

Example 4

A potentiometer balances a known 100 V cell at AC = 50 cm. An unknown cell balances at 40 cm. Find its emf.

Show solution
  1. $E = \dfrac{L}{L_0}E_0 = \dfrac{40}{50} \times 100$.

$E = 80$ V

Example 5

A standard cell of 1.02 V balances at 51.0 cm; a dry cell balances at 75.0 cm. Find the dry cell's emf. Why doesn't its internal resistance matter?

Show solution
  1. $E = \dfrac{75.0}{51.0} \times 1.02 = 1.50$ V.
  2. At balance no current flows through the dry cell, so its internal resistance drops no voltage ($rI = 0$). We measure the true emf, which an ordinary voltmeter (which draws some current) can't do.

1.50 V; no current through it at balance

$R_1/R_3 = R_2/R_4$ is true only when the bridge current is zero. If the bridge carries current, use Kirchhoff's laws instead.
Whenever a question says "the galvanometer reads zero", colour both ends of that branch the same. That one step usually solves the problem.
  • Balanced bridge: the midpoints share a potential, so $R_1/R_3 = R_2/R_4$.
  • Potentiometer: $E/E_0 = L/L_0$; at balance no current, so internal resistance doesn't matter.
  • Zero current in a branch → no potential difference across its resistors.

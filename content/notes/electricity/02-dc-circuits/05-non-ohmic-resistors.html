Non-Ohmic Resistors

A bulb's I–V graph bends, so find the working point where the graph meets a straight circuit line

For an ordinary (ohmic) resistor, $I = V/R$. The graph of current against voltage is a straight line through the origin, with slope $1/R$. A steeper line means a smaller resistance.

A filament lamp is non-ohmic. As the voltage goes up, more current flows and the filament heats up. A hotter metal has a higher resistance, so the current grows more and more slowly and the graph bends over. At each point, $R = V/I$ for that point.

How to solve a circuit with a lamp in it. You can't write the lamp's drop as $RI$, because its $R$ changes. Instead:

  1. call the lamp's current $I$ and the potential difference across it $V$ (always do this first);
  2. write Kirchhoff's second law for the circuit. It gives a straight line linking $I$ and $V$;
  3. draw that line on the lamp's graph. The easiest way is to find where it meets the axes (set $I = 0$, then $V = 0$) and join the two points;
  4. where the line crosses the curve, both are true at once: that is the actual current and voltage.

Lamp in series with a resistor $R$ on a cell $E$

$$E = RI + V \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad I = \frac{E - V}{R}$$

$V$, $I$: the lamp's voltage and current. The line meets the axes at $(V, I) = (E, 0)$ and $(0, E/R)$. The lamp's resistance at its working point is $V/I$.

  1. The lamp can only be at points on its own curve: that is how the lamp behaves.
  2. The circuit (the cell and the resistor) forces $E = RI + V$, so the point must also lie on that straight line.
  3. Only the crossing point satisfies both, so that is the lamp's voltage and current.

Example 2

The same lamp is connected in series with 5.0 Ω across the 1.0 V cell. Find the current.

Show solution
  1. Let the lamp have $I$ and $V$. Second law: $1.0 = 5.0I + V$.
  2. Line through $(V, I) = (1.0, 0)$ and $(0, 0.20)$.
  3. It crosses the curve at $V = 0.30$ V, $I = 0.14$ A. Check: $5.0 \times 0.14 + 0.30 = 1.0$ ✓.

0.14 A (lamp voltage 0.30 V)

Example 3

Find the lamp's resistance in Example 1 and in Example 2. Explain the difference.

Show solution
  1. Example 1: $1.0/0.20 = 5.0$ Ω. Example 2: $0.30/0.14 \approx 2.1$ Ω.
  2. With more current the filament is hotter, and a hotter metal resists more.

5.0 Ω and about 2.1 Ω

Example 5

Two ohmic resistors have I–V lines through (2.0 V, 0.50 A) and (2.0 V, 0.20 A). Find their resistances. Which line is steeper?

Show solution
  1. $R = V/I$: $2.0/0.50 = 4.0$ Ω and $2.0/0.20 = 10$ Ω.
  2. The 4.0 Ω line is steeper: a bigger current for the same voltage.

4.0 Ω (steeper) and 10 Ω

Read the axes carefully: here $V$ is the lamp's voltage (not the cell's) and $I$ the lamp's current. Using the cell voltage to read the graph is only right when nothing else is in series.
Don't rearrange into $y = mx + c$. Just put $I = 0$ and then $V = 0$ into the Kirchhoff equation, mark the two axis points and join them with a ruler.
  • Ohmic: straight line through the origin, slope $1/R$.
  • Lamp: the curve bends because a hotter filament has a higher resistance.
  • Set the lamp's $I$, $V$; Kirchhoff gives a line; the crossing is the answer.

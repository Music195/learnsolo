Metal Plates and Dielectrics

A metal slab removes its thickness from the gap; a dielectric weakens the field inside it by $\varepsilon_r$

A metal slab in the gap. Slide an uncharged metal slab of thickness $t$ between the plates, without touching them. The free electrons in the metal move until there is no field inside the metal. This leaves $-Q$ on the face nearer the $+Q$ plate and $+Q$ on the other face. So the field lines stop at one face and start again at the other. Outside the slab, the field is the same $E = Q/(\varepsilon_0 S)$ as before.

The field now acts only across $d - t$, so the potential difference is $V = E(d - t)$. The capacitor behaves as if the gap were only $d - t$:

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d - t}.$$

Where the slab sits in the gap does not matter, only its thickness.

A dielectric (an insulator) in the gap. An insulator has no free electrons, but its molecules stretch slightly (they polarise), with their $-$ ends towards the $+$ plate. This puts some charge on its faces, which cancels part of the field inside. The field inside is weaker by a factor $\varepsilon_r$, the relative permittivity (about 1 for air; 2–10 for plastics, glass and ceramics; about 80 for water).

If the dielectric fills the whole gap, $V$ falls by $\varepsilon_r$ for the same $Q$, so

$$C = \varepsilon_r\frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d} = \varepsilon_r C_0.$$

A metal slab is the extreme case, $\varepsilon_r \to \infty$: no field at all inside.

Slab of thickness $t$ in a gap $d$

$$\text{metal: } C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d - t} \qquad \text{dielectric: } C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{(d - t) + t/\varepsilon_r} \qquad \text{full: } C = \varepsilon_r C_0$$

$C_0 = \varepsilon_0 S/d$: the capacitance with nothing in the gap. $\varepsilon_r$: relative permittivity of the dielectric (no unit). The product $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_r\varepsilon_0$ is the dielectric's permittivity.

  1. Gauss's law at the plates: in the empty part of the gap, $E = Q/(\varepsilon_0 S)$, the same as with no slab.
  2. Inside the slab the field is $E/\varepsilon_r$ (zero for metal).
  3. Add the potential drops across the gap: $V = E(d - t) + \dfrac{E}{\varepsilon_r}t = \dfrac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 S}\left[(d - t) + \dfrac{t}{\varepsilon_r}\right]$.
  4. $C = Q/V$ gives the formula. For metal, $t/\varepsilon_r = 0$. For a full dielectric, $t = d$, which gives $\varepsilon_r\varepsilon_0 S/d$.

Example 1

A capacitor has capacitance $C_0$. An uncharged metal slab of thickness $d/2$ is slid into the gap, parallel to the plates. Find the new capacitance.

Show solution
  1. $C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d - d/2} = 2\dfrac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d}$.

$2C_0$

Example 2

A capacitor is charged to $V$ and disconnected. Then the gap is completely filled with a dielectric of $\varepsilon_r = 3$. Find the new capacitance and voltage.

Show solution
  1. $C = 3C_0$.
  2. Disconnected, so $Q$ is fixed: $V' = Q/(3C_0) = V/3$.

$3C_0$; $V/3$

Example 3

Half the gap (thickness $d/2$) is filled with a dielectric of $\varepsilon_r = 3$, and the capacitor is connected to a battery $V$. Find the field in the air and in the dielectric, and the voltage across each part.

Show solution
  1. Call the field in the air $E_1$. In the dielectric it is $E_2 = E_1/3$.
  2. $V = E_1\dfrac d2 + \dfrac{E_1}{3}\cdot\dfrac d2 = \dfrac{2}{3}E_1 d$, so $E_1 = \dfrac{3V}{2d}$ and $E_2 = \dfrac{V}{2d}$.
  3. Air part: $E_1 \cdot d/2 = 3V/4$. Dielectric part: $E_2 \cdot d/2 = V/4$. Check: $3V/4 + V/4 = V$ ✓.

$E_1 = 3V/(2d)$, $E_2 = V/(2d)$; voltages $3V/4$ and $V/4$

Example 4

A capacitor $C$ is charged to $V$ and disconnected. A metal slab of thickness $d/3$ is slid in. Find the new capacitance and voltage.

Show solution
  1. $C' = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d - d/3} = \dfrac32 C$.
  2. $Q$ is fixed, so $V' = Q/C' = \dfrac23 V$.

$C' = 3C/2$, $V' = 2V/3$

Example 5

A 6.0 μF capacitor is charged on 12 V and disconnected. Its gap is then filled with a dielectric of $\varepsilon_r = 4.0$. Find the charge, the new capacitance and the new voltage.

Show solution
  1. $Q = 6.0 \times 12 = 72$ μC, and it stays.
  2. $C = 4.0 \times 6.0 = 24$ μF.
  3. $V = 72/24 = 3.0$ V.

72 μC; 24 μF; 3.0 V

The field in the air part of the gap depends only on the charge on the plates, $E = Q/(\varepsilon_0 S)$. If the capacitor is disconnected, inserting a slab leaves the field in the air the same; it only removes or weakens the field inside the slab.
On the potential graph, the slope is the field. A flat part means no field (metal). A gentle slope means a weak field (dielectric). The total drop across the gap is the capacitor's voltage.
  • Metal slab: $C = \varepsilon_0 S/(d - t)$, wherever it sits in the gap.
  • Dielectric fills the gap: $C = \varepsilon_r C_0$.
  • Partial dielectric: add the drops, $V = E(d - t) + Et/\varepsilon_r$.

Combining Capacitors

Find the isolated pieces of metal: the total charge on each one never changes

Charge cannot jump across the gap of a capacitor. So look at the circuit and find any piece of metal that is cut off from the battery and from earth: only capacitor plates and the wires between them, with no path out. Call it an island.

The total charge on an island stays the same, whatever you switch elsewhere. Charge can move around inside the island, from one plate to another, but none can get in or out.

Two common islands:

  • the middle of two capacitors in series (the lower plate of the first, the upper plate of the second, and the wire between). If they were uncharged at the start, its total is 0. That is exactly why series capacitors carry equal charges;
  • a plate that was connected to the battery, after the battery switch is opened.

Method for switching problems.

  1. Before the switch: find every plate's charge.
  2. After the switch: find the islands and add up their charges from step 1.
  3. Write each plate's charge as $C \times$ (its own potential $-$ the facing plate's potential), using an unknown for each unknown potential.
  4. Set each island's total equal to its old total and solve.

Islands and plate charges

$$\text{plate charge} = C\,(V_\text{this plate} - V_\text{facing plate}), \qquad \sum_\text{island}\text{plate charges} = \text{constant}$$

A plate at the higher potential holds positive charge. An island is a piece of metal joined only to capacitor plates, so no charge can reach it or leave it.

  1. Charge moves only through conductors. A capacitor's gap is an insulator.
  2. An island touches the rest of the circuit only across capacitor gaps, so no charge can flow in or out.
  3. Charge is conserved, so the island's total stays what it was.

C₁ = 2 μF and C₂ = 3 μF in series on 10 V, bottom earthed. Then open S₁ and close S₂

Example 1

Uncharged capacitors $C_1 = 2.0$ μF and $C_2 = 3.0$ μF are in series across a 10 V battery. The battery's $+$ is joined to $C_1$, and the point below $C_2$ is earthed (0 V). Find the charge on each and the potential $V_B$ of the point between them.

Show solution
  1. The middle island starts uncharged, so both carry the same $Q$. Combined $C = 1.2$ μF, so $Q = 12$ μC.
  2. $C_2$'s voltage is $12/3.0 = 4.0$ V, and its lower end is at 0 V, so $V_B = 4.0$ V.

12 μC each; $V_B = 4.0$ V

Example 2

Next, the battery switch S₁ is opened. Then an uncharged $C_3 = 1.0$ μF is connected between B and earth. Find the new $V_B$ and the new potential $V_A$ of $C_1$'s upper plate.

Show solution
  1. Island A ($C_1$'s upper plate) is cut off, so it keeps $+12$ μC: $2.0(V_A - V_B) = 12$, so $V_A - V_B = 6.0$ V.
  2. Island B ($C_1$'s lower plate, $C_2$'s and $C_3$'s upper plates) keeps $-12 + 12 + 0 = 0$: $2.0(V_B - V_A) + 3.0V_B + 1.0V_B = 0$.
  3. The first term is $-12$, so $4.0V_B = 12$ and $V_B = 3.0$ V. Then $V_A = 9.0$ V.
  4. Check: $C_2$ has 9.0 μC and $C_3$ has 3.0 μC, together 12 μC ✓.

$V_B = 3.0$ V, $V_A = 9.0$ V

Example 3

Uncharged $C_1 = 1.0$ μF and $C_2 = 2.0$ μF are in parallel, and that pair is in series with $C_3 = 3.0$ μF across 12 V. Find the charge on each.

Show solution
  1. The pair acts as 3.0 μF. In series with 3.0 μF: $C = 1.5$ μF, so $Q = 1.5 \times 12 = 18$ μC. $C_3$ has 18 μC.
  2. The pair has the same 18 μC, and its voltage is $18/3.0 = 6.0$ V.
  3. $Q_1 = 1.0 \times 6.0 = 6.0$ μC and $Q_2 = 2.0 \times 6.0 = 12$ μC.

$Q_1 = 6.0$ μC, $Q_2 = 12$ μC, $Q_3 = 18$ μC

Example 4

$C_1 = 2.0$ μF was charged on 6.0 V and removed. It is now put in series with an uncharged $C_2 = 4.0$ μF across 12 V: the battery's $+$ goes to $C_1$'s positive plate, and $C_2$'s far end is at 0 V. Find the potential $x$ of the point between them.

Show solution
  1. The middle island is $C_1$'s lower plate ($-12$ μC) plus $C_2$'s upper plate (0). Its total is $-12$ μC, not 0, so the series rule does not apply.
  2. After: $2.0(x - 12) + 4.0(x - 0) = -12$.
  3. $6.0x - 24 = -12$, so $x = 2.0$ V.
  4. $C_1$ now has $2.0 \times 10 = 20$ μC and $C_2$ has 8.0 μC. Check: $-20 + 8 = -12$ ✓.

$x = 2.0$ V

Example 5

An island is made of two plates holding $-100$ μC and $+300$ μC. After a switch elsewhere is closed, the first plate holds $+100$ μC. Find the charge on the second plate.

Show solution
  1. The island's total is $-100 + 300 = +200$ μC, and it stays that way.
  2. $100 + q = 200$, so $q = 100$ μC.

+100 μC

In Example 4 the series rule would give $Q = \frac43 \times 12 = 16$ μC on each capacitor: wrong. The middle island already had charge, so the two capacitors end up with different charges. When anything was charged earlier, always use the island method.

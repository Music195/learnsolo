Charge Conservation in Capacitor Circuits

Call the unknown potential $x$, write each plate's charge as $C(x - V_\text{other})$, and add them up for the island

This is the general method behind the island idea. It works for any capacitor network, however it is drawn, so you never have to decide whether things are "in series" or "in parallel".

  1. Give every point whose potential you know its value (battery terminals, earth = 0).
  2. Call the unknown potential of an island $x$.
  3. Every plate on the island holds $C(x - V_\text{other})$, where $V_\text{other}$ is the potential of the plate facing it. The sign takes care of itself: a negative answer means a negative plate.
  4. Add these charges and set the sum equal to the island's charge before the switching (often 0).

With all capacitors uncharged at the start, the result is a weighted average:

$$x = \frac{C_1V_1 + C_2V_2 + C_3V_3}{C_1 + C_2 + C_3}.$$

The island sits between the potentials it is joined to, pulled hardest towards the side with the biggest capacitance.

One island at unknown potential $x$

$$\sum_i C_i\,(x - V_i) = Q_\text{island} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x = \frac{Q_\text{island} + \sum_i C_iV_i}{\sum_i C_i}$$

$C_i$: each capacitor touching the island. $V_i$: the potential of its other plate. $Q_\text{island}$: the island's total charge, which stays what it was before the switching.

  1. A capacitor with plates at potentials $V_a$ and $V_b$ has voltage $V_a - V_b$. The plate at $V_a$ carries $C(V_a - V_b)$, and the other plate carries the opposite charge.
  2. So a plate on the island carries $C_i(x - V_i)$. This is positive when the island is higher than the other side.
  3. No charge can reach the island, so these add up to its starting total. That is one equation for the one unknown $x$.

Island X joined to 1 μF, 2 μF and 3 μF whose other plates are at V₁, V₂, V₃

Example 1

A 10 μF capacitor joins point X (at 20 V) to a point at 40 V. Find the charge on the plate joined to X.

Show solution
  1. $Q = C(V_X - V_\text{other}) = 10(20 - 40)$.
  2. $= -200$ μC: X is the lower side, so its plate is negative.

$-200$ μC

Example 2

A 15 μF capacitor joins X (at 30 V) to a point at 10 V. Find the charge on the plate joined to X.

Show solution
  1. $Q = 15(30 - 10) = 300$ μC.

+300 μC

Example 3

Island X is joined to three uncharged capacitors: 30 μF to a point at 60 V, 20 μF to earth, and 10 μF to earth. Find $x$ and the charge on each of X's plates.

Show solution
  1. $30(x - 60) + 20(x - 0) + 10(x - 0) = 0$.
  2. $60x = 1800$, so $x = 30$ V.
  3. Plates: $30(30 - 60) = -900$ μC, $20 \times 30 = 600$ μC, $10 \times 30 = 300$ μC. Check: $-900 + 600 + 300 = 0$ ✓.

$x = 30$ V; $-900$, $+600$, $+300$ μC

Example 4

Island X joins an uncharged 1.0 μF to a point at 20 V and an uncharged 2.0 μF to earth. Find $x$.

Show solution
  1. $1.0(x - 20) + 2.0x = 0$, so $3.0x = 20$.
  2. $x = 6.7$ V: closer to 0 V, because the bigger capacitor goes to earth.

6.7 V

Example 5

Island X joins 2.0 μF to a point at 12 V and 4.0 μF to earth, but this time the island already holds $+12$ μC. Find $x$.

Show solution
  1. $2.0(x - 12) + 4.0(x - 0) = 12$.
  2. $6.0x - 24 = 12$, so $x = 6.0$ V. (With an uncharged island it would be 4.0 V.)

6.0 V

Always write (island − other side), the same way round for every capacitor, even if you expect a negative answer. If you mix the orders, the sum is no longer the island's charge.
After solving, work out each plate's charge and add them up. They must give the island's starting charge. This catches most sign slips.
  • Plate charge on the island $= C(x - V_\text{other})$.
  • Sum over the island $=$ its starting charge.
  • Starting at 0: $x$ is the $C$-weighted average of the other potentials.

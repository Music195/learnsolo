Complex Plane

Every complex number is a point

Put the complex number $z = a + bi$ ($a$, $b$ real) at the point $(a, b)$. The plane used this way is the complex plane (複素数平面). Its horizontal axis is the real axis (実軸) and its vertical axis is the imaginary axis (虚軸).

Adding complex numbers adds their coordinates, just like adding vectors. So $z + w$ is the fourth corner of the parallelogram on $0$, $z$, $w$, and $z - w$ is the arrow from $w$ to $z$.

$z = a + bi \leftrightarrow$ point $(a, b)$

$$(a + bi) \pm (c + di) = (a \pm c) + (b \pm d)i, \qquad kz = ka + kbi \;\; (k \text{ real})$$

Real numbers lie on the real axis ($b = 0$). Purely imaginary numbers ($a = 0$, $b \ne 0$) lie on the imaginary axis. We write A($\alpha$) for "point A representing $\alpha$".

  1. The point $z = a + bi$ has position vector $(a, b)$, and $w = c + di$ has $(c, d)$.
  2. $z + w$ has coordinates $(a + c, b + d)$, which is the vector sum. Vector addition follows the parallelogram rule.
  3. $kz$ is the vector $(a, b)$ scaled by $k$, so $0$, $z$ and $kz$ lie on one line through the origin.

Example 1

Plot $3 + 2i$, $-2 + i$, $-3i$ and $4$. Which are on an axis?

Show solution
  1. Points $(3, 2)$, $(-2, 1)$, $(0, -3)$, $(4, 0)$.

$-3i$ is on the imaginary axis; $4$ is on the real axis

Example 2

$\alpha = 3 + i$, $\beta = 1 + 2i$. Find $\alpha + \beta$ and $\alpha - \beta$ and describe them in the plane.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = 4 + 3i$: the fourth corner of the parallelogram on $0$, $\alpha$, $\beta$.
  2. $\alpha - \beta = 2 - i$: the same arrow as from $\beta$ to $\alpha$, moved to start at $0$.

$4 + 3i$ and $2 - i$

Example 3

$0$, $\alpha = 2 + i$ and $\beta = x - 3i$ ($x$ real) lie on one straight line. Find $x$.

Show solution
  1. $\beta = k\alpha$ for a real $k$: $x - 3i = 2k + ki$.
  2. Compare: $k = -3$, $x = 2k$.

$x = -6$

Example 4

Points A($1 + i$), B($4 + 2i$), C($3 + 5i$). Find D so that ABCD is a parallelogram.

Show solution
  1. In ABCD, $\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{BC}$: $\delta - \alpha = \gamma - \beta$.
  2. $\delta = \alpha + \gamma - \beta = (1 + i) + (3 + 5i) - (4 + 2i)$

D($4i$)

The point for $a + bi$ is $(a, b)$, not $(a, bi)$: the coordinates are both real. Also, complex numbers can't be compared with $<$ or $>$. There is no "bigger" point in the plane.
For addition, subtraction and real multiples, a complex number behaves exactly like a plane vector. The difference $\beta - \alpha$ is the arrow from A to B.
  • $a + bi \leftrightarrow (a, b)$; real axis across, imaginary axis up.
  • $z + w$: parallelogram rule; $\beta - \alpha$: arrow from A to B.
  • $0$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ on one line $\Leftrightarrow$ $\beta = k\alpha$ ($k$ real).

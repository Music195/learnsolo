Modulus and Argument of Product and Quotient

Rules you can use without the polar form

Note 05 gives two rules, $|z_1z_2| = |z_1||z_2|$ and $\arg(z_1z_2) = \arg z_1 + \arg z_2$. You can use them directly, without writing out the full polar form. For example, $|(3 + 4i)^5| = 5^5$ needs no expansion.

A picture of the product rule: the triangle O, $1$, $z_2$ and the triangle O, $z_1$, $z_1z_2$ are similar. The second is the first scaled by $|z_1|$ and turned through $\arg z_1$.

$z_1$, $z_2 \ne 0$ (arguments are equal up to adding $2n\pi$)

$$|z_1z_2| = |z_1||z_2|, \qquad \left|\frac{z_1}{z_2}\right| = \frac{|z_1|}{|z_2|}, \qquad |z^n| = |z|^n$$
$$\arg(z_1z_2) = \arg z_1 + \arg z_2, \qquad \arg\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \arg z_1 - \arg z_2$$
  1. $|z_1z_2|^2 = z_1z_2\overline{z_1z_2} = z_1\overline{z_1} \cdot z_2\overline{z_2} = |z_1|^2|z_2|^2$, using $\overline{z_1z_2} = \overline{z_1}\,\overline{z_2}$ (note 03).
  2. Both sides are positive, so $|z_1z_2| = |z_1||z_2|$.
  3. The argument rule is the angle part of note 05's product formula.

Triangle O, $1$, $z_2$ (blue) and triangle O, $z_1$, $z_1z_2$ (orange) are similar

Example 1

Find $\left|\dfrac{(3 + 4i)(1 - i)}{2 + i}\right|$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{|3 + 4i||1 - i|}{|2 + i|} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{5}}$

$\sqrt{10}$

Example 2

$\arg\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\arg\beta = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. Find $\arg(\alpha\beta)$, $\arg\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}$ and $\arg\alpha^2$ ($0 \le \arg < 2\pi$).

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{13\pi}{12}$.
  2. $\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$; add $2\pi$: $\dfrac{19\pi}{12}$.
  3. $2 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

$\dfrac{13\pi}{12}$, $\dfrac{19\pi}{12}$, $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

Example 3

$|\alpha| = 2$ and $|\beta| = 3$. Find $|\alpha^3\overline{\beta}|$ and $\left|\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta^2}\right|$.

Show solution
  1. $|\alpha|^3|\overline{\beta}| = 8 \times 3$
  2. $\dfrac{|\alpha|}{|\beta|^2} = \dfrac{2}{9}$

$24$ and $\dfrac{2}{9}$

Example 4

$\alpha = 1 + i$, $\beta = 1 + \sqrt{3}i$. Find the modulus and argument of $\dfrac{\beta}{\alpha}$ ($0 \le \arg < 2\pi$).

Show solution
  1. $|\alpha| = \sqrt{2}$, $\arg\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$; $\;|\beta| = 2$, $\arg\beta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
  2. Modulus $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}}$; argument $\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

$\sqrt{2}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$

These rules are for products and quotients only. In general $|z_1 + z_2| \ne |z_1| + |z_2|$; the triangle inequality only gives $|z_1 + z_2| \le |z_1| + |z_2|$. And $\arg(z_1 + z_2)$ has no simple rule.
An argument equation like $\arg(z_1z_2) = \arg z_1 + \arg z_2$ holds "up to $2n\pi$". After adding or subtracting, bring the answer back into the range the question asks for.
  • $|z_1z_2| = |z_1||z_2|$, $|z_1/z_2| = |z_1|/|z_2|$, $|z^n| = |z|^n$.
  • $\arg(z_1z_2) = \arg z_1 + \arg z_2$, $\arg(z_1/z_2) = \arg z_1 - \arg z_2$ (mod $2\pi$).
  • Picture: multiplying by $z_1$ scales by $|z_1|$ and turns by $\arg z_1$.

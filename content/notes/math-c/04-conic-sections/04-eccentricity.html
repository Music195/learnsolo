Conic Eccentricity Properties

One rule makes all three curves

Take a focus F and a line $\ell$, and look at the points P where $\dfrac{PF}{PH} = e$, with H the foot of the perpendicular from P to $\ell$. The constant $e > 0$ is the eccentricity (離心率).

$e = 1$ is the parabola from note 01. A smaller $e$ keeps P closer to F, and the curve closes up into an ellipse. A larger $e$ lets it open out into a hyperbola.

$PF : PH = e : 1$

$$0 < e < 1: \text{ ellipse}, \qquad e = 1: \text{ parabola}, \qquad e > 1: \text{ hyperbola}$$

For $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($a > b$): $e = \dfrac{\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}}{a}$. For $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$: $e = \dfrac{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}{a}$. In both cases the directrices are $x = \pm\dfrac{a}{e}$.

  1. Take $F(1, 0)$, directrix $x = 4$ and $e = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $\sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + y^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}|x - 4|$.
  2. Square: $4(x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2) = x^2 - 8x + 16$, so $3x^2 + 4y^2 = 12$.
  3. That is $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$, an ellipse with $a = 2$, $\sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = 1$, and $e = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as expected.

Focus F at the origin, directrix $x = -2$: points with $PF = e \cdot PH$

Example 1

Find the eccentricity of $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$ and of $\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$.

Show solution
  1. Ellipse: $\dfrac{\sqrt{9 - 4}}{3}$.
  2. Hyperbola: $\dfrac{\sqrt{16 + 9}}{4}$.

$\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5}{4}$

Example 2

Find the locus of P with $PF : PH = 2 : 1$, where $F(2, 0)$ and H is the foot of the perpendicular from P to $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. $(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 4\left(x - \dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2$
  2. $x^2 - 4x + 4 + y^2 = 4x^2 - 4x + 1$, so $3x^2 - y^2 = 3$.

The hyperbola $x^2 - \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$ ($e = 2$)

Example 3

An ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ has $a = 5$ and eccentricity $\dfrac{4}{5}$. Find $b$ and the foci.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = ae = 4$, so $b^2 = 25 - 16 = 9$.

$b = 3$; foci $(\pm 4, 0)$

Example 4

Which conic is $PF : PH = 1 : 3$? And $3 : 3$?

Show solution
  1. $e = \dfrac{1}{3} < 1$; $\;e = 1$.

An ellipse (fairly round, since $e$ is small); a parabola

$e$ is (distance to the focus) ÷ (distance to the directrix), not the other way round. Writing it upside down turns an ellipse into a hyperbola.
For an ellipse, $e$ near $0$ means nearly a circle (the foci are close together), and $e$ near $1$ means long and thin. The Earth's orbit has $e \approx 0.017$, almost a circle.
  • $PF : PH = e$: ellipse $e < 1$, parabola $e = 1$, hyperbola $e > 1$.
  • Ellipse $e = \dfrac{\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}}{a}$, hyperbola $e = \dfrac{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}{a}$.
  • To find the curve: write $PF^2 = e^2 PH^2$ and simplify.

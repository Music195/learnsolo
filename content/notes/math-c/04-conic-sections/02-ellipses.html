Ellipse: Basic Properties

A constant sum of two distances

Fix two points F and F′ (the foci). The points P with $PF + PF'$ equal to a fixed constant form an ellipse (楕円). You can draw one with a loop of string around two pins.

An ellipse is also a circle stretched in one direction. The longer axis is the major axis (長軸), and the foci lie on it.

$a > b > 0$: foci on the $x$-axis

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$$
$$\text{foci } (\pm\sqrt{a^2 - b^2},\, 0), \qquad PF + PF' = 2a$$

Major axis $2a$, minor axis $2b$, vertices $(\pm a, 0)$, $(0, \pm b)$. If $b > a > 0$, the foci are $(0, \pm\sqrt{b^2 - a^2})$ and $PF + PF' = 2b$.

  1. Foci $F(c, 0)$, $F'(-c, 0)$ with $a > c > 0$. Write $\sqrt{(x + c)^2 + y^2} = 2a - \sqrt{(x - c)^2 + y^2}$ and square both sides.
  2. The squares cancel, leaving $a\sqrt{(x - c)^2 + y^2} = a^2 - cx$. Squaring again gives $(a^2 - c^2)x^2 + a^2y^2 = a^2(a^2 - c^2)$.
  3. Put $b^2 = a^2 - c^2$ and divide by $a^2b^2$: $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.

Drag $P$: the sum $PF + PF'$ never changes

Example 1

For $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$, find the foci, the vertices and the lengths of the axes.

Show solution
  1. $a = 5$, $b = 4$, $\sqrt{25 - 16} = 3$.

Foci $(\pm 3, 0)$; vertices $(\pm 5, 0)$, $(0, \pm 4)$; major axis $10$, minor axis $8$

Example 2

Find the ellipse with foci $(0, \pm 2)$ for which $PF + PF' = 8$.

Show solution
  1. Foci on the $y$-axis, so the major axis is vertical: $2b = 8$, $b = 4$.
  2. $b^2 - a^2 = 2^2$, so $a^2 = 12$.

$\dfrac{x^2}{12} + \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$

Example 3

The circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ is squashed vertically by a factor $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Find the resulting curve.

Show solution
  1. A point $(s, t)$ on the circle moves to $(x, y) = \left(s, \dfrac{2}{3}t\right)$, so $s = x$, $t = \dfrac{3}{2}y$.
  2. $x^2 + \dfrac{9}{4}y^2 = 9$

$\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$

Example 4

Is the point $(3, 2)$ inside, on, or outside $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$?

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{9}{9} + \dfrac{4}{4} = 2 > 1$.

Outside (less than $1$: inside; equal: on the curve)

For an ellipse the focus distance is $\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ (bigger minus smaller), not $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$; the plus sign belongs to the hyperbola. The foci are on the longer axis, so check whether $a$ or $b$ is bigger first.
Points of the ellipse can be written $(a\cos\theta, b\sin\theta)$. This is the circle's parametrisation stretched, and it is the easiest way to handle a general point on the ellipse.
  • Ellipse: $PF + PF' = $ constant ($2 \times$ semi-major axis).
  • $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($a > b$): foci $(\pm\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}, 0)$.
  • A circle stretched or squashed in one direction is an ellipse.

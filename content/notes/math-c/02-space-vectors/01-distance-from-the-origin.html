Distance of Origin and Point 3-D

Pythagoras, used twice

In space, a point P has three coordinates $(a, b, c)$, measured along the $x$-, $y$- and $z$-axes, which are all perpendicular to each other.

OP is the long diagonal of a box with edges $|a|$, $|b|$, $|c|$. First find the diagonal of the base with Pythagoras, then use Pythagoras again with the height.

O$(0, 0, 0)$, P$(a, b, c)$

$$\text{OP} = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$$

The three coordinate planes are the $xy$-plane ($z = 0$), the $yz$-plane ($x = 0$) and the $zx$-plane ($y = 0$).

  1. Let Q$(a, b, 0)$ be the foot of the perpendicular from P to the $xy$-plane. In the $xy$-plane, $\text{OQ}^2 = a^2 + b^2$.
  2. PQ is parallel to the $z$-axis, so PQ ⊥ OQ and $\text{PQ} = |c|$.
  3. In the right triangle OQP: $\text{OP}^2 = \text{OQ}^2 + \text{PQ}^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2$.

P$(a, b, c)$, its box, and the two right triangles

Example 1

Find the distance from O to P$(2, 3, 6)$ and to Q$(-2, -3, 4)$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49}$
  2. $\sqrt{4 + 9 + 16} = \sqrt{29}$

$7$ and $\sqrt{29}$

Example 2

For P$(1, -2, 3)$, find the points symmetric to P about the $xy$-plane, about the $z$-axis, and about O.

Show solution
  1. About the $xy$-plane: only $z$ changes sign.
  2. About the $z$-axis: $x$ and $y$ change sign.
  3. About O: all three change sign.

$(1, -2, -3)$, $\;(-1, 2, 3)$, $\;(-1, 2, -3)$

Example 3

P$(t, 2t, 2)$ is at distance $3$ from O. Find $t$.

Show solution
  1. $t^2 + 4t^2 + 4 = 9$, so $5t^2 = 5$.

$t = \pm 1$

Example 4

Find the distance from P$(3, 4, 12)$ to the $z$-axis, and to the $xy$-plane.

Show solution
  1. The nearest point on the $z$-axis is $(0, 0, 12)$: distance $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}$.
  2. The nearest point on the $xy$-plane is $(3, 4, 0)$: distance $|12|$.

$5$ and $12$ (and OP $= 13$)

The legs of the triangles are $|a|$, $|b|$, $|c|$. Squaring removes the signs, so negative coordinates are fine. Don't drop a negative sign before squaring, though: $(-3)^2 = 9$.
For space problems, drop a perpendicular to a coordinate plane. This turns a 3D distance into a flat Pythagoras problem plus one height.
  • OP $= \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$.
  • Distance to the $z$-axis: $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$; to the $xy$-plane: $|c|$.
  • Symmetry about a plane changes one sign; about an axis, two; about O, three.

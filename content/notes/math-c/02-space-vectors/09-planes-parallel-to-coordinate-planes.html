Planes Parallel To Coordinate Planes

Fix one coordinate, let the other two run free

The set of all points with $x = 3$ is a flat sheet: $y$ and $z$ can be anything. That sheet is parallel to the $yz$-plane and cuts the $x$-axis at $(3, 0, 0)$, at a right angle.

In the same way, $y = b$ is parallel to the $zx$-plane and $z = c$ is parallel to the $xy$-plane. The coordinate planes themselves are $x = 0$, $y = 0$, $z = 0$.

Through the point $(a, b, c)$

$$x = a \;\;(\parallel yz\text{-plane}), \qquad y = b \;\;(\parallel zx\text{-plane}), \qquad z = c \;\;(\parallel xy\text{-plane})$$

The plane $x = a$ is perpendicular to the $x$-axis and meets it at $(a, 0, 0)$. Two such planes, e.g. $x = a$ and $y = b$, meet in the line through $(a, b, 0)$ parallel to the $z$-axis.

  1. Take any two points P$(a, y_1, z_1)$, Q$(a, y_2, z_2)$ with $x = a$. Then $\overrightarrow{PQ} = (0, y_2 - y_1, z_2 - z_1)$.
  2. Its dot product with $(1, 0, 0)$ is $0$, so every segment in the set is perpendicular to the $x$-axis.
  3. So the set is the plane through $(a, 0, 0)$ perpendicular to the $x$-axis, which is parallel to the $yz$-plane.

The plane $x = k$, $y = k$ or $z = k$

Example 1

Find the plane through $(3, -1, 5)$ parallel to the $yz$-plane, and the plane through it parallel to the $xy$-plane.

Show solution
  1. Parallel to the $yz$-plane: $x$ is fixed at $3$.
  2. Parallel to the $xy$-plane: $z$ is fixed at $5$.

$x = 3$ and $z = 5$

Example 2

Find the plane through $(2, 4, -3)$ perpendicular to the $y$-axis.

Show solution
  1. Perpendicular to the $y$-axis means parallel to the $zx$-plane: $y$ is fixed.

$y = 4$

Example 3

Describe the set of points with $x = 1$ and $z = -2$.

Show solution
  1. Only $y$ is free: points $(1, y, -2)$.

The line through $(1, 0, -2)$ parallel to the $y$-axis

Example 4

Find the distance from P$(4, -2, 3)$ to the plane $x = 1$, and the foot of the perpendicular.

Show solution
  1. The perpendicular from P is parallel to the $x$-axis, so only $x$ changes: foot $(1, -2, 3)$.
  2. Distance $= |4 - 1|$.

$3$, foot $(1, -2, 3)$

In the plane, $x = 3$ is a line. In space, $x = 3$ is a plane; a line needs two equations, such as $x = 3$, $y = 0$. And "parallel to the $yz$-plane" means $x$ is fixed, not $y$ or $z$.
The plane parallel to the $yz$-plane fixes the letter missing from "$yz$", that is $x$. Same for $zx$ → $y$ and $xy$ → $z$.
  • $x = a$: parallel to the $yz$-plane, perpendicular to the $x$-axis.
  • $y = b$: parallel to the $zx$-plane; $z = c$: parallel to the $xy$-plane.
  • Distance from $(p, q, r)$ to $x = a$ is $|p - a|$.

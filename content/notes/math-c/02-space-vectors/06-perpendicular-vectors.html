Perpendicular Condition For Vectors

Right angle ⇔ dot product zero

For nonzero vectors, $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta$ is $0$ exactly when $\cos\theta = 0$, that is, when $\theta = 90°$. So checking "perpendicular" (垂直) is one multiplication-and-add.

In space, a single vector has a whole plane of directions perpendicular to it, not just one line. That is why "find a vector perpendicular to $\vec{a}$" has many answers, and you need a second condition to pin one down.

$\vec{a} = (a_1, a_2, a_3) \ne \vec{0}$, $\vec{b} = (b_1, b_2, b_3) \ne \vec{0}$

$$\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \iff \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0 \iff a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3 = 0$$

A vector perpendicular to both $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ (not parallel to each other): set $\vec{n} = (x, y, z)$ and solve $\vec{n} \cdot \vec{a} = 0$, $\vec{n} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$.

  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta$ with $|\vec{a}| \ne 0$ and $|\vec{b}| \ne 0$.
  2. So $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0 \iff \cos\theta = 0 \iff \theta = 90°$ (for $0 \le \theta \le 180°$).

All vectors perpendicular to $\vec{a} = (1, 1, 2)$ (blue) fill a plane

Example 1

Are $\vec{a} = (1, 2, 3)$ and $\vec{b} = (2, -1, 0)$ perpendicular? And $\vec{c} = (2, 5, 3)$ and $\vec{d} = (4, -2, 1)$?

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 2 - 2 + 0 = 0$.
  2. $\vec{c} \cdot \vec{d} = 8 - 10 + 3 = 1 \ne 0$.

$\vec{a} \perp \vec{b}$; $\vec{c}$ and $\vec{d}$ are not perpendicular

Example 2

$\vec{a} = (2, x, -1)$ and $\vec{b} = (x, 3, 4)$ are perpendicular. Find $x$.

Show solution
  1. $2x + 3x - 4 = 0$.

$x = \dfrac{4}{5}$

Example 3

Find a vector perpendicular to $\vec{v} = (3, 4, 5)$.

Show solution
  1. We need $3x + 4y + 5z = 0$. Choose $z = 0$: then $3x + 4y = 0$, e.g. $x = 4$, $y = -3$.
  2. Check: $3 \cdot 4 + 4 \cdot (-3) + 5 \cdot 0 = 0$.

e.g. $(4, -3, 0)$ (any nonzero solution of $3x + 4y + 5z = 0$ works)

Example 4

Find the unit vectors perpendicular to both $\vec{a} = (1, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{b} = (0, 1, 1)$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{n} = (x, y, z)$: $x + y = 0$ and $y + z = 0$, so $x = -y$, $z = -y$.
  2. $\vec{n} = y(-1, 1, -1)$, and $|\vec{n}| = 1$ gives $3y^2 = 1$, $y = \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.

$\pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, -1, 1)$

The test needs both vectors nonzero: $\vec{0} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$ always, but $\vec{0}$ has no direction. When asked for a unit vector perpendicular to two vectors, there are always two answers, $\vec{e}$ and $-\vec{e}$.
To find just one perpendicular vector, set one component to $0$ and swap-and-negate the other two: for $(a_1, a_2, a_3)$, the vector $(a_2, -a_1, 0)$ always works (if it isn't $\vec{0}$).
  • $\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \iff a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3 = 0$ (both nonzero).
  • Vectors perpendicular to one vector fill a plane.
  • Perpendicular to two vectors: solve two dot-product equations, then scale.

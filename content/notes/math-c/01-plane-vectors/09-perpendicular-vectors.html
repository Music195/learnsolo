Vector Perpendicularity

Right angle ⇔ dot product zero

For nonzero vectors, $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta$ is zero only when $\cos\theta = 0$, that is $\theta = 90°$. So $\vec{a} \perp \vec{b}$ (垂直) is the single equation $a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 = 0$.

In the plane, the directions perpendicular to $(a_1, a_2)$ are all multiples of $(a_2, -a_1)$: swap the components and change one sign.

$\vec{a} = (a_1, a_2) \ne \vec{0}$, $\vec{b} = (b_1, b_2) \ne \vec{0}$

$$\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \iff \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0 \iff a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 = 0$$

Vectors perpendicular to $(a_1, a_2)$: $k(a_2, -a_1)$, $k \ne 0$.

  1. $|\vec{a}| > 0$ and $|\vec{b}| > 0$, so $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0 \iff \cos\theta = 0$.
  2. In $0° \le \theta \le 180°$, $\cos\theta = 0$ only at $\theta = 90°$.
  3. Check the swap rule: $(a_1, a_2) \cdot (a_2, -a_1) = a_1a_2 - a_2a_1 = 0$.

Drag the tips of $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$; $\vec{b}$ snaps onto the perpendicular line (dashed) when close

Example 1

Are these perpendicular? (i) $(2, 3)$ and $(-3, 2)$ (ii) $(4, 2)$ and $(-1, 2)$ (iii) $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 1)$

Show solution
  1. (i) $-6 + 6 = 0$. (ii) $-4 + 4 = 0$. (iii) $2 + 2 = 4 \ne 0$.

(i) and (ii) are perpendicular; (iii) is not

Example 2

Find $k$ so that $(k, 1) \perp (2, 3)$.

Show solution
  1. $2k + 3 = 0$.

$k = -\dfrac{3}{2}$

Example 3

Find the unit vectors perpendicular to $\vec{a} = (3, 4)$.

Show solution
  1. Perpendicular direction: $(4, -3)$, with length $5$.

$\left(\dfrac{4}{5}, -\dfrac{3}{5}\right)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}, \dfrac{3}{5}\right)$

Example 4

$|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{2}$, $|\vec{b}| = 1$, and $\vec{a} - \vec{b}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{b}$. Find the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$.

Show solution
  1. $(\vec{a} - \vec{b}) \cdot \vec{b} = 0$: $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} - |\vec{b}|^2 = 0$, so $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1$.
  2. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} \cdot 1}$.

$\theta = 45°$

"Perpendicular" is only defined for nonzero vectors: $\vec{0} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$, but $\vec{0}$ has no direction. When solving $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$ for an unknown, check the answer doesn't make a vector $\vec{0}$. Unit vectors perpendicular to $\vec{a}$ come in a pair, $\pm$.
Swap-and-negate is the quickest way to write a perpendicular vector: $(3, 4) \to (4, -3)$ or $(-4, 3)$.
  • $\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \iff a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 = 0$ (both nonzero).
  • Perpendicular to $(a_1, a_2)$: $k(a_2, -a_1)$.
  • Conditions like $(\vec{a} - \vec{b}) \perp \vec{b}$: expand the dot product.

Vector Equation of a Straight Line

Start at A, then walk along $\vec{d}$

A line is fixed by one point A$(\vec{a})$ on it and a nonzero direction vector (方向ベクトル) $\vec{d}$ parallel to it. Every point P on the line is reached by starting at A and walking some multiple $t$ of $\vec{d}$.

So $\vec{p} = \vec{a} + t\vec{d}$, where the parameter (媒介変数) $t$ runs over all real numbers. Writing it in components gives $x$ and $y$ in terms of $t$; removing $t$ gives the usual equation of the line.

Through A$(\vec{a}) = (x_1, y_1)$, direction $\vec{d} = (l, m) \ne \vec{0}$

$$\vec{p} = \vec{a} + t\vec{d}, \qquad \begin{cases} x = x_1 + lt \\ y = y_1 + mt \end{cases}$$

Removing $t$: $m(x - x_1) - l(y - y_1) = 0$.

  1. P is on the line $\iff$ $\overrightarrow{AP} \parallel \vec{d}$ or P = A $\iff$ $\overrightarrow{AP} = t\vec{d}$ for some real $t$.
  2. $\overrightarrow{AP} = \vec{p} - \vec{a}$, so $\vec{p} = \vec{a} + t\vec{d}$.
  3. Components: $x - x_1 = lt$, $y - y_1 = mt$. Multiply the first by $m$, the second by $l$, and subtract.

Drag A and the tip D of $\vec{d}$ (drawn from A); change $t$ to move P along the line

Example 1

Write the line through A$(1, 2)$ with direction $\vec{d} = (3, 4)$ in parametric form, and as an equation in $x$ and $y$.

Show solution
  1. $x = 1 + 3t$, $y = 2 + 4t$.
  2. $4(x - 1) - 3(y - 2) = 0$.

$(x, y) = (1, 2) + t(3, 4)$; $\;4x - 3y + 2 = 0$

Example 2

Is $(7, 10)$ on the line $\vec{p} = (1, 2) + t(3, 4)$? Is $(4, 5)$?

Show solution
  1. $(7, 10)$: $1 + 3t = 7$ gives $t = 2$, and $2 + 4 \cdot 2 = 10$ ✓.
  2. $(4, 5)$: $1 + 3t = 4$ gives $t = 1$, but $2 + 4 \cdot 1 = 6 \ne 5$.

$(7, 10)$ is on the line ($t = 2$); $(4, 5)$ is not

Example 3

Find a direction vector of the line $2x + 3y = 6$, and write it as a vector equation.

Show solution
  1. Two points: $(3, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$. Direction: $(0, 2) - (3, 0) = (-3, 2)$.

$\vec{p} = (3, 0) + t(-3, 2)$ (other answers are possible)

Example 4

Find where the lines $(1, 0) + s(1, 1)$ and $(0, 3) + t(1, -1)$ meet.

Show solution
  1. $1 + s = t$ and $s = 3 - t$.
  2. Add: $1 + 2s = 3$, so $s = 1$, $t = 2$.

$(2, 1)$

When two lines meet, use different letters for their parameters ($s$ and $t$). Using $t$ for both would wrongly demand that they reach the point at the same parameter value.
The vector equation of a line is not unique: any point on the line and any nonzero multiple of $\vec{d}$ give the same line. Check answers by substituting, not by comparing forms.
  • $\vec{p} = \vec{a} + t\vec{d}$: point + parameter × direction.
  • Parametric form: $x = x_1 + lt$, $y = y_1 + mt$.
  • Remove $t$ to get $m(x - x_1) - l(y - y_1) = 0$.

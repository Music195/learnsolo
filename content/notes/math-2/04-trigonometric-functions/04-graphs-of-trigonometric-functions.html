Graphs of Trigonometric Functions

Waves that repeat

As $P$ goes round the unit circle, its height $\sin\theta$ rises and falls between $-1$ and $1$, repeating every $2\pi$. $\cos\theta$ is the same wave shifted left by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. $\tan\theta$ repeats every $\pi$ and has vertical asymptotes where $\cos\theta = 0$.

$y = a\sin k(\theta - p)$ stretches the wave to height $|a|$, squeezes it to period $\dfrac{2\pi}{|k|}$, and moves it $p$ to the right.

Basic graphs

$$y = \sin\theta,\; y = \cos\theta: \text{ period } 2\pi, \text{ range } -1 \le y \le 1; \qquad y = \tan\theta: \text{ period } \pi$$

Asymptotes of $y = \tan\theta$: $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$.

Transformations

$$y = a\sin k(\theta - p): \quad \text{height } |a|, \quad \text{period } \frac{2\pi}{|k|}, \quad \text{shift } p \text{ to the right}$$
  1. $\sin k\left(\theta + \dfrac{2\pi}{k}\right) = \sin(k\theta + 2\pi) = \sin k\theta$, so the graph repeats after $\dfrac{2\pi}{k}$ ($k > 0$).
  2. No smaller positive number works, because $\sin$ itself has no period smaller than $2\pi$.

$y = a \cdot f\,k(\theta - p)$

Example 1

Find the period and range of $y = 3\cos 2\theta$.

Show solution
  1. Period $\dfrac{2\pi}{2}$; height $3$.

Period $\pi$; $-3 \le y \le 3$

Example 2

Describe how to get $y = \sin\left(2\theta - \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$ from $y = \sin 2\theta$.

Show solution
  1. Take out the $2$: $\sin 2\left(\theta - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$.

Shift $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to the right (not $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$)

Example 3

Find the period and the asymptotes of $y = \tan 3\theta$.

Show solution
  1. Period $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Asymptotes where $3\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$.

Period $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$; $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{n\pi}{3}$

The shift is read from $k(\theta - p)$, so factor out $k$ first. $\sin(2\theta - \dfrac{\pi}{3})$ moves by $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, not $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
To sketch, mark one period and split it into four equal parts: the wave hits zero, top, zero, bottom (for $\sin$) at those points.
  • $\sin$, $\cos$: period $2\pi$; $\tan$: period $\pi$.
  • $y = a\sin k(\theta - p)$: height $|a|$, period $\dfrac{2\pi}{|k|}$, shift $p$.
  • Factor out $k$ before reading the shift.

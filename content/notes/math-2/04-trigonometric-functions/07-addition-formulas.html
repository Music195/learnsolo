Addition and Subtraction

$\sin(\alpha + \beta)$ is not $\sin\alpha + \sin\beta$

Turning by $\alpha$ and then by $\beta$ is a turn by $\alpha + \beta$. The addition formulas give its $\sin$ and $\cos$ from those of $\alpha$ and $\beta$. All the formulas in notes 08 to 10 come from these.

Addition formulas (double signs in the same order)

$$\sin(\alpha \pm \beta) = \sin\alpha\cos\beta \pm \cos\alpha\sin\beta$$
$$\cos(\alpha \pm \beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta \mp \sin\alpha\sin\beta$$
$$\tan(\alpha \pm \beta) = \frac{\tan\alpha \pm \tan\beta}{1 \mp \tan\alpha\tan\beta}$$
  1. $P(\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha)$ and $Q(\cos\beta, \sin\beta)$ on the unit circle. By the distance formula, $PQ^2 = 2 - 2(\cos\alpha\cos\beta + \sin\alpha\sin\beta)$.
  2. Turn the picture by $-\beta$: $Q$ goes to $(1, 0)$ and $P$ to $(\cos(\alpha - \beta), \sin(\alpha - \beta))$. Then $PQ^2 = 2 - 2\cos(\alpha - \beta)$.
  3. The length did not change, so $\cos(\alpha - \beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta + \sin\alpha\sin\beta$.
  4. Replace $\beta$ by $-\beta$ for $\cos(\alpha + \beta)$; use $\sin\theta = \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)$ for $\sin$; divide for $\tan$.

Example 1

Find $\sin 75°$ and $\cos 15°$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin(45° + 30°) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. $\cos(45° - 30°) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Both equal $\dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$ (as they should: $\sin 75° = \cos 15°$)

Example 2

Find $\tan 15°$.

Show solution
  1. $\tan(45° - 30°) = \dfrac{1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}{1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{\sqrt{3} + 1}$.
  2. Rationalise: $\dfrac{(\sqrt{3} - 1)^2}{2} = \dfrac{4 - 2\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

$2 - \sqrt{3}$

Example 3

$\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$ ($0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$), $\cos\beta = -\dfrac{5}{13}$ ($\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \beta < \pi$). Find $\cos(\alpha + \beta)$.

Show solution
  1. $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$, $\sin\beta = \dfrac{12}{13}$ (signs from the quadrants).
  2. $\dfrac{4}{5} \cdot \left(-\dfrac{5}{13}\right) - \dfrac{3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{12}{13} = \dfrac{-20 - 36}{65}$.

$-\dfrac{56}{65}$

Example 4

Find the acute angle between the lines $y = 3x$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}x$.

Show solution
  1. The lines make angles $\alpha$, $\beta$ with the $x$-axis where $\tan\alpha = 3$, $\tan\beta = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. $\tan(\alpha - \beta) = \dfrac{3 - \frac{1}{2}}{1 + 3 \cdot \frac{1}{2}} = 1$.

$\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ ($45°$)

In the cosine formula the sign flips: $\cos(\alpha + \beta)$ has a minus, $\cos(\alpha - \beta)$ a plus.
$\sin$: "sin cos, cos sin", same sign. $\cos$: "cos cos, sin sin", opposite sign.
  • $\sin(\alpha \pm \beta) = \sin\alpha\cos\beta \pm \cos\alpha\sin\beta$.
  • $\cos(\alpha \pm \beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta \mp \sin\alpha\sin\beta$.
  • $\tan(\alpha \pm \beta) = \dfrac{\tan\alpha \pm \tan\beta}{1 \mp \tan\alpha\tan\beta}$.

Trigonometric Equations and Inequalities

Read the answers off the circle or the graph

$\sin\theta = c$ asks: where is the height of $P$ equal to $c$? On the unit circle that is where the horizontal line $y = c$ meets the circle, usually at two points per turn. On the graph of $y = \sin\theta$ it is where the wave meets the line $y = c$.

For an inequality, take the part of the wave above (or below) the line.

Within $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$

$$\sin\theta = c: \; y = c \text{ meets the circle}, \qquad \cos\theta = c: \; x = c, \qquad \tan\theta = c: \; \text{line } OT,\; T(1, c)$$

For all real $\theta$, add $2n\pi$ ($n\pi$ for $\tan$) to each answer.

  1. If $-1 < c < 1$, the line $y = c$ cuts the circle at two points, mirror images in the $y$-axis.
  2. So if $\alpha$ is one answer, the other is $\pi - \alpha$ (note 05: $\sin(\pi - \alpha) = \sin\alpha$).

Solve $\sin\theta \;\square\; c$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$

Example 1

Solve $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

Show solution
  1. The line $y = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ meets the circle in quadrants III and IV, at reference angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

$\theta = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi, \dfrac{5}{3}\pi$

Example 2

Solve $\cos\theta \ge \dfrac{1}{2}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

Show solution
  1. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5}{3}\pi$. The points with $x \ge \dfrac{1}{2}$ are on the right of the circle.

$0 \le \theta \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\;\dfrac{5}{3}\pi \le \theta < 2\pi$

Example 3

Solve $2\sin^2\theta - \cos\theta - 1 = 0$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

Show solution
  1. Use $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta$: $2\cos^2\theta + \cos\theta - 1 = 0$, so $(2\cos\theta - 1)(\cos\theta + 1) = 0$.
  2. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $\dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{5}{3}\pi$. $\;\cos\theta = -1$: $\pi$.

$\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \pi, \dfrac{5}{3}\pi$

Example 4

Solve $\tan\theta > 1$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

Show solution
  1. $\tan\theta = 1$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{5}{4}\pi$; $\tan$ grows up to each asymptote $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{3}{2}\pi$.

$\dfrac{\pi}{4} < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\;\dfrac{5}{4}\pi < \theta < \dfrac{3}{2}\pi$

If the angle is $2\theta$ or $\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, first work out its range. For $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$, $2\theta$ runs over $0 \le 2\theta < 4\pi$: twice as many answers.
With both $\sin$ and $\cos$ in a quadratic, use $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ to leave only one of them (Example 3). Then it's a quadratic in that function.
  • $\sin$: horizontal line; $\cos$: vertical line; $\tan$: line through $(1, c)$.
  • Find the range of the angle first.
  • Mixed $\sin^2$, $\cos$: rewrite in one function.

Trigonometric Properties

Each rule is a symmetry of the unit circle

Changing the angle moves $P(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ to another point $Q$ of the circle. Each rule below is just where $Q$ lands: a reflection in an axis, a half turn, or a reflection in the line $y = x$.

The properties

$$\begin{array}{lll} \sin(\theta + 2n\pi) = \sin\theta & \cos(\theta + 2n\pi) = \cos\theta & \tan(\theta + n\pi) = \tan\theta \\ \sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta & \cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta & \tan(-\theta) = -\tan\theta \\ \sin(\theta + \pi) = -\sin\theta & \cos(\theta + \pi) = -\cos\theta & \tan(\theta + \pi) = \tan\theta \\ \sin(\pi - \theta) = \sin\theta & \cos(\pi - \theta) = -\cos\theta & \tan(\pi - \theta) = -\tan\theta \\ \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) = \cos\theta & \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) = \sin\theta & \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) = \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta} \end{array}$$
  1. $-\theta$: reflect $P$ in the $x$-axis, $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$.
  2. $\theta + \pi$: half turn about $O$, $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$.
  3. $\pi - \theta$: reflect in the $y$-axis, $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$.
  4. $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta$: reflect in $y = x$, $(x, y) \to (y, x)$.

Drag $P$ (angle $\theta$); $Q$ is the changed angle

Example 1

Find $\sin\dfrac{25}{6}\pi$, $\cos\left(-\dfrac{11}{3}\pi\right)$, $\tan\dfrac{5}{4}\pi$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{25}{6}\pi = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 4\pi$, so $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. $\cos\left(-\dfrac{11}{3}\pi\right) = \cos\dfrac{11}{3}\pi = \cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 4\pi\right) = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  3. $\tan\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \pi\right) = \tan\dfrac{\pi}{4} = 1$.

$\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\;\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\;1$

Example 2

Simplify $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} + \theta\right) + \cos(\pi - \theta)$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} + \theta\right) = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - (-\theta)\right) = \cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$.
  2. $\cos(\pi - \theta) = -\cos\theta$.

$0$

Example 3

Find $\sin^2\dfrac{\pi}{8} + \sin^2\dfrac{3\pi}{8}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{3\pi}{8} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{8}$, so $\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{8} = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{8}$.
  2. $\sin^2\dfrac{\pi}{8} + \cos^2\dfrac{\pi}{8} = 1$.

$1$

$\tan$ repeats every $\pi$, not $2\pi$: $\tan(\theta + \pi) = \tan\theta$, while $\sin$ and $\cos$ change sign.
Don't memorise the table: draw $P$ and $Q$ on the circle and read off which coordinate changes sign or swaps.
  • $-\theta$: $\cos$ even, $\sin$ and $\tan$ odd.
  • $\theta + \pi$: $\sin$, $\cos$ change sign; $\tan$ the same.
  • $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta$: $\sin$ and $\cos$ swap.

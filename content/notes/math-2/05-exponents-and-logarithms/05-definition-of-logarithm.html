Exponent and Logarithm

A logarithm is an exponent

$\log_a M$ answers one question: "$a$ to what power gives $M$?" Since $2^5 = 32$, $\log_2 32 = 5$.

$a$ is the base and $M$ the antilogarithm (真数). Because $a^p$ is always positive, $M$ must be positive.

Definition ($a > 0$, $a \ne 1$, $M > 0$)

$$a^p = M \iff p = \log_a M$$

Special values: $\log_a 1 = 0$, $\;\log_a a = 1$, $\;\log_a a^p = p$, $\;a^{\log_a M} = M$.

$\log_a M$: "$a$ to what power gives $M$?"

Example 1

Find $\log_3 81$, $\;\log_2 \dfrac{1}{8}$ and $\log_{10} 0.01$.

Show solution
  1. $3^4 = 81$, $\;2^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{8}$, $\;10^{-2} = 0.01$.

$4$, $\;-3$, $\;-2$

Example 2

Find $\log_4 8$ and $\log_{\sqrt{2}} 8$.

Show solution
  1. $4^p = 8 \Rightarrow 2^{2p} = 2^3 \Rightarrow p = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
  2. $(\sqrt{2})^p = 2^{\frac{p}{2}} = 2^3 \Rightarrow p = 6$.

$\dfrac{3}{2}$, $\;6$

Example 3

Find $x$: (i) $\log_5 x = 2$ (ii) $\log_x 27 = 3$.

Show solution
  1. (i) $x = 5^2$.
  2. (ii) $x^3 = 27$, and the base must be positive and not 1.

(i) $x = 25$ (ii) $x = 3$

$\log_a M$ needs base $a > 0$, $a \ne 1$ and antilogarithm $M > 0$. $\log_2 (-4)$ and $\log_1 5$ have no meaning.
Stuck on a log? Rewrite it as an exponent equation: $\log_a M = p$ ⟺ $a^p = M$, then solve with the exponent laws.
  • $\log_a M$ = the power you put on $a$ to get $M$.
  • $\log_a 1 = 0$, $\log_a a = 1$.
  • Conditions: $a > 0$, $a \ne 1$, $M > 0$.

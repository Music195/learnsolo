Exponent Laws

Extend exponents so the laws keep working

$a^3$ means three $a$'s multiplied together, but what could $a^0$, $a^{-2}$ or $a^{1/2}$ mean? We choose their meanings so that the old laws stay true. For example, $a^0 \times a^3 = a^{0 + 3} = a^3$ forces $a^0 = 1$.

Definitions ($a > 0$; $m, n$ positive whole numbers)

$$a^0 = 1, \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}, \qquad a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m$$

Laws ($a, b > 0$; $r, s$ any rational numbers)

$$a^r a^s = a^{r+s}, \qquad \frac{a^r}{a^s} = a^{r-s}, \qquad (a^r)^s = a^{rs}, \qquad (ab)^r = a^r b^r$$
  1. $a^0 \cdot a^n = a^n$ needs $a^0 = 1$.
  2. $a^{-n} \cdot a^n = a^0 = 1$ needs $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$.
  3. $\left(a^{1/n}\right)^n = a^1 = a$ needs $a^{1/n}$ to be the positive $n$th root, $\sqrt[n]{a}$.

Work out $a^{p/q}$

Example 1

Find $2^{-3}$, $\;8^{\frac{2}{3}}$ and $27^{-\frac{1}{3}}$.

Show solution
  1. $2^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{8}$
  2. $8^{\frac{2}{3}} = (\sqrt[3]{8})^2 = 2^2$
  3. $27^{-\frac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{27}}$

$\dfrac{1}{8}$, $\;4$, $\;\dfrac{1}{3}$

Example 2

Simplify $a^{\frac{1}{2}} \times a^{\frac{1}{3}} \div a^{\frac{1}{6}}$ ($a > 0$).

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3} - \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{3 + 2 - 1}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

$a^{\frac{2}{3}}$

Example 3

Calculate $\sqrt[3]{4} \times \sqrt[6]{2} \div \sqrt{2}$.

Show solution
  1. Write as powers of 2: $2^{\frac{2}{3}} \times 2^{\frac{1}{6}} \div 2^{\frac{1}{2}}$.
  2. $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{6} - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{4 + 1 - 3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

$2^{\frac{1}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{2}$

Example 4

$a^{\frac{1}{2}} + a^{-\frac{1}{2}} = 3$. Find $a + a^{-1}$.

Show solution
  1. Square: $a + 2a^{\frac{1}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}} + a^{-1} = 9$, and $a^{\frac{1}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}} = a^0 = 1$.

$a + a^{-1} = 7$

A negative exponent does not make the number negative: $2^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{8}$, not $-8$.
With roots of different orders, change everything to powers of the same base (usually a prime) and just add or subtract fractions.
  • $a^0 = 1$, $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$, $a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$.
  • The laws hold for all rational exponents (with $a > 0$).
  • Convert roots to fractional exponents to simplify.

Equation of a Line (Two-Point Form)

Two points give the slope

Through two different points there is exactly one line. Their slope is $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$; then use the point-slope form from note 05.

If the two points have the same $x$-coordinate, the line is vertical.

Through $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$

$$x_1 \ne x_2: \; y - y_1 = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}(x - x_1), \qquad x_1 = x_2: \; x = x_1$$

Through $(a, 0)$ and $(0, b)$ ($ab \ne 0$): $\dfrac{x}{a} + \dfrac{y}{b} = 1$ (intercept form).

  1. Slope from $(a, 0)$ to $(0, b)$: $\dfrac{b - 0}{0 - a} = -\dfrac{b}{a}$.
  2. $y = -\dfrac{b}{a}(x - a) = -\dfrac{b}{a}x + b$. Divide by $b$: $\dfrac{y}{b} = -\dfrac{x}{a} + 1$, i.e. $\dfrac{x}{a} + \dfrac{y}{b} = 1$.

Example 1

Find the line through $(3, 4)$ and $(7, 10)$, and the line through $(5, 2)$ and $(5, 9)$.

Show solution
  1. Slope $\dfrac{10 - 4}{7 - 3} = \dfrac{3}{2}$: $y - 4 = \dfrac{3}{2}(x - 3)$, i.e. $3x - 2y - 1 = 0$.
  2. Same $x$-coordinate: vertical.

$3x - 2y - 1 = 0$; $\;x = 5$

Example 2

Find the line with $x$-intercept $3$ and $y$-intercept $-2$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{x}{3} + \dfrac{y}{-2} = 1$; multiply by $6$: $2x - 3y = 6$.

$2x - 3y - 6 = 0$

Example 3

$A(1, 1)$, $B(3, 5)$, $C(k, 9)$ lie on one line. Find $k$.

Show solution
  1. Line $AB$: slope $2$, $y = 2x - 1$.
  2. $C$ on it: $9 = 2k - 1$.

$k = 5$

Example 4

Find the line through the origin and the intersection point of $2x + y - 4 = 0$ and $x - y + 1 = 0$.

Show solution
  1. Solve: adding gives $3x - 3 = 0$, so $x = 1$, $y = 2$.
  2. Line through $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 2)$: $y = 2x$.
  3. (Shortcut: every line $(2x + y - 4) + k(x - y + 1) = 0$ passes through the intersection; $(0, 0)$ gives $k = 4$, so $6x - 3y = 0$.)

$y = 2x$

If $x_1 = x_2$, the slope formula divides by $0$. Don't force it: the line is $x = x_1$.
Give answers in general form with whole numbers ($3x - 2y - 1 = 0$): it's tidy and also works for vertical lines.
  • Slope from two points, then point-slope form.
  • $x_1 = x_2$: the line is $x = x_1$.
  • Intercepts $a$, $b$: $\dfrac{x}{a} + \dfrac{y}{b} = 1$.

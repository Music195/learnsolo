Distance Formula

Pythagoras on the grid

Go from $A$ to $B$ across and then up. The horizontal step $x_2 - x_1$ and the vertical step $y_2 - y_1$ are the two short sides of a right triangle, and $AB$ is its hypotenuse.

Distance between $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$

$$AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

From the origin: $OA = \sqrt{x_1^2 + y_1^2}$.

  1. Let $C(x_2, y_1)$. Then $AC = |x_2 - x_1|$, $CB = |y_2 - y_1|$ and $\angle ACB = 90°$.
  2. Pythagoras: $AB^2 = AC^2 + CB^2 = (x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2$.

Drag $A$ and $B$

Example 1

Find the distance between $P(-1, 2)$ and $Q(3, -4)$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{(3 + 1)^2 + (-4 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 36} = \sqrt{52}$.

$2\sqrt{13}$

Example 2

Find the point $P$ on the $x$-axis with $PA = PB$, where $A(1, 3)$ and $B(4, 2)$.

Show solution
  1. $P(x, 0)$: $PA^2 = (x - 1)^2 + 9$, $PB^2 = (x - 4)^2 + 4$.
  2. $x^2 - 2x + 10 = x^2 - 8x + 20 \Rightarrow 6x = 10$.

$P\left(\dfrac{5}{3}, 0\right)$

Example 3

Is the triangle $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 2)$, $C(2, 4)$ right-angled or isosceles?

Show solution
  1. $AB^2 = 9 + 1 = 10$, $AC^2 = 1 + 9 = 10$, $BC^2 = 4 + 4 = 8$.
  2. $AB = AC$; and $AB^2 \ne AC^2 + BC^2$ etc., so no right angle.

Isosceles with $AB = AC$ (not right-angled)

Example 4 (proof with coordinates)

$M$ is the midpoint of $BC$ in $\triangle ABC$. Prove $AB^2 + AC^2 = 2(AM^2 + BM^2)$.

Show solution
  1. Choose easy coordinates: $M(0, 0)$, $B(-c, 0)$, $C(c, 0)$, $A(a, b)$.
  2. $AB^2 + AC^2 = (a + c)^2 + b^2 + (a - c)^2 + b^2 = 2(a^2 + b^2 + c^2)$.
  3. $2(AM^2 + BM^2) = 2(a^2 + b^2 + c^2)$.

Both sides are equal (this is the median theorem).

Square the whole difference, sign included: $(-4 - 2)^2 = 36$. Don't add the coordinates or forget the brackets.
To compare or equate distances, work with $AB^2$; the square roots disappear.
  • $AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.
  • Use squared distances for "equal distance" problems.
  • For proofs, place the figure so the coordinates are simple.

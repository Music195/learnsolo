Linear Inequalities and Regions

A line splits the plane into two halves

On the line $y = mx + n$, $y$ equals $mx + n$. Directly above a point of the line, $y$ is bigger; directly below, smaller. So $y > mx + n$ is the whole half-plane above the line, and $y < mx + n$ the half-plane below.

With $>$ or $<$ the line itself is not included; with $\ge$ or $\le$ it is.

Half-planes

$$y > mx + n: \text{ above the line}, \qquad y < mx + n: \text{ below}, \qquad x > k: \text{ right of } x = k$$

Several inequalities together: the region is the overlap of all the half-planes.

  1. Take any $x = x_0$. The vertical line $x = x_0$ meets the boundary at $y = mx_0 + n$.
  2. Points $(x_0, y)$ on that vertical line satisfy $y > mx_0 + n$ exactly when they are above the meeting point.
  3. Doing this for every $x_0$ gives the whole half-plane above the line.

$y \;\square\; mx + n$; drag the test point $P$

Example 1

Describe the regions $y \ge x + 2$ and $2x - y < 4$.

Show solution
  1. $y \ge x + 2$: above the line, line included. (Check: $(0, 0)$ gives $0 \ge 2$, false, and $(0, 0)$ is below the line.)
  2. $2x - y < 4 \iff y > 2x - 4$: above the line, line excluded.

Above $y = x + 2$ (with the line); above $y = 2x - 4$ (without the line)

Example 2

Describe the region $x + y \le 4$, $\;y \ge x$, $\;y \ge 0$.

Show solution
  1. Below $y = -x + 4$, above $y = x$, above the $x$-axis.
  2. Corners: $y = x$ and $x + y = 4$ meet at $(2, 2)$; $y = x$ meets $y = 0$ at $(0, 0)$; $x + y = 4$ meets $y = 0$ at $(4, 0)$.

The triangle $(0, 0)$, $(4, 0)$, $(2, 2)$, edges included

Example 3

Describe the region $(x - y)(x + y - 2) > 0$.

Show solution
  1. A product is positive when both factors have the same sign.
  2. Both positive: $y < x$ and $y > -x + 2$. Both negative: $y > x$ and $y < -x + 2$.

The right and left "wedges" between the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x + 2$ (boundaries excluded)

When you divide by a negative number to get $y$ alone, the sign flips: $x - 2y > 4 \iff y < \dfrac{1}{2}x - 2$ (below, not above).
Not sure which side? Test a point not on the line, usually $(0, 0)$. If it satisfies the inequality, shade its side; if not, the other side.

  • $\ge$, $\le$ include the line; $>$, $<$ don't.

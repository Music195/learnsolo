Centroid Coordinates

The average of the three corners

The centroid $G$ is where the three medians (corner to midpoint of the opposite side) meet. It divides each median in the ratio $2 : 1$ from the corner. In coordinates this works out to the plain average of the three vertices.

$A(x_1, y_1)$, $B(x_2, y_2)$, $C(x_3, y_3)$

$$G\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3},\; \frac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3}\right)$$
  1. The midpoint of $BC$ is $M\left(\dfrac{x_2 + x_3}{2}, \dfrac{y_2 + y_3}{2}\right)$.
  2. $G$ divides $AM$ internally in the ratio $2 : 1$, so its $x$-coordinate is $\dfrac{1 \cdot x_1 + 2 \cdot \frac{x_2 + x_3}{2}}{3} = \dfrac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3}$.
  3. The same for $y$.

Drag $A$, $B$, $C$

Example 1

Find the centroid of $A(3, 4)$, $B(7, 6)$, $C(5, 9)$.

Show solution
  1. $\left(\dfrac{3 + 7 + 5}{3}, \dfrac{4 + 6 + 9}{3}\right)$.

$G\left(5, \dfrac{19}{3}\right)$

Example 2

The centroid of $\triangle ABC$ is $(4, 5)$, with $A(2, 3)$ and $B(6, 7)$. Find $C$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{2 + 6 + x}{3} = 4 \Rightarrow x = 4$; $\;\dfrac{3 + 7 + y}{3} = 5 \Rightarrow y = 5$.

$C(4, 5)$

Example 3

$A(1, 2)$, $B(5, 0)$, $C(3, 7)$. $P$, $Q$, $R$ divide $BC$, $CA$, $AB$ internally in the ratio $1 : 2$. Show $\triangle PQR$ has the same centroid as $\triangle ABC$.

Show solution
  1. $P = \dfrac{2B + C}{3} = \left(\dfrac{13}{3}, \dfrac{7}{3}\right)$, $Q = \dfrac{2C + A}{3} = \left(\dfrac{7}{3}, \dfrac{16}{3}\right)$, $R = \dfrac{2A + B}{3} = \left(\dfrac{7}{3}, \dfrac{4}{3}\right)$.
  2. Centroid of $PQR$: $\left(\dfrac{27}{9}, \dfrac{27}{9}\right) = (3, 3)$. Centroid of $ABC$: $\left(\dfrac{9}{3}, \dfrac{9}{3}\right) = (3, 3)$.

Both are $(3, 3)$.

$AG : GM = 2 : 1$ is measured from the corner. $G$ is closer to the midpoint $M$ than to $A$.
If the centroid and two corners are known, the third is $C = 3G - A - B$ (coordinate by coordinate).
  • $G$ = average of the three vertices.
  • $G$ divides each median $2 : 1$ from the corner.
  • $C = 3G - A - B$.

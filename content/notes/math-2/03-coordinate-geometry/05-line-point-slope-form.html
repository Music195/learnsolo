Equation of a Line (Point-Slope Form)

One point and a steepness fix the line

The slope $m$ says how much $y$ goes up for each step of $1$ in $x$. Knowing one point $(x_1, y_1)$ and the slope fixes the line.

Every line, including vertical ones, can be written in the general form $ax + by + c = 0$.

Through $(x_1, y_1)$ with slope $m$

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

Vertical line through $(x_1, y_1)$ (no slope): $x = x_1$.

General form

$$ax + by + c = 0 \qquad (a, b \text{ not both } 0)$$

If $b \ne 0$: slope $-\dfrac{a}{b}$, $y$-intercept $-\dfrac{c}{b}$. If $b = 0$: a vertical line.

  1. A point $(x, y) \ne (x_1, y_1)$ with $x \ne x_1$ lies on the line exactly when the slope from $(x_1, y_1)$ to it is $m$: $\dfrac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = m$.
  2. Multiply by $x - x_1$: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$. The point $(x_1, y_1)$ itself also satisfies this.

Example 1

Find the line through $(2, 3)$ with slope $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the line through $(-1, 4)$ with slope $-2$.

Show solution
  1. $y - 3 = \dfrac{1}{2}(x - 2) \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{1}{2}x + 2$.
  2. $y - 4 = -2(x + 1) \Rightarrow y = -2x + 2$.

$y = \dfrac{1}{2}x + 2$; $\;y = -2x + 2$

Example 2

Find the slope and $y$-intercept of $2x + 3y - 6 = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $3y = -2x + 6 \Rightarrow y = -\dfrac{2}{3}x + 2$.

Slope $-\dfrac{2}{3}$, $y$-intercept $2$

Example 3

Find the lines through $(3, -1)$ parallel to the $y$-axis and parallel to the $x$-axis.

Show solution
  1. Parallel to the $y$-axis: vertical, so $x = 3$. Parallel to the $x$-axis: slope $0$, so $y = -1$.

$x = 3$; $\;y = -1$

A vertical line has no slope, so $y = mx + n$ can't describe it. Use $x = x_1$, or the general form with $b = 0$.
Check your equation by putting the given point in: it must make the equation true.

  • Vertical: $x = x_1$.

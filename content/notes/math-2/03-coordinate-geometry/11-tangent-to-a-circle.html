Tangent Equation at a Point on a Circle

The tangent is perpendicular to the radius

At the point of contact $P$, the tangent meets the radius $OP$ at a right angle. This gives a very short formula: in $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, replace one $x$ by $x_1$ and one $y$ by $y_1$.

Tangent at $P(x_1, y_1)$ on the circle

$$x^2 + y^2 = r^2: \quad x_1x + y_1y = r^2$$
$$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2: \quad (x_1 - a)(x - a) + (y_1 - b)(y - b) = r^2$$
  1. The radius $OP$ is the line $y_1x - x_1y = 0$.
  2. By note 07, a line $x_1x + y_1y = c$ is perpendicular to it, since $x_1 \cdot y_1 + y_1 \cdot (-x_1) = 0$.
  3. It passes through $P$ when $c = x_1^2 + y_1^2 = r^2$. For a centre $(a, b)$, shift everything by $(a, b)$.

Drag $P$ round the circle

Example 1

Find the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ at $(3, 4)$, and to $x^2 + y^2 = 36$ at $(-6, 0)$.

Show solution
  1. $3x + 4y = 25$.
  2. $-6x + 0 \cdot y = 36$, i.e. $x = -6$: a vertical tangent (the radius is horizontal there).

$3x + 4y = 25$; $\;x = -6$

Example 2

Find the tangent to $(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 13$ at $(5, 3)$.

Show solution
  1. Check: $3^2 + 2^2 = 13$, so the point is on the circle.
  2. $(5 - 2)(x - 2) + (3 - 1)(y - 1) = 13 \Rightarrow 3x - 6 + 2y - 2 = 13$.

$3x + 2y = 21$

Example 3

Find the tangents to $x^2 + y^2 = 5$ that pass through $A(3, 1)$ (outside the circle).

Show solution
  1. Let the contact point be $(p, q)$: tangent $px + qy = 5$, and $p^2 + q^2 = 5$.
  2. Through $A$: $3p + q = 5$, so $q = 5 - 3p$. Then $p^2 + (5 - 3p)^2 = 5 \Rightarrow p^2 - 3p + 2 = 0$.
  3. $p = 1, q = 2$ or $p = 2, q = -1$.

$x + 2y = 5$ and $2x - y = 5$

The formula only works for a point on the circle. Check $x_1^2 + y_1^2 = r^2$ first; for a point outside, use the contact point $(p, q)$ as in Example 3.
The formula handles vertical tangents automatically ($y_1 = 0$ gives $x = \dfrac{r^2}{x_1}$). That's an advantage over "slope = $-\dfrac{1}{\text{slope of }OP}$".
  • $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ at $(x_1, y_1)$: $x_1x + y_1y = r^2$.
  • Shifted circle: $(x_1 - a)(x - a) + (y_1 - b)(y - b) = r^2$.
  • From an outside point: unknown contact point $(p, q)$.

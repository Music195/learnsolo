Section Formula in 2D

Use the number-line formula twice

Drop $A$, $P$, $B$ onto the $x$-axis: the ratio $AP : PB$ stays the same (parallel lines cut segments in equal ratios). So the $x$-coordinate of $P$ comes from the number-line formula (note 01), and the same for $y$.

$A(x_1, y_1)$, $B(x_2, y_2)$, ratio $m : n$

$$\text{internal: } \left(\frac{nx_1 + mx_2}{m + n},\; \frac{ny_1 + my_2}{m + n}\right)$$
$$\text{external: } \left(\frac{-nx_1 + mx_2}{m - n},\; \frac{-ny_1 + my_2}{m - n}\right)$$

Midpoint: $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$.

  1. Let $A'$, $P'$, $B'$ be the feet of the perpendiculars from $A$, $P$, $B$ to the $x$-axis.
  2. $AA'$, $PP'$, $BB'$ are parallel, so $A'P' : P'B' = AP : PB = m : n$.
  3. By note 01, $P'$ is at $\dfrac{nx_1 + mx_2}{m + n}$. The $y$-coordinate works the same way with the $y$-axis.

Drag $A$ and $B$; choose the ratio

Example 1

$A(2, 2)$, $B(8, 7)$. Find the points dividing $AB$ internally and externally in the ratio $2 : 1$.

Show solution
  1. Internal: $\left(\dfrac{2 + 16}{3}, \dfrac{2 + 14}{3}\right) = \left(6, \dfrac{16}{3}\right)$.
  2. External: $\left(\dfrac{-2 + 16}{1}, \dfrac{-2 + 14}{1}\right) = (14, 12)$.

$\left(6, \dfrac{16}{3}\right)$ and $(14, 12)$

Example 2

$A(1, 3)$, $B(5, 7)$. Find the point dividing $AB$ externally in the ratio $1 : 3$.

Show solution
  1. $\left(\dfrac{-3 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 5}{1 - 3}, \dfrac{-3 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 7}{1 - 3}\right) = \left(\dfrac{2}{-2}, \dfrac{-2}{-2}\right)$.

$(-1, 1)$ (beyond $A$, since $m < n$)

Example 3

$M(3, -1)$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $A(1, 4)$. Find $B$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1 + x}{2} = 3$, $\dfrac{4 + y}{2} = -1$.

$B(5, -6)$

$n$ multiplies the coordinates of $A$ and $m$ those of $B$. Check with a sketch: for $2 : 1$ internal, the point must be closer to $B$.
External division = internal division with $n$ replaced by $-n$. One formula covers both.
  • Internal: $\left(\dfrac{nx_1 + mx_2}{m + n}, \dfrac{ny_1 + my_2}{m + n}\right)$.
  • External: replace $n$ by $-n$.
  • Each coordinate is worked out separately.

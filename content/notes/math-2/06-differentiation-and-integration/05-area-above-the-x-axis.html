Definite Integral and Area 1

$F(b) - F(a)$ measures the area under the curve

The definite integral $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b = F(b) - F(a)$, where $F$ is any antiderivative (the $C$ cancels).

If the graph is above the $x$-axis between $a$ and $b$, this number is exactly the area between the curve, the $x$-axis and the lines $x = a$, $x = b$.

Definite integral

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b = F(b) - F(a)$$

Properties: $\displaystyle\int_a^a f\,dx = 0$, $\;\int_b^a f\,dx = -\int_a^b f\,dx$, $\;\int_a^c f\,dx + \int_c^b f\,dx = \int_a^b f\,dx$.

Area when $f(x) \ge 0$ on $a \le x \le b$

$$S = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$

$f(x) = -x^2 + 2x + 3$: drag the ends $A$ and $B$

Example 1

Calculate $\displaystyle\int_1^3 (x^2 - 2x + 3)\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[\dfrac{x^3}{3} - x^2 + 3x\Big]_1^3 = (9 - 9 + 9) - \left(\dfrac{1}{3} - 1 + 3\right)$
  2. $= 9 - \dfrac{7}{3}$

$\dfrac{20}{3}$

Example 2

Find the area between $y = x^2 + 1$, the $x$-axis, $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 + 1 > 0$, so $S = \displaystyle\int_0^2 (x^2 + 1)\,dx = \Big[\dfrac{x^3}{3} + x\Big]_0^2$.

$\dfrac{14}{3}$

Example 3

Find the area enclosed by $y = -x^2 + 2x + 3$ and the $x$-axis.

Show solution
  1. $-x^2 + 2x + 3 = -(x + 1)(x - 3)$: it meets the axis at $x = -1, 3$ and is above it in between.
  2. $S = \Big[-\dfrac{x^3}{3} + x^2 + 3x\Big]_{-1}^{3} = 9 - \left(-\dfrac{5}{3}\right)$

$\dfrac{32}{3}$

Shortcut: $\displaystyle\int_\alpha^\beta (x - \alpha)(x - \beta)\,dx = -\frac{(\beta - \alpha)^3}{6}$. For Example 3: $1 \times \dfrac{4^3}{6} = \dfrac{32}{3}$ ✓.
$\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ equals the area only when $f(x) \ge 0$ on the whole interval. Below the axis it comes out negative (see note 05).
  • $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$.
  • Above the axis: area $= \displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
  • Find where the curve meets the axis first.

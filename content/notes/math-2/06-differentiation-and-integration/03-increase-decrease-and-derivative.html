Monotonicity and Derivative Sign

The sign of $f'$ tells you uphill or downhill

Where the slope $f'(x)$ is positive the graph goes up; where it is negative it goes down. Where $f'$ changes from $+$ to $-$ the graph has a peak, a local maximum (極大). From $-$ to $+$ gives a valley, a local minimum (極小).

On an interval

$$f'(x) > 0 \Rightarrow f \text{ increasing}, \qquad f'(x) < 0 \Rightarrow f \text{ decreasing}$$

Method: solve $f'(x) = 0$, make a sign table (増減表) for $f'$, read off increasing/decreasing and the local max/min.

$f(x) = x^3 + bx^2 + cx$: change $b$ and $c$

Example 1

Find the intervals of increase/decrease and the extreme values of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2$.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x = 3x(x - 2)$, zero at $x = 0, 2$.
  2. Sign of $f'$: $+$ for $x < 0$, $-$ for $0 < x < 2$, $+$ for $x > 2$.
  3. $f(0) = 2$ (peak), $f(2) = -2$ (valley).

Increasing for $x \le 0$, $x \ge 2$; decreasing for $0 \le x \le 2$. Local max $2$ ($x = 0$), local min $-2$ ($x = 2$).

Example 2

Find the maximum and minimum of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ on $-2 \le x \le 3$.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 3(x + 1)(x - 1)$: local max $f(-1) = 2$, local min $f(1) = -2$.
  2. Ends: $f(-2) = -2$, $f(3) = 18$.
  3. Compare all four values.

Maximum $18$ ($x = 3$); minimum $-2$ ($x = -2, 1$)

Example 3

Find $a$ so that $f(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + 3x$ is always increasing.

Show solution
  1. Need $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 2ax + 3 \ge 0$ for every $x$.
  2. The parabola must not dip below the axis: $\dfrac{D}{4} = a^2 - 9 \le 0$.

$-3 \le a \le 3$

$f'(a) = 0$ alone doesn't mean an extreme value. For $f(x) = x^3$, $f'(0) = 0$ but $f'$ is positive on both sides, so there is no max or min. The sign must change.
For the maximum/minimum on an interval, compare the local extremes inside it with the values at the two ends.
  • $f' > 0$ up, $f' < 0$ down.
  • $+ \to -$: local max; $- \to +$: local min.
  • Always make the sign table.

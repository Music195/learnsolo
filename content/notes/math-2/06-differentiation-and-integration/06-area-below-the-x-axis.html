Definite Integral and Area 2

Below the axis the integral is negative

Where $f(x) < 0$, the definite integral counts the region as negative. An area can't be negative, so flip the sign there by integrating $-f(x)$.

If the curve crosses the axis, split the interval at the crossing points and handle each piece separately.

Area when $f(x) \le 0$ on $a \le x \le b$

$$S = \int_a^b \{-f(x)\}\,dx = -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$

Curve crosses the axis at $x = c$ (below, then above)

$$S = -\int_a^c f(x)\,dx + \int_c^b f(x)\,dx$$

$f(x) = x^2 - 1$: drag the ends $A$ and $B$

Blue parts count $+$, orange parts count $-$ in the integral. The area counts both as $+$.

Example 1

Find the area enclosed by $y = x^2 - 2x$ and the $x$-axis.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 - 2x = x(x - 2)$: meets the axis at $0, 2$, below it in between.
  2. $S = -\displaystyle\int_0^2 (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = -\Big[\dfrac{x^3}{3} - x^2\Big]_0^2 = -\left(\dfrac{8}{3} - 4\right)$

$\dfrac{4}{3}$

Example 2

Find the area between $y = x^2 - 1$, the $x$-axis, $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.

Show solution
  1. Below the axis for $0 \le x \le 1$, above for $1 \le x \le 2$. With $F(x) = \dfrac{x^3}{3} - x$: $F(0) = 0$, $F(1) = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, $F(2) = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
  2. $S = -\{F(1) - F(0)\} + \{F(2) - F(1)\} = \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{4}{3}$

$S = 2$

Example 3

Compare $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (x^2 - 1)\,dx$ with the area in Example 2.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (x^2 - 1)\,dx = F(2) - F(0) = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
  2. It is the area above ($\tfrac{4}{3}$) minus the area below ($\tfrac{2}{3}$).

$\dfrac{2}{3} \ne 2$: the integral is not the area here

Always sketch the graph and find where it meets the $x$-axis before calculating an area. Integrating straight across a crossing point gives the wrong answer.
Region enclosed by $y = a(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)$ and the $x$-axis: $S = \dfrac{|a|(\beta - \alpha)^3}{6}$. Example 1: $\dfrac{1 \cdot 2^3}{6} = \dfrac{4}{3}$ ✓.
  • Below the axis: area $= -\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
  • Crossing the axis: split at the crossing points.
  • Integral = (area above) − (area below).

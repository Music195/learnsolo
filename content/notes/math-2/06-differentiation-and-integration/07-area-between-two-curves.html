Definite Integral and Area 3

(upper) − (lower), then integrate

The area between two graphs is the height of the gap, $f(x) - g(x)$ with $f$ on top, added up from $x = a$ to $x = b$. This works wherever the curves are, above or below the $x$-axis.

$f(x) \ge g(x)$ on $a \le x \le b$

$$S = \int_a^b \{f(x) - g(x)\}\,dx$$

If the curves cross inside the interval, split there and swap which one is on top.

Parabola and line meeting at $x = \alpha, \beta$

$$\int_\alpha^\beta (x - \alpha)(x - \beta)\,dx = -\frac{(\beta - \alpha)^3}{6}$$

So the area between $y = ax^2 + \ldots$ and a line it meets at $\alpha, \beta$ is $\dfrac{|a|(\beta - \alpha)^3}{6}$.

Area between $y = x^2$ and $y = mx + k$

Example 1

Find the area enclosed by $y = x^2$ and $y = x + 2$.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 = x + 2 \Rightarrow (x + 1)(x - 2) = 0$: $x = -1, 2$. The line is on top in between.
  2. $S = \displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (x + 2 - x^2)\,dx = \Big[\dfrac{x^2}{2} + 2x - \dfrac{x^3}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{2} = \dfrac{10}{3} - \left(-\dfrac{7}{6}\right)$

$\dfrac{9}{2}$ (or $\dfrac{3^3}{6}$)

Example 2

Find the area between $y = x^2 + 1$ and $y = 2x + 1$ for $0 \le x \le 2$.

Show solution
  1. At $x = 1$: $2 < 3$, so the line is on top. Gap: $(2x + 1) - (x^2 + 1) = 2x - x^2$.
  2. $S = \Big[x^2 - \dfrac{x^3}{3}\Big]_0^2 = 4 - \dfrac{8}{3}$

$\dfrac{4}{3}$

Example 3

Find the area enclosed by $y = x^2 - 4x + 3$ and $y = -x^2 + 2x + 3$.

Show solution
  1. Equal when $2x^2 - 6x = 0$: $x = 0, 3$.
  2. Gap: $(-x^2 + 2x + 3) - (x^2 - 4x + 3) = -2x^2 + 6x = -2x(x - 3)$.
  3. $S = \dfrac{2 \cdot 3^3}{6}$

$9$

Subtract in the right order: upper − lower. If you get a negative "area", you subtracted the wrong way round.
To see which curve is on top, pick any $x$ between the meeting points and compare the two $y$ values.
  • Find the meeting points; they are the limits.
  • $S = \displaystyle\int_a^b (\text{upper} - \text{lower})\,dx$.
  • Parabola and line: $\dfrac{|a|(\beta - \alpha)^3}{6}$.

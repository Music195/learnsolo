Power Function Derivative

Slope of a curve at one point

The average rate of change from $x = a$ to $x = a + h$ is the slope of the line through the two points of the graph. Let $h$ shrink to $0$: that slope settles on one value, the slope of the curve at $x = a$. This is the derivative $f'(a)$ (微分係数).

Doing this for every $a$ gives a new function $f'(x)$ (導関数). For powers of $x$ there is a simple rule.

Definition

$$f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$$

Rules ($n$ a positive whole number, $k$ a constant)

$$(x^n)' = nx^{n-1}, \qquad (k)' = 0, \qquad \{kf(x) + lg(x)\}' = kf'(x) + lg'(x)$$

Differentiate a polynomial term by term: bring the power down in front and lower it by 1.

  1. $f(x) = x^2$: $\dfrac{(x + h)^2 - x^2}{h} = \dfrac{2xh + h^2}{h} = 2x + h \to 2x$.
  2. $f(x) = x^3$: $\dfrac{(x + h)^3 - x^3}{h} = 3x^2 + 3xh + h^2 \to 3x^2$.
  3. In general, $(x + h)^n = x^n + nx^{n-1}h + (\text{terms with } h^2, h^3, \ldots)$, so the limit is $nx^{n-1}$.

$f(x) = x^2$: drag $P$, then shrink $h$

Example 2

Differentiate $(2x + 1)(x - 3)$.

Show solution
  1. Expand first: $2x^2 - 5x - 3$.

$4x - 5$

Example 3

$f(x) = x^3 - 2x$. Find the average rate of change from $x = 1$ to $x = 3$, and $f'(1)$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{f(3) - f(1)}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{21 - (-1)}{2} = 11$.
  2. $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2$, so $f'(1) = 1$.

$11$; $\;1$

Example 4

$f(x) = ax^2 + bx$ satisfies $f'(1) = 4$ and $f'(-1) = 0$. Find $a, b$.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 2ax + b$: $2a + b = 4$ and $-2a + b = 0$.

$a = 1$, $b = 2$

$\{f(x)g(x)\}' \ne f'(x)g'(x)$. In Mathematics 2, expand products first, then differentiate term by term.
The variable doesn't have to be $x$: for $s = 5t^2$, $\dfrac{ds}{dt} = 10t$. Differentiate with respect to whichever letter is changing.
  • $f'(a)$ = limit of the average rate of change = slope of the curve at $a$.
  • $(x^n)' = nx^{n-1}$; constants differentiate to $0$.
  • Polynomials: term by term (expand products first).

Multiplication & Division of Fractional Expressions

Factorise, then cancel factors

Fractional expressions follow the rules for ordinary fractions. Factorise every numerator and denominator first; then cancel factors that appear above and below. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal.

A fraction is undefined where its denominator is $0$. Those $x$ values stay excluded even after cancelling.

Rules

$$\frac{A}{B} \times \frac{C}{D} = \frac{AC}{BD}, \qquad \frac{A}{B} \div \frac{C}{D} = \frac{A}{B} \times \frac{D}{C} = \frac{AD}{BC}$$

$\dfrac{AC}{BC} = \dfrac{A}{B}$ (cancelling the common factor $C \ne 0$).

$y = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 9} \times \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 2}$ is $\dfrac{x + 2}{x - 3}$ with two holes. Drag $P$.

Example 1

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 9} \times \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 2}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} \times \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 2}$.
  2. Cancel $x - 2$ and $x + 3$.

$\dfrac{x + 2}{x - 3}$

Example 2

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x + 2} \div \dfrac{x - 1}{x}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x + 2} \times \dfrac{x}{x - 1}$.
  2. Cancel $x - 1$.

$\dfrac{x(x + 1)}{x + 2}$

Example 3

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 + x - 2}{x^2 - 3x} \times \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 5x + 6}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{(x + 2)(x - 1)}{x(x - 3)} \times \dfrac{(x + 3)(x - 3)}{(x + 2)(x + 3)}$.
  2. Cancel $x + 2$, $x - 3$, $x + 3$.

$\dfrac{x - 1}{x}$

Example 4

Simplify the complex fraction $\dfrac{1 - \frac{1}{x}}{x - \frac{1}{x}}$.

Show solution
  1. Multiply top and bottom by $x$: $\dfrac{x - 1}{x^2 - 1} = \dfrac{x - 1}{(x - 1)(x + 1)}$.

$\dfrac{1}{x + 1}$

Cancel factors, never single terms: $\dfrac{x + 2}{x + 3}$ cannot be simplified by "cancelling the $x$".
Factorise everything before multiplying. Multiplying out first makes the cancelling much harder.
  • Factorise, cancel common factors, then multiply.
  • Division: multiply by the reciprocal.
  • Values that made any denominator $0$ stay excluded.

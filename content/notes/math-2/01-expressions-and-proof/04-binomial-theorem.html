Binomial Theorem

Choosing $b$ from $r$ of the $n$ brackets

$(a + b)^n = (a + b)(a + b)\cdots(a + b)$. Every term of the expansion comes from taking $a$ or $b$ from each bracket. The term $a^{n-r}b^r$ appears once for every way to choose which $r$ brackets give $b$: that is ${}_n\mathrm{C}_r$ ways.

Binomial theorem

$$(a + b)^n = {}_n\mathrm{C}_0\, a^n + {}_n\mathrm{C}_1\, a^{n-1}b + \cdots + {}_n\mathrm{C}_r\, a^{n-r}b^r + \cdots + {}_n\mathrm{C}_n\, b^n$$

General term: ${}_n\mathrm{C}_r\, a^{n-r} b^r$ ($r = 0, 1, \ldots, n$). The coefficients ${}_n\mathrm{C}_r$ are the binomial coefficients.

Pascal's triangle

$${}_n\mathrm{C}_r = {}_{n-1}\mathrm{C}_{r-1} + {}_{n-1}\mathrm{C}_r, \qquad {}_n\mathrm{C}_r = {}_n\mathrm{C}_{n-r}$$

Each number is the sum of the two above it; each row reads the same from both ends.

  1. $(a + b)^n = (a + b)^{n-1}(a + b)$. The term $a^{n-r}b^r$ arises in two ways:
  2. $a^{n-r}b^{r-1}$ from $(a + b)^{n-1}$ times $b$ (coefficient ${}_{n-1}\mathrm{C}_{r-1}$), or $a^{n-1-r}b^r$ times $a$ (coefficient ${}_{n-1}\mathrm{C}_r$).
  3. Adding gives the Pascal rule, so the coefficients of $(a + b)^n$ are exactly row $n$ of the triangle.

Coefficient of $x^k$ in $(px + q)^n$

Example 1

Expand $(2x - 3)^4$.

Show solution
  1. Row 4: $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$. Terms: ${}_4\mathrm{C}_r (2x)^{4-r}(-3)^r$.
  2. $16x^4 + 4 \cdot 8x^3 \cdot (-3) + 6 \cdot 4x^2 \cdot 9 + 4 \cdot 2x \cdot (-27) + 81$.

$16x^4 - 96x^3 + 216x^2 - 216x + 81$

Example 2

Find the coefficient of $x^2$ in $(3x - 2)^5$.

Show solution
  1. General term ${}_5\mathrm{C}_r (3x)^{5-r}(-2)^r$. For $x^2$: $5 - r = 2$, $r = 3$.
  2. ${}_5\mathrm{C}_3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot (-2)^3 = 10 \cdot 9 \cdot (-8)$.

$-720$

Example 3

Find the constant term in $\left(x^2 - \dfrac{1}{x}\right)^6$.

Show solution
  1. General term: ${}_6\mathrm{C}_r (x^2)^{6-r}\left(-\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^r = {}_6\mathrm{C}_r (-1)^r x^{12 - 3r}$.
  2. Constant: $12 - 3r = 0$, $r = 4$: ${}_6\mathrm{C}_4 \cdot 1 = 15$.

$15$

Example 4

Show that ${}_n\mathrm{C}_0 + {}_n\mathrm{C}_1 + \cdots + {}_n\mathrm{C}_n = 2^n$.

Show solution
  1. Put $a = b = 1$ in the binomial theorem: $(1 + 1)^n = {}_n\mathrm{C}_0 + {}_n\mathrm{C}_1 + \cdots + {}_n\mathrm{C}_n$.

The sum is $2^n$.

The coefficient of $x^k$ includes the numbers inside the brackets. In Example 2 it is $10 \cdot 9 \cdot (-8)$, not just ${}_5\mathrm{C}_3 = 10$.
For "find the coefficient of …" questions, don't expand everything. Write the general term, simplify the power of $x$, and solve for $r$.
  • General term ${}_n\mathrm{C}_r\, a^{n-r}b^r$.
  • Row $n$ of Pascal's triangle gives the coefficients.
  • $a = b = 1$ gives ${}_n\mathrm{C}_0 + \cdots + {}_n\mathrm{C}_n = 2^n$.

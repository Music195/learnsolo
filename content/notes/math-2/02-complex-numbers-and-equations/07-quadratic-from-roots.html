Quadratic From Roots

Need only the sum and the product

The equation $(x - \alpha)(x - \beta) = 0$ has roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Expanding it, only $\alpha + \beta$ and $\alpha\beta$ appear, so you never need the roots one by one.

Read backwards: two numbers with sum $p$ and product $q$ are the roots of $x^2 - px + q = 0$.

Equation with roots $\alpha, \beta$

$$x^2 - (\alpha + \beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0$$

Any non-zero multiple also works; usually make the coefficients whole numbers.

Roots $p \pm q\sqrt{r}$ or $p \pm qi$

Example 1

Find a quadratic equation with roots $2 + \sqrt{3}$ and $2 - \sqrt{3}$.

Show solution
  1. Sum $4$, product $4 - 3 = 1$.

$x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$

Example 2

Find two numbers whose sum is 2 and product is 5.

Show solution
  1. They are the roots of $x^2 - 2x + 5 = 0$.
  2. $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 5} = 1 \pm 2i$.

$1 + 2i$ and $1 - 2i$

Example 3

$\alpha, \beta$ are the roots of $x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0$. Find an equation whose roots are $\alpha^2$ and $\beta^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = 3$, $\alpha\beta = 1$.
  2. New sum: $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 9 - 2 = 7$. New product: $(\alpha\beta)^2 = 1$.

$x^2 - 7x + 1 = 0$

Example 4

$\alpha, \beta$ are the roots of $x^2 + px + q = 0$. Find an equation whose roots are $\alpha + 1$ and $\beta + 1$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = -p$, $\alpha\beta = q$.
  2. New sum $-p + 2$; new product $\alpha\beta + (\alpha + \beta) + 1 = q - p + 1$.

$x^2 + (p - 2)x + q - p + 1 = 0$

The middle term is minus the sum: roots $3$ and $-2$ give $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$, not $x^2 + x - 6 = 0$.
Pairs like $p \pm q\sqrt{r}$ or $p \pm qi$ always give a sum and product with no roots or $i$ left, so the equation has rational coefficients.
  • Roots $\alpha, \beta$ → $x^2 - (\alpha + \beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0$.
  • "Sum $p$, product $q$" → solve $x^2 - px + q = 0$.
  • New roots from old: compute the new sum and product with note 05.

Remainder Theorem

The remainder is just a substitution

When you divide a polynomial $P(x)$ by $x - k$, the remainder is a number. You can find it without dividing: just work out $P(k)$.

Remainder theorem

$$P(x) \div (x - k) \text{ leaves remainder } P(k), \qquad P(x) \div (ax + b) \text{ leaves remainder } P\!\left(-\frac{b}{a}\right)$$
  1. Dividing by a degree-1 polynomial leaves a constant remainder $R$: $P(x) = (x - k)Q(x) + R$.
  2. Put $x = k$: $P(k) = 0 \cdot Q(k) + R = R$.

Divide $P(x)$ by $x - k$ (synthetic division, 組立除法)

Example 1

Find the remainder when $P(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 1$ is divided by $x - 1$ and by $x + 2$.

Show solution
  1. $P(1) = 1 - 2 + 3 - 1 = 1$.
  2. $x + 2 = x - (-2)$: $P(-2) = -8 - 8 - 6 - 1 = -23$.

$1$; $\;-23$

Example 2

Find the remainder when $2x^3 + x - 3$ is divided by $2x + 1$.

Show solution
  1. $2x + 1 = 0$ at $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. $2\left(-\dfrac{1}{8}\right) - \dfrac{1}{2} - 3 = -\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{2} - 3$.

$-\dfrac{15}{4}$

Example 3

$P(x)$ leaves remainder 5 when divided by $x - 1$ and $-3$ when divided by $x + 3$. Find the remainder when $P(x)$ is divided by $(x - 1)(x + 3)$.

Show solution
  1. Dividing by a quadratic leaves a remainder of degree ≤ 1: $P(x) = (x - 1)(x + 3)Q(x) + ax + b$.
  2. $P(1) = a + b = 5$ and $P(-3) = -3a + b = -3$.
  3. Subtract: $4a = 8$, so $a = 2$, $b = 3$.

$2x + 3$

Example 4

$x^3 + ax^2 - 4x + 1$ leaves remainder 3 when divided by $x - 2$. Find $a$.

Show solution
  1. $P(2) = 8 + 4a - 8 + 1 = 4a + 1 = 3$.

$a = \dfrac{1}{2}$

For $x + 2$, substitute $x = -2$ (the value that makes the divisor $0$), not $x = 2$.
The remainder always has a lower degree than the divisor: a constant for a linear divisor, $ax + b$ for a quadratic divisor.
  • Remainder of $P(x) \div (x - k)$ is $P(k)$.
  • Divisor $ax + b$: substitute $x = -\dfrac{b}{a}$.
  • Quadratic divisor: write the remainder as $ax + b$ and use two substitutions.

Factor Theorem

A zero of $P$ gives a factor of $P$

By the remainder theorem (note 08), $P(k)$ is the remainder of $P(x) \div (x - k)$. If $P(k) = 0$, the division is exact, so $x - k$ is a factor.

To solve a cubic: guess a value $k$ with $P(k) = 0$, divide by $x - k$, then solve the leftover quadratic.

Factor theorem

$$P(k) = 0 \iff P(x) \text{ has the factor } x - k$$

With whole-number coefficients, try $k = \pm\dfrac{\text{divisor of the constant term}}{\text{divisor of the leading coefficient}}$.

Find a factor of $P(x)$ and solve $P(x) = 0$

Example 1

Solve $x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6 = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $P(1) = 1 - 6 + 11 - 6 = 0$, so $x - 1$ is a factor.
  2. Divide: $P(x) = (x - 1)(x^2 - 5x + 6) = (x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)$.

$x = 1, 2, 3$

Example 2

Solve $x^3 = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1) = 0$.
  2. $x^2 + x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{3}\,i}{2}$.

$x = 1, \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{3}\,i}{2}$

Example 3

Find $a$ so that $x - 3$ is a factor of $x^3 - ax^2 + 7x - 6$.

Show solution
  1. $P(3) = 27 - 9a + 21 - 6 = 42 - 9a = 0$.

$a = \dfrac{14}{3}$

Example 4

$x^3 + ax^2 + bx + 10 = 0$ ($a, b$ real) has the root $1 + 2i$. Find $a$, $b$ and the other roots.

Show solution
  1. Real coefficients, so $1 - 2i$ is also a root. Those two are the roots of $x^2 - 2x + 5$ (note 07).
  2. So $x^3 + ax^2 + bx + 10 = (x^2 - 2x + 5)(x + c)$. Constant terms: $5c = 10$, so $c = 2$.
  3. Expand: $x^3 + (c - 2)x^2 + (5 - 2c)x + 5c = x^3 + 0x^2 + x + 10$.

$a = 0$, $b = 1$; other roots $1 - 2i$ and $-2$

After dividing, don't stop: the quadratic quotient gives the other two roots (possibly imaginary). A cubic always has 3 roots counted with repetition.
Try $k = 1$ first (just add the coefficients), then $k = -1$, then other divisors of the constant term.
  • $P(k) = 0$ ⟺ $(x - k)$ is a factor.
  • Cubic: find one root, divide, solve the quadratic.
  • Real coefficients: imaginary roots come in conjugate pairs.

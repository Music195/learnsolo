Discriminant

The sign of $D$ tells you the kind of solutions

For real coefficients, $D = b^2 - 4ac$ decides whether the two solutions are different real numbers, one repeated real number, or a pair of imaginary numbers.

On the graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$: two crossings, a touch, or no contact with the $x$-axis.

$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with real $a, b, c$

$$D = b^2 - 4ac: \qquad \begin{cases} D > 0 & \text{two different real solutions} \\ D = 0 & \text{one repeated real solution} \\ D < 0 & \text{two different imaginary solutions} \end{cases}$$

"Has real solutions" ⟺ $D \ge 0$. For $b = 2b'$, use $\dfrac{D}{4} = b'^2 - ac$ (same sign as $D$).

Slide $c$ in $x^2 + bx + c = 0$

Example 1

Classify the solutions of (i) $3x^2 - 5x + 1 = 0$ (ii) $4x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0$ (iii) $2x^2 + 3x + 4 = 0$.

Show solution
  1. (i) $D = 25 - 12 = 13 > 0$. (ii) $D/4 = 36 - 36 = 0$. (iii) $D = 9 - 32 = -23 < 0$.

(i) two real (ii) repeated real (iii) two imaginary

Example 2

Find the range of $k$ for which $x^2 + 2kx + k + 6 = 0$ has imaginary solutions.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{D}{4} = k^2 - (k + 6) < 0 \Rightarrow (k - 3)(k + 2) < 0$.

$-2 < k < 3$

Example 3

For which $m$ do both $x^2 + mx + 1 = 0$ and $x^2 - 2x + m = 0$ have imaginary solutions?

Show solution
  1. First: $m^2 - 4 < 0 \Rightarrow -2 < m < 2$.
  2. Second: $\dfrac{D}{4} = 1 - m < 0 \Rightarrow m > 1$.
  3. Both: take the overlap.

$1 < m < 2$

If the $x^2$ coefficient contains a letter, e.g. $(k - 1)x^2 + \ldots$, the equation is only quadratic when $k \ne 1$. Check $k = 1$ separately.
"Both have imaginary solutions" → overlap (and). "At least one has real solutions" → combine (or). Draw the conditions on a number line.
  • $D > 0$, $D = 0$, $D < 0$: two real, repeated, two imaginary.
  • Parameter questions: turn the condition into an inequality in $D$.
  • Imaginary solutions ⟺ the graph misses the $x$-axis.

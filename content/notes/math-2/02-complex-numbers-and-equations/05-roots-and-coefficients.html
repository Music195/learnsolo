Roots and Coefficients

Sum and product without solving

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, their sum and product can be read straight from the coefficients. This works for imaginary solutions too, and it lets you find values like $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$ without ever finding $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

Roots $\alpha, \beta$ of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$

$$\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}, \qquad \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$$

Useful rewrites (symmetric expressions)

$$\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta, \qquad \alpha^3 + \beta^3 = (\alpha + \beta)^3 - 3\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta), \qquad (\alpha - \beta)^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 4\alpha\beta$$
  1. $ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - \alpha)(x - \beta) = ax^2 - a(\alpha + \beta)x + a\alpha\beta$ (note 06).
  2. Compare coefficients: $b = -a(\alpha + \beta)$ and $c = a\alpha\beta$.

Values from $a, b, c$ alone

Example 1

$\alpha, \beta$ are the roots of $x^2 - 3x + 5 = 0$. Find $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$ and $\alpha^3 + \beta^3$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = 3$, $\alpha\beta = 5$.
  2. $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 9 - 10 = -1$ (negative: the roots are imaginary).
  3. $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = 27 - 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 3 = -18$.

$-1$, $\;-18$

Example 2

$\alpha, \beta$ are the roots of $2x^2 + 4x - 3 = 0$. Find $\dfrac{1}{\alpha} + \dfrac{1}{\beta}$ and $\dfrac{\beta}{\alpha} + \dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = -2$, $\alpha\beta = -\dfrac{3}{2}$.
  2. $\dfrac{1}{\alpha} + \dfrac{1}{\beta} = \dfrac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta} = \dfrac{-2}{-3/2} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.
  3. $\dfrac{\alpha^2 + \beta^2}{\alpha\beta} = \dfrac{4 + 3}{-3/2} = -\dfrac{14}{3}$.

$\dfrac{4}{3}$, $\;-\dfrac{14}{3}$

Example 3

One root of $x^2 - 6x + k = 0$ is twice the other. Find $k$ and the roots.

Show solution
  1. Roots $\alpha, 2\alpha$: $3\alpha = 6 \Rightarrow \alpha = 2$.
  2. $k = \alpha \cdot 2\alpha = 8$.

$k = 8$; roots $2$ and $4$

The sum has a minus sign: $\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{b}{a}$. For $x^2 - 3x + 5$, the sum is $+3$.
An expression that doesn't change when you swap $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (symmetric) can always be written using $\alpha + \beta$ and $\alpha\beta$ only.
  • $\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{b}{a}$, $\alpha\beta = \dfrac{c}{a}$.
  • Rewrite symmetric expressions in terms of the sum and product.
  • Also works for imaginary roots.

Square Root of Negative

$\sqrt{-a}$ means $\sqrt{a}\,i$

For $a > 0$, both $\sqrt{a}\,i$ and $-\sqrt{a}\,i$ square to $-a$. We define $\sqrt{-a}$ to be $\sqrt{a}\,i$.

Always turn $\sqrt{-a}$ into $\sqrt{a}\,i$ first, before multiplying or dividing roots.

For $a > 0$

$$\sqrt{-a} = \sqrt{a}\,i, \qquad x^2 = -a \iff x = \pm\sqrt{a}\,i$$

Powers of $i$ repeat every 4

$$i^1 = i, \quad i^2 = -1, \quad i^3 = -i, \quad i^4 = 1, \quad i^5 = i, \ \ldots$$

For $i^n$, only the remainder of $n \div 4$ matters.

Powers of $i$ go round in a cycle of 4

Example 1

Calculate $\sqrt{-3} \times \sqrt{-12}$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{3}\,i \times 2\sqrt{3}\,i = 6i^2$

$-6$

Example 2

Calculate $\dfrac{\sqrt{-8}}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{-3}}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}\,i}{\sqrt{2}} = 2i$
  2. $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{3}\,i} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{i} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}\,i}{i^2} = -\sqrt{2}\,i$

$2i$, $\;-\sqrt{2}\,i$

Example 3

Solve $x^2 = -5$ and $4x^2 + 9 = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $x = \pm\sqrt{5}\,i$.
  2. $x^2 = -\dfrac{9}{4} \Rightarrow x = \pm\dfrac{3}{2}i$.

$\pm\sqrt{5}\,i$; $\;\pm\dfrac{3}{2}i$

Example 4

Find $i^{25}$ and $i + i^2 + i^3 + \cdots + i^{10}$.

Show solution
  1. $25 = 4 \cdot 6 + 1$, so $i^{25} = i$.
  2. Every 4 consecutive powers add to $i - 1 - i + 1 = 0$. So the sum is $i^9 + i^{10} = i - 1$.

$i$; $\;-1 + i$

$\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$ is false when both are negative: $\sqrt{-2}\sqrt{-3} = \sqrt{2}\,i \cdot \sqrt{3}\,i = -\sqrt{6}$, not $\sqrt{6}$.
  • $\sqrt{-a} = \sqrt{a}\,i$ ($a > 0$): convert first, then calculate.
  • $x^2 = -a$ has the two solutions $\pm\sqrt{a}\,i$.
  • $i^n$ depends on $n \bmod 4$: $1, i, -1, -i$.

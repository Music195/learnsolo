Conservation of Mechanical Energy

Kinetic + potential energy stays the same; only the mix changes

Kinetic energy and potential energy together are called mechanical energy. On a roller coaster you are slow at the tops and fast at the bottoms: going down, potential energy turns into kinetic energy; going up, kinetic turns back into potential.

If friction and air resistance can be ignored, the total stays the same the whole time. This is the law of conservation of mechanical energy. Think of a fixed amount of money that is sometimes all in notes and sometimes all in coins: the total never changes, only how it is split.

This topic only uses gravitational potential energy. (Other kinds, such as the energy stored in a spring, come later.)

Conservation of mechanical energy

$$\tfrac{1}{2}mv_A^2 + mgh_A = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_B^2 + mgh_B$$

$K + U$ at any point A equals $K + U$ at any other point B. It holds when only gravity does work (a normal force at right angles to the motion does no work, so it's allowed). Two points at the same height have the same speed.

  1. Between A and B, the work–energy relation says: change in $K$ = work done. Here only gravity does work.
  2. Gravity's work going from height $h_A$ to $h_B$ is $mg(h_A - h_B)$, which is the drop in potential energy, $U_A - U_B$.
  3. So $K_B - K_A = U_A - U_B$, and rearranging, $K_A + U_A = K_B + U_B$.

Let go of a ball at A on a smooth curved track of radius $R$ (mass $1\ \mathrm{kg}$)

Example 1

In the demo's track, the ball (mass $m$) is let go at A, a height $R$ above the bottom B. Taking B as the reference level, find its speed at B.

Show solution
  1. A: $K = 0$ (let go gently), $U = mgR$. B: $K = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_B^2$, $U = 0$.
  2. $mgR = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_B^2$.

$v_B = \sqrt{2gR}$

Example 2

C is the end of the track, at $60^\circ$ from the bottom. Find its height and the speed there.

Show solution
  1. Height of C above B: $R - R\cos 60^\circ = \tfrac{1}{2}R$.
  2. $mgR = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_C^2 + mg \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}R$, so $v_C^2 = gR$.

$\tfrac{1}{2}R$; $v_C = \sqrt{gR}$

Example 3

After C the ball flies through the air. At its highest point D, find its speed and height $h$.

Show solution
  1. It leaves C moving at $60^\circ$ above the horizontal (along the track). In the air it is a projectile, so its horizontal velocity stays $v_C\cos 60^\circ = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{gR}$. At the top only this is left: $v_D = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{gR}$.
  2. Energy, A to D: $mgR = \tfrac{1}{2}m \cdot \tfrac{gR}{4} + mgh$, so $h = R - \tfrac{R}{8}$.

$v_D = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{gR}$, $h = \tfrac{7}{8}R$

Example 4

The ball lands at E, at the same height as B. Find its speed there.

Show solution
  1. E and B are at the same height, so they have the same $U$, and therefore the same $K$.

$v_E = v_B = \sqrt{2gR}$ (only the direction is different)

At the top of a flight through the air the speed is not zero, unless it was thrown straight up. Keep the horizontal velocity (projectile motion), otherwise the energy equation gives the wrong height.
Write $K + U$ for every point in one long chain, A = B = C = D = E, each with its own $v$ and $h$. Then solve whichever pair has just one unknown. Energy gives speeds; to get a direction you need something else (like the projectile motion here).
  • Mechanical energy = kinetic + potential.
  • With no friction or air resistance, $K + U$ is the same at every point.
  • Same height means same speed.

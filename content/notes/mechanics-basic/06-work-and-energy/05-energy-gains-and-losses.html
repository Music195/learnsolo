Energy Conservation: Gains Equal Losses

What some energies gain, others must lose

Imagine two friends, K with 3000 coins and U with 2000 coins, who only pass money between themselves. Afterwards K has 4000 coins. How much does U have? You could say "the total was 5000, so U has 1000". Or, more quickly: K gained 1000, so U must have lost 1000.

Energy conservation works the same way. Instead of "total before = total after", you can write total increase = total decrease. This version is often quicker, especially with two objects, and it doesn't need a reference level for $U$: you only use changes in height.

A safe routine: list every energy ($K_A$, $U_A$, $K_B$, $U_B$), mark each one as increase, decrease or no change, then write "increases = decreases".

Conservation of energy as gains and losses

$$(\text{sum of the increases}) = (\text{sum of the decreases})$$

Each term is a positive size: a speed change gives $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ (from rest), a height change gives $mgh$. For a block A on a smooth table pulled by a hanging block B that falls $h$: $\tfrac{1}{2}m_A v^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}m_B v^2 = m_B g h$.

  1. Conservation says $K_{\text{before}} + U_{\text{before}} = K_{\text{after}} + U_{\text{after}}$, adding up over all the objects.
  2. Move everything to one side: $(K_{\text{after}} - K_{\text{before}}) + (U_{\text{after}} - U_{\text{before}}) = 0$.
  3. Some of these changes are positive (increases), some negative (decreases). Put the negative ones on the other side, as sizes: increases = decreases.

Two blocks joined by a string, let go from rest: drag the slider to move them

Example 1

Block A ($2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$) is on a smooth table, tied over a pulley to block B ($3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$), which hangs. They start at rest. Find their speed when B has fallen $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$.

Show solution
  1. Increases: $K_A$, $K_B$. Decrease: $U_B$. No change: $U_A$ (A stays at the same height).
  2. $\tfrac{1}{2}(2.0 + 3.0)v^2 = 3.0 \times 9.8 \times 1.0 = 29.4$.
  3. $v^2 = 11.76$.

$v \approx 3.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 2

A ($2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$) and B ($3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$) hang over a pulley and are let go. Find their speed after B has gone down $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$.

Show solution
  1. Increases: $K_A$, $K_B$, $U_A$ (A goes up $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$). Decrease: $U_B$.
  2. $\tfrac{1}{2}(5.0)v^2 + 2.0 \times 9.8 \times 1.0 = 3.0 \times 9.8 \times 1.0$.
  3. $2.5v^2 = 9.8$.

$v \approx 2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 3

A $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball is thrown straight up at $14\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Using gains and losses, find how high it rises.

Show solution
  1. Decrease: $K$ (from $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.50 \times 14^2 = 49\ \mathrm{J}$ to $0$). Increase: $U$, by $0.50 \times 9.8 \times h$.
  2. $4.9h = 49$.

$h = 10\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 4

In Example 2, check the answer with equations of motion.

Show solution
  1. Pulley: $a = \dfrac{(3.0 - 2.0) \times 9.8}{5.0} = 1.96\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
  2. $v^2 = 2 \times 1.96 \times 1.0 = 3.92$.

$v \approx 2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$, the same

Objects joined by a taut string always have the same speed, so use the same $v$ for both. And don't forget an object whose height changes "the wrong way", like A rising in Example 2: its $U$ is an increase.
Before writing any equation, make a small table: $K_A$, $U_A$, $K_B$, $U_B$, each marked ↑, ↓ or "no change". Then the equation writes itself: ↑ terms on the left, ↓ terms on the right.
  • Energy conservation can be written as: total increase = total decrease.
  • List each object's $K$ and $U$, and mark them increase / decrease / no change.
  • Height changes give $mgh$ terms directly: no reference level needed.

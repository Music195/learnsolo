Uniformly Accelerated Motion

Constant acceleration: three formulas, all read off one graph

Motion along a straight line with a constant acceleration is called uniformly accelerated motion. A car pulling away steadily, a ball rolling down a slope and a falling stone (ignoring air) all move like this.

Its $v$–$t$ graph is a straight line: it starts at $v_0$ and its slope is $a$. Everything about the motion can be read from that line: the velocity from the line itself, the displacement from the area under it.

That gives three formulas. You don't need to learn them by heart: you can rebuild them from the graph in a minute, and the derivation is worth practising.

Starting at $x = 0$ with velocity $v_0$, constant acceleration $a$

$$v = v_0 + at \qquad x = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \qquad v^2 - v_0^2 = 2ax$$

$v_0$ initial velocity, $v$ velocity and $x$ displacement at time $t$, $a$ acceleration. All of $v_0$, $v$, $a$, $x$ carry signs: choose a positive direction first. The third formula has no $t$: use it when time isn't given or asked.

  1. The $v$–$t$ graph is a straight line with intercept $v_0$ and slope $a$. Like $y = ax + b$ in maths, its equation is $v = at + v_0$. That is formula 1.
  2. The displacement is the area under the line from $0$ to $t$: a trapezium with parallel sides $v_0$ and $v$, and width $t$. Area $= \tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v)\,t$.
  3. Put in $v = v_0 + at$: $\tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v_0 + at)\,t = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$. That is formula 2.
  4. To remove $t$, write formula 1 as $t = \dfrac{v - v_0}{a}$ and put it into $x = \tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v)\,t$: $x = \dfrac{(v + v_0)(v - v_0)}{2a} = \dfrac{v^2 - v_0^2}{2a}$.
  5. Multiply by $2a$: $v^2 - v_0^2 = 2ax$. That is formula 3.

Set $v_0$, $a$ and the time $t$: the shaded area is the displacement

Example 1

A car moving at $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ accelerates at $2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$. Find its final velocity and the distance covered.

Show solution
  1. $v = v_0 + at = 10 + 2.0 \times 5.0 = 20$.
  2. $x = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 = 10 \times 5.0 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 25 = 50 + 25 = 75$.

$20\ \mathrm{m/s}$; $75\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 2

A car at $20\ \mathrm{m/s}$ brakes with an acceleration of $-4.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. How far does it go before stopping, and how long does it take?

Show solution
  1. No time given, so use formula 3 with $v = 0$: $0 - 20^2 = 2 \times (-4.0) \times x$, so $x = \dfrac{400}{8.0} = 50$.
  2. Formula 1: $0 = 20 - 4.0t$, so $t = 5.0$.

$50\ \mathrm{m}$; $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$

Example 3

A cart speeds up steadily from $5.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ to $15\ \mathrm{m/s}$ over $50\ \mathrm{m}$. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. Formula 3: $15^2 - 5.0^2 = 2a \times 50$, so $200 = 100a$.

$a = 2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$

Example 4

A stone is dropped from rest and falls freely ($a = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, down positive). Find its velocity and how far it has fallen after $2.0\ \mathrm{s}$.

Show solution
  1. $v = 0 + 9.8 \times 2.0 = 19.6$.
  2. $x = 0 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times 2.0^2 = 19.6$.

$19.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$; $19.6\ \mathrm{m}$

These formulas are only for constant acceleration. And put every quantity in with its sign: braking means $a$ is negative when $v_0$ is positive (Example 2). Forgetting a minus sign is the most common mistake here.
Pick the formula by what's missing. No $x$ given or asked: formula 1. No $v$: formula 2. No $t$: formula 3.
  • $v = v_0 + at$: the equation of the straight $v$–$t$ line.
  • $x = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$: the trapezium area under it.
  • $v^2 - v_0^2 = 2ax$: the first two with $t$ removed.

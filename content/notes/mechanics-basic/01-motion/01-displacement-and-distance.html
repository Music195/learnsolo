Displacement and Distance

Displacement is "where you ended up", distance is "how far you went"

To say where something is, stand at a reference point (an observer) and draw an arrow to the object. This arrow is its position vector (位置ベクトル), $\vec{r}$. On a straight line we just use a coordinate, $x$.

The displacement (変位) is the change in position: the position after minus the position before. It is an arrow straight from the start to the end, so it has a size and a direction. It ignores the route.

The distance travelled (移動距離) is the length of the actual route. A butterfly that flutters about covers a long distance, but its displacement is just the straight arrow from where it started to where it is now.

Displacement = after − before

$$\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1 \qquad\qquad \Delta x = x_2 - x_1$$

$\vec{r}_1$, $x_1$ position before; $\vec{r}_2$, $x_2$ position after; $\Delta$ ("delta") means "change in". On a line, the sign of $\Delta x$ shows the direction. Distance travelled has no direction and is never negative.

  1. Any "change" in physics is always after − before, so the change in position is $\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1$.
  2. In the picture, $\vec{r}_1 + \Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}_2$: go from the observer to the start, then along the displacement, and you reach the end. Rearranged, $\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1$.
  3. Only the start and end positions appear, so the route doesn't matter.
  4. A straight line is the shortest route between two points, so distance travelled $\ge$ size of the displacement. They are equal only when the object moves in a straight line without turning back.

Example 1

You walk $3\ \mathrm{m}$ east, then $4\ \mathrm{m}$ north. Find the distance travelled and the size of the displacement.

Show solution
  1. Distance: $3 + 4 = 7$.
  2. Displacement: the arrow from start to end is the hypotenuse of a $3$–$4$ right triangle: $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$.

Distance $7\ \mathrm{m}$; displacement $5\ \mathrm{m}$ (towards the north-east)

Example 2

A runner does one full lap of a $400\ \mathrm{m}$ track. Find the distance and the displacement.

Show solution
  1. The route is $400\ \mathrm{m}$ long.
  2. The runner ends where they started: after − before $= \vec{0}$.

Distance $400\ \mathrm{m}$; displacement $0$

Example 3

On an $x$-axis (right is positive), a ball moves from $x = 2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ to $x = -3.0\ \mathrm{m}$. Find its displacement.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1 = -3.0 - 2.0$.

$\Delta x = -5.0\ \mathrm{m}$: $5.0\ \mathrm{m}$ to the left

Example 4

A ball is thrown straight up, rises $5.0\ \mathrm{m}$ and falls back into the hand. Find the distance and the displacement.

Show solution
  1. Distance: $5.0$ up plus $5.0$ down $= 10$.
  2. Displacement: it ends where it started.

Distance $10\ \mathrm{m}$; displacement $0$

Always subtract in the order after − before. Doing it the other way flips the sign, and in physics a wrong sign is a wrong answer.
A quantity with only a size (distance, time, mass) is a scalar (スカラー). One with a size and a direction (displacement, velocity, force) is a vector (ベクトル). Knowing which one a question asks for tells you whether a sign or direction belongs in the answer.
  • Displacement = position after − position before: a vector, route ignored.
  • Distance travelled = length of the route: a scalar, never negative.
  • Distance $\ge$ size of displacement; equal only for straight motion without turning back.

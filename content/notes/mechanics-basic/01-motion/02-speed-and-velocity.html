Speed and Velocity

Velocity is displacement per second; speed is its size

In everyday life we say "speed". Physics separates two ideas. Speed (速さ) is the distance travelled per unit time: a scalar, never negative. Velocity (速度) is the displacement per unit time: a vector, so on a line it has a sign ($+$ one way, $-$ the other).

Driving from Tokyo to Aomori, about $700\ \mathrm{km}$, takes about 10 hours: roughly $70\ \mathrm{km/h}$. But nobody drives at exactly $70$ the whole way: there are red lights and motorways. $70\ \mathrm{km/h}$ is the average velocity (平均の速度) over the whole trip.

The speedometer shows something else: the velocity at one instant, the instantaneous velocity (瞬間の速度). It is the average velocity over a time interval so short that it shrinks towards zero.

Average and instantaneous velocity

$$\bar{v} = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \qquad\qquad v = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{dx}{dt}$$

$x_1, x_2$ positions at times $t_1, t_2$; $\bar{v}$ ("v bar") average velocity; $v$ instantaneous velocity, in $\mathrm{m/s}$. On an $x$–$t$ graph, $\bar{v}$ is the slope of the line through two points and $v$ is the slope of the tangent.

  1. Plot position $x$ against time $t$. Mark the points $(t_1, x_1)$ and $(t_2, x_2)$ and join them with a straight line.
  2. Its slope is $\dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1}$: displacement over time, the average velocity.
  3. Now slide $t_2$ closer and closer to $t_1$. The line through the two points turns towards the tangent at $t_1$.
  4. So the instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent: $v = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$, which in maths is the derivative $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$.

Example 1

A car leaves Tokyo at 12:00 and arrives in Aomori, $700\ \mathrm{km}$ away, at 22:00. Find its average speed in $\mathrm{km/h}$ and in $\mathrm{m/s}$.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta t = 22 - 12 = 10\ \mathrm{h}$, so $\bar{v} = \dfrac{700}{10} = 70\ \mathrm{km/h}$.
  2. $70\ \mathrm{km/h} = \dfrac{70\,000\ \mathrm{m}}{3600\ \mathrm{s}} \approx 19.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

$70\ \mathrm{km/h} \approx 19\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 2

Convert $72\ \mathrm{km/h}$ to $\mathrm{m/s}$.

Show solution
  1. $1\ \mathrm{km/h} = \dfrac{1000}{3600} = \dfrac{1}{3.6}\ \mathrm{m/s}$, so divide by $3.6$.
  2. $72 \div 3.6 = 20$.

$20\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 3

A cat walks $30\ \mathrm{m}$ east, then $10\ \mathrm{m}$ west, taking $10\ \mathrm{s}$ in all. Taking east as positive, find its average velocity and its average speed.

Show solution
  1. Displacement $= +30 - 10 = +20\ \mathrm{m}$, so $\bar{v} = \dfrac{+20}{10} = +2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
  2. Distance $= 30 + 10 = 40\ \mathrm{m}$, so average speed $= \dfrac{40}{10} = 4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

Velocity $+2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ (east); speed $4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 4

With right as positive, a ball's velocity is $-3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$. What are its speed and its direction?

Show solution
  1. Speed is the size of the velocity: $3.0$. The minus sign means "left".

Speed $3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$, moving left

If a question asks for velocity, give the sign (or the direction). If it asks for speed, never write a minus sign: speed is only a size.
To change $\mathrm{km/h}$ into $\mathrm{m/s}$, divide by $3.6$; to go back, multiply by $3.6$. Worth remembering: $36\ \mathrm{km/h} = 10\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
  • Speed: distance per time (scalar). Velocity: displacement per time (vector, with a sign).
  • Average velocity $= \Delta x / \Delta t$: slope of the line through two points of the $x$–$t$ graph.
  • Instantaneous velocity $= dx/dt$: slope of the tangent.

Negative Acceleration

Slow down, stop, come back: all with one constant acceleration

Roll a ball up a slope. It slows down, stops for an instant at the top, then rolls back, faster and faster. Taking "up the slope" as positive, its acceleration is negative and constant the whole time. It is one uniformly accelerated motion, not three separate ones.

On the $v$–$t$ graph this is a single straight line sloping down. It crosses the $t$-axis at the turning point ($v = 0$). Above the axis the ball moves up the slope; below it, it moves back down.

This is where displacement and distance travelled (note 01) part ways. The displacement is the area above the axis minus the area below; the distance is the two areas added.

Reading the $v$–$t$ graph when it crosses the axis

$$\text{displacement} = S_{\text{above}} - S_{\text{below}} \qquad \text{distance} = S_{\text{above}} + S_{\text{below}}$$

$S_{\text{above}}$, $S_{\text{below}}$: the areas between the $v$–$t$ graph and the $t$-axis, above and below it. The turning point is where $v = 0$: $t = -\dfrac{v_0}{a}$.

  1. Area under the $v$–$t$ graph is displacement (note 04). Where $v < 0$, each thin strip $v\,\Delta t$ is negative: the object moves in the negative direction.
  2. So the total displacement adds the area above the axis and the (negative) area below: $S_{\text{above}} - S_{\text{below}}$.
  3. Distance travelled has no direction: every strip counts as positive, so it is $S_{\text{above}} + S_{\text{below}}$.
  4. The turning point is where the line crosses the axis: $0 = v_0 + at$, so $t = -v_0/a$.

A ball rolled up a slope ($a = -2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, up the slope is $+$): set $v_0$ and move $t$

Example 1

A cart starts from rest. Its $v$–$t$ graph rises in a straight line to $6.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ at $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$, then falls in a straight line to $0$ at $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$. Find the two accelerations, and the displacement and distance at $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$.

Show solution
  1. $0$–$3\ \mathrm{s}$: $a_1 = \dfrac{6.0 - 0}{3.0} = 2.0$. $3$–$5\ \mathrm{s}$: $a_2 = \dfrac{0 - 6.0}{2.0} = -3.0$ (after − before!).
  2. Areas: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 3.0 \times 6.0 = 9.0$ and $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 6.0 = 6.0$, both above the axis.
  3. It never turns back, so displacement and distance are both $9.0 + 6.0$.

$a_1 = 2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, $a_2 = -3.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; displacement $+15\ \mathrm{m}$, distance $15\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 2

A cart's $v$–$t$ graph is a straight line from $6.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ at $t = 0$, through $0$ at $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$, continuing down to $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$. Find the acceleration, the velocity at $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$, and the displacement and distance at $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$.

Show solution
  1. $a = \dfrac{0 - 6.0}{3.0} = -2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, the same for the whole straight line.
  2. At $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$: $v = 6.0 - 2.0 \times 5.0 = -4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$. (Or by similar triangles: the two triangles have sides in the ratio $3 : 2$, so $6 : 4$.)
  3. Area above: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 3.0 \times 6.0 = 9.0$ (moving forwards). Area below: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 4.0 = 4.0$ (coming back).
  4. Displacement $= 9.0 - 4.0 = 5.0$; distance $= 9.0 + 4.0 = 13$.

$a = -2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, $v = -4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$; displacement $+5.0\ \mathrm{m}$, distance $13\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 3

A ball is thrown straight up at $14.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$ ($a = -9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, up positive). When does it reach the top, how high does it go, and what is its velocity when it is back at the hand?

Show solution
  1. Top: $v = 0$, so $0 = 14.7 - 9.8t$ and $t = 1.5\ \mathrm{s}$.
  2. Height: area of the triangle above the axis, $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 1.5 \times 14.7 \approx 11.0\ \mathrm{m}$.
  3. Back at the hand the displacement is $0$, so the area below must equal the area above: another $1.5\ \mathrm{s}$, ending at $v = 14.7 - 9.8 \times 3.0 = -14.7$.

$1.5\ \mathrm{s}$; about $11\ \mathrm{m}$; $-14.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$ (same speed, now downwards)

At the turning point the velocity is $0$, but the acceleration is not: it is still $-2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ (or $-9.8$ for a thrown ball). If $a$ were $0$ there, the ball would stay at the top forever.
Before calculating, turn the graph into a picture on an $x$-axis: "forwards 9 m, stop, back 4 m". Then displacement and distance are easy to see. The same shape fits a ball rolled up a slope and a ball thrown straight up.
  • One constant negative acceleration can slow an object, stop it and bring it back.
  • The turning point is where the $v$–$t$ line crosses the axis; there $v = 0$ but $a \ne 0$.
  • Displacement $=$ area above $-$ area below; distance $=$ area above $+$ area below.

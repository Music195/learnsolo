Types of Force

Most forces need touching, a few act across space

To push a balloon you have to touch it. Forces that act only where two objects touch are called contact forces (接触力): the pull of a string (tension), the push of a floor (normal force), friction, and the force of a spring.

A few forces act without touching: gravity (重力), the electric force between charges (クーロン力) and the magnetic force (磁気力). The space they act through is called a field (場), so these are field forces (場の力). Near the Earth, gravity acts on every object, wherever it is.

A third kind, inertial forces (慣性力) such as the centrifugal force, only appear to an observer who is accelerating. They come much later; this topic uses the first two kinds.

The forces on one object

$$\underbrace{mg}_{\text{field: always}} \; + \; \underbrace{T,\ N,\ f,\ kx,\ \dots}_{\text{contact: one for each thing it touches}}$$

$mg$ gravity, $T$ tension (張力), $N$ normal force (垂直抗力), $f$ friction (摩擦力), $kx$ spring force (弾性力). Each has its own note in this topic.

  1. A force is always one object acting on another: a hand on a balloon, the Earth on an apple.
  2. A contact force exists only while the two objects touch. Let go of the balloon and the hand's force is gone.
  3. A field force needs no touching, so it can't be found by looking for contact. Gravity acts on everything near the Earth.
  4. So list gravity first (it is always there), then go round the object and add one contact force for each thing touching it.

Example 1

Which of these are field forces: tension, gravity, friction, the electric force, the normal force?

Show solution
  1. Gravity and the electric force act without touching.
  2. Tension, friction and the normal force all need contact.

Gravity and the electric force

Example 2

A ball is thrown straight up. Ignoring air resistance, which forces act on it while it is rising?

Show solution
  1. Field forces: gravity, as always.
  2. Contact forces: the ball touches nothing once it has left the hand.

Only gravity $mg$, pointing down

Example 3

A book rests on a table while a hand pushes it sideways, but it does not move. List the forces on the book.

Show solution
  1. Field force: gravity.
  2. It touches the table: the normal force (up) and friction (sideways).
  3. It touches the hand: the push.

Gravity, normal force, friction and the push: 4 forces

Example 4

A steel clip floats below a magnet, tied to the floor by a thread. Which forces act on the clip, and which kind is each?

Show solution
  1. Field forces: gravity (down) and the magnetic force (up, towards the magnet).
  2. Contact force: the thread is the only thing it touches, so tension (down, along the thread).

Gravity and the magnetic force (field), tension (contact)

A contact force ends when the contact ends. A thrown ball carries no "force of the throw" with it: in flight, only gravity (and air resistance, if it isn't ignored) acts on it.
Always the same two steps: gravity first, then trace round the outline of the object and add a force at every point it touches something. You will miss far fewer forces.
  • Field forces (gravity, electric, magnetic) act without touching.
  • Contact forces (tension, normal force, friction, spring force) act only where objects touch.
  • List gravity first, then one contact force per thing touched.

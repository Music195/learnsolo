Drawing Force Arrows

Pick one object, and draw only the forces it receives

A force is drawn as an arrow (力の矢印). The arrow starts at the point of application (作用点), points in the force's direction (向き), and its length shows the size (大きさ). The straight line through the arrow is the line of action (作用線).

With one object, this is easy. With two or three objects touching each other, people start drawing too many or too few arrows. The fix is a rule: choose one object, and draw only the forces acting on it (その物体が受ける力). Forces that it puts on other objects belong in their pictures, not this one.

The three parts of a force

$$\text{force} = \text{point of application} \;+\; \text{direction} \;+\; \text{size}$$

Size is measured in newtons, $\mathrm{N}$: about the weight of a $100\ \mathrm{g}$ object. Gravity starts at the centre of gravity (重心); a contact force starts where the objects touch.

  1. We draw forces to find out whether an object stays still or how it moves.
  2. That depends only on the forces acting on it. A force it exerts acts on something else, and moves that something else.
  3. So every force can be named "who on whom", e.g. "floor on box". Keep it only if "whom" is the object you chose.
  4. Search in two steps (note 01): gravity first, then one contact force at each place the object touches something.

Example 1

A box hangs from a string tied to the ceiling. Which forces act on the box?

Show solution
  1. Gravity: from the centre of gravity, straight down.
  2. The box touches only the string: tension, pulling up along the string.
  3. "Box pulls the string" and "ceiling pulls the string" act on the string, not the box.

Gravity and tension: 2 forces

Example 2

A student drew 4 forces on a book lying on a table: gravity on the book, the normal force from the table, the book pushing on the table, and gravity on the table. Which ones should be removed?

Show solution
  1. "Book on table" acts on the table: remove it.
  2. "Earth on table" acts on the table: remove it.

The book's push on the table, and gravity on the table

Example 3

Block A rests on block B, and B rests on the floor. List the forces on B.

Show solution
  1. Gravity on B, from its centre, down.
  2. B touches A on its top face: A pushes B down.
  3. B touches the floor on its bottom face: the floor pushes B up.

Gravity on B, the push from A (down), the normal force from the floor (up)

Example 4

Blocks A and B lie on a smooth floor, joined by a string. A hand pulls B to the right with another string, so both move right. List the forces on A.

Show solution
  1. Gravity on A, down.
  2. A touches the floor: the normal force, up. The floor is smooth, so no friction.
  3. A touches the string to B: tension, pulling A to the right. The hand doesn't touch A, so its pull is not on A's list.

Gravity, normal force, tension: 3 forces

"The weight of A" is not a force on B. Gravity on A acts on A only. What B feels is A's push on its top face, a contact force. When A is at rest on B it happens to be the same size, $m_A g$, but it is a different force.
Say each force aloud as "who on whom": "Earth on box", "string on box", "box on floor". Any force whose "whom" isn't your object doesn't belong in the picture.

  • Choose one object; draw only the forces acting on it.
  • Gravity first, then one contact force at each place it touches.

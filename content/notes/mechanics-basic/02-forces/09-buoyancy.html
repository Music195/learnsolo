Buoyancy

Water pushes up harder on the bottom than down on the top

Water pressure grows with depth (previous note). An object in water has its bottom deeper than its top, so the water pushes up on the bottom harder than it pushes down on the top. The difference is an upward force: buoyancy.

Archimedes' principle: the buoyancy equals the weight of the liquid the object pushes aside. It depends on the volume under the surface and the density of the liquid, not on what the object is made of.

An object less dense than the liquid floats, with just enough of itself under the surface that buoyancy equals its weight. A denser object sinks.

Buoyancy on an object in a liquid

$$F = \rho V g$$

$\rho$ density of the liquid ($\mathrm{kg/m^3}$), $V$ volume of the object under the surface ($\mathrm{m^3}$), $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. It points straight up. $\rho V$ is the mass of the liquid pushed aside, so $\rho V g$ is its weight.

  1. Take a block with top and bottom faces of area $S$, fully under water: the top at depth $h_1$, the bottom at depth $h_2$. Its height is $h_2 - h_1$, so its volume is $V = (h_2 - h_1)S$.
  2. The water pushes on the sides too, but the pushes on opposite sides are at the same depths, so they cancel.
  3. On the bottom, upwards: $p_2 S = (p_0 + \rho h_2 g)S$. On the top, downwards: $p_1 S = (p_0 + \rho h_1 g)S$.
  4. Up minus down: $(p_0 + \rho h_2 g)S - (p_0 + \rho h_1 g)S = \rho (h_2 - h_1) S g = \rho V g$.
  5. $p_0$ cancelled, and so did the depth: buoyancy doesn't change as a fully sunk object goes deeper.

Change what the block is made of (a $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ cube in water)

Example 1

A stone of volume $2.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^3}$ lies fully under water ($\rho = 1.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$). Find the buoyancy on it.

Show solution
  1. $F = \rho V g = 1.0 \times 10^3 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 9.8$.

$F = 19.6\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 2

A block floats in water with $\tfrac{3}{4}$ of its volume under the surface. Find the density of the block.

Show solution
  1. Let the block's volume be $V$ and its density $\rho_B$. Gravity: $\rho_B V g$.
  2. Volume under water: $\tfrac{3}{4}V$, so buoyancy: $1.0 \times 10^3 \times \tfrac{3}{4}V \times g$.
  3. Floating at rest: $\rho_B V g = 1.0 \times 10^3 \times \tfrac{3}{4} V g$. $V$ and $g$ cancel.

$\rho_B = 7.5 \times 10^2\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$

Example 3

Wood of density $6.0 \times 10^2\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ floats in water. What fraction of it is under the surface?

Show solution
  1. As in Example 2: $\rho_B V g = \rho_W V_{\text{under}}\, g$, so $\dfrac{V_{\text{under}}}{V} = \dfrac{\rho_B}{\rho_W} = \dfrac{600}{1000}$.

$0.60$ of it ($60\%$)

Example 4

A $3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ metal block of volume $1.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^3}$ hangs from a string, fully under water. Find the tension.

Show solution
  1. Forces: gravity $3.0 \times 9.8 = 29.4$ down; buoyancy $1.0 \times 10^3 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 9.8 = 9.8$ up; tension $T$ up.
  2. At rest: $T + 9.8 = 29.4$.

$T = 19.6\ \mathrm{N}$: the block seems lighter in water

In $F = \rho V g$, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid, not of the object, and $V$ is only the volume under the surface. For a floating object, $V$ is less than the object's whole volume.
Think "buoyancy = weight of the water pushed aside". A ship floats because its hull pushes aside a huge volume of water whose weight equals the ship's.
  • Buoyancy comes from water pressure being bigger at the bottom than at the top.
  • $F = \rho V g$: liquid's density, volume under the surface. It points up.
  • Floating: buoyancy = gravity, and the fraction under water is $\rho_{\text{object}} / \rho_{\text{liquid}}$.

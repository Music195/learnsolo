Normal Force

A surface pushes back, at right angles to itself

When an object touches a surface, the surface pushes on it. This push is the normal force (垂直抗力), $N$. "Normal" means perpendicular: it acts at right angles to the surface, from the point or face of contact, out of the surface.

A surface can only push, never pull, so $N$ is never negative. And like tension, it has no formula: the surface pushes as hard as needed to stop the object sinking into it. Its size comes from the balance of forces across the surface.

For a block on a slope of angle $\theta$, that balance gives $N = mg\cos\theta$. Only on level ground, with nothing else pushing or pulling up or down, is $N = mg$.

A block at rest on a slope of angle $\theta$

$$N = mg\cos\theta$$

$N$ normal force, $mg$ gravity, $\theta$ the slope's angle with the horizontal. With an extra force across the slope, it joins the balance: a push in makes $N$ bigger, a pull away makes it smaller.

  1. Forces on the block: gravity $mg$ (down), the normal force $N$ (out of the slope), and friction $f$ (along the slope, holding it).
  2. Split gravity along the slope and across it. The angle between "straight down" and "into the slope" equals $\theta$, so the parts are $mg\cos\theta$ into the slope and $mg\sin\theta$ down the slope.
  3. The block doesn't move across the slope, so the forces across it balance: $N = mg\cos\theta$.
  4. On level ground $\theta = 0$, and $\cos 0 = 1$ gives $N = mg$. On a slope, $\cos\theta < 1$, so $N < mg$.

Tilt the slope and push or pull with the hand ($m = 2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$)

Example 1

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ book lies on a level table. Find the normal force on it.

Show solution
  1. Forces: gravity $2.0 \times 9.8$ down, $N$ up.
  2. Up = down: $N = 19.6$.

$N = 19.6\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 2

The same book is pressed down by a hand with $5.0\ \mathrm{N}$. Find the normal force now.

Show solution
  1. Down: gravity $19.6$ and the hand $5.0$. Up: $N$.
  2. $N = 19.6 + 5.0$.

$N = 24.6\ \mathrm{N}$, more than $mg$

Example 3

A $3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ box on the floor is pulled straight up by a string with $10\ \mathrm{N}$, but stays on the floor. Find the normal force. How hard must the string pull to just lift the box?

Show solution
  1. Up: $N + 10$. Down: $3.0 \times 9.8 = 29.4$. So $N = 29.4 - 10 = 19.4$.
  2. The box just lifts when the floor no longer pushes, $N = 0$: the string pulls $29.4\ \mathrm{N}$.

$N = 19.4\ \mathrm{N}$; it lifts at $29.4\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 4

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ block rests on a slope of $30°$. Find the normal force.

Show solution
  1. $N = mg\cos 30° = 2.0 \times 9.8 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 9.8\sqrt{3}$.

$N = 9.8\sqrt{3} \approx 17\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 5

A ladder leans against a smooth vertical wall, with its foot on the floor. In which directions do the two normal forces on the ladder act?

Show solution
  1. Each is perpendicular to its own surface, pushing out of it.
  2. The wall is vertical, so its push is horizontal, away from the wall. The floor is horizontal, so its push is straight up.

From the wall: horizontal, away from the wall. From the floor: vertically up

$N$ is not always $mg$. Many students write $N = mg$ by habit and lose the marks. It is only true on level ground with no other vertical force. Always find $N$ from the balance across the surface.
$N = 0$ means the surface has stopped pushing: the object is just about to leave it. Questions like "when does the box lift off?" or "when does the car leave the road?" are solved by setting $N = 0$.
  • The normal force is perpendicular to the surface, and always a push.
  • No formula: find it from the balance across the surface.
  • On a slope at rest, $N = mg\cos\theta$; $N = mg$ only on level ground with nothing else pressing.

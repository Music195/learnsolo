Static and Kinetic Friction

At rest, about to slide, sliding: three cases, three rules

Try dragging a heavy desk. You pull a little: it doesn't move. You pull harder: still nothing. Then suddenly it lurches, and once it is moving, keeping it moving is easier. Friction has three cases:

1. At rest: static friction. It has no formula: it is exactly as big as needed to stop the sliding, like tension and the normal force. Pull with $3\ \mathrm{N}$ and it is $3\ \mathrm{N}$; pull with $5\ \mathrm{N}$ and it is $5\ \mathrm{N}$.

2. About to slide: static friction has a limit, the maximum static friction, $f_0 = \mu N$. It is still static friction, just at its biggest. $\mu$ is the coefficient of static friction.

3. Sliding: kinetic friction, $f' = \mu' N$, with the coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu'$. It stays the same however hard you pull, and $\mu' < \mu$.

Friction on an object on a rough surface

$$\underbrace{f \le \mu N}_{\text{at rest}} \qquad \underbrace{f_0 = \mu N}_{\text{about to slide}} \qquad \underbrace{f' = \mu' N}_{\text{sliding}}$$

$N$ normal force, $\mu$ coefficient of static friction, $\mu'$ coefficient of kinetic friction (no units), and $\mu' < \mu$. At rest, find $f$ from the balance of forces.

  1. Pull a block with force $F$ and plot friction $f$ against $F$.
  2. While the block stays still, the forces along the floor balance: $f = F$. The graph is the straight line $f = F$ through the origin. That is why static friction has no formula.
  3. The line stops at the limit $f_0 = \mu N$: pull any harder and the block starts to slide.
  4. Once it slides, friction drops to $f' = \mu' N$ and stays flat, whatever $F$ is. Since $\mu' < \mu$, starting is the hardest part.
  5. Both limits are proportional to $N$: press the surfaces together harder and they grip more.

Pull harder and harder, then ease off ($m = 2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$, $\mu = 0.50$, $\mu' = 0.30$)

Example 1

A $5.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ box rests on a floor with $\mu = 0.40$. You pull it sideways with $10\ \mathrm{N}$ and it does not move. Find the friction on it.

Show solution
  1. At rest, so static friction: no formula. The forces along the floor balance.
  2. $f = 10$. ($\mu N$ is not used: the box is not about to slide.)

$f = 10\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 2

For the same box, what is the smallest sideways pull that makes it start to slide?

Show solution
  1. $N = mg = 5.0 \times 9.8 = 49$.
  2. It starts to slide when the pull passes the maximum static friction: $f_0 = \mu N = 0.40 \times 49$.

$19.6\ \mathrm{N}$ (about $20\ \mathrm{N}$)

Example 3

Once the box slides, $\mu' = 0.25$. Find the kinetic friction, and say what happens if you keep pulling with $15\ \mathrm{N}$.

Show solution
  1. $f' = \mu' N = 0.25 \times 49 \approx 12.3$.
  2. $15 > 12.3$, so the forces don't balance: the box speeds up.

$f' \approx 12\ \mathrm{N}$; with $15\ \mathrm{N}$ it keeps speeding up

Example 4

A block rests on a board. The board is slowly tilted, and the block starts to slide when the angle reaches $\theta$. Show that $\mu = \tan\theta$, and find $\mu$ when $\theta = 30°$.

Show solution
  1. Across the slope: $N = mg\cos\theta$. Along the slope, about to slide: $f_0 = mg\sin\theta$.
  2. $f_0 = \mu N$ gives $mg\sin\theta = \mu\, mg\cos\theta$, so $\mu = \tan\theta$.
  3. $\tan 30° = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.58$.

$\mu = \tan\theta$; at $30°$, $\mu \approx 0.58$

Example 5

You press a $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ book against a wall with a $20\ \mathrm{N}$ push at right angles to the wall. $\mu = 0.50$. Does the book stay up, and what is the friction?

Show solution
  1. Across the wall: $N = 20$, so the maximum friction is $\mu N = 0.50 \times 20 = 10$.
  2. Gravity is $0.50 \times 9.8 = 4.9$, less than $10$: the book stays up.
  3. At rest, friction just balances gravity: $f = 4.9$ (not $10$).

It stays up; $f = 4.9\ \mathrm{N}$, upwards

Don't write $f = \mu N$ for every object at rest. $\mu N$ is only the limit. An object at rest has static friction from the balance of forces (Examples 1 and 5); use $\mu N$ only when the question says "about to slide" or "starts to slide".
Before writing anything, ask: is it at rest, about to slide, or sliding? That one question picks the rule: balance, $\mu N$, or $\mu' N$.
  • At rest: static friction, no formula, from the balance of forces ($f \le \mu N$).
  • About to slide: maximum static friction $f_0 = \mu N$.
  • Sliding: kinetic friction $f' = \mu' N$, constant, and $\mu' < \mu$.

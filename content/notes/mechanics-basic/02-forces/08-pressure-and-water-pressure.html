Pressure and Water Pressure

Pressure is force per square metre

You can stand on a wooden floor all day, but a drawing pin goes straight into it. The force may be similar; what differs is the area it is spread over. Pressure (圧力) is the force at right angles to a surface per unit area: $p = \dfrac{F}{S}$. Its unit is the pascal, $1\ \mathrm{Pa} = 1\ \mathrm{N/m^2}$.

A liquid presses on everything in it. This water pressure (水圧) depends only on the depth, not on direction: at one depth, it pushes equally from above, below and the sides. It grows with depth because there is more water above, plus the air pressing on the surface: the atmospheric pressure (大気圧) $p_0$.

Pressure, and water pressure at depth $h$

$$p = \frac{F}{S} \qquad\qquad p = p_0 + \rho h g$$

$p$ in $\mathrm{Pa} = \mathrm{N/m^2}$, $F$ force in $\mathrm{N}$, $S$ area in $\mathrm{m^2}$, $p_0$ atmospheric pressure ($\approx 1.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$), $\rho$ density of the liquid in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ (water: $1.0 \times 10^3$), $h$ depth in $\mathrm{m}$. In a force balance, use the force $F = pS$.

  1. Imagine a thin, weightless sheet of area $S$, lying flat at depth $h$ in water of density $\rho$. Left alone, it stays still.
  2. From below, the water pushes it up with $pS$, where $p$ is the water pressure we want.
  3. From above, it carries the column of water over it: volume $hS$, mass $\rho hS$, weight $\rho hSg$. On top of that column, the air pushes down with $p_0 S$.
  4. The sheet is at rest, so up = down: $pS = p_0 S + \rho h S g$.
  5. Divide by $S$: $p = p_0 + \rho h g$.

Drag the sheet deeper ($S = 0.010\ \mathrm{m^2}$, $p_0 = 1.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$)

Example 1

A $60\ \mathrm{kg}$ person stands on both feet, with $0.030\ \mathrm{m^2}$ of shoe touching the floor. Find the pressure on the floor.

Show solution
  1. $F = mg = 60 \times 9.8 = 588$.
  2. $p = \dfrac{F}{S} = \dfrac{588}{0.030} = 19600$.

About $2.0 \times 10^4\ \mathrm{Pa}$

Example 2

A drawing pin is pushed with $10\ \mathrm{N}$. Its point has an area of $1.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m^2}$. Find the pressure at the point.

Show solution
  1. $p = \dfrac{10}{1.0 \times 10^{-7}}$.

$1.0 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{Pa}$: 5000 times the person in Example 1

Example 3

Find the water pressure $10\ \mathrm{m}$ below the surface of a lake. Use $p_0 = 1.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$ and $\rho = 1.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

Show solution
  1. $\rho h g = 1.0 \times 10^3 \times 10 \times 9.8 = 0.98 \times 10^5$.
  2. $p = 1.0 \times 10^5 + 0.98 \times 10^5$.

About $2.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$: every $10\ \mathrm{m}$ of water adds about one atmosphere

Example 4

An aquarium window of area $0.050\ \mathrm{m^2}$ is $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ below the water surface, with air on the other side. Find the extra force the water puts on it. (The air pushes on both sides, so $p_0$ cancels.)

Show solution
  1. Extra pressure from the water: $\rho h g = 1.0 \times 10^3 \times 2.0 \times 9.8 = 19600\ \mathrm{Pa}$.
  2. Force: $F = pS = 19600 \times 0.050$.

$F = 980\ \mathrm{N}$ (the weight of about $100\ \mathrm{kg}$)

Pressure is not a force. Don't put $p$ into a balance of forces: turn it into a force first with $F = pS$. Writing "$p + mg = \dots$" adds $\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathrm{N}$, which is like adding metres to seconds.
Weather forecasts use hectopascals: $1\ \mathrm{hPa} = 100\ \mathrm{Pa}$. Normal air pressure is about $1013\ \mathrm{hPa} \approx 1.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$: the weight of a $10\ \mathrm{tonne}$ mass on every square metre.
  • Pressure $p = F/S$, in $\mathrm{Pa} = \mathrm{N/m^2}$; for forces use $F = pS$.
  • Water pressure $p = p_0 + \rho h g$: it depends on depth only, and acts in every direction.
  • Derive it from a thin sheet: push from below = air + water column above.

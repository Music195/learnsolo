Equilibrium of Forces

Split every force into two directions, then balance each one

When the forces on an object cancel out completely, they are in equilibrium. Their total, the net force, is zero, and an object at rest stays at rest.

Forces are vectors, so "cancel out" means as arrows, not just as numbers. The practical way: choose two directions at right angles (usually horizontal and vertical), split every force into its components along them, and then write up = down and left = right.

This is how every tension, normal force and static friction in this topic was found. It is the main tool for the whole of mechanics.

Forces in equilibrium

$$\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \cdots = \vec{0} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \begin{cases} \text{(right)} = \text{(left)} \\ \text{(up)} = \text{(down)} \end{cases}$$

A force $F$ at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal splits into $F\cos\theta$ (horizontal) and $F\sin\theta$ (vertical). On a slope, use "along the slope" and "across the slope" instead.

  1. Every force arrow is the sum of a horizontal arrow and a vertical arrow: its components.
  2. Adding forces means adding the horizontal parts together and the vertical parts together, separately.
  3. The total is the zero vector only if both totals are zero: the right-pointing parts equal the left-pointing parts, and the upward parts equal the downward parts.
  4. Drawn head to tail, forces in equilibrium make a closed shape: you end where you started.

Drag the tips of $F_1$ and $F_2$; $F_3$ is the force that keeps the ring still

Example 1

A $1.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ lamp hangs from two strings: string 1 at $60°$ to the vertical, string 2 horizontal. Find both tensions.

Show solution
  1. Split $T_1$: vertical part $T_1\cos 60° = \tfrac{1}{2}T_1$, horizontal part $T_1\sin 60° = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}T_1$.
  2. Up = down: $\tfrac{1}{2}T_1 = 1.0 \times 9.8$, so $T_1 = 19.6$.
  3. Left = right: $T_2 = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}T_1 = 9.8\sqrt{3} \approx 17.0$.

$T_1 = 19.6\ \mathrm{N}$, $T_2 \approx 17\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 2

A $3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ block rests on a smooth slope of $30°$, held by a string parallel to the slope. Find the tension and the normal force.

Show solution
  1. Use directions along and across the slope. Gravity splits into $mg\sin 30°$ down the slope and $mg\cos 30°$ into it.
  2. Along: $T = 3.0 \times 9.8 \times \tfrac{1}{2} = 14.7$.
  3. Across: $N = 3.0 \times 9.8 \times \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 25.5$.

$T = 14.7\ \mathrm{N}$, $N \approx 25\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 3

A $5.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ box on a rough floor is pulled by a rope at $30°$ above the horizontal with $20\ \mathrm{N}$. It doesn't move. Find the normal force and the friction.

Show solution
  1. Split the pull: $20\cos 30° = 10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.3$ horizontal, $20\sin 30° = 10$ up.
  2. Up = down: $N + 10 = 5.0 \times 9.8 = 49$, so $N = 39$.
  3. Left = right: $f = 17.3$ (static friction, from the balance).

$N = 39\ \mathrm{N}$, $f \approx 17\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 4

Two forces act on a ring: $3.0\ \mathrm{N}$ east and $4.0\ \mathrm{N}$ north. What third force keeps it in equilibrium?

Show solution
  1. East–west: the third force needs $3.0\ \mathrm{N}$ west. North–south: $4.0\ \mathrm{N}$ south.
  2. Its size: $\sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2} = 5.0$.

$5.0\ \mathrm{N}$, pointing south-west (opposite to the sum of the first two)

Only forces on the same object go into its balance (note 02). And one equation is not enough: a 2D problem needs both directions, otherwise you'll have two unknowns and one equation.
Choose the directions so that as many forces as possible lie along them. On a slope, "along" and "across" the slope leave only gravity to split.
  • Equilibrium: the net force is zero.
  • Split forces into two directions at right angles; balance each: up = down, left = right.
  • Head to tail, forces in equilibrium close up into a polygon.

Inside an Accelerating Train

In an accelerating train, "down" tilts backwards

A train speeds up with acceleration $a$ to the right. A ball of mass $m$ hangs from the ceiling. Seen from inside the train, the ball hangs still, but tilted backwards by an angle $\theta$.

For a passenger, three forces act on the ball: gravity $mg$ (down), the inertial force $ma$ (backwards), and the tension $T$. Gravity and the inertial force add up to one arrow, the apparent gravity $mg'$. Inside the train it behaves exactly like gravity, just tilted. The string simply lines up with it.

This picture answers three classic questions at once:

  • The angle: $\tan\theta = a/g$.
  • Cut the string: inside the train, the ball "falls" straight along the apparent gravity, a straight slanted line. From the ground, only real gravity acts, and the ball already had the train's velocity, so it follows a horizontal projectile path.
  • A helium balloon tied to the floor tilts forwards. Buoyancy always points opposite to "down", and here "down" is the apparent gravity, so up is tilted forwards.

Apparent gravity in a train with acceleration $a$

$$\tan\theta = \frac{a}{g} \qquad g' = \sqrt{g^2 + a^2} \qquad T = mg'$$

$\theta$: angle of the string from the vertical (tilted backwards). $mg'$: gravity $mg$ and the inertial force $ma$ added as arrows. The tension balances it: same size, opposite direction, on the same line.

  1. Inside the train the ball is still, so $T$, $mg$ and $ma$ balance.
  2. Add $mg$ (down) and $ma$ (backwards) tip to tail: they form the two short sides of a right-angled triangle. The long side is the apparent gravity $mg' = m\sqrt{g^2 + a^2}$.
  3. The tension must be equal and opposite to $mg'$, so the string lies along the long side. The angle between the string and the vertical is the angle in that triangle: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{ma}{mg} = \dfrac{a}{g}$.
  4. You could also split $T$ into $T\sin\theta$ and $T\cos\theta$ and solve two equations; the triangle is quicker.

A train speeding up to the right ($m = 0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$)

Example 1

A train speeds up at $4.9\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find $\tan\theta$ for a ball hanging from its ceiling, and the angle.

Show solution
  1. $\tan\theta = a/g = 4.9/9.8 = 0.50$.

$\tan\theta = 0.50$, so $\theta \approx 27^\circ$, tilted backwards

Example 2

In Example 1 the ball has mass $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$. Find the tension.

Show solution
  1. $g' = \sqrt{9.8^2 + 4.9^2} = 4.9\sqrt{5} \approx 11.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
  2. $T = mg' = 0.50 \times 11.0$.

$T \approx 5.5\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 3

The string is cut. Describe the ball's path (a) for a passenger, (b) for someone standing beside the track.

Show solution
  1. (a) The passenger sees the ball start from rest under the constant apparent gravity $mg'$: it moves in a straight line along the string's direction, speeding up at $g'$.
  2. (b) From the ground there is no inertial force; only gravity acts. At the moment of cutting the ball has the train's horizontal velocity, so it moves like a ball thrown horizontally.

(a) a straight line slanting backwards; (b) a horizontal projectile (a parabola)

Example 4

A helium balloon is tied to the floor of the train in Example 1. Which way does its string lean, and by what angle?

Show solution
  1. Gravity and the inertial force on the balloon add up to an apparent gravity tilted backwards by $\theta$.
  2. Buoyancy acts opposite to the apparent gravity, so it points up and forwards; the balloon's string lines up with it.

Forwards, at about $27^\circ$ from the vertical ($\tan\theta = 0.50$)

Don't forget the balloon also has weight and an inertial force, just like the ball. Buoyancy is not "always straight up": it is always opposite to the (apparent) gravity.
Inside any vehicle with constant acceleration, first find the apparent gravity $\vec{g}' = \vec{g} - \vec{a}$. After that, treat the problem exactly like one with ordinary gravity that happens to point in a slanted direction.
  • In a train speeding up at $a$: hanging objects tilt backwards with $\tan\theta = a/g$.
  • Apparent gravity $g' = \sqrt{g^2 + a^2}$; the tension is $mg'$.
  • Cut string: a straight line inside; a horizontal projectile from the ground.
  • A helium balloon leans forwards.

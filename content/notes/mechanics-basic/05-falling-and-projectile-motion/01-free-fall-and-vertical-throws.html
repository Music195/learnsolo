Free Fall and Vertical Throws

Only gravity acts: constant acceleration $g$, so use the constant-acceleration formulas

When an object moves straight up or down and only gravity acts on it (no air resistance), its equation of motion is $ma = mg$, so its acceleration is always $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, downwards. It is just uniformly accelerated motion (topic 01) with $a = \pm g$. There are three standard cases:

  • Free fall: let go "gently" (that means $v_0 = 0$).
  • Throwing down: thrown downwards with speed $v_0$.
  • Throwing up: thrown upwards with speed $v_0$. It slows down, stops for an instant at the top, then falls back.

Choose the positive direction first. Most books take down as positive for free fall and throwing down ($a = +g$), and up as positive for throwing up ($a = -g$). You don't need new formulas: put $a = \pm g$ and $x \to y$ into the three you already know.

Vertical motion under gravity

$$\begin{aligned} &\text{free fall (down +):} && v = gt, && y = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2, && v^2 = 2gy \\ &\text{throw down (down +):} && v = v_0 + gt, && y = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2, && v^2 - v_0^2 = 2gy \\ &\text{throw up (up +):} && v = v_0 - gt, && y = v_0 t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2, && v^2 - v_0^2 = -2gy \end{aligned}$$

For a throw up: it reaches the top at $t_1 = \dfrac{v_0}{g}$, at height $H = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2g}$, and lands back at $2t_1$ with velocity $-v_0$.

  1. Throw up (up positive): the v–t graph is a straight line starting at $v_0$ with slope $-g$. Each second the velocity drops by $g$.
  2. At the top the velocity is $0$: $0 = v_0 - g t_1$, so $t_1 = \dfrac{v_0}{g}$.
  3. The height is the area under the line up to $t_1$, a triangle: $H = \tfrac{1}{2} v_0 t_1 = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2g}$.
  4. To come back down, the area below the axis must also be $H$. The line keeps the same slope, so this second triangle is the same as the first: it takes another $t_1$, and ends at $-v_0$. Going up and coming down are symmetric.

Drop or throw a ball and watch its v–t graph

Example 1

A stone is dropped from a cliff and hits the ground after $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$. How high is the cliff, and how fast does the stone hit the ground?

Show solution
  1. Free fall, down positive: $y = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times 3.0^2 = 44.1$.
  2. $v = 9.8 \times 3.0 = 29.4$.

About $44\ \mathrm{m}$; $29\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 2

A ball is thrown down at $5.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ from a height of $10\ \mathrm{m}$. Find its speed when it hits the ground.

Show solution
  1. Down positive: $v^2 - 5.0^2 = 2 \times 9.8 \times 10$, so $v^2 = 221$.

$v \approx 15\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 3

A ball is thrown straight up at $19.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Find the time to the top, the maximum height, and when it lands back.

Show solution
  1. $t_1 = \dfrac{19.6}{9.8} = 2.0\ \mathrm{s}$.
  2. $H = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 19.6 \times 2.0 = 19.6\ \mathrm{m}$ (area of the triangle).
  3. By symmetry it lands at $2t_1$.

$2.0\ \mathrm{s}$; $19.6\ \mathrm{m}$; after $4.0\ \mathrm{s}$, at $19.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$ downwards

Example 4

A ball is thrown straight up at $14.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Where is it, and which way is it moving, after $2.0\ \mathrm{s}$?

Show solution
  1. Up positive: $v = 14.7 - 9.8 \times 2.0 = -4.9$: moving down.
  2. $y = 14.7 \times 2.0 - 4.9 \times 2.0^2 = 29.4 - 19.6 = 9.8$.

$9.8\ \mathrm{m}$ up, falling at $4.9\ \mathrm{m/s}$

At the top of a throw the velocity is $0$, but the acceleration is still $g$ downwards. Gravity never switches off: that is why the ball starts falling again.
You don't need to memorise the three sets of formulas. Draw the v–t graph: the time to the top is where the line crosses the axis, the height is a triangle's area, and the symmetry gives the rest.
  • Under gravity alone, the acceleration is always $g$ downwards: use the constant-acceleration formulas with $a = \pm g$.
  • Throw up: top at $t_1 = v_0/g$, height $H = v_0^2/2g$.
  • It takes as long to come down as to go up, and lands with the same speed.

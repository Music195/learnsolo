Vertical Circles: Rod or String

Going round a vertical circle: a rod only needs speed at the top, a string needs it to stay tight

Now the circle stands up (a vertical circle). Unlike a Ferris wheel, which a motor turns at a steady speed, a ball on its own speeds up on the way down and slows down on the way up. So this is circular motion that is not uniform.

A ball of mass $m$ at the bottom of a circle of radius $r$ is given a speed $v_0$. Will it go all the way round? It depends on one word in the question:

  • On a light rod: a rod can push as well as pull, and never goes slack. Even if the ball almost stops at the top, the rod holds it on the circle and it carries on. It only needs some speed at the top: $v > 0$.
  • On a string: a string can only pull. Near the top, if the ball is too slow, the string goes slack and the ball falls inside the circle. It needs the tension at the top to be $T \ge 0$, which takes a faster start.

Problems like this always use the same two equations: conservation of energy (bottom to top) and, for the string, the balance towards the centre using the centrifugal force.

To go all the way round a vertical circle of radius $r$

$$\text{rod: } v_0 > 2\sqrt{gr} \qquad \text{string: } v_0 \ge \sqrt{5gr}$$

Energy, bottom to top: $\tfrac12 mv_0^2 = \tfrac12 mv^2 + mg(2r)$. At the top, for the string (seen riding with the ball): $T + mg = m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ (the centrifugal force $mv^2/r$ points up, out of the circle).

  1. Energy (both cases): $\tfrac12 mv_0^2 = \tfrac12 mv^2 + 2mgr$, so $v^2 = v_0^2 - 4gr$ at the top.
  2. Rod: it just needs $v^2 > 0$ at the top, so $v_0^2 > 4gr$, giving $v_0 > 2\sqrt{gr}$. Not "$\ge$": with $v = 0$ exactly, the ball would stop at the top and stay balanced there.
  3. String: riding with the ball at the top, $T$ and $mg$ point down and the centrifugal force $mv^2/r$ points up. Balance: $T + mg = mv^2/r$, so $T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} - mg$.
  4. The string must not go slack: $T \ge 0$, so $v^2 \ge gr$. With $T = 0$ the string is just tight for that one instant and tight again either side, so "$=$" is allowed.
  5. Put in $v^2 = v_0^2 - 4gr$: $v_0^2 - 4gr \ge gr$, so $v_0 \ge \sqrt{5gr}$.

A $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball on a circle of radius $0.80\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 1

A ball on a light rod of length $0.80\ \mathrm{m}$ swings in a vertical circle. Find the smallest speed at the bottom that takes it all the way round.

Show solution
  1. $v_0 > 2\sqrt{gr} = 2\sqrt{9.8 \times 0.80} = 2 \times 2.8$.

$v_0$ must be more than $5.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 2

Same circle, but now the ball is on a string. Find the smallest speed at the bottom.

Show solution
  1. $v_0 \ge \sqrt{5gr} = \sqrt{5 \times 9.8 \times 0.80} = \sqrt{39.2}$.

$v_0 \ge 6.3\ \mathrm{m/s}$, faster than with the rod

Example 3

A $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball on that string starts at $7.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Find its speed and the tension at the top.

Show solution
  1. $v^2 = 7.0^2 - 4 \times 9.8 \times 0.80 = 49 - 31.36 = 17.64$, so $v = 4.2\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
  2. $T = mv^2/r - mg = 0.50 \times 17.64/0.80 - 4.9 = 11.0 - 4.9$.

$v = 4.2\ \mathrm{m/s}$, $T \approx 6.1\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 4

Find the tension at the bottom for the ball in Example 3, just after it starts.

Show solution
  1. At the bottom the centrifugal force points down, with $mg$; $T$ points up. Balance: $T = mg + mv_0^2/r$.
  2. $T = 4.9 + 0.50 \times 49/0.80 = 4.9 + 30.6$.

$T \approx 35.5\ \mathrm{N}$: the string is tightest at the bottom

"Rod" or "string": one word in the question changes the answer. A rod case uses only energy ($v > 0$ at the top). A string case needs energy and the force balance at the top ($T \ge 0$).
For any vertical circle: (1) write conservation of energy between the start and the point you care about, (2) write the balance towards the centre at that point, with the centrifugal force $mv^2/r$ pointing out. Then solve the two together.
  • Speed changes round a vertical circle: use conservation of energy.
  • Rod: $v > 0$ at the top, $v_0 > 2\sqrt{gr}$.
  • String: $T \ge 0$ at the top, $v_0 \ge \sqrt{5gr}$.

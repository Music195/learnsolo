Leaving a Curved Surface

On a rounded top, the surface pushes less and less, and at $N = 0$ the object flies off

A small object slides along a smooth flat surface at speed $v_0$ and then over the top A of a rounded surface, part of a circle of radius $r$ (a dome).

  • Just before A it moves in a straight line at steady speed, so the forces balance: $N = mg$, whatever $v_0$ is.
  • Just after A it is in circular motion. Riding with it, the centrifugal force $mv_0^2/r$ points up, away from the centre, so $N' = mg - mv_0^2/r$. The faster it goes, the smaller $N'$. If $v_0^2 \ge gr$, then $N'$ would be $0$ or less: it leaves the surface straight away at A.
  • Starting very slowly (almost $v_0 = 0$), it speeds up as it slides down, $N$ falls, and it always leaves the surface partway down. This is why no real slide is shaped like a dome: a child on a "scary slide" like that would fly off before the bottom!

The method is the same as for any vertical circle: energy plus the balance towards the centre, with the condition "leaves the surface" meaning $N = 0$.

Sliding over a smooth dome (angle $\theta$ measured from the top)

$$N = mg\cos\theta - m\frac{v^2}{r} \qquad \tfrac12 mv^2 = \tfrac12 mv_0^2 + mgr(1 - \cos\theta)$$

It leaves where $N = 0$: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{v_0^2}{3gr}$. Starting from rest: $\cos\theta = \dfrac23$, at a height $h = r\cos\theta = \dfrac23 r$ above the centre. It leaves at A itself if $v_0 \ge \sqrt{gr}$.

  1. Call the angle from the top $\theta$ (choosing an angle makes the algebra easier than using the height directly). The drop from A is $r(1 - \cos\theta)$.
  2. Energy, starting from rest: $mgr(1 - \cos\theta) = \tfrac12 mv^2$.
  3. Riding with the object, along the line to the centre: gravity's part $mg\cos\theta$ points in, the normal force $N$ and the centrifugal force $mv^2/r$ point out. Balance: $mg\cos\theta = N + mv^2/r$.
  4. Leaving means $N = 0$: $mv^2 = mgr\cos\theta$. Put this into the energy equation: $mgr(1 - \cos\theta) = \tfrac12 mgr\cos\theta$, so $1 - \cos\theta = \tfrac12\cos\theta$ and $\cos\theta = \tfrac23$.
  5. The height above the centre is $h = r\cos\theta = \tfrac23 r$.

A $1.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ object over a smooth dome of radius $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 1

A $1.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ object moves at $2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ on the flat part, towards a dome of radius $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$. Find the normal force just before and just after the top A.

Show solution
  1. Before A: steady straight-line motion, $N = mg = 9.8\ \mathrm{N}$.
  2. After A: $N' = mg - mv_0^2/r = 9.8 - 1.0 \times 4.0/1.0$.

$9.8\ \mathrm{N}$ before; $5.8\ \mathrm{N}$ just after

Example 2

For that dome, find the smallest $v_0$ for which the object leaves the surface right at A.

Show solution
  1. Set $N' = 0$: $mg = mv_0^2/r$, so $v_0 = \sqrt{gr} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 1.0}$.

$v_0 \approx 3.1\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 3

The object starts at A almost at rest. At what height above the centre does it leave the dome ($r = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$), and how fast is it then?

Show solution
  1. From the proof, $\cos\theta = 2/3$, so $h = \tfrac23 r \approx 0.67\ \mathrm{m}$ ($\theta \approx 48^\circ$).
  2. $v^2 = gr\cos\theta = 9.8 \times \tfrac23 = 6.5$.

$h \approx 0.67\ \mathrm{m}$; $v \approx 2.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 4

The object passes A at $2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ instead. Where does it leave the dome?

Show solution
  1. $\cos\theta = \dfrac23 + \dfrac{v_0^2}{3gr} = 0.667 + \dfrac{4.0}{29.4} = 0.80$.
  2. $h = r\cos\theta = 0.80\ \mathrm{m}$, $\theta \approx 37^\circ$.

At $0.80\ \mathrm{m}$ above the centre: earlier than when starting from rest

On the outside of a curve the surface can only push outwards, so $N$ can't go below $0$. Never assume a smooth object slides all the way down a dome: check where $N$ becomes $0$.
Even when the question asks for a height $h$, introduce an angle $\theta$ yourself. Both equations are simple in $\cos\theta$, and at the end $h = r\cos\theta$.
  • On the flat: $N = mg$. On the dome: $N = mg\cos\theta - mv^2/r$.
  • "Leaves the surface" means $N = 0$.
  • From rest at the top: leaves at $\cos\theta = 2/3$, height $\tfrac23 r$ above the centre. If $v_0 \ge \sqrt{gr}$ it leaves at the top.

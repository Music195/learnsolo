Problems Where the Total Momentum Is Zero

Total momentum 0 means the centre of mass never moves

Many exam problems start with everything at rest and only internal forces acting. Then the total momentum is $0$ at the start, and stays $0$ for the whole problem. Three shortcuts follow at once:

  • The speeds are always in the inverse ratio of the masses: $mv = MV$, so $v : V = M : m$.
  • The kinetic energies are also in the inverse ratio, $M : m$, so any energy released is shared out in that ratio.
  • $v_G = 0$: the centre of mass stays still. So the ground's view is the centre-of-mass view, and a collision between the two simply multiplies both velocities by $-e$.

The demo is a typical problem. A bead on a smooth wire is fixed to a cart that can roll on a smooth floor. The bead is let go at height $h_1$ on a curved part, runs along the flat part, and hits a wall on the cart (coefficient $e$).

When the total momentum is $0$

$$v : V = M : m, \qquad K_m : K_M = M : m, \qquad v_G = 0$$

A collision between the two (coefficient $e$): $v' = -ev$, $V' = -eV$, so all kinetic energy becomes $e^2$ times as big. A height reached afterwards (where both stop together) is $e^2$ times the height before.

  1. Horizontally only internal forces act (bead and cart push each other), and everything starts at rest: $0 = MV - mv$ on the flat part.
  2. Only gravity does work on the pair: $mgh_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}MV^2$. Share $mgh_1$ in the ratio $M : m$: $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{M}{m + M}\,mgh_1$, so $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2Mgh_1}{m + M}}$, $V = \dfrac{m}{M}v$.
  3. The wall collision: $v_G = 0$, so seen from the ground (= from G), both velocities are multiplied by $-e$. The speeds just after are $ev$ and $eV$.
  4. Climbing back, the highest point is where both stop (same velocity, and the total momentum is $0$). Kinetic energy after the wall is $e^2 mgh_1$, all of which becomes $mgh_2$: $h_2 = e^2h_1$.

A bead slides down a wire on a free cart and hits the cart's wall

Example 1

In the demo, $m = 1.0\ \mathrm{kg}$, $M = 2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$, $h_1 = 0.60\ \mathrm{m}$. Find the speeds of the bead and the cart on the flat part.

Show solution
  1. $mgh_1 = 1.0 \times 9.8 \times 0.60 = 5.88\ \mathrm{J}$, shared $M : m = 2 : 1$: the bead gets $3.92\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 1.0 \times v^2 = 3.92$, so $v = 2.8\ \mathrm{m/s}$; $V = \tfrac{1.0}{2.0} \times 2.8 = 1.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

Bead $2.8\ \mathrm{m/s}$, cart $1.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$, opposite ways

Example 2

In Example 1 the bead hits the wall with $e = 0.70$. Find the speeds just after.

Show solution
  1. Total momentum $0$, so $v_G = 0$: both velocities are multiplied by $-e$.

Bead $0.70 \times 2.8 = 1.96\ \mathrm{m/s}$, cart $0.98\ \mathrm{m/s}$, both reversed

Example 3

In Example 2, how high does the bead climb back up the curve?

Show solution
  1. At the highest point both stop (same velocity, total momentum $0$).
  2. All kinetic energy (now $e^2$ times the original $mgh_1$) becomes $mgh_2$.

$h_2 = e^2h_1 = 0.49 \times 0.60 \approx 0.29\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 4

A $50\ \mathrm{kg}$ person walks from one end of a $150\ \mathrm{kg}$ boat to the other, $4.0\ \mathrm{m}$ away, on still water (ignore water resistance). How far does the boat move?

Show solution
  1. Total momentum $0$ ⇒ the centre of mass stays still. The person moves $x$ forwards and the boat $y$ backwards: $50x = 150y$, and $x + y = 4.0$ (relative movement).
  2. $x = 3y$, so $4y = 4.0$.

The boat moves $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ backwards (the person $3.0\ \mathrm{m}$ forwards)

These shortcuts need the total momentum to be $0$, not just constant. If the cart had been pushed first, or one object started moving, $v_G \neq 0$: then subtract $v_G$ first (previous note).
"Let go gently", "at rest at first", "on a smooth floor": when you see these together, write $0 = MV - mv$ straight away, and think "inverse ratio" and "$v_G = 0$".
  • Start at rest + only internal forces ⇒ total momentum $0$ forever.
  • Speeds and kinetic energies are in the inverse ratio of the masses.
  • $v_G = 0$: a collision multiplies both velocities by $-e$; heights reached afterwards scale by $e^2$.

Collisions and the Coefficient of Restitution

Two unknown velocities need two equations: momentum and restitution

When two objects collide and bounce apart, there are two unknowns: both velocities afterwards. Conservation of momentum gives one equation. The second comes from the coefficient of restitution $e$, which you met for a ball hitting a fixed floor (topic 05). For two moving objects, it compares their relative velocities: how fast they separate afterwards, compared with how fast they approached before.

  • $e = 1$: elastic collision: they separate as fast as they approached, and kinetic energy is conserved.
  • $0 < e < 1$: inelastic.
  • $e = 0$: perfectly inelastic: they stick together.

Always write every velocity with the same positive direction, even one you think will go backwards. If it really goes backwards, the answer simply comes out negative. Guessing directions is the easiest way to make a sign mistake.

Coefficient of restitution for two moving objects

$$e = -\frac{v_A' - v_B'}{v_A - v_B}$$

Bottom: relative velocity before; top: relative velocity after (dashes). Subtract in the same order on top and bottom (A − B both times, or B − A both times). Don't forget the minus sign. Solve it together with $m_A v_A + m_B v_B = m_A v_A' + m_B v_B'$.

  1. P (mass $m$, velocity $v_0$) hits Q (mass $M$, at rest). Afterwards: $v$ and $V$, both taken as positive in P's direction.
  2. Momentum: $mv_0 = mv + MV$.   Restitution: $-e = \dfrac{v - V}{v_0 - 0}$, so $-ev_0 = v - V$.
  3. Eliminate $v$ (adding and subtracting, rather than substituting, is safer): multiply the restitution equation by $m$ and subtract it from the momentum equation: $(1 + e)mv_0 = (m + M)V$.
  4. So $V = \dfrac{(1 + e)mv_0}{m + M}$ and, from $v = V - ev_0$, $v = \dfrac{(m - eM)v_0}{m + M}$.
  5. $V$ is always positive (Q is pushed forwards). $v$ is negative when $m < eM$: a light object bounces back off a heavy one.

P hits Q (at rest) at $v_0 = 4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ on a smooth floor

Example 1

P ($2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$, $4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$) hits Q ($3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$, at rest), with $e = 0.50$. Find both velocities afterwards.

Show solution
  1. Momentum: $8.0 = 2.0v + 3.0V$. Restitution: $v - V = -0.50 \times 4.0 = -2.0$.
  2. From the second, $v = V - 2.0$. Then $8.0 = 2.0V - 4.0 + 3.0V$, so $V = 2.4$, $v = 0.40$.

P: $0.40\ \mathrm{m/s}$, Q: $2.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$, both forwards

Example 2

In Example 1, find the size of the impulse on P.

Show solution
  1. The impulses on P and Q are an action–reaction pair: the same size. Q is easier (it started at rest).
  2. On Q: $3.0 \times 2.4 - 0 = 7.2$.

$7.2\ \mathrm{N \cdot s}$ (backwards on P)

Example 3

Two equal masses collide elastically ($e = 1$); one was at rest. What happens?

Show solution
  1. $v = \dfrac{(m - m)v_0}{2m} = 0$, $V = \dfrac{2mv_0}{2m} = v_0$.

They swap velocities: the moving one stops dead, and the other moves off at $v_0$ (like a pool ball hit straight on)

Example 4

A ($1.0\ \mathrm{kg}$, $+3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$) and B ($2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$, $-1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$) collide head on with $e = 0.50$. Find the velocities afterwards.

Show solution
  1. Momentum: $3.0 - 2.0 = 1.0 = v_A' + 2.0v_B'$.
  2. Restitution: $v_A' - v_B' = -0.50 \times (3.0 - (-1.0)) = -2.0$.
  3. Subtract: $3.0v_B' = 3.0$, so $v_B' = 1.0$, $v_A' = -1.0$.

A: $-1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ (backwards), B: $+1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Two classic mistakes: forgetting the minus sign in $e = -\dfrac{v_A' - v_B'}{v_A - v_B}$, and drawing a velocity backwards because you "expect" it to bounce back. Take every velocity positive in one direction and let the algebra decide.
The impulse in a collision can't be found from $F\Delta t$ (the force isn't constant), so use change in momentum. And since the two impulses are an action–reaction pair, work it out for whichever object is simpler.
  • Two unknowns, two equations: momentum and $e = -\dfrac{v_A' - v_B'}{v_A - v_B}$.
  • Same positive direction for every velocity; negative answers mean backwards.
  • $e = 1$ elastic (kinetic energy conserved), $e = 0$ they stick.

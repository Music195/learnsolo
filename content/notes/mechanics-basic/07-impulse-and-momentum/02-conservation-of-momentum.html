Conservation of Momentum

If only internal forces act, the total momentum doesn't change

When two objects A and B collide, A pushes B and B pushes A back: an action–reaction pair, equal in size, opposite in direction, acting for exactly the same time. So their impulses are equal and opposite: whatever momentum A loses, B gains. The total momentum stays the same.

This is the law of conservation of momentum. Its condition: only internal forces (forces between the objects in the group) act, or at least only internal forces act in the direction you're looking at. If something outside pushes one of them (an external force), the impulses no longer cancel.

Momentum and energy are separate laws. In a collision where objects stick together, momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is lost (it turns into heat and sound).

Conservation of momentum (two objects)

$$m_A v_A + m_B v_B = m_A v_A' + m_B v_B'$$

Velocities with signs (choose one positive direction); dashes mean after. Holds when only internal forces act. If the total starts at $0$: $m_A v_A' = -m_B v_B'$, so the speeds are in the inverse ratio of the masses, $v_A' : v_B' = m_B : m_A$, and so are the kinetic energies.

  1. During the collision B pushes A with force $F$ (right positive) and A pushes B with $-F$, for the same time.
  2. Change in momentum = impulse for each: $m_A v_A' - m_A v_A = \int F\,dt$ and $m_B v_B' - m_B v_B = -\int F\,dt$.
  3. Add the two: the impulses cancel. $(m_A v_A' - m_A v_A) + (m_B v_B' - m_B v_B) = 0$.
  4. Rearrange: total momentum before = total momentum after. If an external force also acted, its impulse would stay in the sum and the total would change.

Two carts on a smooth track: momentum and energy before and after

Example 1

A bullet of mass $m$ at speed $v_0$ hits a block of mass $M$ resting on a smooth floor and stays inside it. Find their speed afterwards.

Show solution
  1. Only internal forces (bullet and block push each other): momentum is conserved.
  2. $mv_0 = (m + M)V$.

$V = \dfrac{mv_0}{m + M}$

Example 2

In Example 1, how much mechanical energy is lost?

Show solution
  1. The floor is level, so only kinetic energy changes. Lost = before − after.
  2. $\tfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}(m + M)\left(\dfrac{mv_0}{m+M}\right)^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2\left(1 - \dfrac{m}{m+M}\right)$.

$\dfrac{mMv_0^2}{2(m + M)}$

Example 3

A small block (mass $m$) is let go at height $h$ on the smooth curved surface of a block (mass $M$) that can slide on a smooth floor. Find their speeds when the small block reaches the floor level.

Show solution
  1. Horizontally only internal forces act: momentum is conserved. Starting at $0$: $0 = MV - mv$, so $v : V = M : m$, and the kinetic energies are also in the ratio $M : m$.
  2. Only gravity does work on the pair: $mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}MV^2$.
  3. Share $mgh$ in the ratio $M : m$: $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{M}{m+M}mgh$. Then $V = \dfrac{m}{M}v$.

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2Mgh}{m + M}}$, $V = \dfrac{m}{M}\sqrt{\dfrac{2Mgh}{m + M}}$, in opposite directions

Example 4

A $60\ \mathrm{kg}$ skater and a $40\ \mathrm{kg}$ skater stand still on smooth ice and push each other apart. The $40\ \mathrm{kg}$ skater moves off at $3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$. Find the other skater's velocity.

Show solution
  1. $0 = 40 \times 3.0 + 60\,v$.

$v = -2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$: $2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ the other way

Momentum being conserved says nothing about kinetic energy. Check each law separately: momentum needs "only internal forces"; mechanical energy needs "only conservative forces do work" (topic 06). In Example 3 both hold; in Example 1 only momentum does.
Whenever the total momentum is $0$ (things start at rest and push apart), the speeds and the kinetic energies are both in the inverse ratio of the masses. Then you can share the energy out in that ratio directly, as in Example 3.
  • Only internal forces act ⇒ total momentum is conserved (because action–reaction impulses cancel).
  • Use signed velocities with one positive direction.
  • Total momentum $0$ ⇒ speeds and kinetic energies in the inverse ratio of the masses.

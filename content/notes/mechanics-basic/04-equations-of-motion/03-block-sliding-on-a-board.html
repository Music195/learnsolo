Stacked Objects: A Block Sliding on a Board

Two stacked objects can move differently: each gets its own acceleration

A board B (mass $M$) lies on a smooth floor, and a block A (mass $m$) is sent sliding along the top of it at $v_0$. There is friction between A and B (coefficient $\mu$). Picture someone running onto ice and sliding on a thin mat: the rough mat stops the person, but nothing stops the mat sliding on the ice.

Here the two objects don't share an acceleration, so give them different ones, $a_A$ and $a_B$. Kinetic friction $\mu mg$ acts on A backwards (against its sliding), and its reaction acts on B forwards. A slows down, B speeds up, until they have the same velocity. After that A stops sliding on B ("A is at rest relative to B") and they move together at a constant velocity.

Block A sliding on board B (smooth floor)

$$m a_A = -\mu m g, \qquad M a_B = \mu m g$$

So $a_A = -\mu g$ and $a_B = \dfrac{\mu m g}{M}$. They reach the same velocity $v = \dfrac{m v_0}{m + M}$ at $t_1 = \dfrac{M v_0}{\mu (m + M) g}$. In that time A slides $L = \tfrac{1}{2} v_0 t_1$ along B.

  1. Draw both v–t lines. A starts at $v_0$ and goes down (slope $-\mu g$); B starts at $0$ and goes up (slope $\mu m g / M$).
  2. They meet at $t_1$: B's line gives $v = \dfrac{\mu m g}{M}\,t_1$, and A's gives $v = v_0 - \mu g\,t_1$. Setting them equal: $t_1 = \dfrac{M v_0}{\mu (m+M) g}$, and then $v = \dfrac{m v_0}{m + M}$.
  3. The area under each line is how far each moved. A moved further: the difference between the areas is the triangle between the two lines.
  4. That triangle has base $v_0$ (on the $v$ axis at $t = 0$) and height $t_1$, so the distance A slid along B is $L = \tfrac{1}{2} v_0 t_1 = \dfrac{M v_0^2}{2\mu (m + M) g}$.

Send block A sliding onto board B at $v_0 = 4\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 1

A $1.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ block slides onto a $3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ board on a smooth floor at $4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$, with $\mu = 0.30$. Find the accelerations of the block and the board.

Show solution
  1. Friction: $\mu m g = 0.30 \times 1.0 \times 9.8 = 2.94\ \mathrm{N}$, backwards on A, forwards on B.
  2. A: $1.0\,a_A = -2.94$.   B: $3.0\,a_B = 2.94$.

$a_A = -2.94\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, $a_B = 0.98\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$

Example 2

In Example 1, when do they reach the same velocity, and what is it?

Show solution
  1. B is simpler (starts from rest): $v = 0.98\,t_1$. A: $v = 4.0 - 2.94\,t_1$.
  2. Equal: $3.92\,t_1 = 4.0$, so $t_1 \approx 1.02\ \mathrm{s}$, and $v = 0.98 \times 1.02 = 1.0$.

After about $1.0\ \mathrm{s}$, both at $1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 3

In Example 1, how far does the block slide along the board?

Show solution
  1. The triangle between the two v–t lines has base $v_0 = 4.0$ and height $t_1 = 1.02$.
  2. $L = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4.0 \times 1.02$.

$L \approx 2.0\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 4

Check Example 3 with relative motion: as seen from B, A starts at $4.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ with acceleration $a_A - a_B$. How far does A go relative to B before it stops relative to B?

Show solution
  1. Relative acceleration: $a_A - a_B = -2.94 - 0.98 = -3.92\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
  2. Use $v^2 - v_0^2 = 2ax$ for the relative motion: $0 - 4.0^2 = 2 \times (-3.92) \times L$.

$L = 16 / 7.84 \approx 2.0\ \mathrm{m}$, the same

The friction on A and on B is an action–reaction pair, so both have size $\mu m g$, with the small $m$ (A's weight is what presses on B). Don't write $\mu M g$ for B. Also, "A is at rest relative to B" means the same velocity, not that A has stopped.
For two objects that move differently, draw both v–t lines on one graph. Where they meet is when they move together, and the area between them is how far one slides on the other. In a later topic, conservation of momentum gives $v = \dfrac{m v_0}{m + M}$ even faster.
  • Stacked objects that slide on each other get different accelerations $a_A$, $a_B$.
  • Kinetic friction $\mu m g$ acts backwards on the sliding block and forwards on the board.
  • They move together once their velocities are equal; the area between the v–t lines is the distance slid.

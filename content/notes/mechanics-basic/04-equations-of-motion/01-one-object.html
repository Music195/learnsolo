The Equation of Motion for One Object

Draw every force first, then write ma = (net force) along the motion

Once you can write equations of motion without mistakes, many mechanics problems become easy. It takes practice, and it always follows the same steps:

  1. Draw every force on the object. Gravity $mg$ first, then one force for each thing it touches: the floor (normal force $N$, friction), a string (tension $T$), a hand.
  2. Choose the positive direction, usually the way it speeds up, and mark the acceleration $a$ that way.
  3. Write $ma =$ the net force in that direction: forces that way with $+$, forces the other way with $-$. Forces at right angles to it don't go in.

If the object can't move in some direction (it stays on the floor or on the slope), the forces in that direction balance. This gives the normal force, and from it the friction.

The equation of motion along one direction

$$ma = (\text{forces in the } + \text{ direction}) - (\text{forces in the } - \text{ direction})$$

The left side is always "mass of the object × its acceleration". Directions it can't move in: forces balance, e.g. $N = mg$ on a level floor, $N = mg\cos\theta$ on a slope at angle $\theta$.

  1. On a slope at angle $\theta$, split gravity $mg$ into two parts: $mg\sin\theta$ down the slope and $mg\cos\theta$ into the slope.
  2. The object slides along the slope, but it never jumps off the slope or sinks into it. So its acceleration at right angles to the slope is $0$.
  3. Equation of motion at right angles to the slope: $m \times 0 = N - mg\cos\theta$, so $N = mg\cos\theta$.
  4. Along the slope, only the parts of the forces along it count: for a pull $T$ up the slope, $ma = T - mg\sin\theta$.

Six problems: see the forces and the equation of motion ($m = 2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$)

Example 1

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ block moves at $3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ to the right on a smooth floor. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. Forces: $mg$ down, $N$ up. Nothing along the floor: $3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ is a velocity, not a force.
  2. Right positive: $2.0\,a = 0$.

$a = 0$: it keeps moving at $3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 2

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ weight hangs on a string, and the string pulls it up with $T = 30\ \mathrm{N}$. Take up as positive. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. Forces: $T = 30$ up, $mg = 19.6$ down.
  2. $2.0\,a = 30 - 19.6 = 10.4$.

$a = 5.2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, upwards

Example 3

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ block is pulled up a smooth $30^\circ$ slope by a $15\ \mathrm{N}$ force along the slope. Find its acceleration and the normal force.

Show solution
  1. Split gravity: $mg\sin 30^\circ = 9.8$ down the slope, $mg\cos 30^\circ \approx 17.0$ into the slope.
  2. At right angles to the slope: $N = 17.0\ \mathrm{N}$.
  3. Up the slope positive: $2.0\,a = 15 - 9.8 = 5.2$.

$a = 2.6\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ up the slope; $N \approx 17\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 4

A block slides to the right on a rough floor. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu = 0.25$. Take right as positive. Find its acceleration (any mass $m$).

Show solution
  1. Up and down: $N = mg$. Kinetic friction $\mu N = \mu mg$, to the left (against the motion).
  2. $ma = -\mu mg$, so $a = -\mu g = -0.25 \times 9.8$.

$a = -2.45\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$: it slows down, whatever its mass

Example 5

A $4.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ box is pulled to the right with $20\ \mathrm{N}$ on a rough floor ($\mu = 0.20$) while it slides. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. Kinetic friction: $\mu mg = 0.20 \times 4.0 \times 9.8 = 7.84\ \mathrm{N}$, to the left.
  2. $4.0\,a = 20 - 7.84 = 12.16$.

$a \approx 3.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$

A velocity like $v_0$ is not a force. A block moving at $v_0$ on a smooth floor has no force along the floor, so $ma = 0$. Never put $v_0$ into the right side of $ma = F$.
Always write the balance at right angles to the motion too ($N = mg$, or $N = mg\cos\theta$ on a slope), even if the question doesn't ask. You need it as soon as there is friction, because kinetic friction is $\mu N$.
  • Steps: draw every force, choose the positive direction, write $ma =$ net force in that direction.
  • Only gravity and forces from things the object touches act on it. A velocity is not a force.
  • Directions it can't move in: forces balance. Kinetic friction is $\mu N$, against the motion.

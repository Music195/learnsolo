Simple Harmonic Motion as a Shadow of Circular Motion

Watch uniform circular motion from the side and you see simple harmonic motion

A ball goes round a circle at a steady rate. Shine a light on it from the side so its shadow falls on a wall: the shadow moves up and down, fastest in the middle and stopping for an instant at each end. That back-and-forth motion is simple harmonic motion (SHM). In one phrase: SHM is the projection of uniform circular motion.

Everything about SHM comes from the circle:

  • The circle's radius becomes the amplitude $A$, the furthest the object gets from the centre.
  • The angular velocity $\omega$ becomes the angular frequency. The period is still $T = 2\pi/\omega$.
  • The circle's velocity ($A\omega$ along the tangent) and acceleration ($A\omega^2$ to the centre) cast shadows too; those shadows are the velocity and acceleration of the SHM.

The key result: the acceleration is always $-\omega^2$ times the position. Turned around, this is the test for SHM: if an equation of motion can be rearranged into $a = -(\text{a positive constant}) \times x$, the motion is simple harmonic.

Simple harmonic motion starting at the centre, moving in the $+$ direction

$$x = A\sin\omega t \qquad v = A\omega\cos\omega t \qquad a = -A\omega^2\sin\omega t = -\omega^2x$$

$A$: amplitude (m). $\omega$: angular frequency (rad/s). $T = 2\pi/\omega$: period. The SHM condition: $a = -\omega^2x$. Other starting points just shift the angle (for example, starting at the top end: $x = A\cos\omega t$).

  1. Position. Start the ball on the circle at the level of the centre at $t = 0$. After time $t$ it has turned through $\theta = \omega t$. Its height above the centre is $A\sin\omega t$, so the shadow is at $x = A\sin\omega t$.
  2. Velocity. On the circle the velocity is $A\omega$ along the tangent. The tangent makes the angle $\omega t$ with the vertical, so its vertical part (the shadow's velocity) is $v = A\omega\cos\omega t$.
  3. Acceleration. On the circle the acceleration is $A\omega^2$ towards the centre. Its vertical part has size $A\omega^2\sin\omega t$ and points down when the ball is above the centre, so $a = -A\omega^2\sin\omega t$.
  4. Since $x = A\sin\omega t$, this is $a = -\omega^2x$.
  5. With calculus you can check the chain directly: differentiate $x$ to get $v$ (the chain rule brings out $\omega$), and differentiate again: $\cos$ becomes $-\sin$ and another $\omega$ comes out. Two differentiations bring $\sin\omega t$ back, now with $-\omega^2$ in front.

Example 1

An object moves with $x = 0.20\sin 5t$ (metres, seconds). Find the amplitude, angular frequency and period.

Show solution
  1. Compare with $x = A\sin\omega t$: $A = 0.20\ \mathrm{m}$, $\omega = 5\ \mathrm{rad/s}$.
  2. $T = 2\pi/\omega = 2\pi/5$.

$A = 0.20\ \mathrm{m}$, $\omega = 5.0\ \mathrm{rad/s}$, $T \approx 1.3\ \mathrm{s}$

Example 2

For the motion in Example 1, find the largest speed and the largest acceleration, and where each happens.

Show solution
  1. Largest speed $A\omega = 0.20 \times 5 = 1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$, when $\cos\omega t = \pm1$: at the centre $x = 0$.
  2. Largest acceleration $A\omega^2 = 0.20 \times 25 = 5.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, when $\sin\omega t = \pm1$: at the ends $x = \pm A$.

$1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$ at the centre; $5.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ at the ends

Example 3

An object's equation of motion is $2.0\,a = -18x$. Is it SHM? If so, find $\omega$ and $T$.

Show solution
  1. $a = -9.0\,x$: the form $a = -\omega^2x$, so yes, with $\omega^2 = 9.0$.
  2. $\omega = 3.0\ \mathrm{rad/s}$, $T = 2\pi/3.0$.

SHM with $\omega = 3.0\ \mathrm{rad/s}$, $T \approx 2.1\ \mathrm{s}$

Example 4

An object in SHM with $\omega = 4.0\ \mathrm{rad/s}$ is at $x = -0.050\ \mathrm{m}$. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. $a = -\omega^2x = -16 \times (-0.050)$.

$a = +0.80\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$: always pointing back towards the centre

Don't drop the minus sign in $a = -\omega^2x$. It says the acceleration always points back towards the centre: on the $+$ side it is negative, on the $-$ side positive. Without the minus sign the object would run away instead of oscillating.
The words change, the maths doesn't: radius → amplitude, angular velocity → angular frequency. When stuck on an SHM question, draw the reference circle and think about the ball going round it.
  • SHM is the projection (shadow) of uniform circular motion.
  • $x = A\sin\omega t$, $v = A\omega\cos\omega t$, $a = -\omega^2x$.
  • Test for SHM: rearrange the equation of motion into $a = -\omega^2x$; then $T = 2\pi/\omega$.

The Spring Oscillator

A mass on a spring obeys $a = -\omega^2x$, so it moves in SHM

A block of mass $m$ on a smooth floor is joined to a spring (spring constant $k$). Put the origin O where the spring has its natural length, and take $x$ as the block's position, positive to the right.

Pull it to $x$ and the spring pulls back with size $kx$ (Hooke's law), towards O. The equation of motion is $ma = -kx$, so $a = -\dfrac{k}{m}x$. That is exactly the SHM condition $a = -\omega^2x$, with $\omega^2 = k/m$. So we get the angular frequency and the period at once.

A classic slip is the compressed side. There the spring pushes to the right, so it is tempting to write $ma = +kx$. But on that side $x$ itself is negative, so $+kx$ would point left. The correct equation is still $ma = -kx$: a negative $x$ times $-k$ gives a positive (rightward) force.

Spring oscillator

$$ma = -kx \qquad \omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$$

$k$: spring constant (N/m). $m$: mass (kg). $x$: position measured from where the forces balance (on a smooth floor, the natural length). The period does not depend on the amplitude.

  1. Stretched by $x$ (with $x > 0$): the spring pulls left with $kx$. Taking right as $+$: $ma = -kx$.
  2. Compressed (now $x < 0$, compression $-x$): the spring pushes right with $k(-x)$. Taking right as $+$: $ma = +k(-x) = -kx$. The same equation.
  3. Rearranged: $a = -\dfrac{k}{m}x$. Compare with $a = -\omega^2x$: $\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$.
  4. $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$.

A block on a spring, pulled out and let go

Example 1

A $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ block on a spring with $k = 50\ \mathrm{N/m}$ oscillates on a smooth floor. Find $\omega$ and $T$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = \sqrt{k/m} = \sqrt{100} = 10\ \mathrm{rad/s}$.
  2. $T = 2\pi/\omega$.

$\omega = 10\ \mathrm{rad/s}$, $T \approx 0.63\ \mathrm{s}$

Example 2

The block in Example 1 is pulled out twice as far before being released. What happens to the period?

Show solution
  1. $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$ has no amplitude in it.

It stays $0.63\ \mathrm{s}$ (it moves faster, over a longer path)

Example 3

What mass on the same spring would double the period?

Show solution
  1. $T \propto \sqrt m$, so doubling $T$ needs $4$ times the mass.

$2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$

Example 4

Right is $+$. The block in Example 1 is at $x = -0.040\ \mathrm{m}$ (spring compressed). Write the equation of motion and find $a$.

Show solution
  1. $ma = -kx$ still: $0.50\,a = -50 \times (-0.040) = 2.0$.

$a = +4.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, to the right, back towards O

$x$ is a position (with a sign), not a length. Writing $ma = kx$ for the compressed side treats $x$ as a length. Keep $ma = -kx$ and let the sign of $x$ do the work.
You can memorise $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$, but also learn the route: equation of motion → $a = -\omega^2x$ → $T = 2\pi/\omega$. That route works for every SHM, not just springs.
  • Spring: $ma = -kx$ on both sides of O.
  • $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$, independent of amplitude.

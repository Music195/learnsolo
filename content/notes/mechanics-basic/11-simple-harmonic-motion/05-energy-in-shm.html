Energy in SHM

Two different "$\tfrac12 kx^2$"s: know which $x$ you mean

For a mass hanging on a spring, the expression $\tfrac12 kx^2$ can mean two different things, and mixing them up is a common source of wrong answers:

  • Elastic potential energy (the spring's own energy): here $x$ is the stretch or squash from the natural length, just like in Hooke's law. Gravity's potential energy $mgh$ must then be added separately.
  • The combined potential energy of the spring force and gravity together (their total force is $-kx$): here $x$ is measured from the equilibrium position. This one already includes gravity, so no $mgh$ term.

Both give the same answers, but the second is far shorter. If a question asks "what is the elastic potential energy?", though, you must use the first meaning (stretch from the natural length).

And there is a third way to get the top speed with no energy at all: SHM is the shadow of circular motion, and at the centre the shadow moves exactly as fast as the point on the circle: $v_{\max} = A\omega$.

A mass on a vertical spring ($kl = mg$; $x$ from equilibrium, down $+$)

$$\tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac12 kx^2 = \text{constant} \qquad v_{\max} = A\omega = A\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

The long way: $\tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac12 k(l + x)^2 + mgh = $ constant, with $l$ the stretch at equilibrium and $h$ the height. At position $x$ (amplitude $A$): $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$.

  1. Released from $x = d$ (at rest). Take the lowest point as the zero of height. Find the speed $v$ at O.
  2. Method 1 (elastic PE + gravity PE): $\tfrac12 k(l + d)^2 + 0 = \tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac12 kl^2 + mgd$.
  3. Expand: $\tfrac12 kl^2 + kld + \tfrac12 kd^2 = \tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac12 kl^2 + mgd$. The $\tfrac12 kl^2$ cancel, and $kld = mgd$ because $kl = mg$. Left: $\tfrac12 kd^2 = \tfrac12 mv^2$.
  4. Method 2 (combined, $x$ from equilibrium): $\tfrac12 kd^2 = \tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac12 k \cdot 0^2$. The same line, at once.
  5. So $v = d\sqrt{k/m}$. Method 3: the reference circle has radius $d$ and turns at $\omega$, so its point moves at $d\omega$; at O the shadow moves exactly that fast: $v = d\omega = d\sqrt{k/m}$.
  6. At $x = d/2$: $\tfrac12 kd^2 = \tfrac12 mu^2 + \tfrac12 k(d/2)^2$, so $u^2 = \tfrac34\cdot\dfrac{k}{m}d^2$ and $u = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}d\sqrt{k/m}$. On the circle the point is $60^\circ$ round, and the vertical part of its velocity is $d\omega\cos 30^\circ$: the same.

Released from $x = d$ ($m = 0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$, $k = 8.0\ \mathrm{N/m}$, $d = 0.20\ \mathrm{m}$)

Example 1

A $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ mass on a spring ($k = 8.0\ \mathrm{N/m}$) is pulled $0.20\ \mathrm{m}$ below equilibrium and released. Find its largest speed.

Show solution
  1. $v_{\max} = d\sqrt{k/m} = 0.20 \times \sqrt{16} = 0.20 \times 4.0$.

$0.80\ \mathrm{m/s}$, at the equilibrium position

Example 2

Find its speed at $x = d/2 = 0.10\ \mathrm{m}$.

Show solution
  1. $u = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_{\max} = 0.866 \times 0.80$.

$u \approx 0.69\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 3

What is the spring's elastic potential energy at the lowest point?

Show solution
  1. Stretch from the natural length: $l + d$, with $l = mg/k = 4.9/8.0 = 0.6125\ \mathrm{m}$, so $l + d = 0.8125\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. $\tfrac12 \times 8.0 \times 0.8125^2$.

About $2.6\ \mathrm{J}$ (not $\tfrac12 \times 8.0 \times 0.20^2 = 0.16\ \mathrm{J}$)

Example 4

Using the combined potential energy, find the total energy of the oscillation.

Show solution
  1. At the lowest point it is at rest: total $= \tfrac12 kd^2 = \tfrac12 \times 8.0 \times 0.20^2$.

$0.16\ \mathrm{J}$ (check: $\tfrac12 \times 0.50 \times 0.80^2 = 0.16$ at O)

Each time you write $\tfrac12 kx^2$, ask "measured from where?" From the natural length: it's elastic PE, add $mgh$. From equilibrium: it's the combined PE, no $mgh$. Never mix the two in one equation.
For top speeds and speeds at simple points, the reference circle is the quickest: $v_{\max} = A\omega$, and at other points take the vertical part of the circle's velocity. Exam time is limited, so knowing this shortcut matters.
  • Elastic PE: $\tfrac12 k(\text{stretch from natural length})^2$, plus $mgh$ separately.
  • Combined PE: $\tfrac12 kx^2$ with $x$ from equilibrium, no $mgh$.
  • $v_{\max} = A\omega$; at $x$, $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$.

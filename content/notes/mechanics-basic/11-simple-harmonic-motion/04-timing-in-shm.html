Timing in SHM

To find "how long until it gets there", measure the angle on the reference circle

A mass $m$ hangs on a spring (constant $k$) with its equilibrium position at O. It is pulled down a distance $d$ and released at $t = 0$. Take down as $+$.

How long until it first passes O, or $x = d/2$, or $x = -d/2$? You can't use the constant-acceleration formulas: the spring force changes with $x$, so the acceleration keeps changing. Instead use the picture from the start of this topic: SHM is the shadow of a point going round a circle (radius $d$) at a steady angular speed. So:

time = (angle turned on the circle ÷ $360^\circ$) × $T$

Because the point turns steadily but the shadow moves fast in the middle and slowly near the ends, equal distances do not take equal times. Going from $d$ to $d/2$ takes $T/6$, not half of the $T/4$ needed to reach O.

Released from $x = d$ at $t = 0$

$$x = d\cos\omega t \qquad t = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}T \qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$$

$\theta$: the angle the reference point has turned (in degrees). First times: $x = 0$ at $T/4$ ($90^\circ$); $x = d/2$ at $T/6$ ($60^\circ$); $x = -d/2$ at $T/3$ ($120^\circ$); $x = -d$ at $T/2$ ($180^\circ$).

  1. Period. With $x$ from equilibrium (down $+$), the equation of motion is $ma = -kx$, so $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$ and $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$.
  2. To O. The reference point starts at the bottom of the circle (level with $x = d$). Its shadow reaches O when the point is level with the centre: a quarter turn, $90^\circ$, so $t = T/4$.
  3. To $x = d/2$. The shadow is at $d/2$ when $\cos\theta = \frac{d/2}{d} = \frac12$. In that right-angled triangle the sides are in the ratio $1 : 2 : \sqrt3$, so $\theta = 60^\circ$ and $t = \frac{60}{360}T = T/6$. Not $45^\circ$!
  4. To $x = -d/2$. Past the centre, $\cos\theta = -\frac12$, so $\theta = 90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ$ and $t = T/3$.

Released from $x = d$ ($m = 0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$, $k = 8.0\ \mathrm{N/m}$, $d = 0.20\ \mathrm{m}$)

Example 1

A $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ mass on a spring with $k = 8.0\ \mathrm{N/m}$ is pulled down and released. Find its period.

Show solution
  1. $T = 2\pi\sqrt{0.50/8.0} = 2\pi \times 0.25$.

$T = \pi/2 \approx 1.6\ \mathrm{s}$

Example 2

How long after release does it first pass the equilibrium position O?

Show solution
  1. A quarter turn: $t = T/4 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac mk} = \dfrac{\pi}{8}$.

$t \approx 0.39\ \mathrm{s}$

Example 3

When does it first reach $x = d/2$?

Show solution
  1. $\cos\theta = 1/2$, so $\theta = 60^\circ$: $t = T/6 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{\dfrac mk} = \dfrac{\pi}{12}$.

$t \approx 0.26\ \mathrm{s}$ (not $T/8$)

Example 4

When does it first reach $x = -d/2$ (above O)?

Show solution
  1. $\theta = 90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ$, so $t = T/3 = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}\sqrt{\dfrac mk} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

$t \approx 0.52\ \mathrm{s}$

SHM is not uniformly accelerated motion, so $x = v_0t + \tfrac12at^2$ is wrong here. And distances don't split time evenly: half the distance to O is not half the time.
For any timing question, draw the reference circle with radius equal to the amplitude, mark the start and the target heights, and read off the angle with a $1 : 2 : \sqrt3$ or $1 : 1 : \sqrt2$ triangle.
  • Time in SHM = (angle on the reference circle ÷ $360^\circ$) × $T$.
  • From an end: to the centre $T/4$; to half-way $T/6$; to the other half-way point $T/3$; to the other end $T/2$.

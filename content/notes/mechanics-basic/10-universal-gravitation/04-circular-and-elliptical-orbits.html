Circular and Elliptical Orbits

Circles use the equation of motion; ellipses use Kepler's second law plus energy

A typical exam problem has two orbits around a planet of mass $M$:

  • Orbit A: a circle of radius $r$.
  • Orbit B: an ellipse that touches A at its nearest point P (distance $r$) and reaches out to its furthest point Q (distance $3r$).

The questions usually go in the same order, and each one has its own tool:

  1. Speed on the circle: equation of motion for circular motion.
  2. Period on the circle: distance round ÷ speed.
  3. Ratio of speeds at P and Q: Kepler's second law.
  4. The actual speeds at P and Q: second law together with conservation of energy. Whenever you use the second law for an ellipse, expect to pair it with energy.
  5. Period of the ellipse: Kepler's third law, comparing B with A.

Circle of radius $r$, and an ellipse from $r$ (P) out to $kr$ (Q)

$$v_A = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} \qquad v_P = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{1+k}}\,v_A \qquad v_Q = \frac{v_P}{k} \qquad T_B = \left(\frac{1+k}{2}\right)^{3/2}T_A$$

$T_A = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v_A} = 2\pi r\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{GM}}$. Semi-major axis of B: $a = \dfrac{r + kr}{2}$. With $k = 3$: $v_P = \sqrt{\dfrac{3GM}{2r}}$, $v_Q = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{6r}}$, $a = 2r$, $T_B = 2\sqrt2\,T_A$.

  1. Circle A: $m\dfrac{v_A^2}{r} = G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$, so $v_A = \sqrt{GM/r}$.
  2. Second law at P and Q: $\tfrac12 r\,v_P = \tfrac12 (kr)\,v_Q$, so $v_P = k\,v_Q$.
  3. Energy from P to Q: $\tfrac12 mv_P^2 - G\dfrac{Mm}{r} = \tfrac12 mv_Q^2 - G\dfrac{Mm}{kr}$.
  4. Put in $v_P = kv_Q$: $\tfrac12 v_Q^2(k^2 - 1) = \dfrac{GM}{r}\left(1 - \dfrac1k\right) = \dfrac{GM}{r}\cdot\dfrac{k - 1}{k}$. Divide by $(k - 1)$: $v_Q^2 = \dfrac{2GM}{rk(k + 1)}$.
  5. Then $v_P^2 = k^2v_Q^2 = \dfrac{2k}{k + 1}\cdot\dfrac{GM}{r}$. For $k = 3$: $v_Q^2 = \dfrac{GM}{6r}$ and $v_P^2 = \dfrac{3GM}{2r}$.
  6. Third law (same planet, so the same constant): $\dfrac{T_B^2}{a^3} = \dfrac{T_A^2}{r^3}$, so $T_B = \left(\dfrac{a}{r}\right)^{3/2}T_A$. Solve for $T_B$ before putting numbers in; it keeps the algebra short.

Orbit A (circle, radius $r$) and orbit B (ellipse from $r$ out to $kr$)

Example 1

A satellite moves on circle A (radius $r$) round a planet of mass $M$. Find its speed and period. ($G$ is the gravitational constant.)

Show solution
  1. $m\dfrac{v_A^2}{r} = G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$ gives $v_A = \sqrt{GM/r}$.
  2. $T_A = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v_A} = 2\pi r\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{GM}}$.

$v_A = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$, $T_A = 2\pi r\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{GM}}$

Example 2

Orbit B goes from P (distance $r$) to Q (distance $3r$). How many times faster is the satellite at P than at Q?

Show solution
  1. Second law: $\tfrac12 r v_P = \tfrac12 (3r) v_Q$.

$v_P = 3v_Q$: 3 times faster at P

Example 3

Find the speed at Q on orbit B.

Show solution
  1. Energy: $\tfrac12 m(3v_Q)^2 - \dfrac{GMm}{r} = \tfrac12 mv_Q^2 - \dfrac{GMm}{3r}$.
  2. $4v_Q^2 = \dfrac{2GM}{3r}$, so $v_Q^2 = \dfrac{GM}{6r}$.

$v_Q = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{6r}}$ (and $v_P = 3v_Q = \sqrt{\dfrac{3GM}{2r}}$)

Example 4

Find the period of orbit B.

Show solution
  1. Semi-major axis: $a = \dfrac{r + 3r}{2} = 2r$.
  2. Third law: $T_B = \left(\dfrac{2r}{r}\right)^{3/2}T_A = 2\sqrt2\,T_A = 4\sqrt2\,\pi r\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{GM}}$.

$T_B = 4\sqrt2\,\pi r\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{GM}}$, about $2.8$ times $T_A$

Example 5

A satellite on circle A wants to move onto orbit B. At P, should its engine speed it up or slow it down, and by how much?

Show solution
  1. On A at P: $v_A = \sqrt{GM/r}$. On B at P: $v_P = \sqrt{3GM/(2r)} = \sqrt{1.5}\,v_A$.

Speed up, to about $1.22$ times its circular speed

The second law only gives the ratio $v_P : v_Q$. To find the actual speeds you must also write conservation of energy, with $U = -GMm/r$ (not $mgh$).
Answers like $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}$ look neater rationalised: $\dfrac{\sqrt6}{6}$. Do that last, after everything has cancelled.
  • Circle: equation of motion gives $v = \sqrt{GM/r}$; then $T = 2\pi r/v$.
  • Ellipse: second law (ratio of speeds) + energy (actual speeds).
  • Ellipse period: third law with $a = (r_{\text{near}} + r_{\text{far}})/2$.

The Second Law (ma = F)

Force gives acceleration: more force, more; more mass, less

The second law of motion (運動の第2法則, also called 運動の法則) says: a force on an object gives it an acceleration in the direction of the force, proportional to the size of the force and inversely proportional to the object's mass.

Written as a formula, this is the equation of motion (運動方程式), $ma = F$. Push twice as hard: twice the acceleration. Push something twice as heavy with the same force: half the acceleration.

Read it as result = cause. The left side, $ma$, is what happens to the object. The right side, $F$, is why: the net force from all the forces acting on it. It is not just an "equals" sign between two numbers.

The equation of motion

$$m\vec{a} = \vec{F}_{\text{net}}$$

$m$ mass in $\mathrm{kg}$, $\vec{a}$ acceleration in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$ the net force in $\mathrm{N}$. $\vec{a}$ points the same way as $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$. $1\ \mathrm{N}$ is the force that gives $1\ \mathrm{kg}$ an acceleration of $1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.

  1. Experiments with carts show: double the force, double the acceleration ($a \propto F$); double the mass, half the acceleration ($a \propto \tfrac{1}{m}$).
  2. Together, $a \propto \dfrac{F}{m}$. The newton is defined so that the constant is exactly $1$: $a = \dfrac{F}{m}$, or $ma = F$.
  3. Check with gravity: a falling object has $a = g$ and only gravity acts on it, so $mg = F$. That is why gravity is $W = mg$.
  4. With several forces, they combine into the net force first. If the net force is zero, $a = 0$: the first law is the special case $F = 0$.

Push a cart: set the pull $F$, the friction $f$ and the mass $m$

Example 1

A net force of $10\ \mathrm{N}$ acts on a $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ cart. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. $2.0\,a = 10$, so $a = 5.0$.

$5.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, in the direction of the force

Example 2

What net force is needed to give a $1200\ \mathrm{kg}$ car an acceleration of $2.5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$?

Show solution
  1. $F = ma = 1200 \times 2.5$.

$3.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{N}$

Example 3

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ box is pulled along a floor with $15\ \mathrm{N}$. Kinetic friction on it is $5.0\ \mathrm{N}$. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. Along the floor (direction of the pull positive): net force $= 15 - 5.0 = 10$.
  2. Up and down, gravity and the normal force balance, so they don't appear.
  3. $2.0\,a = 10$.

$a = 5.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$

Example 4

A $1.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ weight is lifted by a string with tension $12.8\ \mathrm{N}$. Find its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. Up positive. Forces: $T = 12.8$ up, $mg = 9.8$ down.
  2. $1.0\,a = 12.8 - 9.8 = 3.0$.

$a = 3.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, upwards

Example 5

Blocks A ($3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$) and B ($2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$) touch on a smooth floor. A hand pushes A with $10\ \mathrm{N}$, so A pushes B along. Find the acceleration and the force A puts on B.

Show solution
  1. Both together (mass $5.0$): $5.0\,a = 10$, so $a = 2.0$.
  2. B alone: the only horizontal force on B is A's push $P$. $2.0 \times 2.0 = P$.

$a = 2.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; A pushes B with $4.0\ \mathrm{N}$

The $F$ in $ma = F$ is the net force: add every force on the object with its sign, as in Examples 3 and 4. And $ma$ is not a force: never draw "$ma$" as an arrow in a force diagram next to the real forces.
Three steps every time: (1) choose the object and draw its forces (topic 02); (2) choose the positive direction, along the acceleration; (3) write $ma =$ (forces that way) $-$ (forces the other way).
  • $ma = F$: acceleration is proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to the mass.
  • Left side: result (the motion). Right side: cause (the net force).
  • $\vec{a}$ points the same way as the net force; $W = mg$ is $ma = F$ with $a = g$.

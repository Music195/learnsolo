Action and Reaction

Forces come in pairs: if A pushes B, B pushes A back

Stand on a skateboard and push a wall: you roll backwards. You pushed the wall, and at the same moment the wall pushed you. Forces always come in pairs like this. This is the third law of motion, the law of action and reaction:

When A exerts a force on B, B exerts a force on A at the same time, along the same line, equal in size and opposite in direction.

One of the pair is called the action and the other the reaction, but it doesn't matter which is which: they start and stop together. To find a reaction, swap the subject and the object: "A pushes B" becomes "B pushes A".

Action and reaction

$$\vec{F}_{\text{A on B}} = -\vec{F}_{\text{B on A}}$$

Same size, opposite directions, same line, same moment. They act on different objects: one on B, one on A. So they never cancel each other out.

  1. Every force is "something on something": the hand on the wall, the Earth on the apple.
  2. The third law says each one has a partner with the words swapped: the wall on the hand, the apple on the Earth.
  3. The two forces are equal, but they act on different objects, and each object reacts according to its own mass: $a = F/m$.
  4. That's why the pair can look very unequal. The Earth pulls an apple down with $1\ \mathrm{N}$ and the apple pulls the Earth up with $1\ \mathrm{N}$, but the Earth's mass is so huge that its acceleration is far too small to notice.

Example 1

You push a wall with $30\ \mathrm{N}$. What is the reaction, and how big is it?

Show solution
  1. Action: "you push the wall". Swap: "the wall pushes you".

The wall pushes you back with $30\ \mathrm{N}$, in the opposite direction

Example 2

The Earth ($6.0 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}$) pulls a $0.10\ \mathrm{kg}$ apple down with $0.98\ \mathrm{N}$. Find the force of the apple on the Earth, and the Earth's acceleration because of it.

Show solution
  1. Swap: the apple pulls the Earth with $0.98\ \mathrm{N}$, upwards (towards the apple).
  2. Earth: $a = \dfrac{0.98}{6.0 \times 10^{24}} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-25}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$0.98\ \mathrm{N}$; about $1.6 \times 10^{-25}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$: far too small to notice

Example 3

A swimmer pushes the water backwards with their hands. What makes them move forwards?

Show solution
  1. Action: the hands push the water backwards.
  2. Reaction: the water pushes the hands forwards. This force acts on the swimmer.

The water's push on their hands (the reaction)

Example 4

"A horse pulls a cart, and the cart pulls the horse back with an equal force. So the forces cancel and they can't move." What is wrong?

Show solution
  1. The two forces act on different objects: one on the cart, one on the horse. Forces on different objects never cancel.
  2. For the cart: the horse's pull is bigger than friction, so it accelerates. For the horse: the ground's friction on its hooves (forwards) is bigger than the cart's pull back.

Action and reaction act on different objects, so they can't cancel

Action and reaction act on different objects, so they never cancel and never appear in the same force diagram. When you draw the forces on one object (topic 02), at most one force of each pair appears.
Name the force as a sentence, "who pushes/pulls whom", then swap the two names. "Earth pulls apple" → "apple pulls Earth". This finds the reaction every time, even when it is hard to picture.
  • If A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A, along the same line, at the same time.
  • Find the reaction by swapping subject and object.
  • The pair acts on different objects: they never cancel, but their effects depend on each object's mass.

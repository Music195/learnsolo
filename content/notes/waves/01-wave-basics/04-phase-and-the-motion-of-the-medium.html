Phase and the Motion of the Medium

Every piece of the medium does simple harmonic motion; its phase says which stage it is at

Look closely at one piece of the medium: it goes up and down exactly like a mass on a spring. It is doing simple harmonic motion (SHM), with the wave's amplitude $A$ and period $T$.

From mechanics, SHM is the shadow of a point going round a circle at steady speed (the reference circle). The angle of that point on its circle is called the phase. It tells you which stage of the vibration the piece is at: phase $90^\circ$ means "at the top", $270^\circ$ means "at the bottom", and so on. Phase is not the same as displacement: two pieces at the same height can have different phases (one going up, one coming down).

  • Pieces further along the wave lag behind: a distance $\lambda$ along the wave is a whole turn ($360^\circ$) of lag. So two pieces a distance $d$ apart differ in phase by $360^\circ \times d/\lambda$.
  • In phase (same phase): always doing the same thing; $d = \lambda, 2\lambda, \ldots$
  • Antiphase (opposite phase, $180^\circ$ apart): always doing the opposite; $d = \tfrac12\lambda, \tfrac32\lambda, \ldots$

Because each piece does SHM, its speed is greatest ($A\omega$) as it passes the rest line, and zero at a crest or trough.

Phase difference and the speed of the medium

$$\Delta\phi = 2\pi\frac{d}{\lambda}\ \left(= 360^\circ \times \frac{d}{\lambda}\right) \qquad v_{\text{medium, max}} = A\omega = \frac{2\pi A}{T}$$

$d$: distance between two pieces along the wave. $\omega = 2\pi/T = 2\pi f$: angular frequency. $v_{\text{medium, max}}$ is the top speed of a piece of the medium, a different thing from the wave speed $v = f\lambda$.

  1. A piece's displacement is $y = A\sin\phi$, where $\phi$ is the angle of its point on the reference circle. The point turns at $\omega = 2\pi/T$.
  2. The wave needs time $d/v$ to reach a piece a distance $d$ further on, so that piece lags by an angle $\omega\,\dfrac dv = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}\cdot\dfrac{d}{\lambda/T} = 2\pi\dfrac d\lambda$.
  3. The point on the circle moves at speed $A\omega$. The piece's velocity is the vertical part of that, which is largest when the point is at the side of the circle, i.e. when $y = 0$: $v_{\max} = A\omega$.

Pieces P and Q on a wave moving right ($\lambda = 4.0$ cm, $T = 2.0$ s)

Example 1

A wave has amplitude 2.0 cm and period 2.0 s. Find the greatest speed of a piece of the medium.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = 2\pi/T = \pi$ rad/s.
  2. $v_{\max} = A\omega = 2.0 \times \pi$.

$2\pi \approx 6.3\ \mathrm{cm/s}$ (in cm/s because $A$ is in cm)

Example 2

A wave has wavelength 4.0 m. Find the phase difference between two pieces 1.0 m apart along it.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta\phi = 360^\circ \times \dfrac{1.0}{4.0}$.

$90^\circ$ ($\pi/2$ rad)

Example 3

A wave with $\lambda = 0.80$ m travels along a rope. Which pieces move in antiphase with the piece at $x = 0$? Give the first two positions on the positive side.

Show solution
  1. Antiphase means $d = \tfrac12\lambda, \tfrac32\lambda, \ldots$

$x = 0.40$ m and $x = 1.2$ m

Example 4

A wave has $A = 0.050$ m, $f = 10$ Hz and $\lambda = 2.0$ m. Compare the wave speed with the greatest speed of the medium.

Show solution
  1. Wave speed: $v = f\lambda = 20$ m/s.
  2. Medium: $A\omega = 0.050 \times 2\pi \times 10 \approx 3.1$ m/s.

Wave 20 m/s; medium at most about 3.1 m/s. Different things!

Example 5

On a y–x graph, where are the pieces that are moving fastest, and where are the ones at rest?

Show solution
  1. In SHM the speed is greatest at the centre of the motion and zero at the ends.

Fastest where the curve crosses $y = 0$; momentarily at rest at crests and troughs

"Same displacement" does not mean "same phase". A piece going up through $y = 1$ cm and a piece coming down through $y = 1$ cm have different phases. In phase means doing the same thing at every moment.
Think in fractions of a wavelength: $\tfrac14\lambda$ apart → $90^\circ$; $\tfrac12\lambda$ → $180^\circ$ (antiphase); $\lambda$ → $360^\circ$ (in phase again).
  • Each piece of the medium does SHM; its phase is the angle on the reference circle.
  • Phase difference $= 360^\circ \times d/\lambda$: in phase at whole wavelengths, antiphase at half-odd wavelengths.
  • Top speed of the medium $A\omega = 2\pi A/T$, at $y = 0$; zero at crests and troughs.

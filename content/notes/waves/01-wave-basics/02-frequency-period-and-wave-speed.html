Frequency, Period and Wave Speed

In one period, the wave moves forward exactly one wavelength

  • Frequency $f$: how many times a piece of the medium vibrates per second. Unit: hertz (Hz) = per second. "100 Hz" means 100 vibrations every second.
  • Period $T$: the time for one vibration, in seconds. "$T = 0.2$ s" means one vibration takes 0.2 s.
  • Wavelength $\lambda$: the length of one complete wave, for example crest to next crest (in m).

Frequency is "vibrations per second", period is "seconds per vibration": one is just the other turned upside down, $f = 1/T$.

Now follow the first piece of the medium through one full vibration (one period $T$). While it does that, its "copying" has been passed down the line, and the wave front has moved on by exactly one whole wave shape, one wavelength $\lambda$. Speed is distance ÷ time, so $v = \lambda/T$, which is the same as $v = f\lambda$.

The basic wave formula

$$f = \frac1T \qquad v = \frac{\lambda}{T} = f\lambda$$

$v$: wave speed (m/s). $f$: frequency (Hz). $T$: period (s). $\lambda$: wavelength (m).

  1. The first piece of the medium starts at rest, goes up, down and back to rest: one full vibration, taking time $T$.
  2. During that time the start of the vibration has been passed along the line. When the first piece finishes, the front of the wave is one complete wave shape ahead of it.
  3. So in time $T$ the wave travels a distance $\lambda$: $v = \dfrac{\lambda}{T}$.
  4. Since $\dfrac1T = f$, this is $v = f\lambda$.

Example 1

A wave has frequency 100 Hz. Find its period.

Show solution
  1. $T = 1/f = 1/100$.

$T = 0.010\ \mathrm{s}$

Example 2

Each piece of a medium takes 0.20 s for one vibration. Find the frequency.

Show solution
  1. $f = 1/T = 1/0.20$.

$f = 5.0\ \mathrm{Hz}$

Example 3

A wave has frequency 5.0 Hz and wavelength 0.40 m. Find its speed.

Show solution
  1. $v = f\lambda = 5.0 \times 0.40$.

$v = 2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$

Example 4

Sound travels at 340 m/s in air. Find the wavelength of a 680 Hz sound.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda = v/f = 340/680$.

$\lambda = 0.50\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 5

A wave travels along a rope at a fixed speed. The person shaking the rope doubles the frequency. What happens to the wavelength?

Show solution
  1. The speed depends on the rope (the medium), not on how fast you shake it, so $v$ stays the same.
  2. $\lambda = v/f$: doubling $f$ halves $\lambda$.

The wavelength halves

$v = f\lambda$ does not mean that a higher frequency makes a faster wave. The speed is set by the medium; changing $f$ changes $\lambda$ instead.
If you forget $v = f\lambda$, start from "speed = distance ÷ time": the wave moves one wavelength in one period, so $v = \lambda/T$.
  • $f$ = vibrations per second; $T$ = seconds per vibration; $f = 1/T$.
  • In one period the wave moves one wavelength: $v = \lambda/T = f\lambda$.
  • The medium sets $v$; for a given medium, higher $f$ means shorter $\lambda$.

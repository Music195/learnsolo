What Is a Wave?

A wave passes on a vibration; the material itself stays where it is

Watch a wave on a rope or on water and it looks as if "stuff" is moving along. It isn't. A wave is a vibration being passed on from one part of a material to the next.

  • The material that carries the wave (the rope, the water, the air) is the medium.
  • Each small piece of the medium only moves up and down (or back and forth) around its own resting place. It copies its neighbour a moment later, and so the shape moves along.
  • What travels is the pattern and the energy, not the material.

A crowd doing a "stadium wave" is a good picture: each person only stands up and sits down when the person next to them does, yet the wave races round the stadium. A leaf floating on rippling water bobs up and down in the same spot; it is not carried along by the ripples.

To describe a wave we use two different quantities. The position $x$ says which piece of the medium we mean (how far from the origin it sits). The displacement $y$ says how far that piece has moved from its resting place right now.

Describing a wave

$$y = y(x, t) \qquad \text{amplitude } A = |y|_{\max}$$

$x$: position of a piece of the medium (its resting place). $y$: its displacement at time $t$. Crest: highest point; trough: lowest point. Amplitude $A$: the largest displacement (from the rest line to a crest, not crest to trough).

  1. Line up pieces of the medium 1, 2, 3, … The first one starts to move up and down.
  2. Each piece copies the one before it, a little later. So piece 2 does what piece 1 did a moment ago, piece 3 does what piece 2 did, and so on.
  3. At any instant, the pieces are at different stages of the same motion. Join them up and you see a wave shape.
  4. A moment later, every piece has moved on one step, so the whole shape appears shifted forward. Nothing has moved sideways; only the stage of the motion has been handed on.

Example 1

A leaf floats on a pond. Ripples pass under it, travelling to the right. What does the leaf do?

Show solution
  1. The leaf moves with the water at that spot, and the water there only moves up and down.

It bobs up and down in the same place; it is not carried to the right

Example 2

A crest on a rope travels 6.0 m in 3.0 s. Find the wave speed. How far does a knot tied in the rope move along the rope?

Show solution
  1. Wave speed = distance ÷ time = $6.0/3.0$.
  2. The knot is part of the medium: it only moves up and down.

$2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$; the knot moves 0 m along the rope

Example 3

At some instant, the piece of rope at $x = 2.0$ m has displacement $y = -0.10$ m. What do the two numbers mean?

Show solution
  1. $x = 2.0$ m: we are looking at the piece whose resting place is 2.0 m from the origin.
  2. $y = -0.10$ m: right now it is 0.10 m below its resting place.

$x$ names the piece; $y$ is how far it has moved from rest (here 0.10 m down)

Example 4

The crests of a wave are at $y = +3.0$ cm and the troughs at $y = -3.0$ cm. Find the amplitude.

Show solution
  1. The amplitude is measured from the rest line ($y = 0$) to a crest.

$A = 3.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ (not 6.0 cm)

Example 5

In a stadium wave, each spectator stands up and sits down once, taking 1 second. Does any spectator travel round the stadium? What does?

Show solution
  1. Each person stays in their seat; they are the medium.
  2. The stage "standing up" is handed from person to person.

No spectator travels; the pattern (and its energy) travels round

The speed of the wave (how fast the shape moves along) and the speed of a piece of the medium (how fast it moves up and down) are completely different things. Don't mix them up.
In mechanics, $x$ was often a displacement. For waves, $x$ is a label for a piece of the medium (where it rests), and the displacement is $y$.
  • A wave is a vibration passed on through a medium; the medium itself stays in place.
  • $x$: which piece of the medium; $y$: its displacement from rest.
  • Amplitude = rest line to crest.

The Equation of a Wave

Build it in two steps: the vibration at the origin, then a time delay

The equation of a wave gives the displacement $y$ of the piece at position $x$ at time $t$: a function of two variables, which is why it feels hard. Don't memorise it; build it.

  • Step 1: the origin. Write the vibration of the piece at $x = 0$, i.e. turn its y–t graph into a formula. If it starts at 0 and goes up: $y = A\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{T}t$. If it starts at 0 and goes down: $y = -A\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{T}t$. If it starts at the top: $y = A\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{T}t$. Check the graph; it isn't always "sin".
  • Step 2: the delay. The wave needs time $x/v$ to travel from the origin to position $x$. So the piece at $x$ does now what the origin did $x/v$ seconds ago: replace $t$ by $t - \dfrac xv$.

With $v = \lambda/T$, the delay $\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\cdot\dfrac{x}{v}$ becomes $2\pi\dfrac{x}{\lambda}$, giving the textbook form. For a wave moving in the $-x$ direction, the piece at positive $x$ is ahead of the origin instead, so the minus sign becomes a plus.

A wave moving in the $+x$ direction (origin starts at 0, going up)

$$y = A\sin\frac{2\pi}{T}\left(t - \frac xv\right) = A\sin 2\pi\left(\frac tT - \frac x\lambda\right)$$

Moving in the $-x$ direction: $y = A\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac tT + \dfrac x\lambda\right)$. If the origin starts differently, change $\sin$ to $-\sin$ or $\cos$ to match its y–t graph.

  1. Origin (from its y–t graph): $y(0, t) = A\sin\omega t$ with $\omega = 2\pi/T$. This is just the SHM equation.
  2. The vibration reaches $x$ after a delay $x/v$, so $y(x, t) = y(0,\ t - x/v)$.
  3. Substitute: $y = A\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\left(t - \dfrac xv\right) = A\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac tT - \dfrac{x}{vT}\right)$.
  4. $vT = \lambda$, so $y = A\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac tT - \dfrac x\lambda\right)$.

$A = 0.10$ m, $T = 2.0$ s, $\lambda = 4.0$ m: pick a piece and watch the formula

Example 1

A wave moves in the $+x$ direction with $A = 0.10$ m, $T = 2.0$ s and $\lambda = 4.0$ m. The piece at the origin starts at $y = 0$ moving up at $t = 0$. Write the equation of the wave.

Show solution
  1. Origin: $y = 0.10\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{2.0}t$.
  2. Delay for $+x$: $t \to t - x/v$, giving $2\pi(t/T - x/\lambda)$.

$y = 0.10\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{2.0} - \dfrac{x}{4.0}\right)$

Example 2

Using Example 1, find the displacement of the piece at $x = 1.0$ m at $t = 1.0$ s.

Show solution
  1. $2\pi\left(\dfrac{1.0}{2.0} - \dfrac{1.0}{4.0}\right) = 2\pi \times 0.25 = \dfrac\pi2$.
  2. $y = 0.10\sin\dfrac\pi2$.

$y = 0.10\ \mathrm{m}$ (at a crest)

Example 3

A wave is $y = 0.20\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{0.50} - \dfrac{x}{2.0}\right)$ in SI units. Find its amplitude, period, wavelength, speed and direction.

Show solution
  1. Compare with $A\sin 2\pi(t/T - x/\lambda)$: $A = 0.20$ m, $T = 0.50$ s, $\lambda = 2.0$ m.
  2. $v = \lambda/T = 4.0$ m/s; the minus sign means the $+x$ direction.

$A = 0.20$ m, $T = 0.50$ s, $\lambda = 2.0$ m, $v = 4.0$ m/s towards $+x$

Example 4

The y–t graph of the origin starts at 0 and goes down first. The wave moves in the $+x$ direction. Write its equation.

Show solution
  1. Origin: $y = -A\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{T}t$.
  2. Add the delay as before.

$y = -A\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac tT - \dfrac x\lambda\right)$

Example 5

The same vibration as in Example 1 starts at the origin, but the wave travels in the $-x$ direction. Write its equation.

Show solution
  1. Now the piece at $x > 0$ is downstream in the other sense: the wave reaches positive $x$ from the right, so the piece at $x$ is $x/v$ ahead of the origin: $t \to t + x/v$.

$y = 0.10\sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{2.0} + \dfrac{x}{4.0}\right)$

Step 1 needs the y–t graph of the origin. If you are given a y–x graph (a photo), first work out which way the origin moves next (shift the wave slightly), then write its y–t formula. Turning the y–x shape straight into a formula is the classic error.
Check your answer: put $x = 0$ and you must get the origin's motion; put $t = 0$ and you must get the starting shape of the wave.
  • Step 1: the origin's vibration from its y–t graph ($\sin$, $-\sin$ or $\cos$).
  • Step 2: replace $t$ by $t - x/v$ (wave towards $+x$) or $t + x/v$ (towards $-x$).
  • Result: $y = A\sin 2\pi(t/T \mp x/\lambda)$.

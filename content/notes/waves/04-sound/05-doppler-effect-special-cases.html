Doppler Effect: Special Cases

Wind, moving reflectors and sideways motion all reduce to the same formula

The basic formula $f' = \dfrac{V - v_o}{V - v_s}f$ (positive from source to observer) handles three trickier situations once you think about what each one changes.

  • Wind: the wind carries the sound, so relative to the ground the sound moves at $V + w$ (wind blowing from source to observer, $w > 0$) or $V - w$ (blowing the other way). Replace $V$ by $V \pm w$ everywhere. If neither source nor observer moves, the wind changes nothing: $f' = f$.
  • A moving reflector (a wall, a car, a ball): do it in two steps. First the reflector is an observer and receives $f_1$. Then it is a source sending $f_1$ back. If you also hear the original sound, the two frequencies make beats.
  • Moving at an angle: only the part of the velocity along the line joining source and observer matters. Use $v\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the velocity and that line.

Three variants (positive direction: source → observer)

$$\text{wind: } f' = \frac{V + w - v_o}{V + w - v_s}f \qquad \text{at an angle: } v \to v\cos\theta$$
$$\text{reflector approaching at } u:\ f_1 = \frac{V + u}{V}f,\quad f_2 = \frac{V}{V - u}f_1 = \frac{V + u}{V - u}f$$

$w$: wind speed, positive if it blows from source to observer. $\theta$: angle between a velocity and the source–observer line. $u$: speed of the reflector towards the source; $f_2$ is what comes back to the source.

  1. Step 1: source at rest, wall = observer moving towards it. Positive: source → wall, so $v_o = -u$: $f_1 = \dfrac{V + u}{V}f$.
  2. Step 2: wall = source of frequency $f_1$, moving towards the listener next to the original source. Positive: wall → listener, so $v_s = +u$: $f_2 = \dfrac{V}{V - u}f_1$.
  3. Combine: $f_2 = \dfrac{V + u}{V - u}f$.
  4. The listener hears $f$ directly and $f_2$ by reflection: beats at $f_2 - f$.

Example 1

A 500 Hz source is at rest. A wall moves towards it at 10 m/s. $V = 340$ m/s. Find the frequency the wall receives, the frequency reflected back to the source, and the beat frequency heard at the source.

Show solution
  1. $f_1 = \dfrac{340 + 10}{340} \times 500 \approx 514.7$ Hz.
  2. $f_2 = \dfrac{340}{340 - 10} \times f_1 = \dfrac{350}{330} \times 500 \approx 530.3$ Hz.
  3. Beats: $530 - 500$.

About 515 Hz, 530 Hz, and 30 beats per second

Example 2

A wind of 10 m/s blows from a 500 Hz source towards an observer. The source moves towards the observer at 30 m/s. $V = 340$ m/s in still air. Find the frequency heard.

Show solution
  1. The sound moves at $340 + 10 = 350$ m/s towards the observer.
  2. $f' = \dfrac{350 - 0}{350 - 30} \times 500 = \dfrac{350}{320} \times 500$.

$f' \approx 547$ Hz

Example 3

A wind blows steadily from a stationary 500 Hz source to a stationary observer. What frequency does the observer hear?

Show solution
  1. $v_o = v_s = 0$: $f' = \dfrac{V + w}{V + w}f = f$.
  2. The wind stretches the wavelength and speeds the sound up by the same factor.

500 Hz, unchanged

Example 4

A 680 Hz source moves at 40 m/s in a direction that makes 60° with the line from it to a stationary observer (it is heading roughly towards them). $V = 340$ m/s. Find the frequency heard at that instant.

Show solution
  1. Component towards the observer: $40\cos60° = 20$ m/s.
  2. $f' = \dfrac{340}{340 - 20} \times 680$.

$f' \approx 723$ Hz

Example 5

A car passes a person at the roadside at constant speed, sounding its horn. At the instant the car is closest (moving at right angles to the line joining them), what frequency does the person hear?

Show solution
  1. $\theta = 90°$, so the component along the line is $v\cos90° = 0$.

The true frequency $f$ (for sound emitted at that instant): the pitch is passing from high to low

For a moving reflector, never try to write one formula straight away. Do it in two steps (reflector as observer, then as source), and reset the positive direction for each step.
Police speed guns and medical ultrasound use exactly this idea: they measure the beat frequency between the sent and reflected waves to find a speed.
  • Wind: replace $V$ by $V \pm w$.
  • Moving reflector: observer first, then source; beats at $|f_2 - f|$.
  • At an angle: use the component $v\cos\theta$ along the source–observer line.

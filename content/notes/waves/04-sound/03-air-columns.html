Air Columns

An open end is an antinode, a closed end is a node

Blow across a bottle or into a flute and the air inside the tube (the air column) vibrates as a standing sound wave. Which notes it can play depends on the ends.

  • Closed end: the air can't move into the wall, so it is a node (like a fixed end of a string).
  • Open end: the air is free to move in and out, so it is an antinode (like a free end).

We draw the standing wave as the displacement of the air along the tube (the transverse picture of a longitudinal wave), so these shapes are just "loops" like on a string.

  • Open pipe (both ends open): antinode at each end. The length holds whole numbers of half-wavelengths, $L = n\lambda/2$. All harmonics are possible.
  • Closed pipe (one end closed): node at the closed end, antinode at the open end. Node to antinode is $\lambda/4$, so $L = \lambda/4, 3\lambda/4, 5\lambda/4, \dots$. Only odd harmonics.

(Real antinodes sit a little beyond an open end; this "end correction" is ignored here.)

Natural frequencies of air columns ($V$: speed of sound)

$$\text{open: } f_n = \frac{nV}{2L}\ (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots) \qquad \text{closed: } f_m = \frac{(2m-1)V}{4L}\ (m = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$

$L$: length of the air column. Open pipe fundamental $V/(2L)$; closed pipe fundamental $V/(4L)$, an octave lower than an open pipe of the same length.

  1. Open pipe: antinode to antinode is $\lambda/2$, and any number of loops fits: $L = n\lambda/2$, so $\lambda = 2L/n$ and $f = V/\lambda = nV/(2L)$.
  2. Closed pipe: from the node at the closed end to the antinode at the open end there must be an odd number of quarter-waves: $L = (2m - 1)\lambda/4$.
  3. So $\lambda = 4L/(2m-1)$ and $f = (2m - 1)V/(4L)$: 1, 3, 5, … times the fundamental.
  4. In a resonance tube experiment (water sets the length of a closed column), neighbouring resonance lengths differ by $\lambda/2$.

A pipe of length 0.85 m, V = 340 m/s (air displacement shown as loops)

Example 1

An open pipe is 0.85 m long. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find its fundamental frequency and its 2nd harmonic.

Show solution
  1. $f_1 = \dfrac{V}{2L} = \dfrac{340}{1.70}$.
  2. $f_2 = 2f_1$.

200 Hz and 400 Hz

Example 2

The same pipe is closed at one end. Find its fundamental frequency and the next frequency it can sound.

Show solution
  1. $f_1 = \dfrac{V}{4L} = \dfrac{340}{3.40} = 100$ Hz.
  2. Only odd multiples: the next is $3 \times 100$.

100 Hz, then 300 Hz (no 200 Hz)

Example 3

In a resonance tube experiment with a tuning fork, loud sounds are heard when the air column is 17 cm and then 51 cm long. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find the frequency of the fork.

Show solution
  1. Neighbouring resonances differ by $\lambda/2$: $51 - 17 = 34$ cm, so $\lambda = 68$ cm.
  2. $f = V/\lambda = 340/0.68$.

500 Hz (and 17 cm $= \lambda/4$, so here there is no end correction)

Example 4

A pipe resonates at 300 Hz, 500 Hz and 700 Hz, and at no frequencies in between. Is it open or closed? Find its length ($V = 340$ m/s).

Show solution
  1. Neighbouring frequencies differ by 200 Hz. For an open pipe that difference would be $f_1$, but 300 is not a multiple of 200.
  2. For a closed pipe the gap is $2f_1$, so $f_1 = 100$ Hz, and 300, 500, 700 are the 3rd, 5th, 7th harmonics. Closed.
  3. $L = V/(4f_1) = 340/400$.

Closed pipe, $L = 0.85$ m

Example 5

A flute (an open pipe) is in tune in a cold room. The room warms up. Does its note go up or down?

Show solution
  1. Warmer air: larger $V = 331.5 + 0.6t$.
  2. The length (and so $\lambda$) is fixed, so $f = V/\lambda$ goes up.

Up (wind instruments go sharp as they warm)

A closed pipe has only odd harmonics. Writing $f = nV/(4L)$ with $n = 2$ is a common slip: there is no 2nd harmonic.
String: fixed–fixed (node–node). Open pipe: antinode–antinode. Both give $L = n\lambda/2$. Closed pipe: node–antinode, $L = (2m - 1)\lambda/4$.
  • Closed end → node; open end → antinode.
  • Open pipe: $f_n = nV/(2L)$, all harmonics.
  • Closed pipe: $f = (2m-1)V/(4L)$, odd harmonics only.

The Doppler Effect

Moving towards each other: higher pitch; moving apart: lower pitch

An ambulance siren sounds higher as it comes towards you and drops suddenly as it passes. The siren itself never changes; what you hear changes because of the motion. This is the Doppler effect.

  • Moving source: the source chases the waves it has just sent forward, so the crests ahead of it are squeezed together (shorter wavelength) and those behind it are spread out. The speed of sound itself doesn't change.
  • Moving observer: the wavelength is unchanged, but an observer running into the waves meets crests more often, as if the waves came past faster.

One formula covers every case, as long as you use signs carefully. Take the positive direction from the source towards the observer (the direction the sound travels to reach you). Each velocity is positive if it points that way and negative otherwise.

Doppler formula (positive direction: source → observer)

$$f' = \frac{V - v_o}{V - v_s}\,f$$

$f$: frequency the source emits. $f'$: frequency heard. $V$: speed of sound. $v_o$: observer's velocity; $v_s$: source's velocity, both with signs. Memory aid: observer on top, source below.

  1. The source emits $f$ crests per second. In one second the first crest moves a distance $V$ forward while the source moves $v_s$, so those $f$ crests fill a length $V - v_s$.
  2. The wavelength in front of the source is $\lambda' = \dfrac{V - v_s}{f}$.
  3. Relative to the observer, the waves come past at speed $V - v_o$ (the observer moving with the waves makes them pass more slowly).
  4. Crests reaching the observer per second: $f' = \dfrac{V - v_o}{\lambda'} = \dfrac{V - v_o}{V - v_s}f$.

V = 340 m/s, f = 400 Hz, speeds 102 m/s (0.3 V, exaggerated to see it)

Example 1

A source of 340 Hz and an observer move towards each other: the source at 10 m/s, the observer at 6.0 m/s. The speed of sound is 330 m/s. Find the frequency heard.

Show solution
  1. Positive: from the source towards the observer. The source moves that way: $v_s = +10$. The observer moves the opposite way: $v_o = -6.0$.
  2. $f' = \dfrac{330 - (-6.0)}{330 - 10} \times 340 = \dfrac{336}{320} \times 340$.

$f' = 357$ Hz

Example 2

Now they move apart: the source at 10 m/s, the observer at 5.0 m/s, each moving away from the other. $f = 340$ Hz, $V = 330$ m/s. Find the frequency heard.

Show solution
  1. Positive: source → observer. The observer moves away from the source, i.e. in the positive direction: $v_o = +5.0$. The source moves away from the observer: $v_s = -10$.
  2. $f' = \dfrac{330 - 5.0}{330 - (-10)} \times 340 = \dfrac{325}{340} \times 340$.

$f' = 325$ Hz

Example 3

An ambulance siren of 680 Hz moves at 20 m/s past a person standing still. $V = 340$ m/s. Find the frequency heard while it approaches and after it has passed.

Show solution
  1. Approaching: $v_s = +20$, $f' = \dfrac{340}{320} \times 680 \approx 723$ Hz.
  2. Moving away: $v_s = -20$, $f' = \dfrac{340}{360} \times 680 \approx 642$ Hz.

About 723 Hz, then about 642 Hz

Example 4

For the ambulance of Example 3, find the wavelength of the sound in front of it.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda' = \dfrac{V - v_s}{f} = \dfrac{340 - 20}{680}$.

$\lambda' \approx 0.47$ m (instead of 0.50 m)

Example 5

A cyclist rides at 34 m/s towards a stationary 500 Hz source (a rather fast cyclist!). $V = 340$ m/s. What frequency do they hear?

Show solution
  1. The observer moves towards the source, against the positive direction: $v_o = -34$; $v_s = 0$.
  2. $f' = \dfrac{340 + 34}{340} \times 500$.

$f' = 550$ Hz

Fix the positive direction as source → observer every time, and give each velocity its sign. Don't memorise "plus when approaching"; just substitute with signs into one formula.
Sanity check: approaching each other must give $f' > f$, moving apart $f' < f$.
  • $f' = \dfrac{V - v_o}{V - v_s}f$, positive direction from source to observer.
  • A moving source changes the wavelength; a moving observer changes how fast crests reach them.
  • Approaching → higher; separating → lower.

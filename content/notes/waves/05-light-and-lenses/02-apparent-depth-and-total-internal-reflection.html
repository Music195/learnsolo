Apparent Depth and Total Internal Reflection

Water looks shallower than it is, and light trying to leave at a steep slant can't get out

Light from a fish in a pond bends away from the normal as it leaves the water (it speeds up). Your brain assumes light travels in straight lines, so you trace the rays straight back and "see" the fish higher than it really is. Seen from straight above, an object at real depth $h$ appears at the apparent depth $h' = h/n$. A pool 2.0 m deep looks only 1.5 m deep, which is why it is dangerous to judge by eye.

Now send light from inside water towards the surface at a larger and larger angle. The refracted ray leaves at an even larger angle, bending more and more towards the surface. At one particular angle of incidence, the critical angle $\theta_c$, the refracted ray just skims along the surface (90°). Beyond it no light can leave at all: it is all reflected back into the water. This is total internal reflection.

  • It only happens going from a slower medium (larger $n$) into a faster one (smaller $n$).
  • A lamp at the bottom of a pool lights up only a circle of the surface. Outside that circle, its light is totally reflected.
  • Optical fibres trap light by total internal reflection at the walls of a thin glass thread, carrying signals for kilometres with little loss.

Apparent depth (viewed from straight above) and critical angle (into air)

$$h' = \frac hn \qquad \sin\theta_c = \frac1n \qquad R = \frac{h}{\sqrt{n^2 - 1}}$$

$h$: real depth; $h'$: apparent depth; $n$: refractive index of the water or glass (water $n = 4/3$, glass about 1.5). $\theta_c$: critical angle. $R$: radius of the lit circle on the surface above a point source at depth $h$.

  1. A ray from the object at depth $h$ meets the surface a distance $x$ from the point straight above it, with $\tan\theta_1 = x/h$ in the water. It leaves at $\theta_2$, and traced back it seems to come from depth $h'$ with $\tan\theta_2 = x/h'$.
  2. Near the vertical, $\tan \approx \sin$, so $\dfrac{h}{h'} = \dfrac{\tan\theta_2}{\tan\theta_1} \approx \dfrac{\sin\theta_2}{\sin\theta_1} = n$, giving $h' = h/n$.
  3. Critical angle: $n\sin\theta_c = 1 \times \sin90°$, so $\sin\theta_c = 1/n$.
  4. Lit circle: $R = h\tan\theta_c$. With $\sin\theta_c = 1/n$, $\cos\theta_c = \sqrt{n^2-1}/n$, so $\tan\theta_c = 1/\sqrt{n^2 - 1}$.

Light leaving water (n = 4/3) or glass (n = 1.5)

Example 1

A pool is 2.0 m deep. How deep does it look from directly above? (Water: $n = 4/3$.)

Show solution
  1. $h' = h/n = 2.0 \div \dfrac43 = 2.0 \times \dfrac34$.

1.5 m

Example 2

Find the critical angle for light going from water ($n = 4/3$) into air, and from glass ($n = 1.5$) into air.

Show solution
  1. Water: $\sin\theta_c = 3/4 = 0.75$, so $\theta_c \approx 48.6°$.
  2. Glass: $\sin\theta_c = 1/1.5 = 0.667$, so $\theta_c \approx 41.8°$.

About 48.6° and 41.8°

Example 3

A small lamp is 0.70 m below the surface of water ($n = 4/3$). Find the radius of the circle of surface through which its light escapes.

Show solution
  1. $n^2 - 1 = \dfrac{16}{9} - 1 = \dfrac79$, so $\sqrt{n^2 - 1} \approx 0.882$.
  2. $R = 0.70/0.882$.

$R \approx 0.79$ m

Example 4

A coin lies under a glass block 6.0 cm thick ($n = 1.5$). How far below the top of the block does it appear, looking straight down?

Show solution
  1. $h' = 6.0/1.5$.

4.0 cm (it seems raised by 2.0 cm)

Example 5

Light travels along a glass optical fibre ($n = 1.5$) in air. It hits the wall at 60° to the normal. Does it escape?

Show solution
  1. The critical angle for glass to air is 41.8°.
  2. 60° > 41.8°, so the light is totally reflected.

No: it is totally reflected and stays in the fibre

Total internal reflection is only possible going from the larger $n$ to the smaller $n$ (water → air, glass → air). Light going from air into water always gets in.
$h' = h/n$ only works for viewing from (nearly) straight above. At a slant, the bottom looks even shallower. Try it in the demo.
  • Apparent depth from above: $h' = h/n$.
  • Critical angle into air: $\sin\theta_c = 1/n$; beyond it, total internal reflection.
  • Lit circle above a lamp at depth $h$: $R = h/\sqrt{n^2-1}$.

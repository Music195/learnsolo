Huygens' Principle and Reflection

Every point on a wavefront sends out little circular waves; their common edge is the next wavefront

Waves on water spread out over a surface, so we need a way to describe them in 2D.

  • A wavefront is a line joining points that are at the same stage of vibration, for example along one crest. Straight wavefronts make a plane wave; circles make a circular wave (in 3D: a spherical wave).
  • A ray is an arrow showing the direction the wave travels. Rays are always perpendicular to the wavefronts.

Huygens' principle: every point on a wavefront acts like a tiny source that sends out a circular secondary wavelet, at the same speed and frequency as the wave. A moment later, the line that touches all these wavelets (their envelope, or common tangent) is the new wavefront.

This one idea explains reflection, refraction and diffraction. For reflection: the parts of a wavefront that hit a wall first start their wavelets first, so those wavelets are bigger. The common tangent then leans the other way, and the reflected wave leaves at the same angle as it arrived.

Law of reflection

$$i = r$$

$i$: angle of incidence, $r$: angle of reflection, both measured between the ray and the normal (the line at right angles to the surface). The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal lie in one plane.

  1. A wavefront AB arrives at angle $i$ to the surface: end A touches the surface first.
  2. While end B travels the remaining distance BB′ to the surface, the wavelet from A grows to radius AA′ $= $ BB′ (same speed, same time).
  3. The reflected wavefront is the tangent from B′ to that wavelet, touching it at A′.
  4. Triangles ABB′ and B′A′A are right-angled (at B and A′), share the side AB′, and have BB′ = AA′. So they are congruent, and the angles at the surface are equal.
  5. The angle between a wavefront and the surface equals the angle between its ray and the normal, so $i = r$.

A plane wavefront meets a mirror: wavelets (green) build the reflected front

Example 1

A ray hits a mirror with angle of incidence 30°. Find the angle of reflection and the angle between the reflected ray and the mirror surface.

Show solution
  1. $r = i = 30°$ (from the normal).
  2. The normal is 90° from the surface: $90° - 30°$.

$r = 30°$; 60° from the surface

Example 2

A ray makes 25° with the surface of a mirror. Find the angle of incidence and the angle between the incident and reflected rays.

Show solution
  1. From the normal: $i = 90° - 25° = 65°$, so $r = 65°$.
  2. The angle between the rays is $i + r = 130°$ (or, measured the other way round, the rays meet the surface at 25° each, $180° - 2 \times 25° = 130°$).

$i = 65°$; 130° between the rays

Example 3

Straight wavefronts on water meet a straight wall at an angle of 40° (angle between the wavefronts and the wall). Find the angle of incidence.

Show solution
  1. Rotating both the wavefront and the wall by 90° gives the ray and the normal, so the angles are equal.

$i = 40°$

Example 4

Water waves travel at 0.30 m/s. How big is a secondary wavelet 0.50 s after it starts?

Show solution
  1. Wavelets travel at the wave speed: radius $= vt = 0.30 \times 0.50$.

Radius 0.15 m

Example 5

Two mirrors meet at a right angle. A ray hits the first at 30° incidence and then reflects off the second. Find the angle between the original ray and the final ray.

Show solution
  1. After the first mirror the ray makes $90° - 30° = 60°$ with the first mirror's surface, so it makes $30°$ with the second (perpendicular) mirror's surface: $i_2 = 60°$.
  2. Each reflection reverses the velocity component along that mirror's normal. Two perpendicular mirrors reverse both components.

180°: the ray goes straight back, parallel to where it came from (a corner reflector)

Angles of incidence and reflection are measured from the normal, not from the surface. If a question gives the angle to the surface, subtract it from 90° first.
Whenever a problem draws wavefronts, sketch one ray at right angles to them. Rays are much easier to work with.
  • Wavefront: points in the same phase; ray: direction of travel, perpendicular to the wavefronts.
  • Huygens: each point on a front sends out wavelets; their envelope is the new front.
  • Reflection: $i = r$, measured from the normal.

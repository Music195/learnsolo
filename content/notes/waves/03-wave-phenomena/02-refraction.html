Refraction

When a wave changes speed at a boundary, it changes direction

Water waves slow down when they move into shallow water; light slows down when it enters glass. When a wave crosses into a medium where its speed is different, its direction bends. This is refraction.

Picture a cart rolling at an angle from a smooth road onto grass. One wheel reaches the grass first and slows down while the other is still fast, so the cart turns. A wave turns the same way: into a slower medium it bends towards the normal, into a faster one it bends away.

  • The frequency does not change. The pieces of the medium at the boundary are shaken by the incoming wave and shake the new medium at the same rate.
  • Since $v = f\lambda$ with the same $f$, the wavelength changes in the same ratio as the speed: slower means shorter wavelength.

The refractive index of medium 2 relative to medium 1 is $n_{12} = v_1/v_2$. Each material also has an absolute refractive index $n = c/v$ (compared with a vacuum), and then $n_{12} = n_2/n_1$.

An everyday example: sound carries further at night. The ground cools, so the air near it is colder than the air higher up, and sound is slower in cold air. Sound heading upwards bends back down towards the slower layer, so it reaches distant listeners.

Law of refraction

$$\frac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2} = \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = n_{12} = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$$

$\theta_1$: angle of incidence, $\theta_2$: angle of refraction, both from the normal. $v$, $\lambda$: speed and wavelength in each medium. $n_1$, $n_2$: absolute refractive indices. The frequency is the same on both sides. Handy form: $n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$.

  1. A wavefront AB meets the boundary: A arrives first. Let B take time $t$ to reach the boundary at B′, so BB′ $= v_1 t$.
  2. In that time the wavelet from A grows in medium 2 to radius AA′ $= v_2 t$. The new front is the tangent from B′ to that wavelet.
  3. In the right-angled triangles with hypotenuse AB′: $\sin\theta_1 = \dfrac{v_1 t}{AB'}$ and $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{v_2 t}{AB'}$.
  4. Divide: $\dfrac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2} = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2}$. With the same $f$, $v_1/v_2 = f\lambda_1/(f\lambda_2) = \lambda_1/\lambda_2$.
  5. With $v = c/n$: $\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{c/n_1}{c/n_2} = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$.

Wavefronts entering medium 2 (below the line)

Example 1

A water wave moving at 2.0 m/s enters shallow water where it moves at 1.0 m/s. The angle of incidence is 30°. Find the angle of refraction.

Show solution
  1. $\sin\theta_2 = \sin\theta_1 \times \dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = 0.50 \times \dfrac{1.0}{2.0} = 0.25$.
  2. $\theta_2 = \sin^{-1}0.25$.

$\theta_2 \approx 14.5°$ (bent towards the normal)

Example 2

The refractive index of medium 2 relative to medium 1 is 1.5. The wavelength in medium 1 is 6.0 cm. Find the wavelength in medium 2.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda_1/\lambda_2 = n_{12}$, so $\lambda_2 = 6.0/1.5$.

$\lambda_2 = 4.0$ cm

Example 3

A 10 Hz water wave with wavelength 4.0 cm moves into shallower water where its speed is 0.30 m/s. Find its new frequency and wavelength.

Show solution
  1. Frequency is unchanged: 10 Hz.
  2. $\lambda_2 = v_2/f = 0.30/10 = 0.030$ m.

10 Hz; 3.0 cm (it was moving at 0.40 m/s before)

Example 4

Light passes from air ($n = 1.0$) into water ($n = 1.33$) at an angle of incidence of 45°. Find the angle of refraction.

Show solution
  1. $1.0 \times \sin45° = 1.33\sin\theta_2$.
  2. $\sin\theta_2 = 0.707/1.33 = 0.532$.

$\theta_2 \approx 32°$

Example 5

Explain why a distant train can be heard more clearly at night than during the day.

Show solution
  1. At night the ground cools, so the air near the ground is colder than the air above it.
  2. Sound is slower in colder air. Sound heading upwards meets faster air and bends away from the normal, back towards the ground.

Sound that would escape upwards is bent back down to the ground, so more of it reaches you

At a boundary, the frequency never changes. Only $v$ and $\lambda$ change, in the same ratio.
Remember: slower = towards the normal. Light entering glass or water from air slows down, so it bends towards the normal.
  • $\dfrac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2} = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = n_{12} = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$.
  • $f$ is unchanged; $v$ and $\lambda$ change together.
  • Into a slower medium the wave bends towards the normal.

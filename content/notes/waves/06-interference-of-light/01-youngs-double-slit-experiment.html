Young's Double-Slit Experiment

Light through two narrow slits makes evenly spaced bright and dark stripes, proof that light is a wave

If light were a stream of tiny particles, two slits would give two bright lines on a screen. In 1805 Thomas Young found a whole row of bright and dark stripes (fringes) instead: the pattern of interference from two sources, just like water waves. This showed that light is a wave.

The set-up:

  • Light of one colour passes a single slit first, so that the light reaching the next screen comes from one point and is coherent (in step).
  • It then reaches two very narrow slits S₁ and S₂, a distance $d$ apart. Diffraction spreads the light from each slit, and since both are lit by the same wave, they act as two sources in phase.
  • On a screen a distance $L$ away (with $L$ much larger than $d$), the two waves overlap and interfere.

Just as for two sources of water waves, a point is bright where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths and dark where it is a whole number plus a half. For a point at distance $x$ from the centre of the screen, the path difference is about $\dfrac{dx}{L}$.

Young's double slit ($m = 0, 1, 2, \dots$)

$$\frac{dx}{L} = m\lambda\ \text{(bright)} \qquad \frac{dx}{L} = \left(m + \tfrac12\right)\lambda\ \text{(dark)} \qquad \Delta x = \frac{L\lambda}{d}$$

$d$: distance between the slits. $L$: distance from the slits to the screen ($L \gg d$, $L \gg x$). $x$: distance from the centre of the screen. $\Delta x$: spacing between neighbouring bright (or dark) fringes. $\lambda$: wavelength.

  1. Put the slits at heights $+\frac d2$ and $-\frac d2$, and P on the screen at height $x$. Then $S_1P^2 = L^2 + \left(x - \frac d2\right)^2$ and $S_2P^2 = L^2 + \left(x + \frac d2\right)^2$.
  2. Subtract: $S_2P^2 - S_1P^2 = 2xd$, so $(S_2P - S_1P)(S_2P + S_1P) = 2xd$.
  3. Since $L$ is huge, $S_2P + S_1P \approx 2L$. So the path difference is $S_2P - S_1P \approx \dfrac{dx}{L}$.
  4. Bright where this is $m\lambda$: $x_m = \dfrac{mL\lambda}{d}$. Neighbouring bright fringes are $\Delta x = x_{m+1} - x_m = \dfrac{L\lambda}{d}$ apart.

Fringes on a screen 1.2 m away (slit spacing drawn much larger than real)

Example 1

Light of wavelength 600 nm falls on two slits 0.30 mm apart. The screen is 1.2 m away. Find the fringe spacing.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta x = \dfrac{L\lambda}{d} = \dfrac{1.2 \times 6.0 \times 10^{-7}}{3.0 \times 10^{-4}}$.

$\Delta x = 2.4 \times 10^{-3}$ m $= 2.4$ mm

Example 2

With slits 0.50 mm apart and a screen 1.0 m away, the bright fringes are 1.2 mm apart. Find the wavelength.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda = \dfrac{d\,\Delta x}{L} = \dfrac{5.0 \times 10^{-4} \times 1.2 \times 10^{-3}}{1.0}$.

$\lambda = 6.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m $= 600$ nm

Example 3

In Example 1, how far from the centre of the screen is the third dark fringe?

Show solution
  1. Dark fringes: $x = \left(m + \frac12\right)\Delta x$ with $m = 0, 1, 2$. The third has $m = 2$.
  2. $x = 2.5 \times 2.4$ mm.

6.0 mm

Example 4

The apparatus of Example 1 is put into water ($n = 4/3$). Find the new fringe spacing.

Show solution
  1. In water the wavelength is $\lambda/n = 600 \times \frac34 = 450$ nm.
  2. $\Delta x \propto \lambda$: $2.4 \times \frac34$.

1.8 mm

Example 5

How does the fringe pattern change if (a) the slits are moved closer together, (b) red light is replaced by blue, (c) white light is used?

Show solution
  1. (a) Smaller $d$: larger $\Delta x$, fringes spread out.
  2. (b) Shorter $\lambda$: fringes closer together.
  3. (c) Every colour has its own spacing. All have a bright centre ($m = 0$), so the centre is white; the side fringes are coloured, with blue on the inside and red on the outside.

(a) wider; (b) closer; (c) a white centre with coloured fringes

$d$ is usually given in mm and $\lambda$ in nm. Convert everything to metres ($1$ mm $= 10^{-3}$ m, $1$ nm $= 10^{-9}$ m) before substituting.
Read $\Delta x = L\lambda/d$ as: a longer distance or longer wavelength spreads the fringes; wider slit spacing squeezes them.
  • Path difference at $x$ on the screen: $dx/L$.
  • Bright: $dx/L = m\lambda$; dark: $(m + \frac12)\lambda$.
  • Fringe spacing $\Delta x = L\lambda/d$, evenly spaced.

Air Wedges and Newton's Rings

A thin gap of air whose thickness changes steadily gives a pattern of fringes

These are thin films made of air between two pieces of glass. Light reflects off the bottom of the upper glass and off the top of the lower glass, and the two reflections interfere.

  • Upper reflection: glass → air (smaller $n$): not inverted.
  • Lower reflection: air → glass (larger $n$): inverted.

That is one inversion, so where the gap has thickness $t$ (and the extra path is $2t$, since $n = 1$ for air): dark when $2t = m\lambda$, bright when $2t = (m + \frac12)\lambda$. Where the glass touches ($t = 0$) it is dark.

Air wedge. Two flat plates touch along one edge and are held apart by a thin spacer (thickness $D$, a distance $L$ from the edge). The gap grows steadily, $t = Dx/L$, so we see evenly spaced straight fringes parallel to the edge. Each next dark fringe is where the gap is $\lambda/2$ thicker.

Newton's rings. A lens with a gently curved face (radius of curvature $R$) rests on flat glass. The gap is zero at the centre and grows like $t \approx r^2/(2R)$ at distance $r$, so the fringes are circles, getting closer together further out. The centre is dark.

Both are used to check how flat or how curved a surface is: any wobble in the fringes shows a bump smaller than a wavelength.

Air wedge and Newton's rings (reflected light, $m = 0, 1, 2, \dots$)

$$\text{wedge: } \Delta x = \frac{\lambda L}{2D} \qquad \text{rings: dark at } r = \sqrt{m\lambda R},\ \text{bright at } r = \sqrt{\left(m + \tfrac12\right)\lambda R}$$

$\Delta x$: spacing of wedge fringes. $L$: distance from the contact edge to the spacer; $D$: spacer thickness. $r$: ring radius; $R$: radius of curvature of the lens. If the gap is filled with a liquid of index $n$, replace $2t$ by $2nt$.

  1. Wedge: dark when $2t = m\lambda$ with $t = Dx/L$, so $x_m = \dfrac{m\lambda L}{2D}$; neighbours are $\Delta x = \dfrac{\lambda L}{2D}$ apart.
  2. Rings: the curved face is part of a circle of radius $R$. At distance $r$ from the contact point, $R^2 = r^2 + (R - t)^2$, so $r^2 = 2Rt - t^2 \approx 2Rt$ (the gap $t$ is tiny), giving $t = \dfrac{r^2}{2R}$.
  3. Dark when $2t = m\lambda$: $\dfrac{r^2}{R} = m\lambda$, so $r = \sqrt{m\lambda R}$.
  4. Bright when $2t = (m + \frac12)\lambda$: $r = \sqrt{(m + \frac12)\lambda R}$.

Example 1

Two glass plates touch at one edge and are separated by a 0.050 mm wire 10 cm away. Light of 600 nm falls straight down. Find the spacing of the fringes.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta x = \dfrac{\lambda L}{2D} = \dfrac{6.0 \times 10^{-7} \times 0.10}{2 \times 5.0 \times 10^{-5}}$.

$\Delta x = 6.0 \times 10^{-4}$ m $= 0.60$ mm

Example 2

In an air wedge lit by 589 nm light, the fringes are 0.50 mm apart and the spacer is 8.0 cm from the contact edge. Find the spacer's thickness.

Show solution
  1. $D = \dfrac{\lambda L}{2\Delta x} = \dfrac{5.89 \times 10^{-7} \times 0.080}{2 \times 5.0 \times 10^{-4}}$.

$D \approx 4.7 \times 10^{-5}$ m (about 0.05 mm)

Example 3

Why is the contact edge of an air wedge (and the centre of Newton's rings) dark in reflected light?

Show solution
  1. There $t = 0$, so there is no extra path.
  2. Only one of the two reflections is inverted, so they arrive half a wavelength out of step and cancel.

One inversion and no path difference: the reflections cancel

Example 4

A lens with $R = 2.0$ m rests on flat glass, lit by 600 nm light. Find the radius of the first dark ring (not counting the dark centre).

Show solution
  1. $r = \sqrt{m\lambda R}$ with $m = 1$: $r = \sqrt{6.0 \times 10^{-7} \times 2.0} = \sqrt{1.2 \times 10^{-6}}$.

$r \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ m $= 1.1$ mm

Example 5

The gap in Example 4 is filled with water ($n = 4/3$). What happens to the radius of that ring?

Show solution
  1. Now dark when $2nt = m\lambda$ (glass → water is still smaller $n$, water → glass larger: still one inversion), so $\dfrac{nr^2}{R} = m\lambda$ and $r = \sqrt{m\lambda R/n}$.
  2. $r$ is multiplied by $\sqrt{3/4} \approx 0.87$: $1.1 \times 0.87$.

About 0.95 mm: the rings shrink

For an air gap between glass, the inversion happens at the lower surface (air → glass), not the upper one. Either way, there is exactly one, so the centre is dark.
A quick way to think about it: each next fringe means the gap is $\lambda/2$ thicker (the light crosses it twice).
  • Air gap between glass: one inversion; dark when $2t = m\lambda$.
  • Wedge: straight, evenly spaced fringes, $\Delta x = \lambda L/(2D)$.
  • Newton's rings: $t = r^2/(2R)$; dark rings at $r = \sqrt{m\lambda R}$, closer together further out.

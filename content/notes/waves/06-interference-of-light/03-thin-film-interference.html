Thin-Film Interference

Light reflected from the top and bottom of a thin film interferes, giving colours

Soap bubbles and oil on a wet road shine with colours. Light reflects from both surfaces of the thin film: some from the top, some from the bottom. The two reflected beams overlap and interfere. Whether a colour is strengthened or cancelled depends on the film's thickness, so different thicknesses show different colours.

Two things decide the result:

  • The extra path. The beam from the bottom goes down and back through the film: an extra $2d$, which is an optical path of $2nd$ (at normal incidence).
  • Phase change on reflection. Light reflecting off a material with a larger refractive index (slower) is turned upside down, a phase change of $\pi$ (half a wavelength), like a wave at a fixed end. Reflecting off a smaller $n$ gives no change, like a free end.

Soap film in air (air → film → air): the top reflection (air→film, larger $n$) is inverted; the bottom one (film→air, smaller $n$) is not. One inversion swaps the usual conditions: bright when $2nd = (m + \frac12)\lambda$.

Anti-reflection coating on glass (air → coating → glass, with $n_{\text{air}} < n_{\text{coating}} < n_{\text{glass}}$): both reflections are inverted, so the inversions cancel. To make the reflections cancel we need $2nd = (m + \frac12)\lambda$; the thinnest such coating has $d = \lambda/(4n)$. Camera lenses and glasses use this.

If light hits the film at a slant (angle of refraction $r$ inside the film), the path difference becomes $2nd\cos r$.

Thin film of thickness $d$, index $n$ ($m = 0, 1, 2, \dots$)

$$\text{one inversion (soap film): } 2nd = \left(m + \tfrac12\right)\lambda \text{ bright}, \quad 2nd = m\lambda \text{ dark}$$

With zero or two inversions (e.g. a coating with $n$ between air and glass), the conditions swap. At a slant, replace $2nd$ by $2nd\cos r$. $\lambda$ is the wavelength in air.

  1. Beam 1 reflects at the top, off a larger $n$: it gains an extra half wavelength (inverted).
  2. Beam 2 goes through the film and back: optical path $2nd$ further. It reflects off air (smaller $n$): no change.
  3. Total difference: $2nd - \frac\lambda2$ (the sign of the half doesn't matter). In step (bright) when this is $m\lambda$, i.e. $2nd = (m + \frac12)\lambda$.
  4. Coating: both beams are inverted, so only $2nd$ counts. They cancel when $2nd = (m + \frac12)\lambda$; the thinnest is $2nd = \lambda/2$, so $d = \lambda/(4n)$.

Reflected brightness of each colour from a thin film (light falling straight on)

Example 1

A soap film ($n = 1.33$) is 100 nm thick. Light falls straight on it. Which visible wavelength is reflected most strongly?

Show solution
  1. One inversion: bright when $2nd = (m + \frac12)\lambda$. $2nd = 2 \times 1.33 \times 100 = 266$ nm.
  2. $m = 0$: $\lambda = 266/0.5 = 532$ nm. $m = 1$: $\lambda = 266/1.5 \approx 177$ nm (ultraviolet, not visible).

532 nm (green)

Example 2

A glass lens ($n = 1.5$) gets an anti-reflection coating of $n = 1.38$. Find the thinnest coating that cancels the reflection of 550 nm light.

Show solution
  1. Both reflections are inverted, so cancelling needs $2nd = \frac12\lambda$ (thinnest).
  2. $d = \dfrac{\lambda}{4n} = \dfrac{550}{4 \times 1.38} \approx 99.6$ nm.

$d \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m

Example 3

Oil ($n = 1.5$) floats on water ($n = 1.33$). Which of the two reflections is inverted? Which condition gives bright reflection?

Show solution
  1. Top: air → oil, larger $n$: inverted.
  2. Bottom: oil → water, smaller $n$: not inverted.
  3. One inversion, as for a soap film.

Only the top one; bright when $2nd = (m + \frac12)\lambda$

Example 4

Just before a soap film bursts, its top part gets extremely thin and looks black in reflected light. Why?

Show solution
  1. As $d \to 0$, the extra path $2nd \to 0$.
  2. Only the half-wavelength from the one inversion is left, so the two reflections cancel for every colour.

The two reflected beams are in antiphase for all wavelengths, so nothing is reflected

Example 5

A soap film ($n = 1.33$, $d = 300$ nm) is viewed so that the light inside it travels at $r = 30°$ to the normal. Which visible wavelength is strongest? Compare with looking straight on.

Show solution
  1. $2nd\cos r = 2 \times 1.33 \times 300 \times 0.866 \approx 691$ nm. Bright: $(m + \frac12)\lambda = 691$: $m = 1$ gives $\lambda \approx 461$ nm.
  2. Straight on: $2nd = 798$ nm; $m = 1$ gives $\lambda = 532$ nm.

About 461 nm (blue) at a slant, 532 nm (green) straight on: the colour changes with the viewing angle

Always count the inversions first: one inversion swaps the bright and dark conditions; zero or two leave them as for a plain path difference.
Remember it with ropes: hitting a slower (larger $n$) medium is like a fixed end, inverted; hitting a faster one is like a free end, not inverted.
  • Reflection off a larger $n$: phase change $\pi$; off a smaller $n$: none.
  • Soap film (one inversion): bright when $2nd = (m + \frac12)\lambda$.
  • Anti-reflection coating: thinnest $d = \lambda/(4n)$.
  • At a slant: $2nd\cos r$.

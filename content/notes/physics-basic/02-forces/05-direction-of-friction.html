Direction of Friction

Friction fights the sliding between two surfaces

A rough surface (粗い面) can push along itself as well as at right angles to itself. The push along the surface is friction (摩擦力), $f$. A smooth surface (なめらかな面) has no friction at all: only the normal force.

Friction always acts along the surface, against the sliding of the object relative to the surface (or against the sliding that would happen if there were no friction). It does not always point against the motion: a box on a truck that speeds up is carried forward by friction.

The normal force $N$ and friction $f$ both come from the same surface. Added together they make one force, the reaction force (抗力), $R$.

Normal force + friction = reaction force

$$R = \sqrt{N^2 + f^2}$$

$N$ at right angles to the surface, $f$ along it, so they form a right angle and Pythagoras gives the size of $R$. On a smooth surface $f = 0$ and $R = N$.

  1. Friction comes from the two surfaces gripping each other. It stops (or slows) them sliding past each other.
  2. So first ask: which way would the object slide, compared with the surface, if the surface were ice?
  3. Friction on the object points the opposite way, along the surface.
  4. "Compared with the surface" matters when the surface moves. On a truck that speeds up, the box would slide backwards on the truck bed, so friction on the box points forwards.
  5. $N$ and $f$ are at right angles, so the reaction force they make has size $R = \sqrt{N^2 + f^2}$.

Example 1

A book slides to the right across a rough table after a push. Which way does friction act on the book?

Show solution
  1. The book slides right compared with the table.
  2. Friction acts against that, along the table.

To the left

Example 2

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ box rests on a rough slope of $30°$. Find the size and direction of the friction on it.

Show solution
  1. Without friction it would slide down the slope, so friction points up the slope.
  2. Along the slope, gravity's part $mg\sin 30°$ pulls it down. At rest, friction balances it.
  3. $f = 2.0 \times 9.8 \times \tfrac{1}{2} = 9.8$.

$f = 9.8\ \mathrm{N}$, up the slope

Example 3

For the box in Example 2, find the size of the reaction force $R$ from the slope.

Show solution
  1. $N = mg\cos 30° = 19.6 \times \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 17.0$ and $f = 9.8$.
  2. $R = \sqrt{17.0^2 + 9.8^2} = \sqrt{289 + 96} \approx 19.6$.
  3. Check: only two forces act on the box, gravity and $R$, so $R$ must equal $mg$ and point straight up.

$R = 19.6\ \mathrm{N}$, straight up

Example 4

A box rides on a truck that is speeding up to the east. The box doesn't slide on the truck bed. Which way does friction act on the box?

Show solution
  1. If the bed were ice, the truck would pull away and the box would slide backwards (west) compared with the bed.
  2. Friction acts against that: east. It is the force that speeds the box up with the truck.

East, the same way the truck moves

Example 5

When you walk forwards, which way does the ground's friction act on your shoe?

Show solution
  1. Your foot pushes back against the ground. On ice, the shoe would slip backwards.
  2. Friction on the shoe acts against that slip: forwards.

Forwards: friction is what moves you forwards

"Friction is opposite to the motion" is only true when the surface stands still. The rule is: opposite to the sliding relative to the surface. A box on a truck, a shoe when walking and the back wheel of a bicycle are all pushed forwards by friction.
Imagine the surface is turned into ice. Which way would the object slip? Friction points the other way.
  • Friction acts along the surface, against sliding relative to the surface.
  • Smooth surface: no friction. Rough surface: friction can act.
  • Normal force and friction together are the reaction force, $R = \sqrt{N^2 + f^2}$.

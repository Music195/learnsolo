Properties of Sigma (Σ)

What does $\Sigma$ mean?

$\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k$ is short for $a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n$: put $k = 1, 2, \dots, n$ into $a_k$ and add the results.

Because $\Sigma$ is just addition, it follows the normal rules of addition. Those rules let you break a complicated sum into the standard sums $\sum 1$, $\sum k$, $\sum k^2$, $\sum k^3$.

Split a sum or difference

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k \pm b_k) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \pm \sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k$$

You can add the terms in any order, so the $a$'s and $b$'s can be summed separately.

Take out a constant

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} c\,a_k = c \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k$$

$c$ must not depend on $k$. It is a common factor of every term.

These are not true in general: $\displaystyle\sum a_k b_k \neq \Bigl(\sum a_k\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum b_k\Bigr)$ and $\displaystyle\sum a_k^2 \neq \Bigl(\sum a_k\Bigr)^2$. Expand the product first, then split the sum.

More useful rewrites

Split at any point

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = \sum_{k=1}^{m} a_k + \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} a_k$$

Shift the index

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_{k+m} = \sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m} a_k$$

Reverse the order

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_{n-k+1}$$

Telescoping

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k - a_{k-1}) = a_n - a_0$$

All the middle terms cancel in pairs.

Example 1

Calculate $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{5} (3k + 2)$.

Show solution
  1. Split the sum: $\;\displaystyle\sum (3k + 2) = 3\sum_{k=1}^{5} k + \sum_{k=1}^{5} 2$
  2. $3 \times \dfrac{5 \times 6}{2} = 45$ and $2 \times 5 = 10$

$45 + 10 = 55$

Example 2

Write $\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{7} k^2$ using $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{7} k^2$.

Show solution
  1. The sum from $k = 1$ includes one extra term, $1^2$.
  2. Remove it: $\;\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{7} k^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{7} k^2 - 1^2$

$\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{7} k^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{7} k^2 - 1 = 140 - 1 = 139$

Example 3

Show that $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} (2k - 1) = n^2$ (the sum of the first $n$ odd numbers).

Show solution
  1. Split the sum: $\;\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{n} k - \sum_{k=1}^{n} 1$
  2. $= 2 \times \dfrac{n(n+1)}{2} - n = n^2 + n - n$

$= n^2$

  • $\sum (a_k \pm b_k) = \sum a_k \pm \sum b_k$
  • $\sum c\,a_k = c \sum a_k$ when $c$ doesn't depend on $k$.
  • You can't split a product or a square of a sum; expand first.
  • Sums can be split, shifted, reversed and telescoped.

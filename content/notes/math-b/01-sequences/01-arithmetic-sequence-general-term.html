General Term of an Arithmetic Sequence

What is an arithmetic sequence?

Each term is made by adding the same number $d$ (the common difference) to the term before it:

$$a,\; a + d,\; a + 2d,\; a + 3d,\; \dots$$

The general term lets you jump straight to any term without listing all the ones before it.

General term

$$a_n = a + (n - 1)d$$

$a_n$ = the $n$th term
$a$ = first term
$d$ = common difference
$n$ = position of the term ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$)

Why $n - 1$? To reach the $n$th term you start at $a$ and take $n - 1$ steps of size $d$. For example, the 5th term is $a + 4d$.

Arithmetic Sequence Explorer

Example 1

Find the 10th term of the arithmetic sequence with first term $5$ and common difference $4$.

Show solution
  1. $a = 5$, $\;d = 4$, $\;n = 10$
  2. $a_{10} = 5 + (10 - 1) \times 4 = 5 + 36$

$a_{10} = 41$

Example 2

In an arithmetic sequence, the 5th term is $18$ and the 12th term is $45$. Find the first term and the common difference.

Show solution
  1. Write both terms with the formula: $\;a + 4d = 18$ and $\;a + 11d = 45$
  2. Subtract the first equation from the second (the $a$ cancels): $\;7d = 27$, so $d = \dfrac{27}{7}$
  3. Substitute back: $\;a = 18 - 4 \times \dfrac{27}{7} = \dfrac{126 - 108}{7} = \dfrac{18}{7}$

$a = \dfrac{18}{7}, \quad d = \dfrac{27}{7}$

Keep answers as fractions until the end. Rounding $d$ to $3.857$ in Example 2 gives $a \approx 2.572$, which is already wrong in the third decimal place.

Example 3

Is $50$ a term of the sequence $3, 7, 11, 15, \dots$? If so, which term?

Show solution
  1. $a = 3$, $\;d = 7 - 3 = 4$
  2. Set the general term equal to $50$: $\;3 + 4(n - 1) = 50$
  3. $4(n - 1) = 47$, so $n - 1 = 11.75$ and $n = 12.75$
  4. $n$ must be a whole number, so there is no such term.

$50$ is not a term of this sequence.

  • $a_n = a + (n - 1)d$ gives any term directly.
  • To find $a$ and $d$ from two terms, write two equations and subtract.
  • To test whether a number is a term, solve for $n$: it must be a positive whole number.
  • Plotted against $n$, the terms lie on a straight line with slope $d$.

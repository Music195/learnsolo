General Term of a Geometric Sequence

What is a geometric sequence?

Each term is made by multiplying by the same number $r$ (the common ratio):

$$a,\; ar,\; ar^2,\; ar^3,\; \dots$$

You find $r$ by dividing any term by the one before it:

$$r = \frac{a_2}{a_1} = \frac{a_3}{a_2} = \frac{a_4}{a_3} = \dots$$

General term

$$a_n = a\,r^{\,n-1}$$

$a_n$ = the $n$th term
$a$ = first term
$r$ = common ratio
$n$ = position of the term ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$)

The power is $n - 1$ for the same reason as in arithmetic sequences: to reach the $n$th term you multiply by $r$ exactly $n - 1$ times. For example, the 5th term is $ar^4$.

What the ratio $r$ tells you

RatioBehaviourExample ($a = 8$)
$r > 1$grows$8, 16, 32, \dots$ ($r = 2$)
$0 < r < 1$shrinks towards $0$$8, 4, 2, \dots$ ($r = \tfrac12$)
$r < 0$signs alternate$8, -16, 32, \dots$ ($r = -2$)
$r = 1$constant$8, 8, 8, \dots$
$r = -1$flips back and forth$8, -8, 8, \dots$
In a geometric sequence $a \neq 0$ and $r \neq 0$. Otherwise the terms become $0$ and you can no longer divide one term by the previous one to find $r$.

Example 1

Find the 8th term of $3, 6, 12, 24, \dots$

Show solution
  1. $a = 3$, $\;r = 6 \div 3 = 2$, $\;n = 8$
  2. $a_8 = 3 \times 2^{8-1} = 3 \times 2^7 = 3 \times 128$

$a_8 = 384$

Example 2

In a geometric sequence the 3rd term is $24$ and the 6th term is $192$. Find the first term and the common ratio.

Show solution
  1. Write both terms with the formula: $\;ar^2 = 24$ and $\;ar^5 = 192$
  2. Divide the second by the first (the $a$ cancels): $\;r^3 = \dfrac{192}{24} = 8$, so $r = 2$
  3. Substitute back: $\;a \times 2^2 = 24$, so $a = 6$

$a = 6, \quad r = 2$

Example 3

Which term of $2, 6, 18, 54, \dots$ equals $4374$?

Show solution
  1. $a = 2$, $\;r = 6 \div 2 = 3$
  2. Set the general term equal to $4374$: $\;2 \times 3^{n-1} = 4374$
  3. $3^{n-1} = 2187 = 3^7$
  4. $n - 1 = 7$

$4374$ is the 8th term.

Arithmetic vs geometric

Arithmetic

Add the same $d$ each time

$a,\; a + d,\; a + 2d,\; \dots$

$a_n = a + (n - 1)d$: straight-line growth

Geometric

Multiply by the same $r$ each time

$a,\; ar,\; ar^2,\; \dots$

$a_n = ar^{n-1}$: exponential growth or decay

  • $a_n = ar^{n-1}$ gives any term directly.
  • Find $r$ by dividing a term by the one before it.
  • To find $a$ and $r$ from two terms, divide the equations so $a$ cancels.
  • $|r| > 1$ grows, $|r| < 1$ shrinks towards $0$, and $r < 0$ alternates in sign.

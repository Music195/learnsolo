Difference Sequence and General Term

What is a difference sequence?

Some sequences are neither arithmetic nor geometric, but the gaps between their terms follow a pattern. Write those gaps as a new sequence $\{b_n\}$, the difference sequence:

$$\begin{array}{ccccccccc} a_n: & 1 & & 2 & & 5 & & 10 & \dots \\ b_n: & & 1 & & 3 & & 5 & & \dots \end{array}$$

If you can find $b_n$, you can rebuild $a_n$ by adding the gaps back on.

Difference sequence

$$b_n = a_{n+1} - a_n$$

General term from the differences

$$a_n = a_1 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} b_k \qquad (n \geq 2)$$

Start at $a_1$ and add the first $n - 1$ gaps to reach $a_n$.

The formula only works for $n \geq 2$ (for $n = 1$ the sum $\sum_{k=1}^{0}$ has no terms). After finding $a_n$, always check that it also gives the right $a_1$, and say so in your answer.

If the $k$th differences are constant, $a_n$ is a polynomial of degree $k$ in $n$.

Example 1

Find the next term of $2, 5, 10, 17, 26, \dots$

Show solution
  1. 1st differences: $3, 5, 7, 9$
  2. 2nd differences: $2, 2, 2$ (constant)
  3. Next 1st difference: $9 + 2 = 11$
  4. Next term: $26 + 11$

$37$

Example 2

Find the general term of $1, 2, 5, 10, 17, \dots$

Show solution
  1. Differences: $1, 3, 5, 7, \dots$, an arithmetic sequence, so $b_k = 2k - 1$
  2. For $n \geq 2$: $\;a_n = 1 + \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (2k - 1)$
  3. $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (2k - 1) = 2 \cdot \frac{(n-1)n}{2} - (n - 1) = (n - 1)^2$
  4. So $a_n = (n - 1)^2 + 1 = n^2 - 2n + 2$
  5. Check $n = 1$: $\;1 - 2 + 2 = 1 = a_1$ ✔

$a_n = n^2 - 2n + 2$ (true for all $n \geq 1$)

Example 3 (another method)

Find the general term of $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, \dots$ by assuming a quadratic.

Show solution
  1. 2nd differences are constant ($2, 2, 2$), so $a_n = pn^2 + qn + r$
  2. Put in $n = 1, 2, 3$: $\;p + q + r = 1,\quad 4p + 2q + r = 4,\quad 9p + 3q + r = 9$
  3. Subtract neighbouring equations: $\;3p + q = 3,\quad 5p + q = 5$, so $p = 1$, $q = 0$, $r = 0$

$a_n = n^2$

Use $a_n = a_1 + \sum b_k$ when you can recognise $b_k$ (arithmetic, geometric, …). Assume a polynomial when the 2nd or 3rd differences are constant and you just need the coefficients.
  • Difference sequence: $b_n = a_{n+1} - a_n$.
  • $a_n = a_1 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} b_k$ for $n \geq 2$; always check $n = 1$ separately.
  • Constant $k$th differences mean $a_n$ is a polynomial of degree $k$.

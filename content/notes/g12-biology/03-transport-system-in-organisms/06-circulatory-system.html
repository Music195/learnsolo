Circulatory System

Concept — Open, Single and Double Circulation

There are two main types of circulatory system: open and closed.

  • Open circulatory system: blood pumped by a tubular heart leaves open-ended vessels into blood spaces around the tissues. When the heart relaxes, blood flows back into the vessels through pores called ostia. Found in most arthropods (insects, spiders, crabs), e.g. the grasshopper.
  • Closed circulatory system: the heart pumps blood into vessels that carry it to the tissues of the whole body and back to the heart. Advantages: (i) the pressure can be raised so blood flows more quickly; (ii) the flow can be directed precisely to the organs that need most oxygen and nutrients. Two types: single and double.
  • Single circulatory system (fish): the heart pumps deoxygenated blood to the gills, where it takes in oxygen and gives up CO₂. The blood then travels round the body, giving oxygen to the cells, and returns to the heart. Blood passes once through the heart in each circuit.
  • Double circulatory system (birds and mammals): two separate circulations. The systemic circulation carries oxygenated blood from the heart to the body cells and deoxygenated blood back. The pulmonary circulation carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs and oxygenated blood back to the heart. Blood passes twice through the heart in each full circuit.

Birds and mammals need much more oxygen than fish: they move without the support of water and keep a constant body temperature, which uses a lot of resources, so their cells need plenty of oxygen and glucose and quick removal of waste.

Advantages of double circulation: (i) oxygenated and deoxygenated blood cannot mix, so tissues get as much oxygen as possible; (ii) fully oxygenated blood reaches the tissues quickly at high pressure; (iii) blood in the tiny lung vessels is at relatively low pressure, so it does not damage them and allows gas exchange; (iv) oxygenated blood returning to the heart is pumped hard and reaches all the capillaries quickly, supplying oxygen for an active way of life.

Counting the Passes
$$\text{fish: heart} \to \text{gills} \to \text{body} \to \text{heart} \quad (1 \text{ pass})$$ $$\text{mammal: heart} \to \text{lungs} \to \text{heart} \to \text{body} \to \text{heart} \quad (2 \text{ passes})$$
  • Open: blood leaves the vessels and bathes the tissues; closed: blood stays inside vessels
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Fish need more oxygen than birds and mammals": birds and mammals need much more (they move without water's support and keep a constant body temperature).
  • Pulmonary vs systemic: pulmonary = heart ↔ lungs; systemic = heart ↔ rest of the body.

Example: State one difference between an open and a closed circulatory system, and one advantage of the closed one.

Show answer

In an open system blood leaves the vessels and bathes the tissues directly (returning through ostia); in a closed system it stays inside vessels. A closed system can raise the pressure for faster flow and can direct blood to the organs that need it most.

Key Takeaways
  • Open (arthropods): tubular heart, open-ended vessels, blood spaces, ostia.
  • Closed: higher pressure and directed flow; single in fish, double in birds and mammals.
  • Double circulation: pulmonary (low pressure) + systemic (high pressure); no mixing of blood.

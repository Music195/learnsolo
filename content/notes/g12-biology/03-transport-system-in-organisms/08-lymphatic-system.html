Lymphatic System

Concept — The Drain for Tissue Fluid

Between the capillaries and the cells is a watery liquid called tissue fluid. It forms when part of the blood plasma leaks out of the capillaries (at the arterial end, where blood pressure is high). It bathes the cells, supplying them with materials and taking away their wastes.

  • Most of the water in tissue fluid re-enters the capillaries by osmosis (at the venous end).
  • Some fluid passes into another system, the lymphatic system: vessels similar to blood capillaries, called lymphatic capillaries, with valves to prevent backflow. The fluid inside them is called lymph.
  • Lymph is carried back to the blood: the lymph vessels join the main lymph vessel (thoracic duct), which opens into the subclavian veins in the shoulders.
  • The lacteals, which carry fats from the villi of the small intestine, are part of the lymphatic system.
  • Before lymph returns to the blood it is filtered in swellings called lymph nodes, which remove dead cells and bacteria. Lymph nodes contain white blood cells that destroy harmful bacteria. One of the first signs of infection is often swelling of the lymph nodes ("swollen glands").
Fluid Balance
$$\text{fluid leaving capillaries} = \text{fluid returning to capillaries} + \text{lymph}$$ $$\text{plasma} \to \text{tissue fluid} \to \text{lymph} \to \text{lymph nodes} \to \text{subclavian veins} \to \text{blood}$$
  • Pushed out by blood pressure at the arterial end; drawn back by osmosis at the venous end
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Lymph is a separate fluid made by the nodes: lymph is simply tissue fluid that has drained into lymph vessels.
  • Lymph returns to the heart through arteries: it rejoins the blood in the subclavian veins.

Tissue fluid lies between the capillaries and the cells. Most of its water re-enters the capillaries by osmosis; some passes into the lymphatic system.

The lymphatic system has vessels like blood capillaries, the lymphatic capillaries, with valves to prevent backflow. They carry the fluid, lymph, back to the blood, opening into the subclavian veins. The lacteals, which carry fats from the villi of the small intestine, are part of it.

Lymph is filtered in lymph nodes, which remove dead cells and bacteria; their white blood cells destroy harmful bacteria. When a person has an infection, the lymph nodes often swell (swollen glands).

Example: Why do the ankles swell if lymph vessels are blocked?

Show answer

Not all of the fluid pushed out of the capillaries returns by osmosis; the rest normally drains away as lymph. If the lymph vessels are blocked, this extra fluid stays in the tissues, so they swell.

Key Takeaways
  • Tissue fluid leaks from capillaries; most returns by osmosis, the rest drains as lymph.
  • Lymph capillaries have valves; lymph rejoins the blood at the subclavian veins; lacteals carry fats.
  • Lymph nodes filter lymph; their white cells destroy bacteria and they swell in infection.

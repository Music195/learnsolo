Transport System in Plants

Concept — Spread Out to Collect, Pipe to Share

Plants make their own organic molecules by photosynthesis: CO₂ is the source of carbon and light the source of energy. The leaves have a large surface area to volume ratio, which gives more area to collect light and CO₂ and a shorter distance for CO₂ to diffuse in and O₂ to diffuse out. So plants are not compact like animals: they have branching bodies with leaves above ground and extensive root systems below ground for water and mineral salts.

Transport systems are needed to move substances:

  • from where they are absorbed to where they are needed: water and mineral ions from the roots, through the xylem, to other parts;
  • from where they are produced to where they are needed for metabolism: sugars made in the leaves go to all parts for respiration and for making cellulose cell walls in growing regions (carried in the phloem as sucrose);
  • to storage places: sugars are changed into starch, e.g. in a potato tuber.

Plants have a branching shape that keeps their surface area to volume ratio large, and their energy needs are small compared with animals, so respiration is slow. Diffusion alone can supply cells with oxygen and remove CO₂; leaves are thin with air spaces inside, so diffusion supplies the mesophyll cells with CO₂ for photosynthesis.

Two transport tissues:

  • Xylem transports water and inorganic ions from the roots to all parts. It has dead, empty, hollow cells with no end walls: xylem vessels in long lines form hollow tubes, and water moves through them by mass flow. Xylem also has tracheids, xylem fibres and xylem parenchyma.
  • Phloem transports substances made in the plant (sucrose, amino acids) from the leaves to all other parts. Its sieve tubes are living cells with cytoplasm and a few organelles but no nucleus; their walls are cellulose. A companion cell sits beside each sieve tube. Phloem also has phloem fibres and phloem parenchyma.
Surface Area to Volume
$$\text{cube of side } s: \quad A = 6s^2, \quad V = s^3, \quad \frac{A}{V} = \frac{6}{s}$$
  • The bigger and more compact the body, the smaller A/V
  • A thin, flat leaf of the same volume has a much larger A/V
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Xylem cells are living": xylem vessels are dead, empty tubes with no end walls. Phloem sieve tubes are living (but have no nucleus).
  • Phloem only carries sugar downwards: phloem carries sugar from leaves to wherever it is needed, up to growing tips and fruits or down to roots and tubers.

Worked Examples — Sample Questions (pp. 59–60)

Example A: Find the surface area to volume ratio of cubes of side 1 cm and 4 cm. Which shape is better for exchange by diffusion?

Show answer

1 cm: A = 6 cm², V = 1 cm³, A/V = 6 per cm. 4 cm: A = 96 cm², V = 64 cm³, A/V = 1.5 per cm.

The small cube has 4 times the surface per unit volume, so diffusion can supply it far more easily. A large plant keeps its A/V high by branching and having thin leaves.

Q1 (i), (ii): TRUE or FALSE. (i) Plants have extensive root systems below the ground. (ii) Xylem tissue contains living, empty cells with no end walls.

Show answer

(i) TRUE. (ii) FALSE: xylem contains dead, empty hollow cells with no end walls.

Q3 (i): (A. Xylem B. Phloem C. Parenchyma D. Fibre) transports water and minerals from the roots to the leaves.

Show answer

A, xylem.

Q4 (ii): "Transport substances made in plant" is matched with (C) phloem.

Show answer

Phloem: it carries sucrose and amino acids made in the leaves.

Key Takeaways
  • Branching bodies and thin leaves keep a large surface area to volume ratio.
  • Transport moves substances from where they are absorbed or made to where they are used or stored.
  • Xylem: dead hollow vessels, water and ions up by mass flow. Phloem: living sieve tubes + companion cells, sugars and amino acids.

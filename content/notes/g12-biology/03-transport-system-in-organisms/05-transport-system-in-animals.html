Transport System in Animals

Concept — Too Big for Diffusion Alone

Large multicellular animals have evolved transport systems because they are bigger and have higher metabolic rates.

  • Principles of circulation: in many large animals, including all vertebrates, the circulatory system is a mass transport system: substances are carried in the flow of a fluid, with a mechanism for moving it around the body.
  • The need for transport: single-celled organisms have a high surface area to volume ratio, so their surface membrane can supply all the oxygen and materials their volume demands. Large multicellular organisms have a low ratio: their surface is not enough for their metabolic needs, so they need a transport system.
  • Every cell carries out chemical reactions and needs glucose and oxygen for cellular respiration; these must be brought from outside a large organism. Respiration releases energy for the other reactions of life but also produces the toxic waste carbon dioxide, which, with other wastes, must be removed before it damages the cells.

Features of mass transport systems:

  1. exchange surfaces to get materials into and out of the system;
  2. a system of vessels, usually tubes, following a specific route or widely branching;
  3. a way of making sure substances move in the right direction (nutrients in, waste out);
  4. a way of moving materials fast enough: mechanical methods such as the pumping of the heart, or keeping a concentration gradient (e.g. facilitated diffusion and active transport);
  5. a suitable transport medium (a fluid);
  6. often, a way of adapting the rate of transport to the organism's needs.
Why Size Matters
$$\text{sphere: } \frac{A}{V} = \frac{4\pi r^2}{\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3} = \frac{3}{r} \qquad t_{\text{diffusion}} \approx \frac{x^2}{2D}$$
  • x = distance to diffuse; D = diffusion coefficient (oxygen in water, about 2 × 10⁻⁹ m² s⁻¹)
  • Ten times farther takes about a hundred times longer
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Big animals have a big surface, so they need no transport": the surface grows as r², but the volume as r³; per unit volume, a big animal has less surface.
  • Diffusion time grows in step with distance: it grows with the square of the distance.

Key Takeaways
  • Big organisms have a low surface area to volume ratio and long diffusion distances.
  • Mass transport carries substances in a moving fluid.
  • Six features: exchange surfaces, vessels, one-way direction, fast movement, a medium, an adjustable rate.

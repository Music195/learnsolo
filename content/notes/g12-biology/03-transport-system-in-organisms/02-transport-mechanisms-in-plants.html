Transport Mechanisms in Plants

Concept — Two Routes Across the Root

Mineral ions and organic compounds (e.g. sucrose) are transported dissolved in water: dissolved mineral ions in the xylem, dissolved organic compounds in the phloem. Roots take up water and mineral ions, and root hairs increase the surface area for absorption.

  • The uptake of water is a passive process by osmosis: the diffusion of water from a higher water potential to a lower water potential.
  • The uptake of minerals can be passive (diffusion) and/or active transport.

Movement of water in a plant. In active absorption water enters the cell sap and passes from one cell to another: movement involving the protoplasm is the symplast. In passive absorption water moves through the apoplast: the cell walls and intercellular spaces. Water and ions go from the root hair, through the root cortex, to the xylem by both routes, at the same time or separately, at different rates.

  • Apoplastic pathway: most water goes this way (when transpiration is high), through the spaces in the cellulose cell walls, dead cells and the hollow xylem tubes. Water moves by diffusion, since it does not cross a partially permeable membrane, from wall to wall or through intercellular spaces. It is faster than the symplastic pathway. At the endodermis, a thick, waterproof, waxy band of suberin in the cell walls, the Casparian strip, blocks the apoplast: an impassable barrier, so the water must enter the cytoplasm.
  • Symplastic pathway: a smaller amount of water travels through the cytoplasm and plasmodesmata (or vacuoles). Water moves by osmosis into the cell across the partially permeable surface membrane, possibly into the vacuole through the tonoplast, and between cells through the plasmodesmata. It is slower than the apoplastic pathway.
Direction of Water Movement
$$\text{soil water} \;\to\; \text{root hair} \;\to\; \text{cortex} \;\to\; \text{endodermis} \;\to\; \text{xylem}$$ $$\text{water moves from higher water potential to lower water potential}$$
  • Apoplast: cell walls and spaces, diffusion, fast, stopped by the Casparian strip
  • Symplast: cytoplasm and plasmodesmata, osmosis, slow
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "The apoplast is slower": water moves through the apoplast more rapidly than through the symplast.
  • The Casparian strip stops water reaching the xylem: it only blocks the wall route; water passes through the endodermis cells' cytoplasm instead.

Example: Why must all water reaching the xylem pass through at least one living membrane?

Show answer

The Casparian strip, a waterproof band of suberin in the endodermis walls, blocks the apoplast. Water travelling in the walls has to enter the endodermis cells (crossing their membranes) before it reaches the xylem.

Key Takeaways
  • Water uptake is passive, by osmosis; mineral uptake can be passive or active.
  • Apoplast: cell walls and spaces, faster, most water; symplast: cytoplasm and plasmodesmata, slower.
  • The Casparian strip (suberin) in the endodermis blocks the apoplast.

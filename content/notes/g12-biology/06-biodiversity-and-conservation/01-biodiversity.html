Biodiversity

Concept — The Variety of Life

Biodiversity comes from bio (life) and diversity (variability). The term was coined by Walter G. Rosen in 1985 and popularised by Edward O. Wilson, the "Father of Biodiversity". It is the variety and variability among living organisms and the ecological complexes in which they occur: the totality of genes, species and ecosystems of a region. It keeps the balance of the ecosystem.

Three levels of biodiversity:

  • Ecosystem diversity: the variation in ecosystems or habitats. An ecosystem is a community of organisms and their physical environment interacting together. Ecosystems (deserts, rainforests, mangroves) differ in niches, trophic structure, food webs and nutrient cycling, and with moisture, temperature, altitude and rainfall.
  • Species diversity: the number of species and the abundance of each species in a place. The number of species alone is species richness. The more species there are, and the more evenly the organisms are spread among them, the greater the species diversity. Coral reefs have very high diversity. Ecosystems with high species diversity tend to be more stable and better able to resist changes.
  • Genetic diversity: the variety of genes within a species (e.g. differently coloured shells of the snail Cepaea nemoralis; varieties of crops and domestic animals). It helps populations adapt to competition, new predators, new diseases and changes in temperature, salinity, humidity and rainfall.

Importance: biodiversity matters for the planet, human health, food security, the economy and livelihoods. Organisms live in interdependence, sharing space, shelter and food. A healthy ecosystem and rich biodiversity will: (a) increase ecosystem productivity (each species has a niche, a role to play); (b) support more plant species and so a greater variety of crops; (c) protect fresh water; (d) promote soil formation and protection; (e) provide nutrient storage and recycling; (f) help break down pollutants; (g) contribute to climate stability; (h) speed recovery from natural disasters; (i) provide more food; (j) provide more medicinal resources and drugs.

Richness vs Diversity
$$\text{species richness} = \text{number of species}$$ $$\text{diversity index } D = 1 - \sum p_i^{2}, \qquad p_i = \frac{\text{number of species } i}{\text{total number}}$$
  • D (Simpson's index, an extra measure not in the textbook) is the chance that two organisms picked at random belong to different species: it rises with both richness and evenness.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Richness = diversity: two places with the same number of species can differ in diversity if one species dominates.
  • Genetic diversity is "between species": it is the variety of genes within a species.

Worked Examples — Sample Questions (pp. 120–121)

Q1 (i)–(iii): True or false? (i) Biodiversity is the variety of all living things. (ii) Genetic diversity is the variety of genes within a genus. (iii) Some organisms in an ecosystem live in interdependence.

Show answer

(i) True. (ii) False: genetic diversity is the variety of genes within a species. (iii) True.

Q2 (i): Healthy ecosystem and rich ___ will provide for nutrient storage and recycling.

Show answer

biodiversity.

Q3 (i): The (A. ecology B. ecosystem C. biodiversity D. conservation) is a community of organisms and their physical environment interacting together.

Show answer

B. ecosystem.

Q4 (i), (ii): Match: Variation of life forms in an ecosystem; Number of species in a particular location.

Show answer

Variation of life forms in an ecosystem → C. Biodiversity; Number of species in a particular location → D. Species diversity.

Q6 (a), (b): Define the term 'biodiversity'. Differentiate the terms species diversity and species richness.

Show answer

(a) Biodiversity is the variety and variability among living organisms and the ecological complexes in which they occur: the totality of genes, species and ecosystems of a region.

(b) Species richness is the number of species in a community. Species diversity is the number of species and the abundance of each species in a location, so it also includes evenness: the more species and the more evenly the organisms are spread among them, the greater the diversity.

Q8 (a): Explain how biodiversity is important.

Show answer

Biodiversity is very important for the planet, human health, food security, the economy and livelihoods. All organisms in an ecosystem live in interdependence, sharing space, shelter and food. Rich biodiversity lets large ecosystems function and self-regulate, and ecosystems are linked across the earth, so a loss in one area can upset the balance elsewhere.

A healthy ecosystem with rich biodiversity will increase productivity (each species has a niche), support more plant species and crop varieties, protect fresh water, promote soil formation, store and recycle nutrients, break down pollutants, contribute to climate stability, speed recovery from natural disasters, and provide more food and more medicines.

Key Takeaways
  • Biodiversity: variety of genes, species and ecosystems (three levels).
  • Species richness counts species; species diversity also counts how evenly organisms are spread.
  • Genetic diversity is within a species and helps it adapt; diverse ecosystems are more stable.

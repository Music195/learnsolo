Biodiversity in Myanmar

Concept — Forests and Endangered Species

Myanmar is the largest country in mainland Southeast Asia, with a land area of 676,577 km2. Because of its size, its large latitudinal range of 2,100 km, and its varied topography and habitats, it is rich in biodiversity. Its habitats range from tropical coral reefs to alpine habitats: moist tropical rain forests in the south, dry deciduous forests in the central areas, and temperate broad leaf and alpine forests toward the north.

Forest diversity. Forests are a carbon sink and stabilise soils. By slowing water flow and increasing infiltration, they regulate the seasonal flow of water downstream and recharge groundwater, which keeps streams flowing in the dry season (water for drinking, irrigation and fisheries). By reducing soil loss they also help dams and reservoirs work.

  • Forest is the dominant vegetation type. The total forest area is 290,410 km2, 43 % of the land area, but only half of it is closed forest; the other half is "open" or "degraded".
  • Rainfall and elevation control the vegetation types. Dry forest occurs where rainfall is usually less than 400 mm a year and supports xerophytic vegetation.
  • Table 6.1 (forest cover): mixed deciduous 38 %, hill and temperate evergreen 25 %, tropical evergreen 16 %, dry 10 %, deciduous dipterocarp (Indaing) 5 %, tidal, beach and dune, and swamp forest 4 %, fallow land 2 %.

Endangered species. The IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources) publishes the Red Data Book, which lists endangered plants and animals; "red" is the warning that these species are likely to become extinct in the near future if not protected. Myanmar is moderately biodiverse: about 250 mammal species, more than 1,000 birds, 370 reptiles and 7,000 plants are recorded; of these 39 mammals, 45 birds, 21 reptiles and 38 plants are globally threatened.

  • Plants: Dipterocarpus alatus (Gurjun tree, Kanyin), Swietenia macrophylla (Mahogany), Cephalotaxus mannii (Mann's plum yew), Rhododendron arboreum (Rhododendron, Taung Zalat Ni): Endangered; Paphiopedilum wardii (Black Orchid): Critically Endangered.
  • Vertebrates: Elephas maximus (Asian Elephant, Sin), Rucervus eldii thamin (Eld's Deer, Shwe Thamin), Orcaella brevirostris (Irrawaddy Dolphin, Linpai), Pavo muticus (Green Peafowl, Daun): Endangered; Geochelone platynota (Burmese Star Tortoise, Kyelait): Critically Endangered.
Numbers to Check
$$\frac{290\,410}{676\,577} \times 100\% \approx 42.9\% \approx 43\%$$ $$\text{area of a forest type} = \text{its share} \times 290\,410\ \text{km}^2$$
  • Example: mixed deciduous forest = 0.38 × 290,410 ≈ 110,356 km2.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "43 % of Myanmar is closed forest": only about half of the forest area is closed; the rest is open or degraded.
  • Endangered vs critically endangered: the Black Orchid and the Burmese Star Tortoise are critically endangered, a higher risk level.

Key Takeaways
  • Myanmar: 676,577 km2, 2,100 km latitudinal range, habitats from coral reefs to alpine forests.
  • Forest is 290,410 km2 (43 %); mixed deciduous forest is the largest type (38 %).
  • The IUCN Red Data Book lists endangered species, e.g. Asian Elephant, Irrawaddy Dolphin; the Black Orchid and Burmese Star Tortoise are critically endangered.

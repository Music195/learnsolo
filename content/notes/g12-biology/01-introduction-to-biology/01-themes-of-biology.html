Themes of Biology

Concept — Five Big Ideas That Run Through All of Biology

Life on Earth is diverse, resilient, powerful, intelligent and mysterious. To make sense of it, biology is organised around five major themes: organization, information, energy and matter, interactions, and evolution.

  • Theme 1: New properties emerge at each level of organization. The hierarchy of life runs: biosphere, ecosystem, community, population, organism, organ system, organ, tissue, cell, organelle, molecule and atom. At each level, emergent properties appear because of interactions between the parts of the level below. Structure and function are related: a hummingbird's wings can rotate at the shoulder, so it can fly backwards and hover while its long, slender beak feeds on nectar.
  • The cell is the basic unit of structure and function, the lowest level that can carry out all the activities of life. Eukaryotic cells have membrane-enclosed organelles and keep their DNA in a nucleus; prokaryotic cells have no membrane-enclosed organelles and no nucleus, and are much smaller.
  • Theme 2: Life's processes involve the expression and transmission of genetic information. Genetic information is coded in the nucleotide sequence of DNA and passes from parents to offspring. A gene is a DNA sequence used to make a protein: it is transcribed into mRNA, which is translated into a protein. This is gene expression. Some RNAs are not translated but have other jobs.
  • Theme 3: Life requires the transfer and transformation of energy and matter. Energy flows through an ecosystem in one direction: producers turn light energy into chemical energy in food, some passes to consumers, and the rest is lost as heat. Chemicals cycle: plants take them from the soil and air, animals eat the plants, and decomposers (fungi and bacteria) return them to the soil.
  • Theme 4: Interactions in biological systems, from molecules to ecosystems. Organisms interact with each other and with physical factors. Many processes are controlled by feedback: in negative feedback the product slows its own production (high blood glucose → insulin → glucose taken up by liver and muscle → glucose falls); in positive feedback the product speeds up its own production.
  • Theme 5: Evolution, the core theme, explains the unity and diversity of life. Evolution is the change that has transformed life on Earth. Life is classified into three domains: Bacteria and Archaea (prokaryotes) and Eukarya (protists, and the kingdoms Plantae, Fungi and Animalia). Darwin (1809–1882) proposed natural selection: individuals with certain heritable traits survive and reproduce more successfully than others, so populations become adapted to their environments.
Key Patterns

  • Energy flows one way (sun → producers → consumers → heat); chemicals go round in cycles
  • Three domains: Bacteria, Archaea (prokaryotes) and Eukarya (eukaryotes)
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Bacteria and Eukarya are prokaryotes": the two prokaryote domains are Bacteria and Archaea. Eukarya are eukaryotes.
  • Energy recycles like chemicals: it does not. Energy leaves the ecosystem as heat, so life needs a constant input of sunlight.
  • The atom is the smallest living level: atoms and molecules are not alive. The cell is the lowest level that can perform all the activities of life.

Q7 (a): How do you understand gene expression?

Show answer

Genetic information is encoded in the nucleotide sequence of DNA. A gene acts as the instructions to make a protein.

The gene is transcribed into mRNA, and the mRNA is translated into a specific protein; this is gene expression. Gene expression can also produce RNAs that are not translated but have other important functions.

Key Takeaways
  • Five themes: organization (emergent properties), information (DNA → RNA → protein), energy and matter, interactions (feedback), evolution.
  • The cell is the lowest level of life; eukaryotes have a nucleus and membrane organelles, prokaryotes do not.
  • Energy flows one way and is lost as heat; chemicals cycle. Negative feedback keeps conditions steady.
  • Three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya. Natural selection makes populations adapted.

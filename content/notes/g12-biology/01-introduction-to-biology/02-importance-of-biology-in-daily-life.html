Importance of Biology in Daily Life

Concept — Biology Feeds, Heals, Clothes and Houses Us

Biology lets people understand their own bodies, their resources and the threats in their environment. Advances in medicine, agriculture and biotechnology have improved the quality of life, and ecology and conservation show how to protect biodiversity.

  • Agriculture produces foods, fibres, forest products, crops and animal feed. Myanmar's main crops are cereals, pulses and oilseeds; it is the leading pulse and bean producer in ASEAN (about 18 types, led by blackgram, then greengram, pigeonpea, chickpea, groundnut, and sesame).
    • Rice (Oryza sativa): rain-fed lowland, winter, deep-water, upland and irrigated rice; the monsoon is the main season. The traditional method floods the fields, which reduces weeds and pests.
    • Wheat (Triticum aestivum): grown once a year, sown by broadcasting, behind the plough, drilling or dibbling; zero-tillage is a newer method.
    • Maize (Zea mays): a short-day tropical plant for warm, humid weather; sensitive to too much or too little soil moisture and to frost; sown by dibbling or drilling, no deeper than 5–6 cm.
  • Horticulture (Latin hortus garden + cultura cultivation) is the cultivation of plants for food and decoration, on small plots with mixed crops. Its types: olericulture (vegetables), pomology or fruticulture (fruits and nuts), viticulture (grapes), floriculture (flowers), turf management, arboriculture (individual trees, shrubs, vines), landscape horticulture and postharvest physiology (storage and transport after harvest).
  • Hydroponics grows plants without soil, with the roots in a mineral nutrient solution (sometimes held by inert perlite or gravel). It is a type of hydroculture: less water, faster growth and higher yields.
  • Aquaculture (aquafarming) farms fish, crustaceans, molluscs and algae under controlled conditions. Kinds: fish farming or pisciculture (carp, salmon, tilapia, catfish), mariculture (offshore in the open sea, inshore in coastal waters, onshore in seawater tanks), shrimp farming, oyster farming, algaculture, and integrated fish farming, where wastes from one sub-system become inputs to another.
  • Livestock are domesticated land animals (cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, horses, chickens, ducks). They give meat, dairy, fibre, manure as fertiliser, labour, land management, breeding, sports and pets, and support food security and nutrient cycling.
  • Food groups: energy foods (carbohydrates, fats), body-building food (protein), protective food (vitamins, minerals) and luxury food. Only plants make food, by photosynthesis. Microbes help: Lactobacillus makes curd from milk, yeast ferments grapes into wine, and soil microbes make compost.
  • Health and medicine: pharmacognosy (Greek: drug + knowledge) studies drugs from natural sources. Plant drugs: aspirin (salicylic acid, willow Salix), quinine (Cinchona, malaria), digitalis (foxglove, heart disease), periwinkle (Catharanthus, Hodgkin's disease and leukaemia), morphine and codeine (opium poppy, pain). Treating illness with animal-based medicines is zootherapy: leeches, viper venom (captopril), maggots, spider silk made by goats, salmon calcitonin and horseshoe crab blood (LAL test).
  • Clothing and shelter: plant fibres are long sclerenchyma cells with walls thickened by cellulose and lignin. Cotton (Gossypium, central Myanmar) and jute (Corchorus, a bast fibre for gunny bags) are plant fibres. Sericulture rears silkworms (Bombyx mori) on mulberry for silk; wool is a protein fibre sheared from sheep. Wood and timber such as teak (Tectona grandis) and pyinkado (Xylia dolabriformis) are Myanmar's most valuable timbers.
Key Terms

  • Pisciculture = fish farming; pomology = fruits and nuts; olericulture = vegetables; viticulture = grapes
  • Pharmacognosy = drugs from natural sources; zootherapy = medicines from animals
  • Metamorphosis = the change from pupa into the adult form inside the cocoon
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Hydroponics "with soil": hydroponics grows plants without soil, in a nutrient solution.
  • Inshore vs offshore vs onshore: offshore = enclosed parts of the open ocean; inshore = fish farms in coastal (littoral) waters; onshore = tanks and ponds filled with seawater on land.
  • Wool and cotton are the same kind of fibre: wool is mainly protein (animal); cotton and other plant fibres are mainly cellulose.

Q6: (a) State how to grow Zea mays. (b) What is integrated fish farming? (c) Give a short account of the uses of wool.

Show answer

(a) Maize is a short-day tropical plant that likes warm, humid weather. Avoid too much or too little soil moisture and frost. Sow by dibbling or drilling, no deeper than 5–6 cm.

(b) Fish are produced together with other farming (crops, livestock) centred on the fish pond, linked so that the by-products or wastes of one sub-system become inputs to another.

(c) Wool is used for clothing, blankets, horse rugs, saddle cloths, carpets, insulation, upholstery, cloth diapers, suits and floor coverings; it is an essential fibre for winter clothing.

Q7 (b), (c): (b) Briefly explain pharmacognosy. (c) Clarify the role of salmon in treating calcium regulation disorders.

Show answer

(b) Pharmacognosy (Greek: drug + knowledge) is the study of the physical, chemical, biochemical and biological properties of drugs from natural sources, and the search for new ones. It works with secondary metabolites of plants, animals and microbes; about 80 % of people still use natural products for primary health care.

(c) Salmon make the hormone calcitonin (from a gland in the neck) to control their calcium. In humans calcitonin from the thyroid slows bone loss; synthetic salmon calcitonin treats calcium regulation disorders, e.g. in postmenopausal women and Paget's disease.

Q8: (a) Write an account of hydroponic culture. (b) Explain the role of leeches and maggots as animal-based medicines. (c) Describe the economic value of livestock.

Show answer

(a) Plants grow without soil, with roots in a water-based mineral nutrient solution, sometimes supported by perlite or gravel; nutrients come from fish waste, manure, or chemical fertilisers. It uses much less water and gives faster growth and higher yields (tomatoes, lettuce, strawberries in greenhouses), so people in dry places could grow food.

(b) Medicinal leeches (Hirudo medicinalis) help reattach fingers and heal skin grafts: their saliva has anticoagulants that keep blood flowing until the veins regrow. Sterile maggots eat dead tissue and bacteria, turning a chronic wound into one that can heal, quickly and cheaply.

(c) Meat, dairy products, fibre (wool, mohair), fertiliser (manure, blood and bone), labour, land management (grazing weeds), breeding, sports and pets.

Key Takeaways
  • Agriculture: rice, wheat, maize, pulses; horticulture has eight branches; hydroponics grows plants without soil.
  • Aquaculture includes pisciculture, mariculture and integrated fish farming; livestock give food, fibre, fertiliser and labour.
  • Medicines come from plants (pharmacognosy) and animals (zootherapy).
  • Fibres: cotton and jute (plant), silk and wool (animal protein); teak and pyinkado are key timbers.

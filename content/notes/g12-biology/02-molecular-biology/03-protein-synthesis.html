Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation

Concept — From Gene to Protein in Two Steps

Protein synthesis has two steps. In transcription, mRNA is made from the DNA template of a gene. In translation, the mRNA's nucleotide sequence is turned into the amino acid sequence of a protein, using the genetic code. This one-way flow, DNA → RNA → protein, is the central dogma of molecular biology.

1. Transcription happens in the nucleus: DNA is copied into mRNA in three stages.

  • Initiation: for each gene only one strand is transcribed: the antisense or template strand. The other, the sense or coding strand, has the same sequence as the mRNA, but with T in place of U.
  • Elongation: the RNA polymerase complex moves along the DNA, making mRNA complementary to the template strand, 5′ → 3′.
  • Termination: at the termination signal the polymerase detaches and the new mRNA is released.

2. Modification of mRNA: the new precursor mRNA (pre-mRNA) is changed into mature mRNA before it leaves the nucleus: (1) a 5′ cap (7-methylguanosine) is added at the 5′ end; (2) a 3′ poly-A tail of 100–200 adenine nucleotides is added at the 3′ end; (3) introns (non-coding regions) are removed by the spliceosome (snRNPs), and the exons (coding regions) are joined. This is splicing.

3. Translation happens in the cytoplasm at the ribosome. The ribosome has a small subunit, where the mRNA binds, and a large subunit with three tRNA sites: A (arriving tRNA), P (holds the growing chain) and E (exit). Each tRNA carries one specific amino acid on its acceptor stem; at the other end its anticodon pairs with the codon on the mRNA.

  • Initiation: the small subunit binds the mRNA near the start codon AUG. The initiator tRNA (anticodon UAC, carrying methionine) pairs with AUG; then the large subunit joins. The initiator tRNA sits in the P site.
  • Elongation: a four-step cycle repeats: (1) a tRNA with the next amino acid enters the A site; (2) a peptide bond forms; (3) the chain is moved onto the amino acid of the tRNA in the A site; (4) the mRNA moves forward one codon, so the chain-holding tRNA is now in the P site, and the empty tRNA leaves from the E site.
  • Termination: when a stop codon reaches the A site, a release factor cuts the polypeptide from the last tRNA. The chain is released and folds into its three-dimensional shape; the ribosome comes apart.
Pairing Rules Along the Way
$$\text{template DNA } 3'\text{-TAC ACC AAA-}5' \;\to\; \text{mRNA } 5'\text{-AUG UGG UUU-}3' \;\to\; \text{Met–Trp–Phe}$$ $$\text{codon AUG} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{anticodon UAC (initiator tRNA, Met)}$$
  • Template → mRNA: A→U, T→A, G→C, C→G. Coding strand = mRNA with T for U
  • Anticodon is complementary to the codon (A–U, G–C)
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Translation occurs in the nucleus": transcription is in the nucleus; translation is in the cytoplasm.
  • Reading the code from the coding strand: mRNA is made from the template strand, so mRNA matches the coding strand, not the template.
  • Introns and exons: introns are cut out; exons are kept and joined (exons are expressed).

Worked Examples — Sample Questions (pp. 36–37)

Example A (Figure 2.8): The template strand reads 3′-ACC AAA CCG AGT-5′. Write the mRNA, the tRNA anticodons and the amino acids.

Show answer

mRNA is complementary to the template (A→U, T→A, G→C, C→G): 5′-UGG UUU GGC UCA-3′.

Anticodons pair with the codons: ACC, AAA, CCG, AGU (the same letters as the template, with U for T).

Amino acids: UGG = Trp, UUU = Phe, GGC = Gly, UCA = Ser: Trp–Phe–Gly–Ser.

Q7 (b): Clarify the different stages of transcription in protein synthesis.

Show answer

Transcription copies DNA into mRNA in the nucleus.

Initiation: only one strand of the gene, the antisense or template strand, is transcribed (the other is the sense or coding strand, with the same sequence as mRNA but T for U). Elongation: RNA polymerase moves along the DNA, making mRNA complementary to the template strand, 5′ → 3′. Termination: at the termination signal the polymerase detaches and the new mRNA is released.

Q7 (c): How does a strand of mRNA elongate during protein synthesis?

Show answer

(The question is about the elongation stage of translation, where the chain made on the mRNA grows.)

A tRNA with the polypeptide is in the P site; a tRNA carrying the next amino acid enters the A site; a peptide bond forms and the chain is transferred to the amino acid in the A site, making it one amino acid longer; the mRNA moves forward one codon, so the chain-holding tRNA is in the P site and the empty tRNA leaves from the E site. The free A site receives the next tRNA, and the cycle repeats until a stop codon.

Key Takeaways
  • Central dogma: DNA → RNA → protein. Transcription in the nucleus, translation in the cytoplasm.
  • Transcription: initiation, elongation, termination; only the template (antisense) strand is read.
  • Pre-mRNA gets a 5′ cap, a poly-A tail, and has its introns removed (splicing).
  • Translation: AUG start with Met-tRNA (UAC); A, P, E sites; peptide bonds; release factor at the stop codon.

Some Techniques in Molecular Biology

Concept — Moving Genes Between Organisms

Research in molecular genetics now lets scientists change the genes of microorganisms, plants and animals: genetically modified organisms (GMOs) make medicines, a tumour's DNA can be profiled, and genetic disorders can be treated by putting the correct form of a disease-related gene into a person's genome. Another research focus is how ageing is related to chromosomes.

  • Genetic engineering is the deliberate alteration of an organism's genetic material. An organism given foreign DNA, so that it has a new phenotype, is transgenic (transgenic bacteria, plants and animals are the GMOs). Recombinant DNA techniques and DNA analysis are central to making GMOs.
  • Recombinant DNA moves a gene from one organism into the genome of another species. A vector carries the gene into the target cell: a bacterial plasmid, a harmless virus, a liposome or a gene gun. Steps: the wanted gene is cut out with a restriction enzyme; a plasmid (e.g. from Agrobacterium tumefaciens) is cut open with the same restriction enzyme; the gene is joined into the plasmid by DNA ligase.
  • Golden rice: lack of vitamin A causes blindness and immune deficiency, a major cause of death in children who cannot afford animal products or a varied diet. (1) Genes for making beta-carotene in the rice endosperm were taken from daffodils and the bacterium Erwinia; (2) they were put into plasmids in Agrobacterium; (3) the agrobacteria were added to a Petri dish of rice embryos, which they infected, transferring the genes; (4) the transgenic rice was crossed with local varieties suited to each region. Later a maize gene in place of the daffodil gene gave even more carotene. The rice is golden because of the orange beta-carotene, which the body turns into vitamin A.
  • Benefits of GM crops: higher yield; heat and drought tolerance; resistance to pests and pathogens; salt tolerance with lower harvesting cost and less pesticide; better nutritional value and slower spoilage. Plants also make medicines: human growth hormone, clotting factors and antibodies (an antibody from corn carries radioisotopes to tumour cells; one from soybeans treats genital herpes).
  • GM animals are much harder to make and controversial. A foreign gene is put into an animal oocyte (egg), which is fertilised and implanted in a host female; the offspring are transgenic (fish, pigs, cows, rabbits, sheep). For pharmaceutical products, a human protein gene is placed next to the β-lactoglobulin promoter, which works only in mammary cells, so the protein appears in the milk. Benefits: more of a product (milk, muscle), disease resistance, human proteins (antibodies, clotting factors) and organs for transplantation (xenotransplantation).
The Tool Kit
$$\text{restriction enzyme (cuts)} \;+\; \text{vector (carries)} \;+\; \text{DNA ligase (joins)} \;\to\; \text{recombinant DNA}$$
  • Vectors: bacterial plasmid, harmless virus, liposome, gene gun
  • Golden rice: β-carotene genes from daffodil (later maize) and Erwinia, carried by Agrobacterium
  • GM sheep: human gene + β-lactoglobulin promoter → human protein in the milk
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Different enzymes for gene and plasmid: both must be cut with the same restriction enzyme so their sticky ends match.
  • Golden rice contains vitamin A: it contains beta-carotene, which the body converts into vitamin A.
  • The promoter makes the protein: the promoter only switches the gene on, and only in mammary cells; the human gene codes for the protein.

Worked Examples — Sample Questions (pp. 36–37)

Q1 (v), (vi): TRUE or FALSE. (v) The disease-free gene was introduced into an individual's genome to treat genetic disorders. (vi) Children of families living in poverty often lack animal products in their diets.

Show answer

(v) TRUE: genetic disorders are treated by introducing the correct form of a disease-related gene. (vi) TRUE (one reason for vitamin A deficiency).

Q2 (v), (vi): (v) Vitamin A deficiency can cause ___ and immune deficiency syndrome. (vi) One type of antibody made by corn can deliver radioisotopes to ___ cells.

Show answer

(v) blindness; (vi) tumour.

Q3 (vi): To insert the recombinant gene into a target cell, a vector such as (A. parasite B. fungus C. plasmid of bacteria D. harmful virus) is needed.

Show answer

C, a plasmid of bacteria (a virus used as a vector must be harmless).

Q4 (iv): Transgenic is matched with (E) genetically modified organism.

Show answer

Transgenic → E.

Q6 (b): State briefly the recombinant DNA method.

Show answer

It transfers a gene from one organism into the genome of an organism of a different species, using a vector (plasmid of bacteria, harmless virus, liposome or gene gun).

The DNA of the other species is cut with a restriction enzyme to isolate the desired gene; a plasmid is isolated (e.g. from Agrobacterium tumefaciens) and cut open with the same restriction enzyme; the gene is joined into the cut plasmid using DNA ligase, and this recombinant plasmid carries the gene into the target cell.

Q6 (c): Mention four benefits of crops engineered by genetic modification.

Show answer

Any four: increased crop yield; heat and drought tolerance; resistance to pests and pathogens; salt tolerance, lower harvesting cost and less pesticide; better nutritional value and food quality with slower spoilage.

Q8 (c): Give an account of the production of genetically modified animals.

Show answer

A foreign gene is inserted into an animal oocyte, which is fertilised and implanted into a host female; the offspring are transgenic (fish, pigs, cows, rabbits, sheep).

For a human protein: (1) the human hormone gene is cloned into a plasmid vector next to the sheep β-lactoglobulin promoter, active only in mammary cells; (2) the plasmid is injected into a sheep oocyte, and its DNA joins the chromosomal DNA; (3) the oocyte is fertilised and implanted into a female, which gives birth to transgenic offspring; (4) milk from the transgenic female contains the human hormone; (5) the hormone is purified from the milk.

Benefits: more product (milk yield, muscle mass), disease resistance, human proteins (antibodies, clotting factors), and organs for transplantation (xenotransplantation).

Key Takeaways
  • Genetic engineering makes transgenic organisms (GMOs); recombinant DNA uses restriction enzymes, vectors and ligase.
  • Golden rice makes β-carotene (from daffodil/maize and Erwinia genes, carried by Agrobacterium), which becomes vitamin A.
  • GM animals: gene into an oocyte; with a mammary promoter, a human protein is collected from the milk.

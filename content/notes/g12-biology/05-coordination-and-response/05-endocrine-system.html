The Endocrine System

Concept — Chemical Messengers in the Blood

The endocrine system is made of endocrine glands and their hormones. Endocrine glands secrete chemical messengers called hormones directly into the bloodstream, which carries them throughout the body. ("Hormone" comes from a Greek word meaning "to excite" or "set in motion".) Compared with the rapid nervous system, the endocrine system usually has slower, longer-lasting effects on a broader range of cells.

  • Steroid hormones are lipid-based: testosterone, estrogen, cortisol. Water-soluble hormones: epinephrine, human growth hormone (hGH), thyroxine (T4), insulin.

Glands and hormones (Table 5.6):

  • Hypothalamus: releasing and inhibiting hormones, which regulate the anterior pituitary.
  • Anterior pituitary: hGH (cell division, bone and muscle growth, metabolism); TSH (thyroid makes thyroxine); ACTH (adrenal cortex secretes glucocorticoids); FSH (ova and sperm production); LH (sex hormones from ovaries and testes); prolactin (milk production).
  • Posterior pituitary: ADH (water retention by the kidneys); oxytocin (uterine contractions, release of milk).
  • Thyroid: thyroxine (all tissues: raises metabolic rate, regulates growth and development); calcitonin (lowers blood calcium).
  • Parathyroid: PTH (raises blood calcium from bones, gut and kidneys).
  • Adrenal cortex: glucocorticoids such as cortisol (raise blood glucose, break down protein); mineralocorticoids such as aldosterone (sodium and water reabsorption by the kidneys); gonadocorticoids (secondary sexual characteristics). Adrenal medulla: epinephrine and norepinephrine (fight-or-flight).
  • Pancreas: insulin (lowers blood glucose, glycogen formation in the liver); glucagon (raises blood glucose by converting liver glycogen to glucose).
  • Ovaries: estrogen (uterine lining growth, female secondary sexual characteristics); progesterone (uterine lining growth, prevents uterine contractions). Testes: testosterone (sperm formation, male secondary sexual characteristics).

Nervous vs endocrine system. Similarities: both coordinate the body's activities, both need a stimulus to produce a response, and both have specific effectors as targets. Differences:

  • Information: nerve impulse (electrical along a neurone, chemical across synapses) vs hormone (chemical).
  • Route: neurones vs bloodstream. Speed: fast vs slow (set by blood flow).
  • Duration: short, within milliseconds vs longer, minutes to days.
  • Localisation: a local region (e.g. the hand muscle in a withdrawal reflex) vs one or more organs in different parts of the body.
Pattern
$$\text{gland} \xrightarrow{\text{secretes}} \text{hormone} \xrightarrow{\text{bloodstream}} \text{target organ} \to \text{response}$$
  • Insulin lowers blood glucose; glucagon raises it (both from the pancreas).
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Insulin is made in the liver": it is made in the pancreas (islets of Langerhans); the liver is its target.
  • "Hormones travel along nerves": they travel in the blood.
  • Anterior vs posterior pituitary: ADH and oxytocin come from the posterior; hGH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH and prolactin from the anterior.

Q8 (c): Compare and contrast the nervous system and the endocrine system.

Show answer

Similarities: both coordinate the body's activities; both need a stimulus to produce a response; both have specific effectors as target organs.

Differences: the nervous system transmits information as nerve impulses (electrical along neurones, chemical across synapses), carried by neurones, fast, short-lived (milliseconds) and localised (e.g. the muscle in the hand during withdrawal from pain). The endocrine system transmits information as hormones, carried by the bloodstream, slowly (set by blood flow), lasting minutes to days, and may affect one or more organs in different parts of the body.

Key Takeaways
  • Endocrine glands secrete hormones into the blood; hormones act on target organs.
  • Pituitary (anterior: hGH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin; posterior: ADH, oxytocin) is controlled by the hypothalamus.
  • Pancreas: insulin lowers and glucagon raises blood glucose; thyroxine raises metabolic rate.
  • Nerves: fast, short, local. Hormones: slow, long-lasting, widespread.

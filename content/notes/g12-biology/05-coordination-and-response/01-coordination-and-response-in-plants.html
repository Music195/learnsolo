Coordination and Response in Plants

Concept — Plants Respond Without Nerves

A stimulus is a detectable change in the internal or external environment that causes a temporary increase in physiological activity, a response, in the whole plant or part of it. Plants have internal and external stimuli.

  • Coordination: the responses to external or environmental changes, produced by the working of various organs in a systematic and controlled manner.
  • External stimuli: water, gravity, light intensity and duration, chemicals, temperature and touch. Internal stimuli (internal control) are chemicals called plant hormones.
  • Plants lack a nervous system like animals, but they can still sense things around them and respond. Their coordination and response is carried out by plant hormones, also called plant growth regulators or phytohormones.
  • Responses have evolved because they give a selective advantage: they make the plant better adapted to survive and reproduce.
  • Plants respond to abiotic or biotic stress. Examples of plant responses: tropisms, responses to touch, responses to herbivory (being eaten), and responses to abiotic stress.

A tropism is a growth response whose direction depends on the direction of the stimulus: shoots grow towards light (phototropism) and away from gravity, while roots grow towards gravity (gravitropism). The hormone auxin (section 5.2) collects on one side of the shoot or root and changes how fast the cells there elongate, so the organ bends.

The Pattern
$$\text{stimulus} \;\to\; \text{detection} \;\to\; \text{plant hormone} \;\to\; \text{response (growth or movement)}$$
  • Shoot: more auxin on the shaded (or lower) side → those cells elongate more → the shoot bends towards light (or upwards).
  • Root: more auxin on the lower side → those cells elongate less → the root bends downwards.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "The light side grows faster": the shaded side elongates more, which pushes the tip towards the light.
  • "Plants cannot respond because they have no nerves": plants coordinate their responses with hormones instead.
Interactive Demo — Tropisms and Touch

How does a plant turn towards light, find "up" and "down", and react to touch?

Light: move the light source and watch auxin (orange dots) gather on the shaded side, where cells grow longer and the shoot bends. Gravity: lay the pot on its side; auxin gathers on the lower side, the shoot bends up and the root bends down. Touch: touch a sensitive plant (Mimosa) and watch its leaflets fold. Growth that takes days is sped up.

environment.

Example: Plants have no nervous system. How do they coordinate their responses?

Show answer

They use chemicals called plant hormones (plant growth regulators, phytohormones). These are made in one part of the plant and act on another, controlling growth and movement in response to stimuli.

Example: A seedling on a windowsill bends towards the window. Explain.

Show answer

Light reaches the shoot from one side. Auxin collects on the shaded side of the shoot tip.

The cells on the shaded side elongate more than those on the lit side, so the shoot curves towards the light (positive phototropism). This gives the leaves more light for photosynthesis, a selective advantage.

Example: List four kinds of plant response named in the textbook.

Show answer

Tropisms, responses to touch, responses to herbivory, and responses to abiotic stress.

Key Takeaways
  • A stimulus is a detectable change; plants respond to external stimuli (light, gravity, water, touch, temperature, chemicals) and internal ones (hormones).
  • Plants have no nervous system; plant hormones (phytohormones) coordinate their responses.
  • Responses give a selective advantage: tropisms, touch responses, responses to herbivory and to abiotic stress.

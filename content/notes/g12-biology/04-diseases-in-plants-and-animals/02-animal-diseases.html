Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Concept — Non-infectious and Infectious Diseases

A disease is an illness or disorder of the body or mind that leads to poor health; each has a set of signs and symptoms. Animal diseases are non-infectious (not caused by pathogens, so they cannot spread from person to person; caused by aging, genetics, malnutrition, lifestyle and environment, e.g. cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes, Alzheimer's) or infectious (caused by pathogens and passed from host to host).

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) mostly starts with atheroma:

  • High blood pressure damages the endothelium (inner lining) of an artery. White blood cells (mostly macrophages) move in, and with lipids from the blood they form fatty streaks under the endothelium.
  • More white cells, lipids and connective tissue build up and harden into a fibrous plaque (atheroma). It partly blocks the lumen, restricts blood flow and raises blood pressure. The hardening of arteries caused by atheroma is atherosclerosis.
  • Angina: plaques in the coronary arteries reduce the blood flow to the heart muscle. During exercise the muscle needs more oxygen than it gets, so it respires anaerobically, causing a gripping chest pain that can spread to the arms (especially the left) and jaw, with breathlessness.
  • Heart attack (myocardial infarction): a blood clot completely blocks a coronary artery, so part of the heart muscle gets no oxygen and dies. Symptoms: chest pain, shortness of breath, sweating.
  • Stroke: the blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted, by a blockage (clot, atheroma) or by bleeding from a burst vessel. Symptoms: dizziness, confusion, slurred speech, blurred or lost vision, numbness, paralysis on one side.
  • Deep vein thrombosis (DVT): a blood clot in a vein deep inside the body, usually the leg veins, caused e.g. by long inactivity such as long-haul flights; the risk rises with age.

Risk factors: non-modifiable (cannot be changed): genes (easily damaged arteries, hypertension, cholesterol balance), age (vessels lose elasticity and narrow), gender (men more at risk). Modifiable (lifestyle): smoking (damages artery linings, narrows arteries, raises blood pressure, upsets lipoproteins), inactivity, high blood pressure (healthy about 120/80 mmHg; regularly above 140/90 is hypertension), diet and obesity (linked to high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes).

Prevention: a balanced diet with plenty of fruit and vegetables, not smoking, a healthy weight (BMI 18.5–25 kg m−2), regular exercise, less stress. Treatment: antihypertensives (beta blockers, ACE inhibitors) lower blood pressure; statins lower blood cholesterol and improve the LDL : HDL balance; plant stanols and sterols reduce LDL; anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin) and platelet inhibitory drugs such as aspirin reduce clotting.

Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens and passed on through a transmission cycle. Endemic: always present in a population; epidemic: a sudden rise in cases; pandemic: a rise across a continent or the world. Examples: cholera (Vibrio cholerae, bacterium), malaria (Plasmodium, protist), HIV/AIDS (virus), TB (Mycobacterium, bacterium), measles (Morbillivirus), Covid (coronavirus).

Malaria is caused by four species of the protist Plasmodium (P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae) and carried by a vector, the female Anopheles mosquito, which feeds on blood for the protein needed for her eggs. It can also pass by blood transfusion, unsterile needles and across the placenta. Incubation: a week to a year; it affects the liver, red blood cells and brain. Clinical features: fever, anaemia, nausea, headache, shivering, sweating, enlarged spleen. Diagnosis: blood microscopy or a dip stick test.

  • Treating: quinine and chloroquine (inhibits protein synthesis, stops the parasite spreading in the body) also act as prophylactic drugs. Proguanil also inhibits the sexual reproduction of Plasmodium in the mosquito. Mefloquine, doxycycline, and artesunate (from artemisinin) combined with mefloquine against P. falciparum.
  • Preventing: reduce the number of mosquitoes (oil on water to suffocate larvae and pupae, drain marshes, stock water with larva-eating fish such as Gambusia affinis, spray Bacillus thuringiensis), avoid bites (insecticide-soaked nets, repellents, covered skin at dusk), and use drugs.
  • Worldwide control: the WHO eradication programme of the 1950s failed because Plasmodium became resistant to the drugs and mosquitoes became resistant to DDT and dieldrin. Concerns now: drug resistance, more P. falciparum, no vaccine yet, climate change and migration. Hopes: gene sequencing and drug design, and vaccines against different stages of the life cycle.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "The heart muscle gets oxygen from the blood in its chambers": it is supplied by the coronary arteries.
  • Calling smoking non-modifiable: smoking, inactivity, blood pressure and diet are modifiable; genes, age and gender are not.
  • "The mosquito causes malaria": the pathogen is Plasmodium; the mosquito is the vector. Plasmodium multiplies in both hosts.

Q5: Complete the paragraph about angina using: anaerobic, coronary, breathlessness, oxygen, chest, painful, heart, frightening.

Show answer

In angina, plaques build up slowly in the (a) coronary arteries, reducing blood flow to the parts of the (b) heart muscle beyond the plaques. Symptoms are first noticed during exercise, when the cardiac muscle is working harder and needs more (c) oxygen.

The narrowed coronary arteries cannot supply enough oxygenated blood and the heart muscle resorts to (d) anaerobic respiration. This causes a gripping pain in the (e) chest that can extend into the arms, particularly the left one, and the jaw, and often also causes (f) breathlessness.

Q6 (b), (c): State the role of genes as non-modifiable risk factors for cardiovascular disease. Mention the symptoms of stroke.

Show answer

(b) There is a genetic tendency in some families and ethnic groups to develop CVDs: (i) arteries which are easily damaged, (ii) a tendency to develop hypertension, which damages arteries and makes CVDs more likely, and (iii) problems with the cholesterol balance of the body.

(c) Dizziness, confusion, slurred speech, blurred vision or partial loss of vision (usually one eye), and numbness; in more severe strokes, paralysis, usually on one side of the body.

Q7 (b), (c): Tabulate some features of infectious diseases of worldwide importance. Give a short account of smoking and inactivity as modifiable risk factors for cardiovascular disease.

Show answer

(b) Cholera: Vibrio cholerae, bacterium. Malaria: four species of Plasmodium, protist. HIV/AIDS: human immunodeficiency virus, virus. Tuberculosis: Mycobacterium tuberculosis and M. bovis, bacterium. Measles: a species of Morbillivirus, virus. Covid: coronavirus, virus.

(c) Smoking: smokers are far more likely to develop atherosclerosis. Substances in tobacco smoke damage artery linings, making plaques more likely; narrow the arteries, raising blood pressure and the risk of atherosclerosis; and change the balance of lipoproteins in the blood.

Inactivity: a sedentary lifestyle can lead to heart disease and causes obesity, high blood pressure and diabetes. Regular exercise lowers blood cholesterol, balances lipoproteins and reduces stress, so it lowers the risk of atherosclerosis and CVDs.

Q8 (b): Explain the prevention and treatment of cardiovascular disease.

Show answer

Prevention: a balanced diet with plenty of fruit and vegetables; not smoking; a healthy weight (BMI 18.5–25 kg m−2), which helps control high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes; regular exercise, which strengthens the heart and controls blood pressure and cholesterol; less stress.

Treatment: antihypertensive drugs (beta blockers, sympathetic nerve inhibitors, ACE inhibitors) reduce blood pressure, though it must be monitored so it does not fall too low. Statins lower cholesterol, improve the LDL : HDL balance and reduce inflammation of artery linings; plant stanols and sterols reduce LDL. Anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin) and platelet inhibitory drugs such as aspirin reduce clotting and so the chance of a blockage.

Q8 (c): Discuss about the preventing malaria.

Show answer

There are three ways: reduce the number of mosquitoes, avoid being bitten, and use drugs to prevent the parasite infecting people. Mosquitoes lay eggs in water. Oil spread on the water stops larvae and pupae breathing; marshes can be drained; ponds and ditches can be stocked with fish that eat larvae (Gambusia affinis); and Bacillus thuringiensis can be sprayed to kill larvae without harming other life. Breeding sites cannot be removed completely, because any small puddle will do.

The best protection is to avoid being bitten: sleep under mosquito nets soaked in insecticide every six months, use repellents, and cover the skin at dusk. Prophylactic drugs (e.g. chloroquine, proguanil) taken before, during and after visiting an endemic area stop an infection taking hold.

Key Takeaways
  • Atheroma (plaque) narrows arteries; in coronary arteries it causes angina, and a clot causes a heart attack; in the brain, a stroke.
  • Non-modifiable risks: genes, age, gender. Modifiable: smoking, inactivity, high blood pressure, diet and obesity.
  • Malaria: Plasmodium (protist) carried by the female Anopheles mosquito; it multiplies in the liver and red blood cells, and in the mosquito.
  • Break the cycle by killing larvae, avoiding bites, and drugs (chloroquine, proguanil, artesunate).

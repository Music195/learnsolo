Limit of sin x / x

For a small angle, $\sin x$ and $x$ are almost equal

Measure $x$ in radians. On the unit circle, $x$ is the length of an arc and $\sin x$ is the height of its end point. For a tiny angle the arc is almost straight, so the two lengths are nearly the same and their ratio tends to $1$.

The key limit (radians)

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$$

Rearranged forms: $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x}{\sin x} = 1$, $\;\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1$, $\;\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin kx}{kx} = 1$.

  1. Take $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $O$ the centre, $A = (1, 0)$, $P$ on the unit circle at angle $x$, and $T = (1, \tan x)$ where line $OP$ meets the tangent at $A$.
  2. $\triangle OAP \subset$ sector $OAP \subset \triangle OAT$, so the areas satisfy $\dfrac{1}{2}\sin x < \dfrac{1}{2}x < \dfrac{1}{2}\tan x$.
  3. Divide by $\dfrac{1}{2}\sin x > 0$: $1 < \dfrac{x}{\sin x} < \dfrac{1}{\cos x}$. Take reciprocals: $\cos x < \dfrac{\sin x}{x} < 1$.
  4. $\cos x \to 1$, so the squeeze theorem gives the limit $1$ as $x \to +0$. Since $\dfrac{\sin(-x)}{-x} = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$, the left side gives the same.

Triangle $OAP$ < sector < triangle $OAT$

Example 1

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{x}$.

Show solution
  1. Make the angle and the denominator match: $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x} = 3 \cdot \dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x}$.
  2. As $x \to 0$, $3x \to 0$, so $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x} \to 1$.

$3$

Example 2

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 5x}{\sin 2x}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{\sin 5x}{\sin 2x} = \dfrac{\sin 5x}{5x} \cdot \dfrac{2x}{\sin 2x} \cdot \dfrac{5}{2}$
  2. $\to 1 \cdot 1 \cdot \dfrac{5}{2}$

$\dfrac{5}{2}$

Example 3

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2}$.

Show solution
  1. Multiply top and bottom by $1 + \cos x$: $\dfrac{1 - \cos^2 x}{x^2(1 + \cos x)} = \left(\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{1 + \cos x}$.
  2. $\to 1^2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\dfrac{1}{2}$

Example 4

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} x\sin\frac{1}{x}$.

Show solution
  1. Put $t = \dfrac{1}{x}$. As $x \to \infty$, $t \to +0$.
  2. $x\sin\dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{\sin t}{t} \to 1$.

$1$

The limit is $1$ only when the angle goes to $0$. For example $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 0$, because $|\sin x| \le 1$ while $x \to \infty$. The angle must also be in radians.

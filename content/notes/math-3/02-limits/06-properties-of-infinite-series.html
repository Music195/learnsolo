Properties of Infinite Series

A series is the limit of its partial sums

$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ means $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} S_n$ where $S_n = a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n$. So to find a sum, find a formula for $S_n$ first, then take the limit.

Convergent series can be added and multiplied by constants term by term, just like finite sums.

If $\sum a_n = S$ and $\sum b_n = T$ both converge

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (k a_n + l b_n) = kS + lT$$

$k$, $l$: constants.

Terms must go to $0$

$$\sum a_n \text{ converges} \implies \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0 \qquad\text{so}\qquad a_n \not\to 0 \implies \sum a_n \text{ diverges}$$

The converse is false: $a_n \to 0$ does not guarantee convergence.

  1. Each term is the difference of two partial sums: $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$.
  2. If the series converges to $S$, then both $S_n \to S$ and $S_{n-1} \to S$.
  3. So $a_n \to S - S = 0$.

Example 1

Find $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(n + 1)}$.

Show solution
  1. Split into partial fractions: $\dfrac{1}{n(n + 1)} = \dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n + 1}$.
  2. $S_n = \left(1 - \dfrac{1}{2}\right) + \left(\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{3}\right) + \cdots + \left(\dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n + 1}\right) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{n + 1}$ (the middle terms cancel).
  3. $S_n \to 1$.

$1$

Example 2

Find $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{1}{2^n} + \frac{2}{3^n}\right)$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\sum \frac{1}{2^n} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 1$ and $\displaystyle\sum \frac{1}{3^n} = \frac{\frac{1}{3}}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \frac{1}{2}$. Both converge.
  2. So the sum is $1 + 2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$2$

Example 3

Does $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{2n + 1}$ converge?

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{n}{2n + 1} = \dfrac{1}{2 + \frac{1}{n}} \to \dfrac{1}{2} \ne 0$.
  2. The terms don't go to $0$, so the series can't converge.

Diverges (to $\infty$)

Example 4

Show that $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n} + \sqrt{n + 1}}$ diverges, even though its terms go to $0$.

Show solution
  1. Rationalise: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n} + \sqrt{n + 1}} = \sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n}$.
  2. $S_n = (\sqrt{2} - 1) + (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}) + \cdots + (\sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n}) = \sqrt{n + 1} - 1$.
  3. $\sqrt{n + 1} - 1 \to \infty$.

Diverges to $\infty$

"$a_n \to 0$, so it converges" is a common mistake. $a_n \to 0$ is needed for convergence, but it is not enough (Example 4). Use it only to prove divergence.
If the terms look like $\dfrac{1}{(\text{something})(\text{something} + 1)}$ or $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\cdot} + \sqrt{\cdot}}$, write each term as a difference so that $S_n$ cancels down to a short formula.
  • $\sum a_n = \lim S_n$: find $S_n$ first.
  • Convergent series add and scale term by term.
  • $a_n \not\to 0 \Rightarrow$ diverges; $a_n \to 0$ proves nothing on its own.

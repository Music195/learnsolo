Velocity and Acceleration (Plane)

Do the line case twice, once per coordinate

A point P moves in the plane with $x = f(t)$, $y = g(t)$. Differentiate each coordinate separately. That gives the velocity vector $\vec{v}$, which points along the tangent to the path, and the acceleration vector $\vec{\alpha}$.

Their lengths are the speed $|\vec{v}|$ and the size of the acceleration $|\vec{\alpha}|$.

Position $(x, y) = (f(t), g(t))$

$$\vec{v} = \left(\frac{dx}{dt}, \frac{dy}{dt}\right), \qquad \vec{\alpha} = \left(\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}, \frac{d^2y}{dt^2}\right)$$
$$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}, \qquad |\vec{\alpha}| = \sqrt{\left(\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{d^2y}{dt^2}\right)^2}$$
  1. Circle of radius $r$ at angular speed $\omega$: $(x, y) = (r\cos\omega t, r\sin\omega t)$.
  2. $\vec{v} = (-r\omega\sin\omega t, r\omega\cos\omega t)$, so $|\vec{v}| = r\omega$ (constant), and $\vec{v} \cdot (x, y) = 0$: the velocity is along the tangent.
  3. $\vec{\alpha} = (-r\omega^2\cos\omega t, -r\omega^2\sin\omega t) = -\omega^2(x, y)$: it points to the centre, with size $r\omega^2$.

Velocity (teal) and acceleration (orange) along the path

Example 1

P moves with $(x, y) = (2t, t^2)$. Find $\vec{v}$, $\vec{\alpha}$ and the speed at $t = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{v} = (2, 2t) = (2, 2)$ at $t = 1$; $\;\vec{\alpha} = (0, 2)$.
  2. Speed $\sqrt{2^2 + 2^2}$.

$\vec{v} = (2, 2)$, $\vec{\alpha} = (0, 2)$, speed $2\sqrt{2}$

Example 2

P moves with $(x, y) = (3\cos 2t, 3\sin 2t)$. Find its speed and the size of its acceleration.

Show solution
  1. Circle with $r = 3$, $\omega = 2$: $\vec{v} = (-6\sin 2t, 6\cos 2t)$, $\;\vec{\alpha} = (-12\cos 2t, -12\sin 2t)$.

Speed $6$, $|\vec{\alpha}| = 12$ (always toward the centre)

Example 3

P moves with $(x, y) = (t - \sin t, 1 - \cos t)$ (a cycloid). Find the greatest speed.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{v} = (1 - \cos t, \sin t)$, so $|\vec{v}|^2 = 1 - 2\cos t + \cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 2 - 2\cos t$.
  2. Largest when $\cos t = -1$: $|\vec{v}|^2 = 4$.

Greatest speed $2$, at $t = \pi, 3\pi, \ldots$ (the top of the arch)

Speed is the length of $\vec{v}$, not $\dfrac{dx}{dt} + \dfrac{dy}{dt}$. Square, add, then take the root.
For the greatest or least speed, work with $|\vec{v}|^2$ (no root). $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$ often simplifies it a lot.
  • $\vec{v} = (x', y')$, $\;\vec{\alpha} = (x'', y'')$; speed $|\vec{v}|$.
  • $\vec{v}$ points along the tangent to the path.
  • Uniform circular motion: speed $r\omega$, acceleration $r\omega^2$ toward the centre.

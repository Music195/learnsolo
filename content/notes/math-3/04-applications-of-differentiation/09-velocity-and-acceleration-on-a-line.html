Velocity and Acceleration (Straight Line)

Differentiate position, then differentiate again

A point P moves along a number line, and its coordinate at time $t$ is $x = f(t)$. Its velocity $v$ is the rate of change of position, and its acceleration $\alpha$ is the rate of change of velocity.

The sign of $v$ tells the direction (positive: moving right). $|v|$ is the speed. When $v$ and $\alpha$ have the same sign, the point is speeding up.

Position $x = f(t)$

$$v = \frac{dx}{dt} = f'(t), \qquad \alpha = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = f''(t), \qquad \text{speed} = |v|$$

$v = 0$: momentarily at rest (it may turn back there). $v\alpha > 0$: speeding up; $v\alpha < 0$: slowing down.

  1. Speed squared is $v^2$, and $\dfrac{d}{dt}(v^2) = 2v\dfrac{dv}{dt} = 2v\alpha$ (chain rule).
  2. If $v\alpha > 0$, then $v^2$ is increasing, so the speed $|v|$ is increasing.
  3. If $v\alpha < 0$, the speed is decreasing.

$x = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$: graph (left) and the point on the line (right)

Example 1

$x = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$ ($t \ge 0$). When is P at rest, and which way does it move?

Show solution
  1. $v = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3(t - 1)(t - 3)$, zero at $t = 1$ and $t = 3$.
  2. $v > 0$ for $0 \le t < 1$ and $t > 3$ (moving right); $v < 0$ for $1 < t < 3$ (moving left).

At rest at $t = 1$ ($x = 4$) and $t = 3$ ($x = 0$); it turns back at each

Example 2

For the same motion, find the total distance travelled from $t = 0$ to $t = 4$.

Show solution
  1. Positions at the turning times: $x(0) = 0$, $x(1) = 4$, $x(3) = 0$, $x(4) = 4$.
  2. Add each leg's length: $|4 - 0| + |0 - 4| + |4 - 0|$.

$12$ (the displacement is only $x(4) - x(0) = 4$)

Example 3

A ball thrown upward has height $h = 20t - 5t^2$ m. Find its greatest height and its velocity when it lands.

Show solution
  1. $v = 20 - 10t = 0$ at $t = 2$: height $h(2) = 20$ m.
  2. Lands when $h = 0$, $t = 4$: $v(4) = -20$ m/s.

$20$ m; $-20$ m/s (speed $20$ m/s, downward)

Example 4

P moves with $x = 2\sin 3t$. Show that $\alpha = -9x$.

Show solution
  1. $v = 6\cos 3t$, $\;\alpha = -18\sin 3t = -9 \cdot 2\sin 3t$.

$\alpha = -9x$: the acceleration always points back toward $O$ (simple harmonic motion)

Distance travelled is not $x(\text{end}) - x(\text{start})$ if P turns back; split at the times where $v = 0$. Also, $\alpha = 0$ at one instant does not mean constant velocity. It only means the velocity is not changing at that moment.
On the $x$–$t$ graph, velocity is the slope and acceleration is the bending. A hilltop on the graph is a moment where P stops and turns back.
  • $v = \dfrac{dx}{dt}$, $\;\alpha = \dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}$, speed $|v|$.
  • $v = 0$: at rest (possible turning point); sign of $v$: direction.
  • $v\alpha > 0$: speeding up; $v\alpha < 0$: slowing down.

Inflection Points of y = f(x)

Where the bending switches

An inflection point (変曲点) is a point of the curve where concavity changes: from concave up to concave down, or the other way. There the curve crosses its own tangent.

To sketch a graph, combine everything: the sign of $f'$ (rising/falling), the sign of $f''$ (bending), intercepts and asymptotes. Put it all in one table with rows for $f'$, $f''$ and $f$.

Inflection point at $x = a$

$$f''(a) = 0 \;\text{ and }\; f'' \text{ changes sign at } a$$

$f''(a) = 0$ alone is not enough: $x^4$ has $f''(0) = 0$ but is concave up on both sides.

  1. Domain, symmetry, intercepts.
  2. $f'$ and $f''$: their zeros and signs, gathered in one table. In the $f$ row, combine both facts for each interval, e.g. "rising, concave down".
  3. Behaviour as $x \to \pm\infty$ and near excluded points (asymptotes). Then draw.

$f(x) = x^4 - kx^2$: inflection points appear when $k > 0$

Example 1

Find the inflection point of $y = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2$.

Show solution
  1. $y'' = 6x - 6$: negative for $x < 1$, positive for $x > 1$.

$(1, 0)$. (Every cubic graph is point-symmetric about its inflection point.)

Example 2

Find the inflection points of $y = e^{-x^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $y' = -2xe^{-x^2}$, $\;y'' = (4x^2 - 2)e^{-x^2}$.
  2. $y'' = 0 \Rightarrow x = \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$; the sign of $4x^2 - 2$ changes at each.

$\left(\pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}\right)$

Example 3

Sketch $y = xe^{-x}$.

Show solution
  1. $y' = (1 - x)e^{-x}$: $+$ for $x < 1$, $-$ for $x > 1$. Local max $\dfrac{1}{e}$ at $x = 1$.
  2. $y'' = (x - 2)e^{-x}$: concave down for $x < 2$, up for $x > 2$. Inflection point $\left(2, \dfrac{2}{e^2}\right)$.
  3. $x \to \infty$: $y \to 0$ (asymptote $y = 0$). $\;x \to -\infty$: $y \to -\infty$. Passes through $(0, 0)$.

Rises through $O$ to the max $\left(1, \frac{1}{e}\right)$, bends at $\left(2, \frac{2}{e^2}\right)$, then sinks toward the $x$-axis

Treat $f''(a) = 0$ as a candidate only. Check that the sign of $f''$ changes there. A double root of $f''$, like $12x^2$ at $0$, is not an inflection point.
Useful limits when sketching: $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x}{e^x} = 0$, $\;\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\log x}{x} = 0$. Exponentials beat powers, and powers beat logarithms.
  • Inflection point: $f''$ changes sign (and $f'' = 0$ there).
  • Sketching table: rows $f'$, $f''$, $f$; add limits and asymptotes.
  • A cubic is symmetric about its inflection point.

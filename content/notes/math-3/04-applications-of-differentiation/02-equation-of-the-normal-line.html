Equation of the Normal Line

The line at right angles to the tangent

The normal at a point of a curve goes through that point and is perpendicular to the tangent there. Two perpendicular slopes multiply to $-1$, so the normal's slope is $-\dfrac{1}{f'(a)}$.

Normal at $(a, f(a))$, when $f'(a) \ne 0$

$$y - f(a) = -\frac{1}{f'(a)}(x - a)$$

If $f'(a) = 0$ the tangent is horizontal, so the normal is the vertical line $x = a$.

  1. The tangent has direction vector $(1, m)$ with $m = f'(a)$. A line with slope $n$ has direction $(1, n)$.
  2. Perpendicular means the dot product is $0$: $1 + mn = 0$.
  3. So $mn = -1$, i.e. $n = -\dfrac{1}{m}$.

Example 1

Find the normal to $y = \sqrt{x}$ at $x = 4$.

Show solution
  1. Point $(4, 2)$. Tangent slope $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{4}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, so the normal slope is $-4$.
  2. $y - 2 = -4(x - 4)$

$y = -4x + 18$

Example 2

Find the normal to $y = \cos x$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. Point $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, 0\right)$. $y' = -\sin x = -1$, so the normal slope is $1$.

$y = x - \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

Example 3

Find the normal to $y = x^3 - 3x$ at $x = 1$.

Show solution
  1. Point $(1, -2)$. $y' = 3x^2 - 3 = 0$: the tangent is horizontal.

The normal is the vertical line $x = 1$

Example 4

The normal to $y = x^2$ at $(1, 1)$ meets the curve again at $Q$. Find $Q$.

Show solution
  1. Slope of the tangent $2$, so the normal is $y - 1 = -\dfrac{1}{2}(x - 1)$, i.e. $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{3}{2}$.
  2. $x^2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{3}{2} \Rightarrow 2x^2 + x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 1)(2x + 3) = 0$.

$Q = \left(-\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{9}{4}\right)$

Take the negative reciprocal: flip the fraction and change the sign. A tangent slope of $\dfrac{2}{3}$ gives a normal slope of $-\dfrac{3}{2}$, not $\dfrac{3}{2}$ or $-\dfrac{2}{3}$.
A form that works even when $f'(a) = 0$: $(x - a) + f'(a)\{y - f(a)\} = 0$.
  • Normal slope $= -\dfrac{1}{f'(a)}$ (tangent × normal $= -1$).
  • $y - f(a) = -\dfrac{1}{f'(a)}(x - a)$.
  • $f'(a) = 0$: the normal is $x = a$.

Volume of Solid of Revolution About y-axis

Horizontal disks, integrated in $y$

Spinning about the $y$-axis makes slices that are horizontal disks. The radius of the slice at height $y$ is the distance from the $y$-axis, $|x|$.

So write $x^2$ in terms of $y$ and integrate over $y$. If $x^2$ is hard to express in $y$, keep $x$ and substitute $y = f(x)$, $dy = f'(x)\,dx$.

Rotating about the $y$-axis, $c \le y \le d$

$$V = \pi\int_c^d x^2\,dy = \pi\int_c^d \{g(y)\}^2\,dy \qquad (x = g(y))$$

With $y = f(x)$ and $dy = f'(x)\,dx$: $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} x^2 f'(x)\,dx$. Here $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the $x$-values where $y = c$ and $y = d$.

  1. The slice at height $y$ is a disk of radius $|g(y)|$, with area $S(y) = \pi g(y)^2$.
  2. Adding thin slices of thickness $dy$: $V = \displaystyle\int_c^d S(y)\,dy$, the formula of note 18 with $y$ in place of $x$.
  3. Changing the variable to $x$ is ordinary substitution (note 09).

The solid filled up to height $h$ (horizontal disks)

Example 1

A bowl is made by rotating $y = x^2$ about the $y$-axis. Find the volume of water when it is filled to height $h$.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 = y$, so $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^h y\,dy$.

$\dfrac{\pi h^2}{2}$

Example 2

The region between $y = 4 - x^2$ and the $x$-axis is rotated about the $y$-axis. Find the volume.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 = 4 - y$, $0 \le y \le 4$. The left and right halves give the same solid, so count it once.
  2. $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^4 (4 - y)\,dy = \pi \cdot 8$

$8\pi$

Example 3

The region bounded by $y = \log x$, the $y$-axis and the lines $y = 0$, $y = 1$ is rotated about the $y$-axis. Find the volume.

Show solution
  1. $x = e^y$, so $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{2y}\,dy = \pi\Big[\frac{e^{2y}}{2}\Big]_0^1$.

$\dfrac{\pi(e^2 - 1)}{2}$

Example 4

The region bounded by $y = \sin x$ ($0 \le x \le \dfrac{\pi}{2}$), the $y$-axis and the line $y = 1$ is rotated about the $y$-axis. Find the volume.

Show solution
  1. $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^1 x^2\,dy$. Writing $x$ in $y$ is hard, so put $y = \sin x$, $dy = \cos x\,dx$. Limits: $y$: $0 \to 1$ gives $x$: $0 \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
  2. $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} x^2\cos x\,dx$. Use parts twice: $\Big[x^2\sin x + 2x\cos x - 2\sin x\Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \dfrac{\pi^2}{4} - 2$.

$\pi\left(\dfrac{\pi^2}{4} - 2\right) = \dfrac{\pi^3}{4} - 2\pi$

Rotating the same region about the $y$-axis usually gives a different volume than about the $x$-axis. Don't reuse $\pi\int y^2\,dx$. Also, a region with parts on both sides of the $y$-axis must not be counted twice. Use the larger $|x|$ at each height.
If $x$ can't be written neatly in terms of $y$, don't try. Substitute $dy = f'(x)\,dx$ and integrate in $x$, as in Example 4.
  • $V = \pi\int_c^d x^2\,dy$: horizontal disks, limits in $y$.
  • Express $x^2$ in terms of $y$, or substitute $y = f(x)$.
  • Bowl $y = x^2$ filled to height $h$: $\dfrac{\pi h^2}{2}$.

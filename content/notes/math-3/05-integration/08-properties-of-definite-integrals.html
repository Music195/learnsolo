Properties of Definite Integrals

Areas add up, and direction matters

Definite integrals split like indefinite ones: constant factors come out and sums split. You can also split the interval: the integral from $a$ to $c$ equals the integral from $a$ to $b$ plus the one from $b$ to $c$.

Going backwards, from $b$ down to $a$, flips the sign. This is how integrals of $|f(x)|$ are handled: split at the points where $f$ changes sign.

Properties ($k$, $l$: constants)

$$\int_a^b \{kf(x) + lg(x)\}\,dx = k\int_a^b f(x)\,dx + l\int_a^b g(x)\,dx$$
$$\int_a^a f(x)\,dx = 0, \qquad \int_b^a f(x)\,dx = -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx + \int_b^c f(x)\,dx = \int_a^c f(x)\,dx$$

The last one holds for any order of $a$, $b$, $c$, even if $b$ is not between $a$ and $c$.

  1. $\displaystyle\int_a^a f\,dx = F(a) - F(a) = 0$, and $\displaystyle\int_b^a f\,dx = F(a) - F(b) = -\{F(b) - F(a)\}$.
  2. $\{F(b) - F(a)\} + \{F(c) - F(b)\} = F(c) - F(a)$: the middle terms cancel.
  3. Linearity: $kF + lG$ is an antiderivative of $kf + lg$ (note 02).

$\displaystyle\int_a^b + \int_b^c = \int_a^c$ for $f(x) = 1 + \sin 2x$

Example 1

$\displaystyle\int_0^2 f(x)\,dx = 3$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^5 f(x)\,dx = 7$. Find $\displaystyle\int_5^2 f(x)\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_2^5 f\,dx = \int_0^5 f\,dx - \int_0^2 f\,dx = 4$.
  2. Reversing the limits flips the sign.

$-4$

Example 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^3 |x - 1|\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. Split at $x = 1$: $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (1 - x)\,dx + \int_1^3 (x - 1)\,dx$.
  2. $= \dfrac{1}{2} + \Big[\dfrac{x^2}{2} - x\Big]_1^3 = \dfrac{1}{2} + \left(\dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

$\dfrac{5}{2}$

Example 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^2 \frac{2x - 1}{x}\,dx + \int_1^2 \frac{1}{x}\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. Same limits, so combine: $\displaystyle\int_1^2 \frac{2x - 1 + 1}{x}\,dx = \int_1^2 2\,dx$.

$2$

Example 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} |\cos x|\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\cos x \ge 0$ on $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$ and $\le 0$ on $\left[\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right]$.
  2. $\Big[\sin x\Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} - \Big[\sin x\Big]_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi} = 1 - (0 - 1)$

$2$

$\displaystyle\int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx$ is not $\left|\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\right|$. You must split at the zeros of $f$ and remove the absolute value on each piece.
When two integrals have the same limits, or limits that join up, combine them first. The integrand often simplifies.
  • Linearity holds for definite integrals.
  • $\int_a^b + \int_b^c = \int_a^c$; swapping the limits flips the sign.
  • For $|f|$, split at the zeros of $f$.

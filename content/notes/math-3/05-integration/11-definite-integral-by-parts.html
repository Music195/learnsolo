Definite Integral: Integration by Parts

Evaluate the $fg$ part straight away

Integration by parts with limits gives a bracket $\Big[f(x)g(x)\Big]_a^b$ and a new integral. Evaluate the bracket as a number at once; often one end gives $0$.

Using parts repeatedly gives a reduction formula, which links $I_n$ to $I_{n-2}$. The classic case is $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^n x\,dx$.

Parts with limits

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,g'(x)\,dx = \Big[f(x)\,g(x)\Big]_a^b - \int_a^b f'(x)\,g(x)\,dx$$
$$I_n = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^n x\,dx: \qquad I_n = \frac{n - 1}{n}I_{n-2} \;\;(n \ge 2), \quad I_0 = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad I_1 = 1$$
  1. Write $\sin^n x = \sin^{n-1}x \cdot \sin x$. Differentiate $\sin^{n-1}x$ and integrate $\sin x$ to $-\cos x$.
  2. $I_n = \Big[-\sin^{n-1}x\cos x\Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} + (n - 1)\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^{n-2}x\cos^2 x\,dx$. The bracket is $0$ at both ends.
  3. Use $\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$: $I_n = (n - 1)(I_{n-2} - I_n)$, so $nI_n = (n - 1)I_{n-2}$.

Area under $\sin^n x$ on $\left[0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$

Example 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x e^x\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[x e^x\Big]_0^1 - \displaystyle\int_0^1 e^x\,dx = e - (e - 1)$

$1$

Example 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^e \log x\,dx$ and $\displaystyle\int_1^e x\log x\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[x\log x - x\Big]_1^e = (e - e) - (0 - 1) = 1$
  2. $\Big[\dfrac{x^2}{2}\log x\Big]_1^e - \displaystyle\int_1^e \frac{x^2}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \frac{e^2}{2} - \Big[\frac{x^2}{4}\Big]_1^e = \frac{e^2}{2} - \frac{e^2 - 1}{4}$

$1$ and $\dfrac{e^2 + 1}{4}$

Example 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} x\sin x\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\Big[-x\cos x\Big]_0^{\pi} + \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \cos x\,dx = \pi + 0$

$\pi$

Example 4

Use the reduction formula to find $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^5 x\,dx$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^6 x\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $I_5 = \dfrac{4}{5} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot I_1 = \dfrac{8}{15}$
  2. $I_6 = \dfrac{5}{6} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot I_0 = \dfrac{15}{48} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

$\dfrac{8}{15}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{32}$

The bracket $\Big[fg\Big]_a^b$ needs both limits. Don't evaluate it at $b$ only. And the new integral keeps the same limits.
For $\displaystyle\int_\alpha^\beta (x - \alpha)(x - \beta)\,dx$, differentiating $(x - \alpha)$ gives a bracket that is $0$ at both ends. The result is $-\dfrac{(\beta - \alpha)^3}{6}$, the well-known "1/6 formula".
  • $\int_a^b fg' = \Big[fg\Big]_a^b - \int_a^b f'g$.
  • Choose $f$, $g$ so the bracket is simple (often $0$ at an end).
  • $I_n = \dfrac{n - 1}{n}I_{n-2}$ for $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^n x\,dx$ (the same holds for $\cos^n x$).

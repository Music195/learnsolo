Properties of Indefinite Integrals

Integrate term by term

Differentiation works term by term and lets constant factors pass through. Integration is its reverse, so it does the same. That means you can split a sum into pieces, integrate each piece, and keep the constant factors in front.

One $+ C$ at the end is enough. The constants from each piece add up to a single constant.

Linearity ($k$, $l$: constants)

$$\int k f(x)\,dx = k\int f(x)\,dx, \qquad \int \{f(x) \pm g(x)\}\,dx = \int f(x)\,dx \pm \int g(x)\,dx$$
$$\int \{k f(x) + l g(x)\}\,dx = k\int f(x)\,dx + l\int g(x)\,dx$$
  1. Let $F' = f$ and $G' = g$.
  2. Then $\{kF + lG\}' = kF' + lG' = kf + lg$, so $kF + lG$ is an antiderivative of $kf + lg$.
  3. Therefore $\displaystyle\int (kf + lg)\,dx = kF + lG + C$, which is the formula.

$f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ (blue) and its antiderivative $F$ (orange)

Example 1

Find $\displaystyle\int (3x^2 - 4x + 5)\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $3 \cdot \dfrac{x^3}{3} - 4 \cdot \dfrac{x^2}{2} + 5x + C$

$x^3 - 2x^2 + 5x + C$

Example 2

Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{(x + 1)^2}{x}\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. Expand and divide first: $\dfrac{x^2 + 2x + 1}{x} = x + 2 + \dfrac{1}{x}$.
  2. Integrate each term: $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + 2x + \log|x| + C$.

$\dfrac{x^2}{2} + 2x + \log|x| + C$

Example 3

Find $\displaystyle\int \frac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{x - 1}{\sqrt{x}} = x^{\frac{1}{2}} - x^{-\frac{1}{2}}$
  2. $\dfrac{2}{3}x^{\frac{3}{2}} - 2x^{\frac{1}{2}} + C$

$\dfrac{2}{3}x\sqrt{x} - 2\sqrt{x} + C$

Example 4

A curve passes through $(1, 2)$, and its slope at every point is $6x^2 - 2$. Find the curve.

Show solution
  1. $y = \displaystyle\int (6x^2 - 2)\,dx = 2x^3 - 2x + C$.
  2. At $x = 1$: $2 - 2 + C = 2$, so $C = 2$.

$y = 2x^3 - 2x + 2$

There is no "product rule" of this kind: $\displaystyle\int f(x)g(x)\,dx$ is not $\displaystyle\int f\,dx \cdot \int g\,dx$. Expand the product first, or use integration by parts (note 06).
Before integrating, rewrite the integrand as a sum of powers: expand brackets, and split fractions into separate terms.
  • Constant factors come out; sums split into separate integrals.
  • Write a single $+ C$ at the end.
  • A condition like "passes through $(1, 2)$" fixes the value of $C$.

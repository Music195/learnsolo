Area Between Two Curves

Top minus bottom

Slice the region into thin vertical strips. Each strip has height (upper curve) − (lower curve), wherever the curves are, even below the axis. Integrate that height across the region.

The curves meet where $f(x) = g(x)$; these points are usually the limits. If the curves cross inside the interval, top and bottom swap there. Split at that point.

$f(x) \ge g(x)$ on $[a, b]$

$$S = \int_a^b \{f(x) - g(x)\}\,dx, \qquad \text{in general } S = \int_a^b |f(x) - g(x)|\,dx$$

For a parabola and a line, or two parabolas, meeting at $\alpha < \beta$: $S = \dfrac{|a|}{6}(\beta - \alpha)^3$. Here $a$ is the $x^2$ coefficient of $f - g$.

  1. Shift both curves up by the same constant $c$ so they lie above the axis. The region keeps its area.
  2. Then the area is (area under $f + c$) − (area under $g + c$) $= \displaystyle\int_a^b \{(f + c) - (g + c)\}\,dx$.
  3. The $c$ cancels, leaving $\displaystyle\int_a^b (f - g)\,dx$, whatever the position of the axis.

Region between $f$ (blue) and $g$ (orange); colour shows which is on top

Example 1

Find the area between $y = x$ and $y = x^2 - 2$.

Show solution
  1. Meet: $x^2 - 2 = x$, $(x - 2)(x + 1) = 0$, so $x = -1, 2$. The line is on top between them.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (x - x^2 + 2)\,dx = \frac{1}{6}(2 - (-1))^3$

$\dfrac{9}{2}$

Example 2

Find the total area enclosed by $y = x^3$ and $y = x$.

Show solution
  1. Meet: $x^3 = x$, so $x = -1, 0, 1$.
  2. On $[-1, 0]$, $x^3 \ge x$ (e.g. at $x = -\frac{1}{2}$: $-\frac{1}{8} > -\frac{1}{2}$). On $[0, 1]$, $x \ge x^3$.
  3. $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^0 (x^3 - x)\,dx + \int_0^1 (x - x^3)\,dx = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4}$

$\dfrac{1}{2}$

Example 3

Find the area enclosed by $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ for $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \le x \le \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

Show solution
  1. They meet at $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, with $\sin x$ on top in between.
  2. $\Big[-\cos x - \sin x\Big]_{\frac{\pi}{4}}^{\frac{5\pi}{4}} = \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) - \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$

$2\sqrt{2}$

Example 4

Find the area between $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = x^2$.

Show solution
  1. Meet at $x = 0, 1$. On $[0, 1]$, $\sqrt{x} \ge x^2$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (\sqrt{x} - x^2)\,dx = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{3}$

$\dfrac{1}{3}$

Example 5

Find the area between $y = e^x$, $y = e^{-x}$ and the line $x = 1$.

Show solution
  1. They meet at $x = 0$; on $[0, 1]$, $e^x \ge e^{-x}$.
  2. $\Big[e^x + e^{-x}\Big]_0^1 = e + \dfrac{1}{e} - 2$

$e + \dfrac{1}{e} - 2$

If the curves cross inside the interval, $\displaystyle\int (f - g)$ over the whole interval lets the pieces cancel (Example 2 would give $0$). Split at every crossing.
To see which curve is on top, plug one $x$ from inside the piece into both functions. That is quicker than sketching.
  • Area $= \int (\text{top} - \text{bottom})\,dx$, wherever the axis is.
  • Limits: solve $f(x) = g(x)$.
  • Split where the curves cross; the 1/6 formula for parabolas.

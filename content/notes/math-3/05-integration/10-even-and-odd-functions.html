Definite Integrals of Even and Odd Functions

Symmetric limits: double it or drop it

An even function ($f(-x) = f(x)$) is symmetric about the $y$-axis. Its left half matches its right half, so the integral over $[-a, a]$ is twice the integral over $[0, a]$.

An odd function ($f(-x) = -f(x)$) is symmetric about the origin. Its left half is the right half flipped below the axis, so the two parts cancel and the integral is $0$.

Integrals over $[-a, a]$

$$f \text{ even: } \int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = 2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx, \qquad f \text{ odd: } \int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = 0$$

Even: $x^2$, $x^4$, constants, $\cos x$, $|x|$. Odd: $x$, $x^3$, $\sin x$, $\tan x$. A polynomial splits into its even-power and odd-power terms.

  1. Split: $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^a f\,dx = \int_{-a}^0 f(x)\,dx + \int_0^a f(x)\,dx$.
  2. In the first part put $x = -t$, $dx = -dt$. Limits: $x$: $-a \to 0$ gives $t$: $a \to 0$. So $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^0 f(x)\,dx = \int_a^0 f(-t)(-dt) = \int_0^a f(-t)\,dt$.
  3. Even: $f(-t) = f(t)$, so the first part equals $\displaystyle\int_0^a f\,dt$ and the total is twice that. Odd: $f(-t) = -f(t)$, so the first part equals $-\displaystyle\int_0^a f\,dt$ and the total is $0$.

Example 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2} (x^3 + 3x^2 - 5x + 1)\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. The odd terms $x^3$ and $-5x$ give $0$.
  2. $2\displaystyle\int_0^2 (3x^2 + 1)\,dx = 2\Big[x^3 + x\Big]_0^2 = 2 \times 10$

$20$

Example 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} (\sin x + \cos x)\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin x$ is odd, so its part is $0$.
  2. $2\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \cos x\,dx = 2\Big[\sin x\Big]_0^{\pi} = 0$

$0$

Example 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{x^3}{x^2 + 1}\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $f(-x) = \dfrac{-x^3}{x^2 + 1} = -f(x)$, so $f$ is odd.

$0$ (no integration needed)

Example 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} (e^x + e^{-x})\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $e^x + e^{-x}$ is even.
  2. $2\Big[e^x - e^{-x}\Big]_0^1 = 2\left(e - \dfrac{1}{e}\right)$

$2e - \dfrac{2}{e}$

These shortcuts need limits that are symmetric about $0$, like $[-a, a]$. Over $[0, a]$ or $[-1, 2]$ an odd function's integral is usually not $0$.
Over $[-a, a]$, drop the odd-power terms of a polynomial straight away and double the integral of the rest over $[0, a]$. It saves work and avoids sign errors.
  • Even: $\int_{-a}^a = 2\int_0^a$.
  • Odd: $\int_{-a}^a = 0$.
  • Proof: substitute $x = -t$ in the left half.

Area Bounded by x = g(y)

Slice sideways

Some regions are easier to cut into horizontal strips. This happens when the curve is given as $x = g(y)$, or when the left and right edges stay the same all the way up but the top and bottom don't.

Each strip has length (right curve) − (left curve) and thickness $dy$. Integrate over $y$ from the bottom of the region to the top.

$c \le y \le d$

$$S = \int_c^d |g(y)|\,dy$$
$$S = \int_c^d \{(\text{right } x) - (\text{left } x)\}\,dy$$

The first is the area between $x = g(y)$ and the $y$-axis. Limits are $y$-values: solve for where the curves meet in terms of $y$.

  1. Swap the roles of $x$ and $y$ (reflect in the line $y = x$). Reflection keeps areas.
  2. After the swap, $x = g(y)$ becomes $y = g(x)$, and the strip is a usual vertical one.
  3. So the earlier formulas hold with $x$ and $y$ exchanged.

Move the horizontal strip: length = right $x$ − left $x$

Example 1

Find the area between $x = 2y - y^2$ and the $y$-axis.

Show solution
  1. $x = 0$ when $y = 0, 2$; between them $x \ge 0$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (2y - y^2)\,dy = 4 - \frac{8}{3}$

$\dfrac{4}{3}$

Example 2

Find the area enclosed by $y^2 = x$ and $y = x - 2$.

Show solution
  1. In terms of $y$: left edge $x = y^2$, right edge $x = y + 2$. They meet where $y^2 = y + 2$, i.e. $y = -1, 2$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (y + 2 - y^2)\,dy = \frac{1}{6}(2 - (-1))^3$

$\dfrac{9}{2}$ (slicing vertically would need two separate integrals)

Example 3

Find the area bounded by $y = \log x$, the $y$-axis, and the lines $y = 0$ and $y = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $x = e^y$; strips run from the $y$-axis to $x = e^y$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^y\,dy = e - 1$

$e - 1$

Example 4

Find the area bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$, the $y$-axis and the line $y = 2$.

Show solution
  1. $x = y^2$ ($y \ge 0$), from the $y$-axis to the curve, $0 \le y \le 2$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^2 y^2\,dy = \frac{8}{3}$

$\dfrac{8}{3}$

When you integrate in $y$, the limits must be $y$-values and the integrand must be written in $y$. Don't use the $x$-coordinates of the meeting points.
Slice sideways if vertical strips would change their top or bottom curve partway, as in Example 2. Also do it if $y = f(x)$ is hard to integrate but $x = g(y)$ is easy, as with $\log x$ and $e^y$.
  • $S = \int_c^d (\text{right} - \text{left})\,dy$.
  • Write each boundary as $x = g(y)$; limits are $y$-values.
  • Pick the direction that gives one simple integral.

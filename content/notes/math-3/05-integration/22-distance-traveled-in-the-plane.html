Distance Traveled On the Coordinate Plane

Add up the speed over time

A point moves with $x = f(t)$, $y = g(t)$. Its speed is the length of the velocity vector, $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{(x')^2 + (y')^2}$ (Applied Differentiation note 10).

Distance is speed × time for each small time step. Integrating the speed gives the total distance travelled along the path. This is usually more than the straight-line distance between the start and the end.

From $t = a$ to $t = b$

$$\text{distance} = \int_a^b |\vec{v}|\,dt = \int_a^b \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$$

The position at time $b$ is $\left(x(a) + \displaystyle\int_a^b x'\,dt,\; y(a) + \int_a^b y'\,dt\right)$, where each coordinate is integrated separately.

  1. In a short time $\Delta t$ the point moves $\Delta x \approx x'\Delta t$ and $\Delta y \approx y'\Delta t$.
  2. By Pythagoras that step has length $\sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2} \approx \sqrt{x'^2 + y'^2}\,\Delta t$.
  3. Adding the steps and letting $\Delta t \to 0$ gives the integral.

Distance along the path (dark blue) vs straight-line distance (dashed)

Example 1

P moves with $(x, y) = (3\cos t, 3\sin t)$. Find the distance travelled from $t = 0$ to $t = \pi$.

Show solution
  1. $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{9\sin^2 t + 9\cos^2 t} = 3$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} 3\,dt$

$3\pi$ (half the circumference), though P ends only $6$ away from its start

Example 2

P moves with $(x, y) = (t - \sin t, 1 - \cos t)$. Find the distance travelled from $t = 0$ to $t = 2\pi$.

Show solution
  1. $|\vec{v}|^2 = (1 - \cos t)^2 + \sin^2 t = 2 - 2\cos t = 4\sin^2\dfrac{t}{2}$.
  2. On $[0, 2\pi]$, $\sin\dfrac{t}{2} \ge 0$, so $|\vec{v}| = 2\sin\dfrac{t}{2}$.
  3. $\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi} 2\sin\frac{t}{2}\,dt = \Big[-4\cos\frac{t}{2}\Big]_0^{2\pi} = 4 + 4$

$8$

Example 3

P moves with $(x, y) = (e^t\cos t, e^t\sin t)$. Find the distance travelled from $t = 0$ to $t = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $x' = e^t(\cos t - \sin t)$, $y' = e^t(\sin t + \cos t)$.
  2. $x'^2 + y'^2 = e^{2t}\{(\cos t - \sin t)^2 + (\sin t + \cos t)^2\} = 2e^{2t}$, so $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{2}e^t$.
  3. $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \sqrt{2}e^t\,dt$

$\sqrt{2}(e - 1)$

Example 4

P starts at $(0, 0)$ with velocity $\vec{v} = (2t, 3t^2)$. Find its position and the distance travelled at $t = 2$.

Show solution
  1. Position: $\left(\displaystyle\int_0^2 2t\,dt, \int_0^2 3t^2\,dt\right) = (4, 8)$.
  2. $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{4t^2 + 9t^4} = t\sqrt{4 + 9t^2}$ for $t \ge 0$.
  3. Put $u = 4 + 9t^2$, $du = 18t\,dt$: $\displaystyle\frac{1}{18}\int_4^{40} \sqrt{u}\,du = \frac{1}{27}\left(40\sqrt{40} - 8\right)$.

Position $(4, 8)$; distance $\dfrac{80\sqrt{10} - 8}{27} \approx 9.07$

Distance travelled is not $\int x'\,dt + \int y'\,dt$, and it is not the straight-line distance between the start and the end. Integrate the length $\sqrt{x'^2 + y'^2}$, and take the square root of a perfect square with its correct sign.
Expand $x'^2 + y'^2$ and look for $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$ or a perfect square. Most textbook problems are designed so the square root comes out cleanly.
  • Distance $= \int_a^b \sqrt{x'^2 + y'^2}\,dt$.
  • Position: integrate $x'$ and $y'$ separately.
  • $1 - \cos t = 2\sin^2\frac{t}{2}$ is the key step for cycloids.

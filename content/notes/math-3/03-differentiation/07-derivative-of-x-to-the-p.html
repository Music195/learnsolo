Derivative of x to the Power p

One rule for every power

You know $(x^n)' = nx^{n-1}$ for whole numbers, and note 05 extended it to negative integers. It also works for fractional powers like $x^{\frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{x}$: bring the power down in front, then lower the power by $1$.

So roots and reciprocals don't need their own formulas. Rewrite them as powers first.

Power rule ($x > 0$, $p$ rational)

$$(x^p)' = px^{p-1}$$

It holds for every real $p$ (proved with logarithms in note 09). Common cases: $(\sqrt{x})' = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$, $\;\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)' = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$.

  1. Let $p = \dfrac{m}{n}$ ($m$ an integer, $n$ a positive integer) and $y = x^{\frac{m}{n}}$. Then $y^n = x^m$.
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$ (chain rule on the left): $ny^{n-1}y' = mx^{m-1}$.
  3. $y' = \dfrac{m}{n} \cdot \dfrac{x^{m-1}}{y^{n-1}} = \dfrac{m}{n} \cdot \dfrac{x^{m-1}}{x^{\frac{m}{n}(n-1)}} = \dfrac{m}{n}x^{\frac{m}{n} - 1}$.

$y = x^p$ (blue) and $y' = px^{p-1}$ (orange) for $x > 0$

Example 1

Differentiate $y = x\sqrt{x}$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$.

Show solution
  1. $x\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{3}{2}}$, so $y' = \dfrac{3}{2}x^{\frac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{x}$.
  2. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}} = x^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, so $y' = -\dfrac{1}{2}x^{-\frac{3}{2}} = -\dfrac{1}{2x\sqrt{x}}$.

$\dfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{x}$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2x\sqrt{x}}$

Example 2

Differentiate $y = \sqrt[3]{x^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $y = x^{\frac{2}{3}}$, so $y' = \dfrac{2}{3}x^{-\frac{1}{3}}$.

$y' = \dfrac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x}}$

Example 3

Differentiate $y = \sqrt{x^2 + 1}$.

Show solution
  1. $y = (x^2 + 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}$. Chain rule: $y' = \dfrac{1}{2}(x^2 + 1)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \cdot 2x$.

$y' = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$

Example 4

Find the equation of the tangent to $y = \sqrt{x}$ at $x = 4$.

Show solution
  1. Point $(4, 2)$. Slope $y' = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{4}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
  2. $y - 2 = \dfrac{1}{4}(x - 4)$

$y = \dfrac{1}{4}x + 1$

Rewrite before differentiating. $\dfrac{1}{x^3}$ is $x^{-3}$, so its derivative is $-3x^{-4}$, not $\dfrac{1}{3x^2}$. Lowering a negative power by $1$ makes it more negative: $-3 \to -4$.
Work with powers, then convert back to roots and fractions for the final answer: $\dfrac{2}{3}x^{-\frac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x}}$.
  • $(x^p)' = px^{p-1}$ for any rational (in fact any real) $p$, $x > 0$.
  • $\sqrt[n]{x^m} = x^{\frac{m}{n}}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^n} = x^{-n}$: rewrite, then use the rule.
  • With an inside function, add the chain rule factor.

Definition of Derivative

The slope of a secant, squeezed to a tangent

Take two points on $y = f(x)$, at $x = a$ and $x = a + h$. The line through them (a secant) has slope $\dfrac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$, the average rate of change.

Let $h \to 0$. The second point slides onto the first and the secant turns into the tangent. The limit of its slope is the derivative $f'(a)$, the rate of change at that instant.

Derivative at $x = a$ (微分係数)

$$f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}$$

Derived function (導関数)

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}$$

Also written $y'$, $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}f(x)$. Finding $f'(x)$ is called differentiating $f$.

  1. Take $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$. Then $f(x + h) - f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x + h} - \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{-h}{x(x + h)}$.
  2. Divide by $h$: $\dfrac{-1}{x(x + h)}$. The troublesome $h$ in the denominator is gone.
  3. Let $h \to 0$: $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$.

Shrink $h$ and watch the secant become the tangent

Example 1

Use the definition to find $f'(3)$ for $f(x) = x^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{(3 + h)^2 - 9}{h} = \dfrac{6h + h^2}{h} = 6 + h$
  2. $h \to 0$

$f'(3) = 6$

Example 2

Use the definition to find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ ($x > 0$).

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{\sqrt{x + h} - \sqrt{x}}{h}$: multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{x + h} + \sqrt{x}$.
  2. $= \dfrac{(x + h) - x}{h(\sqrt{x + h} + \sqrt{x})} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x + h} + \sqrt{x}}$
  3. $h \to 0$: $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$

$f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$

Example 3

If $f'(a) = 3$, find $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + 2h) - f(a)}{h}$.

Show solution
  1. Make the step in the bracket match the denominator: $\dfrac{f(a + 2h) - f(a)}{h} = 2 \cdot \dfrac{f(a + 2h) - f(a)}{2h}$.
  2. As $h \to 0$, $2h \to 0$, so the fraction tends to $f'(a)$.

$2f'(a) = 6$

You can't just put $h = 0$: that gives $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Simplify first so that the $h$ in the denominator cancels, then let $h \to 0$.
In limits like Example 3, the step added inside $f(a + \square)$ must equal the denominator $\square$. Multiply and divide by a constant to make them match.

  • $f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}$.

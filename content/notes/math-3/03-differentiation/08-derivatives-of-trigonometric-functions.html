Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions

The slope of a sine wave is a cosine wave

Walk along $y = \sin x$ (in radians). At $x = 0$ it climbs most steeply (slope $1$). At $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ it is flat (slope $0$). At $x = \pi$ it falls most steeply (slope $-1$). Plot those slopes and you get exactly $\cos x$.

The proof rests on $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin h}{h} = 1$ (Limit note 08), which is why $x$ must be in radians.

Basic derivatives (radians)

$$(\sin x)' = \cos x, \qquad (\cos x)' = -\sin x, \qquad (\tan x)' = \frac{1}{\cos^2 x}$$

With an inside function (chain rule): $\{\sin(ax + b)\}' = a\cos(ax + b)$, $\;\{\cos(ax + b)\}' = -a\sin(ax + b)$.

  1. Addition formula: $\dfrac{\sin(x + h) - \sin x}{h} = \sin x \cdot \dfrac{\cos h - 1}{h} + \cos x \cdot \dfrac{\sin h}{h}$.
  2. $\dfrac{\cos h - 1}{h} = \dfrac{\cos^2 h - 1}{h(\cos h + 1)} = -\dfrac{\sin h}{h} \cdot \dfrac{\sin h}{\cos h + 1} \to -1 \cdot \dfrac{0}{2} = 0$.
  3. So the limit is $\sin x \cdot 0 + \cos x \cdot 1 = \cos x$. The same steps with $\cos(x + h) = \cos x\cos h - \sin x\sin h$ give $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$.
  4. $(\tan x)' = \left(\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\right)' = \dfrac{\cos^2 x + \sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$ (quotient rule).

Trace the slope of the curve

Example 1

Differentiate $y = \sin 2x$ and $y = \cos^3 x$.

Show solution
  1. $(\sin 2x)' = \cos 2x \cdot 2$
  2. $(\cos^3 x)' = 3\cos^2 x \cdot (\cos x)' = 3\cos^2 x \cdot (-\sin x)$

$2\cos 2x$ and $-3\cos^2 x\sin x$

Example 2

Differentiate $y = \tan 3x$ and $y = x\sin x$.

Show solution
  1. $(\tan 3x)' = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 3x} \cdot 3$
  2. Product rule: $(x\sin x)' = 1 \cdot \sin x + x\cos x$

$\dfrac{3}{\cos^2 3x}$ and $\sin x + x\cos x$

Example 3

Differentiate $y = \dfrac{\sin x}{1 + \cos x}$.

Show solution
  1. $y' = \dfrac{\cos x(1 + \cos x) - \sin x(-\sin x)}{(1 + \cos x)^2} = \dfrac{\cos x + \cos^2 x + \sin^2 x}{(1 + \cos x)^2}$
  2. $= \dfrac{1 + \cos x}{(1 + \cos x)^2}$

$y' = \dfrac{1}{1 + \cos x}$

Example 4

Differentiate $y = \sin^2 x$.

Show solution
  1. $y' = 2\sin x \cdot (\sin x)' = 2\sin x\cos x$

$y' = 2\sin x\cos x = \sin 2x$

The minus sign belongs to $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, not to $(\sin x)'$. These formulas need radians: in degrees, $(\sin x°)' = \dfrac{\pi}{180}\cos x°$.
Differentiating repeatedly cycles: $\sin x \to \cos x \to -\sin x \to -\cos x \to \sin x$. Each step shifts the graph $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the left.
  • $(\sin x)' = \cos x$, $\;(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, $\;(\tan x)' = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$.
  • Proof: addition formula plus $\dfrac{\sin h}{h} \to 1$.
  • Inside functions: multiply by the inner derivative.

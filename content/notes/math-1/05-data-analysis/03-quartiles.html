Quartiles

Cut the ordered data into four

The median $Q_2$ splits the ordered data into a lower half and an upper half. The median of the lower half is the first quartile $Q_1$; the median of the upper half is the third quartile $Q_3$.

Roughly a quarter of the data is below $Q_1$, a quarter between each pair of quartiles, and a quarter above $Q_3$.

Finding $Q_1$, $Q_2$, $Q_3$

$$\underbrace{\;x_1 \cdots\;}_{\text{lower half} \to Q_1}\;\; Q_2 \;\;\underbrace{\;\cdots x_n\;}_{\text{upper half} \to Q_3}$$

If $n$ is odd, the median itself belongs to neither half. If $n$ is even, the data splits exactly in two.

Interquartile range

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$

The width of the middle half of the data (see note 05).

Quartile finder

Example 1 ($n$ odd)

Find the quartiles of 12, 7, 15, 9, 21, 18, 13.

Show solution
  1. In order: 7, 9, 12, 13, 15, 18, 21. $Q_2 = 13$.
  2. Lower half 7, 9, 12 → $Q_1 = 9$. Upper half 15, 18, 21 → $Q_3 = 18$.

$Q_1 = 9$, $Q_2 = 13$, $Q_3 = 18$

Example 2 ($n$ even)

Find the quartiles of 4, 7, 2, 9, 5, 8, 10, 6.

Show solution
  1. In order: 2, 4, 5, 6 | 7, 8, 9, 10. $Q_2 = \dfrac{6 + 7}{2} = 6.5$.
  2. Lower half 2, 4, 5, 6 → $Q_1 = 4.5$. Upper half 7, 8, 9, 10 → $Q_3 = 8.5$.

$Q_1 = 4.5$, $Q_2 = 6.5$, $Q_3 = 8.5$

Example 3

Find $Q_1$, $Q_3$ and the IQR of 3, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18.

Show solution
  1. Lower half 3, 7, 9 → $Q_1 = 7$. Upper half 12, 15, 18 → $Q_3 = 15$.
  2. IQR $= 15 - 7$

$Q_1 = 7$, $Q_3 = 15$, IQR $= 8$

Spreadsheets and some textbooks use other quartile rules and can give slightly different numbers. In Japanese high school maths, use the halves method above.
  • $Q_2$ = median; $Q_1$, $Q_3$ = medians of the lower and upper halves.
  • $n$ odd: leave the median out of both halves.
  • IQR $= Q_3 - Q_1$.

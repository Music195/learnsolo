Quartile Range & Deviation

How spread out is the middle half?

The range (max − min) uses only the two most extreme values, so one odd value can make it huge.

The interquartile range looks only at the middle half of the data, between $Q_1$ and $Q_3$, so extreme values hardly affect it.

Measures of spread

$$\text{range} = \max - \min, \qquad \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1, \qquad \text{quartile deviation} = \frac{Q_3 - Q_1}{2}$$

IQR: interquartile range (四分位範囲). Quartile deviation (四分位偏差) is half of it.

Range vs IQR

Add a very large value and watch which measure jumps.

Example 1

Find the IQR and quartile deviation of 5, 8, 12, 15, 18, 20, 22.

Show solution
  1. $n = 7$, median 15 (left out). Lower half 5, 8, 12 → $Q_1 = 8$. Upper half 18, 20, 22 → $Q_3 = 20$.
  2. IQR $= 20 - 8 = 12$

IQR $= 12$, quartile deviation $= 6$

Example 2

Find the IQR and quartile deviation of 3, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18.

Show solution
  1. $n = 6$. Lower half 3, 7, 9 → $Q_1 = 7$. Upper half 12, 15, 18 → $Q_3 = 15$.

IQR $= 8$, quartile deviation $= 4$

Example 3

For 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 100, compare the range and the IQR.

Show solution
  1. Range $= 100 - 1 = 99$.
  2. Lower half 1, 3, 5 → $Q_1 = 3$. Upper half 9, 11, 100 → $Q_3 = 11$. IQR $= 8$.

Range 99, IQR 8. The single value 100 blows up the range but barely changes the IQR.

Find $Q_1$ and $Q_3$ correctly first (note 03): when $n$ is odd, the median is left out of both halves.
  • IQR $= Q_3 - Q_1$: spread of the middle half.
  • Quartile deviation $= \dfrac{Q_3 - Q_1}{2}$.
  • Range reacts strongly to extreme values; the IQR doesn't.

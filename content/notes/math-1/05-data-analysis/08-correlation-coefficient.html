Correlation Coefficient

Do two things go up together?

Plot pairs of data $(x, y)$ as a scatter plot. If the points drift up to the right, there is a positive correlation; down to the right, a negative correlation; no direction, no correlation.

The correlation coefficient $r$ measures how close the points are to a straight line, from $-1$ to $1$.

Covariance

$$s_{xy} = \frac{(x_1 - \overline{x})(y_1 - \overline{y}) + \cdots + (x_n - \overline{x})(y_n - \overline{y})}{n}$$

Positive when $x$ and $y$ are usually on the same side of their means.

Correlation coefficient

$$r = \frac{s_{xy}}{s_x\, s_y}, \qquad -1 \le r \le 1$$

$s_x$, $s_y$: standard deviations. $r$ near $1$: strong positive; near $-1$: strong negative; near $0$: weak or none.

Drag the points

Line them up, scatter them, or tilt them down and watch $r$.

Example 1

Find $r$ for $x$: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and $y$: 2, 4, 5, 4, 5.

Show solution
  1. $\overline{x} = 3$, $\overline{y} = 4$. Deviations: $x$: $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2$; $y$: $-2, 0, 1, 0, 1$.
  2. Products: $4, 0, 0, 0, 2$, total $6$, so $s_{xy} = \dfrac{6}{5}$.
  3. $s_x^2 = \dfrac{10}{5}$, $s_y^2 = \dfrac{6}{5}$.
  4. $r = \dfrac{6/5}{\sqrt{10/5}\sqrt{6/5}} = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{60}}$

$r \approx 0.77$: a fairly strong positive correlation

Example 2

Which shows the stronger correlation: $r = 0.3$ or $r = -0.7$?

Show solution
  1. Strength is how close $|r|$ is to 1; the sign only gives the direction.

$r = -0.7$ (a fairly strong negative correlation)

Example 3

In summer, ice cream sales and the number of drownings are positively correlated. Does ice cream cause drowning?

Show solution
  1. Both go up with hot weather, a third factor.

No. Correlation shows the two move together, not that one causes the other.

$r$ only measures straight-line patterns: points on a U-shaped curve can have $r \approx 0$ even though $y$ clearly depends on $x$. And correlation never proves cause and effect.
  • $r = \dfrac{s_{xy}}{s_x s_y}$, always between $-1$ and $1$.
  • Sign = direction; $|r|$ = strength.
  • Draw the scatter plot too: one outlier can change $r$ a lot.

Median

The middle value

Put the data in order. The median is the value in the middle: half the data is at or below it, half at or above it.

With an even number of values there are two middle values; the median is their average.

Median of $n$ ordered values

$$n \text{ odd: the } \frac{n+1}{2}\text{-th value} \qquad n \text{ even: average of the } \frac{n}{2}\text{-th and } \left(\frac{n}{2} + 1\right)\text{-th}$$

Median finder

Example 1

Find the median of 12, 7, 15, 9, 21.

Show solution
  1. In order: 7, 9, 12, 15, 21. $n = 5$, so the 3rd value.

Median $= 12$

Example 2

Find the median of 8, 12, 15, 7, 10, 22.

Show solution
  1. In order: 7, 8, 10, 12, 15, 22. $n = 6$, so the 3rd and 4th values.
  2. $\dfrac{10 + 12}{2}$

Median $= 11$

Example 3

Compare the mean and the median of 2, 4, 6, 8, 100.

Show solution
  1. Median: the 3rd value, 6.
  2. Mean: $\dfrac{120}{5} = 24$.

Median 6, mean 24. The one large value moves the mean but not the median.

Example 4

9 numbers have median 25. What does that tell you?

Show solution
  1. The median is the 5th value in order.
  2. So the 1st–5th values are $\le 25$ and the 5th–9th values are $\ge 25$.

At least 5 values are $\le 25$ and at least 5 are $\ge 25$.

Sort first. The middle of the unsorted list is not the median.
  • Sort, then take the middle value (or the average of the two middle values).
  • The median ignores how extreme the end values are; the mean doesn't.
  • For skewed data (incomes, house prices) the median is often the better "typical value".

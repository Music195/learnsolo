Distributive Law

Multiply every term inside the brackets

A number in front of brackets multiplies each term inside. Think of a rectangle of height $a$ and width $b + c$: its area $a(b + c)$ is the same as the two pieces $ab$ and $ac$ added together.

Distributive law

$$a(b + c) = ab + ac, \qquad (a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd$$

Two brackets: every term of the first times every term of the second (here $2 \times 2 = 4$ products).

Area model for $(a + b)(c + d)$

Example 1

Expand $-3x(2x - 5)$.

Show solution
  1. $(-3x)(2x) + (-3x)(-5)$

$-6x^2 + 15x$

Example 2

Expand $(2x + 3)(x - 4)$.

Show solution
  1. $2x \cdot x + 2x \cdot (-4) + 3 \cdot x + 3 \cdot (-4) = 2x^2 - 8x + 3x - 12$
  2. Collect like terms.

$2x^2 - 5x - 12$

Example 3

Expand $(x + 2)(x^2 - 3x + 1)$.

Show solution
  1. $x(x^2 - 3x + 1) + 2(x^2 - 3x + 1) = x^3 - 3x^2 + x + 2x^2 - 6x + 2$

$x^3 - x^2 - 5x + 2$

A minus sign in front multiplies every term: $-(x - 3) = -x + 3$, not $-x - 3$.
Before collecting, count your products: a 2-term bracket times a 3-term bracket must give $2 \times 3 = 6$ products.
  • $a(b + c) = ab + ac$.
  • Two brackets: every term times every term, then collect like terms.
  • Read backwards, it is factorisation: $ab + ac = a(b + c)$.

Properties of Square Roots

$\sqrt{a}$ is the non-negative square root

For $a \ge 0$, the numbers whose square is $a$ are $\sqrt{a}$ and $-\sqrt{a}$. The symbol $\sqrt{a}$ always means the one that is $\ge 0$.

That is why $\sqrt{(-3)^2} = \sqrt{9} = 3$, not $-3$: squaring loses the sign, and the root gives back a non-negative number.

Properties

$$a \ge 0:\; (\sqrt{a})^2 = (-\sqrt{a})^2 = a, \qquad \sqrt{a} \ge 0$$
$$\sqrt{a^2} = |a| = \begin{cases} a & (a \ge 0) \\ -a & (a < 0) \end{cases}$$

Example 1

Find $\sqrt{(-5)^2}$ and $(-\sqrt{7})^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{(-5)^2} = |-5|$
  2. $(-\sqrt{7})^2 = (\sqrt{7})^2$

$5$, $\;7$

Example 2

Simplify $\sqrt{(\sqrt{3} - 2)^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{(\sqrt{3} - 2)^2} = |\sqrt{3} - 2|$
  2. $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.73 < 2$, so the inside is negative.

$2 - \sqrt{3}$

Example 3

Simplify $\sqrt{(x - 3)^2}$ for (i) $x \ge 3$ and (ii) $x < 3$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{(x - 3)^2} = |x - 3|$
  2. (i) $x - 3 \ge 0$: keep it. (ii) $x - 3 < 0$: change the sign.

(i) $x - 3$   (ii) $3 - x$

Example 4

Simplify $\sqrt{x^2 + 2x + 1} + \sqrt{x^2 - 4x + 4}$ when $-1 \le x \le 2$.

Show solution
  1. $= \sqrt{(x + 1)^2} + \sqrt{(x - 2)^2} = |x + 1| + |x - 2|$
  2. $x + 1 \ge 0$ and $x - 2 \le 0$: $(x + 1) + (2 - x)$.

$\sqrt{a^2} = a$ is only true for $a \ge 0$. In general it is $|a|$. This is the most common mistake in this topic.
When you see $\sqrt{\;\text{quadratic}\;}$, check whether the quadratic is a perfect square; then the root becomes an absolute value.
  • $\sqrt{a}$ means the root that is $\ge 0$, and needs $a \ge 0$.
  • $(\sqrt{a})^2 = a$ for $a \ge 0$.
  • $\sqrt{a^2} = |a|$: split into cases on the sign of $a$.

Exponent Rules

An exponent counts how many times you multiply

$a^3$ means $a \times a \times a$: three copies of $a$ multiplied together. Every exponent rule comes from simply counting those copies.

For example, $a^2 \times a^3 = (a \cdot a)(a \cdot a \cdot a)$ has $2 + 3 = 5$ copies, so it is $a^5$.

Exponent laws ($m$, $n$ positive whole numbers)

$$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}, \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}, \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n$$

Multiply powers of the same base → add exponents. Power of a power → multiply exponents. Power of a product → each factor gets the power.

Count the copies

Example 1

Simplify $x^4 \times x^5$ and $(x^3)^4$.

Show solution
  1. $x^4 \times x^5 = x^{4+5}$
  2. $(x^3)^4 = x^{3 \times 4}$

$x^9$, $\;x^{12}$

Example 2

Simplify $(-2a^2b)^3$.

Show solution
  1. Each factor gets the cube: $(-2)^3 (a^2)^3 b^3$.
  2. $(-2)^3 = -8$, $(a^2)^3 = a^6$.

$-8a^6b^3$

Example 3

Simplify $3x^2y \times (-2xy^3)^2$.

Show solution
  1. $(-2xy^3)^2 = 4x^2y^6$
  2. $3x^2y \times 4x^2y^6 = 12\,x^{2+2}\,y^{1+6}$

$12x^4y^7$

$a^2 \times a^3$ is $a^5$, not $a^6$. Only a power of a power multiplies the exponents. Also, $(a + b)^2 \ne a^2 + b^2$: the rule $(ab)^n = a^nb^n$ is for products, not sums.

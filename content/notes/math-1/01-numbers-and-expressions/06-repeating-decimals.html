Repeating Decimals

Decimals that repeat forever are fractions

A repeating decimal is an infinite decimal in which a block of digits repeats forever, such as $0.666\ldots$ or $0.3181818\ldots$. We mark the repeating block with a bar: $0.\overline{6}$, $0.3\overline{18}$.

Every fraction is either a terminating decimal or a repeating decimal, and every repeating decimal can be turned back into a fraction.

Repeating decimal → fraction

$$x = 0.\overline{27} \;\Rightarrow\; 100x = 27.\overline{27} \;\Rightarrow\; 100x - x = 27 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{27}{99} = \frac{3}{11}$$

Multiply by $10^k$ where $k$ is the length of the repeating block, so that the two tails line up and cancel when you subtract.

Convert $0.\text{(fixed part)}\overline{\text{(repeating part)}}$ to a fraction

Example 1

Write $0.\overline{4}$ as a fraction.

Show solution
  1. $x = 0.444\ldots$, $\;10x = 4.444\ldots$
  2. $10x - x = 4 \Rightarrow 9x = 4$

$\dfrac{4}{9}$

Example 2

Write $0.1\overline{6}$ as a fraction.

Show solution
  1. $x = 0.1666\ldots$. Shift past the fixed digit: $10x = 1.666\ldots$; one more block: $100x = 16.666\ldots$
  2. $100x - 10x = 15 \Rightarrow 90x = 15$

$\dfrac{15}{90} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

Example 3

Write $1.\overline{234}$ as a fraction.

Show solution
  1. $1000x = 1234.234\ldots$, $\;x = 1.234\ldots$
  2. $999x = 1233 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{1233}{999}$

$\dfrac{137}{111}$

Example 4

Write $\dfrac{5}{7}$ as a repeating decimal.

Show solution
  1. Long division: $5 \div 7 = 0.714285\,714285\ldots$. The remainder 5 comes back after 6 steps, so the digits repeat.

$0.\overline{714285}$

$0.\overline{9} = 1$ exactly: $x = 0.999\ldots$, $10x - x = 9$, so $x = 1$. It is not "just below 1".
A fraction in lowest terms gives a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5 (e.g. $\dfrac{3}{8} = 0.375$). Any other denominator gives a repeating decimal.

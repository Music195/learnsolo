Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q

Slide $y = ax^2$ so its vertex lands on $(p, q)$

$y = a(x - p)^2 + q$ is the graph of $y = ax^2$ moved $p$ to the right and $q$ up. The shape (set by $a$) doesn't change; only the position does.

Any $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ can be rewritten in this form by completing the square (平方完成), which shows its vertex.

Vertex form

$$y = a(x - p)^2 + q: \qquad \text{vertex } (p, q), \qquad \text{axis } x = p$$

Completing the square

$$ax^2 + bx + c = a\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 - \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a}$$

In practice: take $a$ out of the $x$ terms, halve the $x$ coefficient, square it, add and subtract it.

Drag the vertex $V$ and change $a$

Example 1

Find the vertex and axis of $y = 2x^2 - 8x + 5$.

Show solution
  1. $2(x^2 - 4x) + 5 = 2\{(x - 2)^2 - 4\} + 5$
  2. $= 2(x - 2)^2 - 3$

Vertex $(2, -3)$, axis $x = 2$

Example 2

Find the vertex of $y = -x^2 + 6x - 4$.

Show solution
  1. $-(x^2 - 6x) - 4 = -\{(x - 3)^2 - 9\} - 4$
  2. $= -(x - 3)^2 + 5$

Vertex $(3, 5)$, opens downward

Example 3

The graph of $y = x^2 + 2x + 3$ is moved 3 to the right and 1 down. Find the new equation.

Show solution
  1. $y = (x + 1)^2 + 2$: vertex $(-1, 2)$.
  2. New vertex $(2, 1)$; same $a = 1$.

$y = (x - 2)^2 + 1 = x^2 - 4x + 5$

Example 4

A parabola has vertex $(1, -2)$ and passes through $(3, 6)$. Find its equation.

Show solution
  1. $y = a(x - 1)^2 - 2$.
  2. $6 = a \cdot 4 - 2 \Rightarrow a = 2$.

$y = 2(x - 1)^2 - 2$

$y = (x + 3)^2$ has vertex $(-3, 0)$, not $(3, 0)$: write it as $(x - (-3))^2$. The sign inside the bracket is the opposite of the vertex.
Moving a graph $p$ right and $q$ up: replace $x$ by $x - p$ and $y$ by $y - q$. This works for any graph, not only parabolas.
  • $y = a(x - p)^2 + q$: vertex $(p, q)$, axis $x = p$.
  • Complete the square to find the vertex of $ax^2 + bx + c$.
  • $a$ is unchanged by sliding the graph.

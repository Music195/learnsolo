The basic parabola

The graph of $y = ax^2$ is a parabola with its vertex at the origin and the $y$-axis as its axis of symmetry ($x$ and $-x$ give the same $y$).

The sign of $a$ sets the direction, and the size of $|a|$ sets how narrow it is.

$y = ax^2$

$$\text{vertex } (0, 0), \qquad \text{axis } x = 0$$

$a > 0$: opens upward (下に凸). $a < 0$: opens downward (上に凸). Larger $|a|$: narrower. $y = ax^2$ and $y = -ax^2$ are mirror images in the $x$-axis.

Example 2

The graph of $y = ax^2$ passes through $(2, -12)$. Find $a$ and say which way it opens.

Show solution
  1. $-12 = a \cdot 4 \Rightarrow a = -3$.

$a = -3$, opens downward

Example 3

For $y = x^2$ with $-1 \le x \le 2$, find the range of $y$.

Show solution
  1. The domain contains $x = 0$ (the vertex), so the smallest value is $0$.
  2. The end farther from 0 is $x = 2$: $y = 4$.

$0 \le y \le 4$

For a parabola you can't just plug in the two ends, as you can for a line. If the vertex is inside the domain, it gives the minimum (or maximum).
  • $y = ax^2$: vertex at the origin, axis $x = 0$.
  • $a > 0$ up, $a < 0$ down; larger $|a|$ is narrower.
  • Symmetric: $x$ and $-x$ give the same $y$.

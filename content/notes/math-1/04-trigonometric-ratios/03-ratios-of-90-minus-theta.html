Trigonometric Ratios of 90°−θ

The other acute angle swaps sine and cosine

In a right triangle the two acute angles add to $90°$: if one is $\theta$, the other is $90° - \theta$.

The side opposite $\theta$ is adjacent to $90° - \theta$, and the other way round. So the sine of one angle is the cosine of the other.

Ratios of $90° - \theta$

$$\sin(90° - \theta) = \cos\theta, \qquad \cos(90° - \theta) = \sin\theta, \qquad \tan(90° - \theta) = \frac{1}{\tan\theta}$$

Example 1

Write $\sin 70°$ and $\cos 65°$ using angles less than $45°$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin 70° = \sin(90° - 20°) = \cos 20°$
  2. $\cos 65° = \cos(90° - 25°) = \sin 25°$

$\sin 70° = \cos 20°$, $\cos 65° = \sin 25°$

Example 2

Find $\sin^2 20° + \sin^2 70°$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin 70° = \cos 20°$
  2. $\sin^2 20° + \cos^2 20° = 1$

$1$

Example 3

Find $\tan 10° \times \tan 80°$.

Show solution
  1. $\tan 80° = \tan(90° - 10°) = \dfrac{1}{\tan 10°}$

$1$

When a question mixes angles that add to $90°$ (like $20°$ and $70°$), convert one of them; the answer is usually a simple number.
  • $\sin(90° - \theta) = \cos\theta$, $\cos(90° - \theta) = \sin\theta$.
  • $\tan(90° - \theta) = \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}$.
  • Reason: the two acute angles of a right triangle swap "opposite" and "adjacent".

Triangle Area Formulas

Area from two sides and the angle between them

"Half base times height" needs the height. If you know two sides and the angle between them, the height is one side times the sine of that angle, so the area follows straight away.

Area $S$ of $\triangle ABC$

$$S = \frac{1}{2}bc\sin A = \frac{1}{2}ca\sin B = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$$

Always two sides and the angle they enclose. With only the three sides, find a cosine first (note 07) or use Heron's formula (note 10).

  1. Take $BC = a$ as the base and let $h$ be the height from $A$, with foot $H$ on line $BC$.
  2. If $C$ is acute, right triangle $AHC$ gives $h = b\sin C$.
  3. If $C$ is obtuse, $H$ lies beyond $C$ and $h = b\sin(180° - C) = b\sin C$. If $C = 90°$, $h = b = b\sin C$.
  4. So $S = \dfrac{1}{2}ah = \dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$. The other two forms come from choosing a different base.

Example 1

Find the area of $\triangle ABC$ with $a = 8$, $b = 6$, $C = 45°$.

Show solution
  1. $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 6 \cdot \sin 45° = 24 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

$S = 12\sqrt{2}$ $(\approx 16.97)$

Example 2

Find the area of $\triangle ABC$ with $b = 5$, $c = 8$, $A = 120°$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin 120° = \sin 60° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (note 05)
  2. $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 5 \cdot 8 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

$S = 10\sqrt{3}$

Example 3

A triangle has sides $a = 7$, $b = 8$, $c = 5$. Find its area.

Show solution
  1. Law of cosines: $\cos A = \dfrac{64 + 25 - 49}{2 \cdot 8 \cdot 5} = \dfrac{40}{80} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $A = 60°$.
  2. $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 5 \cdot \sin 60° = 20 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

$S = 10\sqrt{3}$

Example 4

$\triangle ABC$ has area $24$, $C = 30°$ and $a = 8$. Find $b$.

Show solution
  1. $24 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot b \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 2b$

$b = 12$

The angle must be the one between the two sides. For $\dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, $C$ is where sides $a$ and $b$ meet.
For fixed $a$ and $b$, $\sin C$ is largest at $C = 90°$, so the area is largest when the two sides are perpendicular.
  • $S = \dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$: two sides and the angle between them.
  • Works for obtuse angles too, since $\sin(180° - C) = \sin C$.
  • Three sides only: get $\cos$ from the law of cosines, then $\sin$, then $S$.

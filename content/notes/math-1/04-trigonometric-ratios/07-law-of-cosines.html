Law of Cosines

Pythagoras with a correction term

For a right angle at $A$, $a^2 = b^2 + c^2$. If $A$ is not a right angle, the third side is shorter (acute $A$) or longer (obtuse $A$), and the term $-2bc\cos A$ corrects for that.

Law of cosines

$$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$$

The same with the letters moved round: $b^2 = c^2 + a^2 - 2ca\cos B$, $\;c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$.

Finding an angle from three sides

$$\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$$

$A$ acute $\iff a^2 < b^2 + c^2$; right $\iff a^2 = b^2 + c^2$; obtuse $\iff a^2 > b^2 + c^2$.

  1. Put $A$ at the origin and $B$ at $(c, 0)$. Then $C = (b\cos A,\; b\sin A)$ (note 04).
  2. $a^2 = BC^2 = (b\cos A - c)^2 + (b\sin A)^2 = b^2(\cos^2 A + \sin^2 A) - 2bc\cos A + c^2$.
  3. $\cos^2 A + \sin^2 A = 1$, so $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$.

Drag the corners

Example 1

In $\triangle ABC$, $b = 8$, $c = 6$, $A = 60°$. Find $a$.

Show solution
  1. $a^2 = 64 + 36 - 2 \cdot 8 \cdot 6 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 52$

$a = 2\sqrt{13}$

Example 2

A triangle has sides $a = 7$, $b = 3$, $c = 5$. Find $A$.

Show solution
  1. $\cos A = \dfrac{9 + 25 - 49}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5} = \dfrac{-15}{30} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

$A = 120°$

Example 3

In $\triangle ABC$, $a = \sqrt{7}$, $b = 3$, $A = 60°$. Find $c$.

Show solution
  1. $7 = 9 + c^2 - 2 \cdot 3 \cdot c \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow c^2 - 3c + 2 = 0$
  2. $(c - 1)(c - 2) = 0$

$c = 1$ or $c = 2$ (two triangles fit)

Example 4

Is the triangle with sides 4, 5, 7 acute, right or obtuse?

Show solution
  1. Check the largest angle, opposite 7: $7^2 = 49$ and $4^2 + 5^2 = 41$.
  2. $49 > 41$, so that angle is obtuse.

Obtuse

Two sides + the angle between them, or all three sides → law of cosines. A side with its opposite angle → law of sines.
  • $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$.
  • $\cos A = \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$ for an angle from three sides.
  • Compare $a^2$ with $b^2 + c^2$ to tell acute / right / obtuse.

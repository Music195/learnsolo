Heron's Formula for Triangle Area

Area from the three sides only

Three side lengths fix a triangle completely, so they must also fix its area. Heron's formula gives that area directly, with no angles and no height.

Heron's formula

$$S = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}, \qquad s = \frac{a + b + c}{2}$$

$s$: half the perimeter (semiperimeter). The sides must satisfy the triangle inequality, which makes every factor positive.

  1. $S = \dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, so $S^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}a^2b^2(1 - \cos C)(1 + \cos C)$.
  2. Law of cosines: $\cos C = \dfrac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$. Then $1 + \cos C = \dfrac{(a + b)^2 - c^2}{2ab} = \dfrac{(a + b + c)(a + b - c)}{2ab}$ and $1 - \cos C = \dfrac{c^2 - (a - b)^2}{2ab} = \dfrac{(c - a + b)(c + a - b)}{2ab}$.
  3. So $S^2 = \dfrac{1}{16}(a + b + c)(a + b - c)(b + c - a)(c + a - b)$.
  4. With $a + b + c = 2s$: $a + b - c = 2(s - c)$, $b + c - a = 2(s - a)$, $c + a - b = 2(s - b)$. Hence $S^2 = s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)$.

Type three side lengths

Example 1

Find the area of the triangle with sides 3, 4, 5.

Show solution
  1. $s = 6$; $S = \sqrt{6 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = \sqrt{36}$

$S = 6$ (same as $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 4$)

Example 2

Find the area of the triangle with sides 5, 6, 7.

Show solution
  1. $s = 9$; $S = \sqrt{9 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{216}$

$S = 6\sqrt{6}$ $(\approx 14.70)$

Example 3

Find the area of the triangle with sides 13, 14, 15, and its inradius $r$.

Show solution
  1. $s = 21$; $S = \sqrt{21 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6} = \sqrt{7056} = 84$
  2. $r = \dfrac{S}{s} = \dfrac{84}{21}$ (note 09)

$S = 84$, $r = 4$

Example 4

Find the area of the triangle with sides 7, 8, 9.

Show solution
  1. $s = 12$; $S = \sqrt{12 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3} = \sqrt{720} = \sqrt{144 \cdot 5}$

$S = 12\sqrt{5}$ $(\approx 26.83)$

Don't multiply everything out under the root. Split the factors into primes and pull out pairs: $\sqrt{21 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6} = \sqrt{2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7^2} = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$.
If the sides contain square roots, Heron's formula gets messy. Use the law of cosines to find $\cos C$, then $\sin C$, then $S = \dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$.
  • $S = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$ with $s = \dfrac{a + b + c}{2}$.
  • It comes from $S = \dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ plus the law of cosines.
  • Best when all three sides are whole numbers.

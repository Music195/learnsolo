Definition of Trigonometric Ratios (Coordinate Plane)

Angles up to 180° using a semicircle

A right triangle only has acute angles. To give $\sin$, $\cos$, $\tan$ a meaning for $0° \le \theta \le 180°$, draw a semicircle of radius $r$ centred at the origin. Turn from the positive $x$-axis by $\theta$ to reach the point $P(x, y)$ on the semicircle.

For acute $\theta$ this gives the same values as the right triangle. Beyond $90°$, $x$ is negative, so $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$ become negative.

Definition

$$\sin\theta = \frac{y}{r}, \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{x}{r}, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x}$$

$\tan 90°$ is not defined ($x = 0$). With $r = 1$: $\cos\theta$ is the $x$-coordinate and $\sin\theta$ the $y$-coordinate.

Ranges for $0° \le \theta \le 180°$

$$0 \le \sin\theta \le 1, \qquad -1 \le \cos\theta \le 1$$

$\tan\theta$ is positive for acute $\theta$, negative for obtuse $\theta$, and is the slope of the line $OP$.

Drag $P$ round the semicircle ($r = 1$)

Example 1

Find $\sin 120°$, $\cos 120°$, $\tan 120°$.

Show solution
  1. Take $r = 2$. The point at $120°$ is $P(-1, \sqrt{3})$ (a $30°$–$60°$–$90°$ triangle with the $y$-axis side on the left).

$\sin 120° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\cos 120° = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\tan 120° = -\sqrt{3}$

Example 2

Solve $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ for $0° \le \theta \le 180°$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$: on the unit semicircle, $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is reached at two points, $\theta = 30°$ and $150°$.
  2. $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$: $x = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ only at $\theta = 135°$.

$\theta = 30°, 150°$; $\;\theta = 135°$

Example 3

Find the angle $\theta$ between the line $y = -\sqrt{3}\,x$ and the positive $x$-axis.

Show solution
  1. The slope equals $\tan\theta$: $\tan\theta = -\sqrt{3}$.

$\theta = 120°$

Read the end values off the circle: $0°$ is $(1, 0)$, $90°$ is $(0, 1)$, $180°$ is $(-1, 0)$. So $\sin 0° = 0$, $\cos 90° = 0$, $\cos 180° = -1$, $\tan 180° = 0$.
$\sin\theta = k$ (with $0 < k < 1$) has two answers between $0°$ and $180°$, symmetric about $90°$. $\cos\theta = k$ has only one.
  • $\sin\theta = \dfrac{y}{r}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{x}{r}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{y}{x}$ for $P(x, y)$ on a semicircle of radius $r$.
  • Obtuse $\theta$: $\sin > 0$, $\cos < 0$, $\tan < 0$.
  • $\tan\theta$ = slope of the line from $O$ at angle $\theta$.

Negation of "And" and "Or"

Negating swaps "and" with "or"

The negation of $p$ (written $\overline{p}$, "not $p$") is true exactly when $p$ is false.

"$p$ and $q$" fails as soon as one of them fails, so its negation is "not $p$ or not $q$". This is De Morgan's law (note 05) in the language of conditions.

De Morgan's laws for conditions

$$\overline{p \text{ and } q} \iff \overline{p} \text{ or } \overline{q}, \qquad \overline{p \text{ or } q} \iff \overline{p} \text{ and } \overline{q}$$

"All" and "some"

$$\overline{\text{all } x \text{ satisfy } p} \iff \text{some } x \text{ does not satisfy } p, \qquad \overline{\text{some } x \text{ satisfies } p} \iff \text{all } x \text{ do not satisfy } p$$

To deny "all", one exception is enough; to deny "some", you must rule out every case.

$x = 1, \ldots, 12$: check both sides of De Morgan

Example 1

Negate "$x > 0$ and $y > 0$" ($x, y$ real).

Show solution
  1. "and" becomes "or", and each part is negated: $\overline{x > 0}$ is $x \le 0$.

$x \le 0$ or $y \le 0$

Example 2

Negate "$x = 1$ or $x = 2$".

Show solution
  1. "or" becomes "and".

$x \ne 1$ and $x \ne 2$

Example 3

Negate "$-1 < x \le 3$".

Show solution
  1. It means "$x > -1$ and $x \le 3$".
  2. Negation: "$x \le -1$ or $x > 3$".

$x \le -1$ or $3 < x$

Example 4

Negate "For all real $x$, $x^2 > 0$", and say which is true.

Show solution
  1. Negation: "For some real $x$, $x^2 \le 0$".
  2. $x = 0$ gives $x^2 = 0$, so the negation is true and the original is false.

"Some real $x$ has $x^2 \le 0$" (true)

The negation of $x > 0$ is $x \le 0$, not $x < 0$. Don't lose the equals case.
With truth sets: "$p$ and $q$" ↔ $P \cap Q$, "$p$ or $q$" ↔ $P \cup Q$, "not $p$" ↔ $\overline{P}$. So these laws are exactly $\overline{P \cap Q} = \overline{P} \cup \overline{Q}$ and $\overline{P \cup Q} = \overline{P} \cap \overline{Q}$.
  • not (p and q) = (not p) or (not q).
  • not (p or q) = (not p) and (not q).
  • not (all) = some … not; not (some) = all … not.

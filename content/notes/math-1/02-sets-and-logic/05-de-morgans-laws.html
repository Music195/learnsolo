De Morgan's Laws

Taking the complement of $\cup$ and $\cap$

When you take the complement of a union or an intersection, the bar splits over each set and the symbol flips: $\cup$ becomes $\cap$, and $\cap$ becomes $\cup$.

Complement of a union

$$\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$$

"Not in $A$ or $B$" = "not in $A$ and not in $B$"

Complement of an intersection

$$\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$$

"Not in both" = "not in $A$ or not in $B$"

Everyday check: "not (tea or coffee)" means "no tea and no coffee". "Not (tea and coffee)" means you are missing at least one of them: no tea or no coffee.

Check De Morgan's laws

Why $\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$

  1. $x \in \overline{A \cup B}$ means $x$ is not in $A \cup B$.
  2. $A \cup B$ contains everything in $A$ or $B$, so $x$ is in neither: $x \notin A$ and $x \notin B$.
  3. That is $x \in \overline{A}$ and $x \in \overline{B}$, i.e. $x \in \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$.
  4. Each step also works backwards, so the two sets have exactly the same elements.

Example 1

$U = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$, $A = \{1, 2, 3\}$, $B = \{3, 4, 5\}$. Check $\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$.

Show solution
  1. Left: $A \cup B = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, so $\overline{A \cup B} = \{6\}$
  2. Right: $\overline{A} = \{4, 5, 6\}$, $\overline{B} = \{1, 2, 6\}$, so $\overline{A} \cap \overline{B} = \{6\}$

Both sides are $\{6\}$. ✔

Example 2

$U = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$, $A = \{1, 2, 3\}$, $B = \{2, 3, 4\}$. Check $\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$.

Show solution
  1. Left: $A \cap B = \{2, 3\}$, so $\overline{A \cap B} = \{1, 4, 5, 6\}$
  2. Right: $\overline{A} = \{4, 5, 6\}$, $\overline{B} = \{1, 5, 6\}$, so $\overline{A} \cup \overline{B} = \{1, 4, 5, 6\}$

Both sides are $\{1, 4, 5, 6\}$. ✔

Example 3

Of 40 students, 25 like maths, 18 like English, and 10 like both. How many like neither?

Show solution
  1. "Neither" is $\overline{M} \cap \overline{E} = \overline{M \cup E}$ (De Morgan)
  2. $n(M \cup E) = n(M) + n(E) - n(M \cap E) = 25 + 18 - 10 = 33$
  3. $n(\overline{M \cup E}) = 40 - 33$

7 students like neither.

The bar does not simply split: $\overline{A \cup B} \neq \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$. When the bar breaks apart, the symbol between the sets must flip.
  • $\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$ and $\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$.
  • Split the bar, flip the symbol.
  • "Neither $A$ nor $B$" $= \overline{A \cup B}$: count it as $n(U) - n(A \cup B)$.

Converse, Contrapositive, and Inverse of Statements

Three relatives of "if $p$ then $q$"

From $p \Rightarrow q$ you can swap the two sides, negate both, or do both. The result of doing both, the contrapositive, is always true or false together with the original. The other two can differ from it.

For the proposition $p \Rightarrow q$

$$\text{converse (逆): } q \Rightarrow p, \qquad \text{inverse (裏): } \overline{p} \Rightarrow \overline{q}, \qquad \text{contrapositive (対偶): } \overline{q} \Rightarrow \overline{p}$$

Original ⟷ contrapositive have the same truth value. Converse ⟷ inverse also have the same truth value (each is the other's contrapositive).

  1. $p \Rightarrow q$ is true ⟺ $P \subset Q$ (note 06).
  2. $P \subset Q$ ⟺ $\overline{Q} \subset \overline{P}$: anything outside $Q$ is outside $P$ too.
  3. $\overline{Q} \subset \overline{P}$ ⟺ $\overline{q} \Rightarrow \overline{p}$ is true.

Pick a statement and see its relatives

Example 1

Write the converse, inverse and contrapositive of "$x = 3 \Rightarrow x^2 = 9$", and decide which are true.

Show solution
  1. Converse: $x^2 = 9 \Rightarrow x = 3$. False ($x = -3$).
  2. Inverse: $x \ne 3 \Rightarrow x^2 \ne 9$. False ($x = -3$).
  3. Contrapositive: $x^2 \ne 9 \Rightarrow x \ne 3$. True, like the original.

Original and contrapositive true; converse and inverse false

Example 2

Write the contrapositive of "$x + y > 2 \Rightarrow x > 1$ or $y > 1$".

Show solution
  1. Negate the conclusion with De Morgan (note 07): "$x \le 1$ and $y \le 1$".
  2. Negate the hypothesis: $x + y \le 2$.

$x \le 1$ and $y \le 1 \Rightarrow x + y \le 2$ (true, so the original is true)

Example 3

Is the converse of "a triangle is equilateral ⇒ its three angles are equal" true? What about its inverse?

Show solution
  1. Converse: equal angles ⇒ equilateral. True (equal angles face equal sides).
  2. The inverse is the contrapositive of the converse, so it has the same truth value.

Both true

A true statement can have a false converse. "It's a square ⇒ it's a rectangle" is true, but "it's a rectangle ⇒ it's a square" is not.
Because the contrapositive has the same truth value, you can prove $p \Rightarrow q$ by proving $\overline{q} \Rightarrow \overline{p}$ instead (note 09).
  • Converse: swap. Inverse: negate both. Contrapositive: swap and negate.
  • Original ≡ contrapositive; converse ≡ inverse.
  • Use De Morgan when negating "and"/"or" parts.

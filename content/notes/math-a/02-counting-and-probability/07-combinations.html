Counting Combinations

Choosing without order

A combination is a choice where order doesn't matter: the team {A, B, C} is the same as {C, A, B}.

Choosing 3 of 5 letters: there are ${}_5\mathrm{P}_3 = 60$ ordered arrangements, but each group of 3 appears $3! = 6$ times (once for each order). So there are $60 \div 6 = 10$ groups.

Choose $r$ of $n$ different things

$${}_n\mathrm{C}_r = \frac{{}_n\mathrm{P}_r}{r!} = \frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1)}{r(r-1)\cdots 1} = \frac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}$$

Same number of factors on top and bottom ($r$ each).

Choosing $r$ = leaving out $n - r$

$${}_n\mathrm{C}_r = {}_n\mathrm{C}_{n-r}$$

Saves work: ${}_{20}\mathrm{C}_{18} = {}_{20}\mathrm{C}_2 = \dfrac{20 \times 19}{2} = 190$.

Example 1

From 6 boys and 4 girls, choose a team of 3 boys and 2 girls. How many ways?

Show solution
  1. Boys: ${}_6\mathrm{C}_3 = \dfrac{6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 20$
  2. Girls: ${}_4\mathrm{C}_2 = \dfrac{4 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 1} = 6$
  3. Choose the boys, then the girls: multiply

$20 \times 6 = 120$

Example 2

8 points lie on a circle. How many triangles have 3 of these points as corners?

Show solution
  1. Any 3 points on a circle are not in a straight line, so every choice of 3 gives a triangle.
  2. ${}_8\mathrm{C}_3 = \dfrac{8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1}$

$56$

Example 3

How many 5-card hands from a 52-card deck are all the same suit (a flush, including straight flushes)?

Show solution
  1. Choose the suit: 4 ways
  2. Choose 5 of that suit's 13 cards: ${}_{13}\mathrm{C}_5 = \dfrac{13 \cdot 12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 \cdot 9}{5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 1287$

$4 \times 1287 = 5148$

Example 4

Split 6 people into two groups of 3 (the groups have no names). How many ways?

Show solution
  1. If the groups were named A and B: choose A's members, ${}_6\mathrm{C}_3 = 20$; B gets the rest.
  2. Without names, swapping A and B gives the same split, so each split was counted $2!$ times.

$20 \div 2 = 10$

When dividing into equal-sized groups without names, divide by the number of ways to reorder the groups ($2!$ for 2 groups, $3!$ for 3 groups). Don't divide if the groups have names or different sizes.
Ask: "If I swap two chosen items, is it a different result?" Yes → permutation (${}_n\mathrm{P}_r$). No → combination (${}_n\mathrm{C}_r$).
  • ${}_n\mathrm{C}_r = \dfrac{{}_n\mathrm{P}_r}{r!} = \dfrac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$; $\;{}_n\mathrm{C}_r = {}_n\mathrm{C}_{n-r}$.
  • Choosing from separate groups: multiply the combinations.
  • Equal-sized unnamed groups: divide by (number of groups)!.

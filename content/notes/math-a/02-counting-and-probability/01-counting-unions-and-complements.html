Counting Elements in Unions and Complements

Don't count the overlap twice

$n(A)$ means the number of elements in $A$. If you add $n(A)$ and $n(B)$, everything in both sets is counted twice, so subtract it once.

Elements outside a set are counted by taking everything and removing what is inside.

Union of two sets

$$n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$$

If $A \cap B = \varnothing$ (no overlap), this is just $n(A) + n(B)$.

Complement

$$n(\overline{A}) = n(U) - n(A)$$

Union of three sets

$$n(A \cup B \cup C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) - n(A \cap B) - n(B \cap C) - n(C \cap A) + n(A \cap B \cap C)$$

Add the singles, subtract the pairs, add back the triple (it was added 3 times and subtracted 3 times).

"Neither $A$ nor $B$" is $\overline{A} \cap \overline{B} = \overline{A \cup B}$ (De Morgan), so $\;n(\text{neither}) = n(U) - n(A \cup B)$.

Fill in the Venn diagram

Example 1

In a class of 30, 18 play soccer, 12 play basketball and 5 play both. How many play at least one? How many play neither?

Show solution
  1. $n(S \cup B) = 18 + 12 - 5 = 25$
  2. $n(\text{neither}) = 30 - 25 = 5$

25 play at least one; 5 play neither.

Example 2

How many integers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 2 or 3?

Show solution
  1. Divisible by 2: $\lfloor 100/2 \rfloor = 50$. Divisible by 3: $\lfloor 100/3 \rfloor = 33$.
  2. Divisible by both means divisible by 6: $\lfloor 100/6 \rfloor = 16$
  3. $50 + 33 - 16 = 67$

67

Example 3

How many integers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 2, 3 or 5?

Show solution
  1. Singles: $50 + 33 + 20 = 103$
  2. Pairs (divisible by 6, 15, 10): $16 + 6 + 10 = 32$
  3. Triple (divisible by 30): $3$
  4. $103 - 32 + 3 = 74$

74

$n(A \cap B)$ can't be bigger than $n(A)$ or $n(B)$, and $n(A \cup B)$ can't be bigger than $n(U)$. If your numbers break these limits, re-read the problem.
  • $n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$
  • $n(\overline{A}) = n(U) - n(A)$; "neither" $= n(U) - n(A \cup B)$.
  • Three sets: add singles, subtract pairs, add the triple.

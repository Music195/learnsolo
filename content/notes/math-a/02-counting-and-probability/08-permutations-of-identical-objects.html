Permutations with Identical Objects

Swapping identical items changes nothing

Arranging the letters of AAB: if the two A's were different (A₁, A₂) there would be $3! = 6$ orders. But A₁A₂B and A₂A₁B look the same, so every real arrangement was counted $2! = 2$ times:

$$\frac{3!}{2!} = 3 \qquad (\text{AAB, ABA, BAA})$$

Arranging $n$ items with repeats

$$\frac{n!}{p!\, q!\, r! \cdots} \qquad (p + q + r + \dots = n)$$

$p$ items of one kind, $q$ of another, and so on. Items that appear once contribute $1! = 1$.

Two kinds = choosing positions

$$\frac{n!}{p!\, q!} = {}_n\mathrm{C}_p$$

With only two kinds, just choose which $p$ of the $n$ places get the first kind.

Arrangements of a word

Example 1

How many different arrangements of the letters of MISSISSIPPI are there?

Show solution
  1. 11 letters: I × 4, S × 4, P × 2, M × 1
  2. $\dfrac{11!}{4!\,4!\,2!} = \dfrac{39916800}{24 \cdot 24 \cdot 2}$

$34650$

Example 2

In a grid, you walk from the bottom-left corner to a point 5 blocks right and 3 blocks up, only moving right or up. How many shortest routes are there?

Show solution
  1. Every shortest route is a sequence of 5 R's and 3 U's, e.g. RRURRUUR.
  2. Count arrangements of RRRRRUUU: $\dfrac{8!}{5!\,3!} = {}_8\mathrm{C}_3$

$56$ routes

Example 3

How many 5-digit numbers can be made from the digits $0, 0, 1, 2, 3$?

Show solution
  1. All arrangements: $\dfrac{5!}{2!} = 60$
  2. Arrangements starting with $0$: arrange the other four ($0, 1, 2, 3$): $4! = 24$
  3. Subtract the ones that aren't 5-digit numbers

$60 - 24 = 36$

When the digits include $0$, numbers can't start with $0$. Count all arrangements, then subtract those starting with $0$.
  • $\dfrac{n!}{p!\,q!\,r!\cdots}$: divide by the factorial of each repeated group.
  • Two kinds of item: same as ${}_n\mathrm{C}_p$ (choose positions).
  • Shortest grid routes = arrangements of R's and U's.

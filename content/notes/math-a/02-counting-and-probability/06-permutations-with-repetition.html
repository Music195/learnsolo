Counting Repeated Permutations

Repeats allowed

When the same item may be used again, every position has the full set of choices. A 3-digit lock with digits $0$–$9$ has $10 \times 10 \times 10 = 1000$ codes, because $000$ and $777$ are allowed.

Repeated permutation

$$\underbrace{n \times n \times \cdots \times n}_{r \text{ positions}} = n^r$$

$n$ = number of choices for each position
$r$ = number of positions ($r$ can be larger than $n$)

The base is the number of options for each choice; the power is how many choices are made. 4 people each picking one of 3 rooms: each person makes a choice from 3 options, so $3^4 = 81$, not $4^3$.

List every sequence

Example 1

How many 4-digit numbers can be made with the digits $0$–$9$ (repeats allowed)?

Show solution
  1. First digit: 9 choices (not 0); the other three: 10 each

$9 \times 10^3 = 9000$

Example 2

How many subsets does $\{a, b, c, d, e\}$ have?

Show solution
  1. Each element is either in or out: 2 choices, 5 times

$2^5 = 32$

Example 3

5 people are split into two rooms A and B. Each room must have at least one person. How many ways?

Show solution
  1. Each person picks room A or B: $2^5 = 32$ ways
  2. Remove the 2 cases with an empty room (everyone in A, everyone in B)

$32 - 2 = 30$

If the two rooms are not named (just "two groups"), swapping A and B gives the same split, so Example 3 becomes $30 \div 2 = 15$.
  • Repeats allowed: $n^r$ ($n$ choices per position, $r$ positions).
  • Restrictions (no leading 0) change the count for that position only.
  • Splitting into non-empty named groups: $2^n - 2$; unnamed: divide by $2$.

Multiplication Rule of Probability

Probability once you know something happened

$P_A(B)$ (also written $P(B \mid A)$) is the probability of $B$ given that $A$ has happened. Knowing $A$ shrinks the set of possible outcomes to $A$ itself, so we count $B$ only inside $A$.

Example: a bag has 5 red and 3 white balls. Take one red ball out. Now 4 of the 7 remaining are red, so $P(\text{2nd red} \mid \text{1st red}) = \dfrac{4}{7}$.

Conditional probability

$$P_A(B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)} = \frac{n(A \cap B)}{n(A)}$$

Multiplication rule

$$P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P_A(B)$$

If $A$ doesn't affect $B$ (independent), $P_A(B) = P(B)$ and this becomes $P(A)\,P(B)$ from note 12.

Two draws without replacement

Example 1

Two cards are drawn one after another, without replacement, from 52. Find the probability that both are hearts.

Show solution
  1. $P(\text{1st heart}) = \dfrac{13}{52}$
  2. Given the first was a heart, 12 hearts remain among 51: $\dfrac{12}{51}$
  3. $\dfrac{13}{52} \times \dfrac{12}{51} = \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{4}{17}$

$\dfrac{1}{17}$

Example 2

There are 10 lottery tickets and 3 are winners. A draws one, then B draws one (no replacement). Find the probability that B wins.

Show solution
  1. A wins, then B wins: $\dfrac{3}{10} \times \dfrac{2}{9} = \dfrac{6}{90}$
  2. A loses, then B wins: $\dfrac{7}{10} \times \dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{21}{90}$
  3. Add the two paths: $\dfrac{27}{90}$

$\dfrac{3}{10}$, the same as A's chance. Order of drawing doesn't matter.

Example 3

In a class of 40, 25 are in a club; 15 of the club members are girls. A student picked at random is in a club. Find the probability that the student is a girl.

Show solution
  1. We know the student is in a club, so only the 25 club members count.
  2. $P_{\text{club}}(\text{girl}) = \dfrac{n(\text{club} \cap \text{girl})}{n(\text{club})} = \dfrac{15}{25}$

$\dfrac{3}{5}$

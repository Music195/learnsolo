Multiplication Rule for Counting

"This, and then that"

When a choice is made in steps, draw a tree: every branch of the first step splits into the same number of branches for the next step. The total number of ends is the product.

3 shirts and 2 pairs of trousers: each shirt goes with 2 trousers, so there are $3 \times 2 = 6$ outfits.

Multiplication rule

$$\text{step 1 in } m \text{ ways, then step 2 in } n \text{ ways} \;\Longrightarrow\; m \times n \text{ ways}$$

Works for any number of steps: $n_1 \times n_2 \times \dots \times n_k$.

The number of choices at each step must be the same whatever was chosen before. The actual options can change: when picking a president then a vice-president from 5 people, the vice-president options depend on who was picked first, but there are always 4 of them, so $5 \times 4 = 20$ is correct.

Example 1

How many 3-digit numbers can be written using the digits $0$–$9$ (repeats allowed)?

Show solution
  1. Hundreds digit: 9 choices (not 0)
  2. Tens digit: 10 choices; units digit: 10 choices

$9 \times 10 \times 10 = 900$

Example 2

How many ways can a president and a vice-president be chosen from 5 people?

Show solution
  1. President: 5 choices
  2. Vice-president: any of the other 4

$5 \times 4 = 20$

Example 3

Expand $(a + b)(p + q + r)(x + y)$. How many terms are there?

Show solution
  1. Each term takes one letter from each bracket.
  2. $2$ choices, then $3$, then $2$

$2 \times 3 \times 2 = 12$ terms

The number-of-divisors formula is the multiplication rule: for $72 = 2^3 \times 3^2$, choose the power of 2 (4 ways) and then the power of 3 (3 ways), giving $4 \times 3 = 12$ divisors.
  • Steps one after another → multiply the number of choices.
  • The count at each step must not depend on earlier choices (the options themselves may).
  • "Or" → add; "and then" → multiply.

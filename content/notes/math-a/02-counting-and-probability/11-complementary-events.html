Probability of the Complement Event

Sometimes "not $A$" is easier

Every outcome is either in $A$ or not in $A$, never both. So the two probabilities add up to 1.

This is most useful for "at least one" questions: "at least one six" has many cases (one six, two sixes, …), but its opposite, "no sixes at all", is a single easy case.

Complement rule

$$P(\overline{A}) = 1 - P(A)$$

Because $A$ and $\overline{A}$ are mutually exclusive and together make $U$: $P(A) + P(\overline{A}) = P(U) = 1$.

"At least one"

$$P(\text{at least one}) = 1 - P(\text{none})$$

At least one success

Example 1

A die is rolled 4 times. Find the probability of at least one 6.

Show solution
  1. Opposite: no 6 in any roll. Each roll avoids 6 with probability $\dfrac{5}{6}$.
  2. $P(\text{no } 6) = \left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^4 = \dfrac{625}{1296}$
  3. $1 - \dfrac{625}{1296}$

$\dfrac{671}{1296} \approx 0.518$

Example 2

From 5 boys and 3 girls, 3 people are chosen. Find the probability that at least one is a girl.

Show solution
  1. All choices: ${}_8\mathrm{C}_3 = 56$
  2. Opposite: all boys, ${}_5\mathrm{C}_3 = 10$, so $P(\text{no girls}) = \dfrac{10}{56}$
  3. $1 - \dfrac{10}{56} = \dfrac{46}{56}$

$\dfrac{23}{28}$

Example 3

Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the product of the numbers is even.

Show solution
  1. Opposite: product is odd, which needs both dice odd: $\dfrac{3}{6} \times \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{4}$
  2. $1 - \dfrac{1}{4}$

$\dfrac{3}{4}$

Look for the words "at least", "not all" or "even product". Their opposites ("none", "all", "odd product") are usually one simple case.
The opposite of "at least one 6" is "no 6", not "exactly one 6" and not "at most one 6".
  • $P(\overline{A}) = 1 - P(A)$.
  • "At least one" $= 1 - P(\text{none})$.
  • Write the opposite event carefully before calculating.

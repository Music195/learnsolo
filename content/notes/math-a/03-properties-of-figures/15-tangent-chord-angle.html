Angle Between Tangent and Chord

The tangent angle acts like an inscribed angle

Draw the tangent at $A$ and a chord $AB$. The angle between them (on one side of $AB$) is equal to the inscribed angle $\angle ACB$ for any point $C$ on the arc on the other side of $AB$.

Think of it as an inscribed angle whose corner $C$ has slid all the way round to $A$.

Tangent–chord angle

$$\angle BAT = \angle ACB$$

$AT$: tangent at $A$. $C$: on the arc $AB$ that is not inside $\angle BAT$.

  1. Draw the diameter $AD$. The tangent is perpendicular to it: $\angle DAT = 90°$, so $\angle BAT = 90° - \angle DAB$.
  2. $AD$ is a diameter, so $\angle ABD = 90°$, and in $\triangle ABD$: $\angle ADB = 90° - \angle DAB$.
  3. So $\angle BAT = \angle ADB$, and $\angle ADB = \angle ACB$ (same arc $AB$).

Drag $A$, $B$ or $C$ round the circle

Example 1

$\triangle ABC$ is inscribed in a circle and $AT$ is the tangent at $A$. The angle between $AT$ and $AB$ (on the side away from $C$) is $65°$, and $\angle BAC = 45°$. Find $\angle ACB$ and $\angle ABC$.

Show solution
  1. Tangent–chord: $\angle ACB = 65°$
  2. $\angle ABC = 180° - 45° - 65°$

$\angle ACB = 65°$, $\angle ABC = 70°$

Example 2

Tangents from $P$ touch the circle at $A$ and $B$, with $\angle APB = 80°$. $C$ is on the larger arc $AB$. Find $\angle ACB$.

Show solution
  1. $PA = PB$, so $\angle PAB = \dfrac{180° - 80°}{2} = 50°$.
  2. $\angle PAB$ is a tangent–chord angle, so $\angle ACB = \angle PAB$.

$\angle ACB = 50°$

Example 3

In Example 2, let $D$ be on the smaller arc $AB$. Find $\angle ADB$.

Show solution
  1. $ACBD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with $C$, $D$ opposite.
  2. $\angle ADB = 180° - 50°$

$\angle ADB = 130°$

The tangent makes two angles with $AB$ (one on each side). Each one equals the inscribed angle on the arc across the chord from it.
  • Angle between tangent and chord = inscribed angle on the other side of the chord.
  • Proof: draw the diameter through the touching point.
  • Often combined with equal tangents (isosceles triangle $PAB$).

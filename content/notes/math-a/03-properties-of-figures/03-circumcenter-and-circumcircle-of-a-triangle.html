Circumcenter and Circumcircle of a Triangle

One circle through all three corners

The perpendicular bisector of a side is the line of points that are the same distance from both ends of that side.

The three perpendicular bisectors of a triangle meet at one point $O$, the circumcenter. $O$ is the same distance $R$ from $A$, $B$ and $C$, so the circle with centre $O$ and radius $R$ (the circumcircle) passes through all three corners.

Circumcenter $O$

$$OA = OB = OC = R$$

Acute triangle: $O$ inside. Right triangle: $O$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. Obtuse triangle: $O$ outside.

  1. Let the perpendicular bisectors of $AB$ and $BC$ meet at $O$.
  2. $O$ is on the bisector of $AB$, so $OA = OB$. It is on the bisector of $BC$, so $OB = OC$.
  3. So $OA = OC$, which means $O$ is also on the perpendicular bisector of $CA$. All three meet at $O$.

Drag the corners

Make the triangle obtuse and watch $O$ leave it.

Example 1

$O$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle ABC$, $\angle OBC = 20°$ and $\angle OCA = 30°$. Find $\angle A$.

Show solution
  1. $OA = OB = OC$, so $\triangle OAB$, $\triangle OBC$, $\triangle OCA$ are isosceles: $\angle OCB = 20°$, $\angle OAC = 30°$, and $\angle OAB = \angle OBA = x$.
  2. Angles of $\triangle ABC$: $2(x + 20° + 30°) = 180° \Rightarrow x = 40°$
  3. $\angle A = \angle OAB + \angle OAC = 40° + 30°$

$\angle A = 70°$

Example 2

A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. Find the radius of its circumcircle.

Show solution
  1. Hypotenuse $= \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10$
  2. For a right triangle, $O$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, so $R$ is half of it.

$R = 5$

Example 3

Find the circumcenter and circumradius of the triangle with $A(0, 0)$, $B(4, 0)$, $C(2, 3)$.

Show solution
  1. Perpendicular bisector of $AB$: $x = 2$
  2. $O(2, y)$ with $OA = OC$: $4 + y^2 = (3 - y)^2 = 9 - 6y + y^2 \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{5}{6}$
  3. $R = OC = 3 - \dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{13}{6}$

$O\left(2, \dfrac{5}{6}\right)$, $R = \dfrac{13}{6}$

For angle questions, join $O$ to all three corners. You get three isosceles triangles, each with two radii as equal sides.
  • Circumcenter = where the perpendicular bisectors meet; $OA = OB = OC$.
  • Inside (acute), midpoint of the hypotenuse (right), outside (obtuse).
  • Angle problems: use the three isosceles triangles $OAB$, $OBC$, $OCA$.

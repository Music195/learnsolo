Triangle Side Length Inequality

Two sides must reach further than the third

Going straight from $B$ to $C$ is always shorter than going via $A$. So in any triangle, any two sides added together are longer than the third side.

If two sides only add up to the third (or less), they lie flat or can't even meet: no triangle.

Triangle inequality

$$|b - c| < a < b + c$$

Three lengths $a, b, c$ make a triangle exactly when this holds (and the same for each side). Quick check: the longest side is less than the sum of the other two.

  1. Extend $BA$ past $A$ to $D$ with $AD = AC$. Then $\triangle ACD$ is isosceles, so $\angle ADC = \angle ACD$.
  2. $\angle BCD = \angle BCA + \angle ACD > \angle ACD = \angle BDC$.
  3. In $\triangle BCD$ the larger angle faces the longer side (note 09), so $BD > BC$.
  4. $BD = BA + AD = AB + AC$, so $AB + AC > BC$.

Can these three lengths make a triangle?

Example 1

Which of these can be the sides of a triangle? (a) 3, 4, 8   (b) 5, 5, 10   (c) 4, 6, 9

Show solution
  1. (a) $3 + 4 = 7 < 8$: no.
  2. (b) $5 + 5 = 10$, not more than 10: no (the sides lie flat).
  3. (c) $4 + 6 = 10 > 9$: yes.

Only (c)

Example 2

Two sides of a triangle are 5 and 7. Find the possible lengths $x$ of the third side. How many are whole numbers?

Show solution
  1. $7 - 5 < x < 7 + 5$
  2. Whole numbers: $3, 4, \dots, 11$

$2 < x < 12$; 9 whole numbers

Example 3

The lengths $2x - 1$, $x + 1$, $5$ are the sides of a triangle. Find the range of $x$.

Show solution
  1. $(2x - 1) + (x + 1) > 5 \Rightarrow x > \dfrac{5}{3}$
  2. $(x + 1) + 5 > 2x - 1 \Rightarrow x < 7$
  3. $(2x - 1) + 5 > x + 1 \Rightarrow x > -3$ (already true). All lengths are then positive.

$\dfrac{5}{3} < x < 7$

The inequality is strict. $5, 5, 10$ is not a triangle: the two short sides lie flat on the long one.

External Angle Bisector Theorem in a Triangle

Same ratio, but outside the side

Extend $BA$ past $A$. The angle between that extension and $AC$ is the exterior angle at $A$. Its bisector meets line $BC$ outside the segment $BC$, at a point $D$.

$D$ divides $BC$ externally in the ratio $AB : AC$: the distances from $D$ to $B$ and to $C$ are still in the ratio $AB : AC$.

External angle bisector theorem

$$BD : DC = AB : AC \qquad (D \text{ outside } BC)$$

$D$ is on the side of the shorter of $AB$, $AC$: beyond $C$ if $AB > AC$, beyond $B$ if $AB < AC$.

  1. Let $AB > AC$ and let $F$ be a point on $BA$ extended past $A$, so $AD$ bisects $\angle CAF$.
  2. Through $C$ draw a line parallel to $AD$, meeting $AB$ at $E$.
  3. $\angle ACE = \angle CAD$ (alternate) and $\angle AEC = \angle FAD$ (corresponding). Since $\angle CAD = \angle FAD$, $\triangle AEC$ is isosceles: $AE = AC$.
  4. $EC \parallel AD$, so $BD : DC = BA : AE = AB : AC$.
If $AB = AC$, the exterior bisector is parallel to $BC$ and never meets it, so there is no point $D$.

Example 1

In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 9$, $AC = 6$, $BC = 8$. The exterior bisector at $A$ meets line $BC$ at $D$. Find $CD$ and $BD$.

Show solution
  1. $AB > AC$, so $D$ is beyond $C$. Let $CD = x$; then $BD = 8 + x$.
  2. $(8 + x) : x = 9 : 6 = 3 : 2 \;\Rightarrow\; 16 + 2x = 3x$

$CD = 16$, $BD = 24$

Example 2

In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 4$, $AC = 6$, $BC = 5$. The exterior bisector at $A$ meets line $BC$ at $D$. Find $BD$.

Show solution
  1. $AB < AC$, so $D$ is beyond $B$. Let $BD = x$; then $DC = x + 5$.
  2. $x : (x + 5) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3 \;\Rightarrow\; 3x = 2x + 10$

$BD = 10$

Example 3

In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 6$, $AC = 4$, $BC = 5$. The bisectors of the interior and exterior angles at $A$ meet line $BC$ at $D$ and $E$. Find $DE$.

Show solution
  1. Interior: $BD : DC = 3 : 2$, so $DC = 5 \times \dfrac{2}{5} = 2$
  2. Exterior: $E$ is beyond $C$. With $CE = x$: $(5 + x) : x = 3 : 2 \Rightarrow x = 10$
  3. $DE = DC + CE = 2 + 10$

$DE = 12$

The interior and exterior bisectors at $A$ are always perpendicular (the two half-angles add to $180° \div 2 = 90°$). So $\angle DAE = 90°$ in Example 3.
  • Exterior bisector at $A$ meets line $BC$ at $D$ with $BD : DC = AB : AC$ (external division).
  • $D$ lies beyond the end of the shorter side.
  • No such point when $AB = AC$.

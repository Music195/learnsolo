Circle Inscribed Angle Theorem

Angles that "look at" the same arc

Take two points $A$, $B$ on a circle with centre $O$. From another point $P$ on the circle, the angle $\angle APB$ is an inscribed angle; the angle $\angle AOB$ at the centre is the central angle on the same arc $AB$.

The inscribed angle is always half the central angle, so it stays the same wherever $P$ moves on that part of the circle.

Inscribed angle theorem

$$\angle APB = \frac{1}{2} \angle AOB$$

Inscribed angles on the same arc are equal. If $AB$ is a diameter, $\angle APB = 90°$.

  1. Case where $O$ is inside $\angle APB$: draw the diameter $PD$.
  2. $OP = OA$, so $\triangle OPA$ is isosceles: $\angle OAP = \angle OPA = x$. The exterior angle is $\angle AOD = 2x$.
  3. In the same way, with $\angle OPB = y$, $\angle BOD = 2y$.
  4. $\angle AOB = 2x + 2y = 2(x + y) = 2\angle APB$. (If $O$ is outside the angle, subtract instead of adding.)

Drag $A$, $B$ or $P$ round the circle

Example 1

In circle $O$, $\angle AOB = 120°$ and $P$ is on the larger arc. Find $\angle APB$.

Show solution
  1. $\angle APB = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 120°$

$60°$

Example 2

$AB$ is a diameter and $C$ is on the circle with $\angle CAB = 28°$. Find $\angle ABC$.

Show solution
  1. $AB$ is a diameter, so $\angle ACB = 90°$.
  2. $\angle ABC = 180° - 90° - 28°$

$62°$

Example 3

In circle $O$, $\angle AOB = 100°$. $P$ is on the smaller arc $AB$. Find $\angle APB$.

Show solution
  1. $P$ looks at the other arc, whose central angle is $360° - 100° = 260°$.
  2. $\angle APB = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 260°$

$130°$

Use the central angle of the arc opposite $P$ (the arc $P$ is not on). If $P$ is on the small arc, that central angle is more than $180°$.
  • Inscribed angle $= \dfrac{1}{2}$ central angle on the same arc.
  • Same arc → equal inscribed angles.
  • Angle in a semicircle $= 90°$.

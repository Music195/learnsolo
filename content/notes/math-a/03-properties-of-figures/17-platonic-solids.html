Platonic Solids

Only five perfect solids

A regular polyhedron (Platonic solid) is a convex solid whose faces are all the same regular polygon, with the same number of faces meeting at every corner.

There are exactly five: the tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron.

Euler's polyhedron formula

$$V - E + F = 2$$

$V$: vertices (corners), $E$: edges, $F$: faces. True for every convex polyhedron.

Counting edges and corners

$$E = \frac{pF}{2}, \qquad V = \frac{pF}{q}$$

Each face has $p$ edges and $q$ faces meet at each corner. Every edge is shared by 2 faces; every corner by $q$ faces.

  1. At least 3 faces meet at a corner, and their angles must add to less than $360°$ (at exactly $360°$ the corner is flat).
  2. Triangles ($60°$): 3, 4 or 5 per corner → tetrahedron, octahedron, icosahedron.
  3. Squares ($90°$): only 3 → cube. Pentagons ($108°$): only 3 → dodecahedron.
  4. Hexagons ($120°$): $3 \times 120° = 360°$ already, so no larger polygon works. That makes exactly five.

Example 1

An icosahedron has 20 triangular faces, 5 meeting at each corner. Find $E$ and $V$ and check Euler's formula.

Show solution
  1. $E = \dfrac{3 \times 20}{2} = 30$, $\;V = \dfrac{3 \times 20}{5} = 12$
  2. $12 - 30 + 20 = 2$ ✓

$E = 30$, $V = 12$

Example 2

A football is made of 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons. How many edges and corners does it have?

Show solution
  1. $F = 32$. Edges: $\dfrac{12 \times 5 + 20 \times 6}{2} = \dfrac{180}{2} = 90$
  2. Euler: $V = 2 - F + E = 2 - 32 + 90$

$E = 90$, $V = 60$

Example 3

A regular tetrahedron has edge length 2. Find its surface area.

Show solution
  1. Each face is an equilateral triangle with area $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 2^2 = \sqrt{3}$.
  2. Four faces: $4\sqrt{3}$

$4\sqrt{3}$

Join the centres of neighbouring faces of a cube and you get an octahedron (and the other way round). The dodecahedron and icosahedron pair up the same way; that's why their $E$ match and their $V$, $F$ swap.
  • Exactly five regular polyhedra.
  • $V - E + F = 2$.
  • $E = \dfrac{pF}{2}$: each edge belongs to two faces.
  • Only five because the face angles at a corner must add to less than $360°$.

Power of a Point Theorem

Same product along every line through $P$

Fix a point $P$ and draw any line through it that cuts the circle at two points $A$ and $B$. Multiply the distances $PA \times PB$. Whichever line you choose, you get the same number. It works whether $P$ is inside or outside the circle.

Power of a point

$$PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$$

Line 1 meets the circle at $A$, $B$; line 2 at $C$, $D$. $P$ can be inside (two chords crossing) or outside (two lines from $P$).

With a tangent ($P$ outside)

$$PA \cdot PB = PT^2$$

$PT$: tangent from $P$, touching at $T$. A tangent is a line whose two meeting points have joined into one.

  1. Inside: $\angle PAC = \angle PDB$ (same arc $CB$) and $\angle APC = \angle DPB$ (vertically opposite), so $\triangle PAC \sim \triangle PDB$. Then $\dfrac{PA}{PD} = \dfrac{PC}{PB}$, i.e. $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$.
  2. Outside ($A$, $C$ nearer to $P$): $ABDC$ is cyclic, so $\angle PAC = 180° - \angle BAC = \angle BDC$. With the shared angle at $P$, again $\triangle PAC \sim \triangle PDB$.
  3. Tangent: $\angle PTA = \angle PBT$ (tangent–chord, note 15) and the angle at $P$ is shared, so $\triangle PTA \sim \triangle PBT$ and $PT^2 = PA \cdot PB$.

Drag $P$, or turn the lines with $Q_1$, $Q_2$

Example 1

Chords $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $P$. $PA = 3$, $PB = 4$, $PC = 2$. Find $PD$.

Show solution
  1. $3 \times 4 = 2 \times PD$

$PD = 6$

Example 2

From $P$ outside a circle, one line meets it at $A$ then $B$ with $PA = 4$, $AB = 5$. Another meets it at $C$ then $D$ with $PC = 3$. Find $CD$.

Show solution
  1. $PB = 4 + 5 = 9$, so $PA \cdot PB = 36$.
  2. $3 \cdot PD = 36 \Rightarrow PD = 12$
  3. $CD = PD - PC = 12 - 3$

$CD = 9$

Example 3

$PT$ is a tangent with $PT = 6$. A line from $P$ meets the circle at $A$ then $B$ with $PA = 4$. Find $AB$.

Show solution
  1. $PA \cdot PB = PT^2 \Rightarrow 4 \cdot PB = 36 \Rightarrow PB = 9$
  2. $AB = 9 - 4$

$AB = 5$

Every length is measured from $P$. Outside the circle, use $PB = PA + AB$, not $AB$ itself.
  • $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$ for any two lines through $P$.
  • With a tangent: $PA \cdot PB = PT^2$.
  • Proof: similar triangles from inscribed angles.

Converse of the Inscribed Angle Theorem

Equal angles put points on one circle

The inscribed angle theorem says: points on the same arc see $AB$ at the same angle. The converse goes the other way: if two points on the same side of $AB$ see it at the same angle, then all four points lie on one circle (they are concyclic).

Converse of the inscribed angle theorem

$$\angle APB = \angle AQB \;\Rightarrow\; A, B, P, Q \text{ are on one circle}$$

$P$ and $Q$ must be on the same side of line $AB$. Special case: $\angle APB = \angle AQB = 90°$ puts $P$, $Q$ on the circle with diameter $AB$.

  1. Draw the circle through $A$, $B$, $P$. Let $Q'$ be where the ray $AQ$ meets this circle; then $\angle AQ'B = \angle APB$.
  2. If $Q$ is inside the circle, $\angle AQB$ is an exterior angle of $\triangle QQ'B$, so $\angle AQB > \angle AQ'B$.
  3. If $Q$ is outside, the same argument gives $\angle AQB < \angle AQ'B$.
  4. So $\angle AQB = \angle APB$ is only possible when $Q$ is on the circle.

Drag $Q$ (or $A$, $B$, $P$)

The circle passes through $A$, $B$, $P$. Compare the two angles inside, on and outside it.

Example 1

In quadrilateral $ABCD$, $\angle ADB = \angle ACB = 40°$ and $\angle BAC = 30°$. Find $\angle BDC$.

Show solution
  1. $C$, $D$ are on the same side of $AB$ and see it at the same angle, so $A, B, C, D$ are on one circle.
  2. $\angle BDC$ and $\angle BAC$ stand on the same arc $BC$, so they are equal.

$\angle BDC = 30°$

Example 2

In $\triangle ABC$, $BE$ and $CF$ are altitudes ($E$ on $CA$, $F$ on $AB$). Show that $B$, $C$, $E$, $F$ are on one circle.

Show solution
  1. $\angle BEC = 90°$ and $\angle BFC = 90°$.
  2. $E$ and $F$ are on the same side of $BC$ and see $BC$ at the same angle.

$B, C, E, F$ lie on the circle with diameter $BC$.

Example 3

$P$ and $Q$ are on the same side of $AB$, with $\angle APB = 50°$ and $\angle AQB = 55°$. Where is $Q$ compared with the circle through $A$, $B$, $P$?

Show solution
  1. The angles are not equal, so the four points are not on one circle.
  2. A bigger angle means $Q$ is closer to $AB$ than the circle: inside.

$Q$ is inside the circle.

Check that the two points are on the same side of $AB$. On opposite sides, equal angles do not give a circle (there the angles must add to $180°$ instead, see note 13).
  • Same side of $AB$ and $\angle APB = \angle AQB$ → $A, B, P, Q$ concyclic.
  • Inside the circle → bigger angle; outside → smaller angle.
  • Two right angles on $AB$ → circle with diameter $AB$.

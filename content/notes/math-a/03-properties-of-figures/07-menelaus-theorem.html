Menelaus' Theorem (メネラウスの定理)

A straight line cutting a triangle

A straight line $\ell$ (not through a corner) crosses the lines $BC$, $CA$, $AB$ at $P$, $Q$, $R$. A straight line can cross two sides of a triangle, or none, so at least one of the points is on an extended side.

Walk $B \to P \to C \to Q \to A \to R \to B$ and multiply the ratios, just like Ceva's theorem. Again you get 1.

Menelaus' theorem

$$\frac{BP}{PC} \cdot \frac{CQ}{QA} \cdot \frac{AR}{RB} = 1$$

All lengths are positive, even when a point is on an extended side.

  1. Through $C$ draw a line parallel to $\ell$, meeting line $AB$ at $T$.
  2. Parallel lines cut lines in equal ratios: $\dfrac{BP}{PC} = \dfrac{BR}{RT}$ and $\dfrac{CQ}{QA} = \dfrac{TR}{RA}$.
  3. So the product is $\dfrac{BR}{RT} \cdot \dfrac{RT}{RA} \cdot \dfrac{AR}{RB} = 1$.

Drag the line (points $L_1$, $L_2$) or the corners

Example 1

A line meets $BC$ extended past $C$ at $P$ with $BP : PC = 3 : 1$, and meets $CA$ at $Q$ with $CQ : QA = 1 : 2$. It meets $AB$ at $R$. Find $AR : RB$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{3}{1} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{AR}{RB} = 1$

$AR : RB = 2 : 3$

Example 2

In $\triangle ABC$, $F$ is on $AB$ with $AF : FB = 1 : 2$ and $D$ is on $BC$ with $BD : DC = 3 : 2$. $AD$ and $CF$ meet at $P$. Find $AP : PD$.

Show solution
  1. Look at $\triangle ABD$ and the straight line $F$–$P$–$C$. It crosses $AB$ at $F$, $AD$ at $P$, and $BD$ extended at $C$.
  2. Walk $A \to F \to B \to C \to D \to P \to A$: $\;\dfrac{AF}{FB} \cdot \dfrac{BC}{CD} \cdot \dfrac{DP}{PA} = 1$
  3. $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot \dfrac{DP}{PA} = 1 \Rightarrow \dfrac{DP}{PA} = \dfrac{4}{5}$

$AP : PD = 5 : 4$

Example 3

Use Menelaus' theorem to show that the centroid $G$ divides the median $AL$ in the ratio $2 : 1$.

Show solution
  1. $N$ is the midpoint of $AB$; the median $CN$ passes through $G$.
  2. Triangle $ABL$ with line $N$–$G$–$C$: $\;\dfrac{AN}{NB} \cdot \dfrac{BC}{CL} \cdot \dfrac{LG}{GA} = 1$
  3. $1 \cdot 2 \cdot \dfrac{LG}{GA} = 1 \Rightarrow \dfrac{LG}{GA} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$AG : GL = 2 : 1$

In ratio problems, first find a triangle and a straight line that crosses all three of its (extended) sides. The unknown ratio should lie on one of those sides.
Ceva: three lines through one point inside. Menelaus: one straight line across the triangle. The formulas look the same, so decide which picture you have first.
  • A line crossing $BC$, $CA$, $AB$ at $P$, $Q$, $R$: $\dfrac{BP}{PC} \cdot \dfrac{CQ}{QA} \cdot \dfrac{AR}{RB} = 1$.
  • Walk round the triangle: corner → point → corner → …
  • Best tool for finding a ratio like $AP : PD$ inside a figure.

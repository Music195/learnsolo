Centroid & Medians (重心・中線)

The balance point

A median joins a corner to the midpoint of the opposite side. A triangle has three medians, and they always meet at one point $G$, the centroid. A cardboard triangle balances on its centroid.

$G$ sits two thirds of the way along each median, measured from the corner.

Centroid divides each median 2 : 1

$$AG : GL = BG : GM = CG : GN = 2 : 1$$

$L$, $M$, $N$: midpoints of $BC$, $CA$, $AB$.

Coordinates

$$G\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3},\; \frac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3}\right)$$

The average of the three corners.

  1. Let the medians $BM$ and $CN$ meet at $G$.
  2. $N$, $M$ are midpoints of $AB$, $AC$, so by the midpoint theorem $NM \parallel BC$ and $NM = \dfrac{1}{2} BC$.
  3. So $\triangle GMN \sim \triangle GBC$ with ratio $1 : 2$, giving $BG : GM = CG : GN = 2 : 1$.
  4. The same argument shows median $AL$ cuts $BM$ at the point dividing it $2 : 1$, which is $G$ again. All three medians meet at $G$.

Drag the corners

The medians cut the triangle into six small triangles. Their areas are always equal.

Example 1

The median $AL$ of $\triangle ABC$ has length 9. $G$ is the centroid. Find $AG$ and $GL$.

Show solution
  1. $AG : GL = 2 : 1$, so $AG = 9 \times \dfrac{2}{3}$, $GL = 9 \times \dfrac{1}{3}$

$AG = 6$, $GL = 3$

Example 2

The area of $\triangle ABC$ is 36 and $G$ is its centroid. Find the area of $\triangle GBC$.

Show solution
  1. $\triangle GBC$ and $\triangle ABC$ share the base $BC$.
  2. $GL : AL = 1 : 3$, so the height from $G$ is $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the height from $A$.

$36 \times \dfrac{1}{3} = 12$

Example 3

$\triangle ABC$ has $A(1, 2)$ and centroid $G(4, 5)$. Find the midpoint $L$ of $BC$.

Show solution
  1. $G$ is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the way from $A$ to $L$, so $L$ is $\dfrac{3}{2}$ of the way: $L = A + \dfrac{3}{2}(G - A)$
  2. $G - A = (3, 3)$, so $L = (1 + 4.5,\; 2 + 4.5)$

$L(5.5,\; 6.5)$

The longer part is next to the corner: $AG = 2 \times GL$, not the other way round.
  • Medians meet at the centroid $G$; $AG : GL = 2 : 1$.
  • $G$ = average of the three corners.
  • $\triangle GBC$, $\triangle GCA$, $\triangle GAB$ each have $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the area; the six small triangles are equal.

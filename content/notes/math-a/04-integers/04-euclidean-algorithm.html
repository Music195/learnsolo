Euclidean Algorithm

Finding the GCD by repeated division

Listing every divisor of two large numbers is slow. Instead, keep dividing the larger number by the smaller one and replacing the pair with (smaller number, remainder). The numbers shrink quickly, and the last non-zero remainder is the greatest common divisor.

The key step

$$a = bq + r \;\Longrightarrow\; \gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$$

Repeat until the remainder is $0$; the divisor at that point is the GCD.

Why $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$

  1. If $d$ divides both $a$ and $b$, it divides $r = a - bq$. So every common divisor of $a, b$ is a common divisor of $b, r$.
  2. If $d$ divides both $b$ and $r$, it divides $a = bq + r$. So every common divisor of $b, r$ is a common divisor of $a, b$.
  3. The two pairs have exactly the same common divisors, so their greatest common divisors are equal.

Step-by-step GCD

Example 1

Find $\gcd(252, 105)$.

Show solution
  1. $252 = 105 \times 2 + 42$
  2. $105 = 42 \times 2 + 21$
  3. $42 = 21 \times 2 + 0$

$\gcd(252, 105) = 21$

Example 2

Are $15$ and $28$ coprime (GCD equal to $1$)?

Show solution
  1. $28 = 15 \times 1 + 13$
  2. $15 = 13 \times 1 + 2$
  3. $13 = 2 \times 6 + 1$
  4. $2 = 1 \times 2 + 0$

$\gcd = 1$, so yes, they are coprime.

Example 3

Simplify the fraction $\dfrac{391}{667}$.

Show solution
  1. $667 = 391 \times 1 + 276$
  2. $391 = 276 \times 1 + 115$
  3. $276 = 115 \times 2 + 46$
  4. $115 = 46 \times 2 + 23$
  5. $46 = 23 \times 2 + 0$, so $\gcd = 23$

$\dfrac{391}{667} = \dfrac{17}{29}$

Once you have the GCD, the LCM follows: $\;\gcd(a, b) \times \operatorname{lcm}(a, b) = ab$. For $252$ and $105$: $\operatorname{lcm} = \dfrac{252 \times 105}{21} = 1260$.
The GCD is the last non-zero remainder (equivalently, the divisor in the step where the remainder becomes $0$), not the final quotient.
  • $a = bq + r \Rightarrow \gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$.
  • Repeat until the remainder is $0$; the last non-zero remainder is the GCD.
  • $\gcd = 1$ means coprime. $\;\gcd \times \operatorname{lcm} = ab$.

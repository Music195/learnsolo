Congruence Rules

Same remainder

Two integers are congruent modulo $m$ when they leave the same remainder after division by $m$. For example, $17$ and $5$ both leave remainder $5$ when divided by $6$:

$$17 \equiv 5 \pmod{6}$$

Congruences let you replace a big number by its small remainder in the middle of a calculation.

Definition

$$a \equiv b \pmod{m} \iff a - b \text{ is a multiple of } m$$

Rules: if $a \equiv c$ and $b \equiv d \pmod{m}$

$$a + b \equiv c + d \qquad a - b \equiv c - d \qquad ab \equiv cd \qquad a^n \equiv c^n$$

All $\pmod{m}$; $n$ is a positive integer. You can add, subtract, multiply and take powers, using remainders instead of the original numbers.

You can't divide both sides in general. $2 \times 3 \equiv 2 \times 0 \pmod{6}$ is true ($6 \equiv 0$), but cancelling the $2$ gives $3 \equiv 0 \pmod{6}$, which is false.

Remainder of a power

Example 1

Find the remainder when $38 \times 45$ is divided by $7$.

Show solution
  1. $38 \equiv 3$ and $45 \equiv 3 \pmod{7}$
  2. $38 \times 45 \equiv 3 \times 3 = 9 \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$

Remainder $2$ (check: $1710 = 7 \times 244 + 2$)

Example 2

Find the remainder when $2^{100}$ is divided by $7$.

Show solution
  1. Look for a power that is $\equiv 1$: $\;2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$
  2. $100 = 3 \times 33 + 1$, so $2^{100} = (2^3)^{33} \times 2$
  3. $\equiv 1^{33} \times 2 = 2 \pmod{7}$

Remainder $2$

Example 3

Find all integers $x$ with $x \equiv 3 \pmod{7}$ and $x \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$.

Show solution
  1. From the second condition, $x = 5k + 4$
  2. Substitute into the first: $\;5k + 4 \equiv 3$, so $5k \equiv -1 \equiv 6 \pmod{7}$
  3. Multiply by $3$ (because $5 \times 3 = 15 \equiv 1$): $\;k \equiv 18 \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$, so $k = 7j + 4$
  4. $x = 5(7j + 4) + 4 = 35j + 24$

$x \equiv 24 \pmod{35}$, i.e. $x = \dots, -11, 24, 59, 94, \dots$

The last digit of a number is its remainder mod $10$. So "find the last digit of $3^{2025}$" is a congruence problem: $3^4 = 81 \equiv 1 \pmod{10}$ and $2025 = 4 \times 506 + 1$, so the last digit is $3$.
  • $a \equiv b \pmod{m}$: same remainder, i.e. $m$ divides $a - b$.
  • You may add, subtract, multiply and take powers of congruences, but not divide.
  • For big powers, find a power that is $\equiv 1$ (or a repeating cycle) first.

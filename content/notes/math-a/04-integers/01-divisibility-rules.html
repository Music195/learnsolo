Divisibility Rules

Checking divisibility without dividing

$a$ is divisible by $b$ when $a = bk$ for some integer $k$ (the remainder is $0$).

For small divisors there are shortcuts that only look at the last digits or the sum of the digits.

Look at the last digits

Add up the digits

Divisible byCheckExample
$3$digit sum divisible by $3$$123$: $1 + 2 + 3 = 6$ ✔
$9$digit sum divisible by $9$$729$: $7 + 2 + 9 = 18$ ✔

Combine two rules

$$6 = 2 \times 3 \qquad 12 = 4 \times 3 \qquad 15 = 3 \times 5$$

Divisible by $6$ ⇔ divisible by $2$ and by $3$. This works because the two factors share no common divisor.

Only combine factors with no common divisor. $24$ is divisible by $4$ and by $6$, but "divisible by $4$ and $6$" does not mean divisible by $24$: $12$ passes both tests and is not divisible by $24$. Use $24 = 8 \times 3$ instead.

Why the rules work

  1. Last digits: $100$ is divisible by $4$, so in $516 = 5 \times 100 + 16$ only the $16$ matters. Likewise $10$ is divisible by $2$ and $5$, and $1000$ by $8$.
  2. Digit sums: write a 3-digit number as $100a + 10b + c$.
  3. Split off multiples of 9: $\;100a + 10b + c = (99a + 9b) + (a + b + c)$
  4. $99a + 9b$ is always a multiple of $9$ (and so of $3$). So the number is divisible by $9$ or $3$ exactly when $a + b + c$ is.

Test a number

Example 1

Is $1728$ divisible by $8$?

Show solution
  1. Last three digits: $728 = 8 \times 91$

Yes

Example 2

Is $43659$ divisible by $9$? By $6$?

Show solution
  1. Digit sum: $4 + 3 + 6 + 5 + 9 = 27 = 9 \times 3$, so divisible by $9$ (and by $3$).
  2. Last digit $9$ is odd, so not divisible by $2$, and therefore not by $6$.

Divisible by $9$, not by $6$.

Example 3

The 4-digit number $3\,\square\,72$ is divisible by $9$. Find the missing digit $\square$.

Show solution
  1. Digit sum: $3 + \square + 7 + 2 = 12 + \square$
  2. $12 + \square$ must be a multiple of 9, and $0 \le \square \le 9$, so $12 + \square = 18$

$\square = 6$ (the number is $3672 = 9 \times 408$)

  • $2, 5, 10$: last digit. $\;4, 25$: last 2 digits. $\;8$: last 3 digits.
  • $3, 9$: sum of the digits.
  • Combine rules only for factors with no common divisor, e.g. $6 = 2 \times 3$, $12 = 4 \times 3$.

Integer Division

Quotient and remainder

Dividing $17$ by $5$: $5$ fits into $17$ three times ($15$), with $2$ left over. So the quotient is $3$ and the remainder is $2$:

$$17 = 5 \times 3 + 2$$

This works for every integer, including negative ones, as long as the remainder is kept between $0$ and $b - 1$.

Division with remainder

$$a = bq + r, \qquad 0 \le r < b$$

$a$ = number being divided (any integer)
$b$ = divisor (a positive integer)
$q$ = quotient, $\;r$ = remainder
For each $a$ and $b$ there is exactly one such pair $q, r$.

The remainder is never negative. Dividing $-17$ by $5$: $-17 = 5 \times (-3) - 2$ is not allowed ($r = -2$). Go one step further: $-17 = 5 \times (-4) + 3$, so $q = -4$, $r = 3$.

Every integer has a remainder type

Divided by $b$, every integer leaves one of the remainders $0, 1, \dots, b - 1$. So every integer can be written in exactly one of $b$ forms. For $b = 3$:

$$3k, \qquad 3k + 1, \qquad 3k + 2 \qquad (k \text{ an integer})$$

Checking each form separately is a standard way to prove facts about all integers (see Example 3).

Example 1

Find the quotient and remainder when $-23$ is divided by $6$.

Show solution
  1. Find the largest multiple of $6$ that is $\le -23$: $6 \times (-4) = -24$
  2. $-23 = 6 \times (-4) + 1$, and $0 \le 1 < 6$ ✔

$q = -4$, $\;r = 1$

Example 2

When $a$ is divided by $7$ the remainder is $3$. Find the remainder when $2a + 5$ is divided by $7$.

Show solution
  1. Write $a = 7k + 3$
  2. $2a + 5 = 14k + 11 = 7(2k + 1) + 4$

Remainder $4$

Example 3

Show that $n^2$ divided by $3$ always leaves remainder $0$ or $1$ (never $2$).

Show solution
  1. $n = 3k$: $\;n^2 = 9k^2 = 3(3k^2)$, remainder $0$
  2. $n = 3k + 1$: $\;n^2 = 9k^2 + 6k + 1 = 3(3k^2 + 2k) + 1$, remainder $1$
  3. $n = 3k + 2$: $\;n^2 = 9k^2 + 12k + 4 = 3(3k^2 + 4k + 1) + 1$, remainder $1$

Every integer has one of these three forms, so the remainder is always $0$ or $1$.

  • $a = bq + r$ with $0 \le r < b$; $q$ and $r$ are unique.
  • For negative $a$, the quotient goes down so the remainder stays $\ge 0$.
  • Split integers by remainder ($3k, 3k+1, 3k+2$) to prove things about all of them.

Chapter 11: Application of Integration

Area Between a Curve and the x-Axis

Definite integration measures the geometric area bounded by a curve $y = f(x)$ and the $x$-axis. When a curve dips below the $x$-axis, we split the interval at its roots and sum the absolute values of each sub-integral.

1. Area Above and Below the $x$-Axis

If $f(x)$ is continuous and non-negative ($f(x) \ge 0$) on the closed interval $[a, b]$, then the area bounded by the curve $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis, and the vertical lines $x = a$ and $x = b$ is: $$\text{Area} = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$ However, on any interval where $f(x) < 0$ (below the $x$-axis), the definite integral $\int f(x)\,dx$ is negative. Since geometric area is always non-negative, we must take the absolute value of the integral over negative regions.

4-Step Procedure to Find the Total Area Between $y = f(x)$ and the $x$-Axis on $[a, b]$
  1. Step 1: Find the $x$-intercepts of the graph by solving $f(x) = 0$ on $(a, b)$.
  2. Step 2: Divide the interval $[a, b]$ into subintervals at the zeros of $f$.
  3. Step 3: Integrate $f(x)$ over each subinterval separately.
  4. Step 4: Add the absolute values of the integrals from Step 3: $$\text{Total Area} = \int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx = \left|\int_a^{c_1} f(x)\,dx\right| + \left|\int_{c_1}^b f(x)\,dx\right|$$

2. Interactive Demo: Net Integral vs. Total Enclosed Area

Compare the net definite integral $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ (where regions below the $x$-axis subtract from regions above) with the total geometric area $\int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx$ (where all enclosed regions add positively).

3. Worked Textbook Examples

Example 3 (Textbook p. 199) — Area Above the $x$-Axis
  • (a) $f(x) = 3$ over $[1, 4]$: $$\text{Area} = \int_1^4 3\,dx = \bigl[3x\bigr]_1^4 = 12 - 3 = 9\text{ unit}^2$$
  • (b) $f(x) = 3x + 2$ over $\left[-\dfrac{2}{3}, 0\right]$: $$\text{Area} = \int_{-2/3}^0 (3x + 2)\,dx = \left[\frac{3}{2}x^2 + 2x\right]_{-2/3}^0 = 0 - \left[\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{4}{9}\right) - \frac{4}{3}\right] = \frac{2}{3}\text{ unit}^2$$
  • (c) $f(x) = -x^2 + 4$ over $[-2, 2]$: $$\text{Area} = \int_{-2}^2 (-x^2 + 4)\,dx = \left[-\frac{x^3}{3} + 4x\right]_{-2}^2 = \left(-\frac{8}{3} + 8\right) - \left(\frac{8}{3} - 8\right) = \frac{32}{3} = 10\frac{2}{3}\text{ unit}^2$$
Example 4 (Textbook pp. 200–201) — Net Integral vs. Total Area for $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$ on $\left[0, \dfrac{5}{2}\right]$

(a) Compute the definite integral $\displaystyle \int_0^{5/2} (x^2 - 2x)\,dx$: $$\int_0^{5/2} (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - x^2\right]_0^{5/2} = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^3 - \left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{125}{24} - \frac{25}{4} = -\frac{25}{24} = -1\frac{1}{24}$$

(b) Find the total area enclosed between $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$ and the $x$-axis over $\left[0, \dfrac{5}{2}\right]$:
Setting $f(x) = x(x - 2) = 0$ gives $x = 0, 2$. Thus we split $\left[0, \dfrac{5}{2}\right]$ into $[0, 2]$ (where $f(x) \le 0$) and $\left[2, \dfrac{5}{2}\right]$ (where $f(x) \ge 0$): $$\int_0^2 (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - x^2\right]_0^2 = \frac{8}{3} - 4 = -\frac{4}{3}$$ $$\int_2^{5/2} (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - x^2\right]_2^{5/2} = -\frac{25}{24} - \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = \frac{7}{24}$$ Therefore, the total enclosed area is: $$\text{Area} = \left|-\frac{4}{3}\right| + \left|\frac{7}{24}\right| = \frac{32}{24} + \frac{7}{24} = \frac{39}{24} = \frac{13}{8} = 1\frac{5}{8}\text{ unit}^2$$

Example 5 & Example 6 (Textbook pp. 201–202) — Cosine Area & Finding an Unknown Constant

(a) Example 5: For $f(x) = \cos x$:

  • Area from $x = 0$ to $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$: $\displaystyle \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos x\,dx = \bigl[\sin x\bigr]_0^{\pi/2} = 1\text{ unit}^2$.
  • Area from $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$: $\displaystyle \int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2} \cos x\,dx = \bigl[\sin x\bigr]_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2} = -1 - 1 = -2 \implies \text{Area} = |-2| = 2\text{ unit}^2$.
  • Total area from $x = 0$ to $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$: $1 + 2 = 3\text{ unit}^2$, whereas the net definite integral is $\displaystyle \int_0^{3\pi/2} \cos x\,dx = 1 + (-2) = -1$.

(b) Example 6: The shaded area under $f(x) = k\sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ is $16\text{ unit}^2$. Find $k$.
Solution: $$\int_0^4 k\sqrt{x}\,dx = \left[\frac{2k}{3}x^{3/2}\right]_0^4 = \frac{2k}{3}(8) - 0 = \frac{16k}{3}$$ Since the area is $16$, we have $\dfrac{16k}{3} = 16 \implies k = 3$.

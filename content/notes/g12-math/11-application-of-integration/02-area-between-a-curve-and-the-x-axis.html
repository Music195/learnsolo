Area Between a Curve and the x-Axis

Concept — Area Between $y = f(x)$ and the $x$-Axis

A definite integral $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ measures signed area: regions above the $x$-axis ($f(x) > 0$) contribute positively, while regions below the $x$-axis ($f(x) < 0$) contribute negatively. To find the total geometric area enclosed between $y = f(x)$ and the $x$-axis over $[a, b]$:

  • Case 1 ($f(x) \ge 0$ on $[a, b]$): The curve lies entirely on or above the $x$-axis, so $\text{Area} = \displaystyle \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
  • Case 2 ($f(x)$ Crosses the $x$-Axis on $[a, b]$ — 4-Step Procedure, Textbook p. 202):
    1. Step 1: Find the intersection point(s) of the graph $y = f(x)$ with the $x$-axis by solving $f(x) = 0$.
    2. Step 2: Divide the interval $[a, b]$ into subintervals at the zeros of $f$.
    3. Step 3: Integrate $f(x)$ over each subinterval separately.
    4. Step 4: Add the absolute values of the integrals from Step 3.
Core Formulas — Net Signed Integral vs. Total Enclosed Area
$$\text{Total Area} = \int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx = \left|\int_a^c f(x)\,dx\right| + \left|\int_c^b f(x)\,dx\right|$$
  • $c \in (a, b)$: An $x$-intercept ($f(c) = 0$) where $f(x)$ changes sign.
  • Units: Geometric area is always positive and written in square units ($\text{unit}^2$).
Why Net Integral and Total Area Differ When $f(x)$ Changes Sign (Example 4)

Consider $f(x) = x^2 - 2x = x(x - 2)$ on $\left[0, \dfrac{5}{2}\right]$:

  • On $[0, 2]$, the parabola lies below the $x$-axis ($f(x) \le 0$), so $\displaystyle \int_0^2 (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = -\frac{4}{3} < 0$.
  • On $\left[2, \dfrac{5}{2}\right]$, the parabola lies above the $x$-axis ($f(x) \ge 0$), so $\displaystyle \int_2^{5/2} (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = +\frac{7}{24} > 0$.

If we integrate directly from $0$ to $\frac{5}{2}$, the two lobes partially cancel: $-\frac{4}{3} + \frac{7}{24} = -\frac{25}{24}$. Taking absolute values before adding gives the true enclosed geometric area: $\left|-\frac{4}{3}\right| + \left|\frac{7}{24}\right| = \frac{39}{24} = 1\frac{5}{8}\text{ unit}^2$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 3, 4, 5 & 6

Example 3 (Textbook p. 199): Find the area enclosed between the $x$-axis and:
(a) $f(x) = 3$ over $[1, 4]$   (b) $f(x) = 3x + 2$ over $\left[-\dfrac{2}{3}, 0\right]$   (c) $f(x) = -x^2 + 4$ over $[-2, 2]$.

Show step-by-step solution
  • (a) $\displaystyle \int_1^4 3\,dx = \bigl[3x\bigr]_1^4 = 12 - 3 = 9\text{ unit}^2$.
  • (b) $\displaystyle \int_{-2/3}^0 (3x + 2)\,dx = \left[\frac{3}{2}x^2 + 2x\right]_{-2/3}^0 = 0 - \left(\frac{2}{3} - \frac{4}{3}\right) = \frac{2}{3}\text{ unit}^2$.
  • (c) $\displaystyle \int_{-2}^2 (-x^2 + 4)\,dx = \left[-\frac{x^3}{3} + 4x\right]_{-2}^2 = \frac{16}{3} - \left(-\frac{16}{3}\right) = 10\frac{2}{3}\text{ unit}^2$.

Answer: (a) $9\text{ unit}^2$; (b) $\frac{2}{3}\text{ unit}^2$; (c) $10\frac{2}{3}\text{ unit}^2$.

Example 4 (Textbook pp. 200–201): For $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$ on $\left[0, \dfrac{5}{2}\right]$, compute:
(a) the definite integral $\displaystyle \int_0^{5/2} (x^2 - 2x)\,dx$   (b) the total area enclosed between $f(x)$ and the $x$-axis.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) $\displaystyle \int_0^{5/2} (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - x^2\right]_0^{5/2} = \frac{125}{24} - \frac{25}{4} = -\frac{25}{24} = -1\frac{1}{24}$.

(b) Solving $x^2 - 2x = 0$ gives $x = 0, 2$. Splitting at $x = 2$:

$$\int_0^2 (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = -\frac{4}{3}, \qquad \int_2^{5/2} (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = -\frac{25}{24} - \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = \frac{7}{24}$$ $$\text{Total Area} = \left|-\frac{4}{3}\right| + \left|\frac{7}{24}\right| = \frac{39}{24} = 1\frac{5}{8}\text{ unit}^2$$

Answer: (a) $-1\frac{1}{24}$; (b) $1\frac{5}{8}\text{ unit}^2$.

Example 5 & Example 6 (Textbook pp. 201–202):
(a) Find the area enclosed between $f(x) = \cos x$ and the $x$-axis from $x = 0$ to $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$.
(b) The shaded area under $f(x) = k\sqrt{x}$ over $[0, 4]$ is $16\text{ unit}^2$. Find $k$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Example 5: Since $\cos x = 0$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, we integrate on $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$ and $\left[\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right]$:

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos x\,dx = 1, \qquad \int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2} \cos x\,dx = -2 \implies \text{Total Area} = |1| + |-2| = 3\text{ unit}^2$$

(b) Example 6: $\displaystyle \int_0^4 k\sqrt{x}\,dx = \left[\frac{2k}{3}x^{3/2}\right]_0^4 = \frac{16k}{3} = 16 \implies k = 3$.

Answer: (a) $3\text{ unit}^2$ (while the net integral is $-1$); (b) $k = 3$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Taking $\left|\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\right|$ Without Splitting at Zeros of $f$: Taking the absolute value of the whole integral from $a$ to $b$ still lets positive and negative lobes cancel! You must split $[a, b]$ at every interior root of $f(x) = 0$ first.
Key Takeaways
  • When $f(x) \ge 0$ on $[a, b]$, the area between $y = f(x)$ and the $x$-axis is $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
  • When $f(x)$ crosses the $x$-axis at $x = c$, the total enclosed area is $\left|\int_a^c f(x)\,dx\right| + \left|\int_c^b f(x)\,dx\right|$.

Volumes Using Cross-Sections, Disc and Washer Methods

Concept — Volume by Slicing, Disc Method & Washer Method

Definite integration extends naturally from 2D areas to 3D volumes by summing the areas $A(x)$ of thin cross-sectional slices perpendicular to the $x$-axis:

  • Volume by Method of Slicing (Textbook p. 207): If a solid extends from $x = a$ to $x = b$ and the plane perpendicular to the $x$-axis at $x$ cuts a cross-section of integrable area $A(x)$, then: $$\text{Volume} = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx$$
  • Solid of Revolution — Disc Method (Textbook p. 208): Rotating the region under $y = f(x)$ ($a \le x \le b$) through $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis creates circular disc cross-sections of radius $r = y = f(x)$ and area $A(x) = \pi y^2$: $$\text{Volume of revolution} = \pi \int_a^b y^2\,dx = \pi \int_a^b \bigl[f(x)\bigr]^2\,dx$$
  • Solid of Revolution — Washer Method (Textbook p. 210): Rotating the region bounded between $y = f(x)$ (on top, outer radius) and $y = g(x) \ge 0$ (below, inner radius) for $a \le x \le b$ about the $x$-axis creates hollow ring (washer) cross-sections of area $A(x) = \pi\bigl([f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2\bigr)$: $$\text{Volume of revolution} = \pi \int_a^b \left(\bigl[f(x)\bigr]^2 - \bigl[g(x)\bigr]^2\right)dx$$
Core Formulas — Slicing, Disc & Washer Volumes
$$V_{\text{slice}} = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx \qquad\quad V_{\text{disc}} = \pi \int_a^b \bigl[f(x)\bigr]^2\,dx \qquad\quad V_{\text{washer}} = \pi \int_a^b \left(\bigl[f(x)\bigr]^2 - \bigl[g(x)\bigr]^2\right)dx$$
  • 3-Step Procedure for the Washer Method (Textbook p. 210):
    1. Find the intersection points of $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ to determine $[a, b]$.
    2. Investigate which function is on top ($f$ or $g$) on $[a, b]$.
    3. Integrate $\pi\bigl([f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2\bigr)$ when $f$ is on top (or $\pi\bigl([g(x)]^2 - [f(x)]^2\bigr)$ when $g$ is on top).
Why Washer Cross-Section Area is $\pi\bigl([f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2\bigr)$ and NOT $\pi\bigl[f(x) - g(x)\bigr]^2$

When the vertical strip between $y = g(x)$ and $y = f(x)$ at position $x$ is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis:

  • The outer boundary $y = f(x)$ sweeps a circle of radius $R = f(x)$ and area $\pi R^2 = \pi\bigl[f(x)\bigr]^2$.
  • The inner boundary $y = g(x)$ sweeps an empty circular hole of radius $r = g(x)$ and area $\pi r^2 = \pi\bigl[g(x)\bigr]^2$.

Subtracting the area of the inner hole from the area of the outer disc gives $A(x) = \pi\bigl([f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2\bigr)$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 9, 10, 11 & 12

Example 9 (Textbook p. 207 — Method of Slicing): A pyramid of height $4\text{ m}$ has a rectangular base and cross-sectional area $A(x) = 2x^2$ at distance $x$ from the apex ($0 \le x \le 4$). Find the volume of the pyramid.

Show step-by-step solution

Integrating the cross-sectional area $A(x) = 2x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$:

$$\text{Volume} = \int_0^4 A(x)\,dx = \int_0^4 2x^2\,dx = \left[\frac{2}{3}x^3\right]_0^4 = \frac{128}{3} = 42\frac{2}{3}\text{ m}^3$$

Answer: $42\dfrac{2}{3}\text{ m}^3$.

Example 10 & Example 11 (Textbook pp. 208–209 — Disc Method): Find the volume of revolution formed when each curve is rotated through $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis:
(a) $y = \sqrt{x}$ for $0 \le x \le 3$   (b) $y = e^x$ for $0 \le x \le 1$   (c) $y = x^{1/3} + x^{4/3}$ for $0 \le x \le 1$.

Show step-by-step solution
  • (a) Example 10(a): $\displaystyle V = \pi\int_0^3 (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx = \pi\int_0^3 x\,dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^3 = \frac{9}{2}\pi\text{ unit}^3$.
  • (b) Example 10(b): $\displaystyle V = \pi\int_0^1 (e^x)^2\,dx = \pi\int_0^1 e^{2x}\,dx = \pi\left[\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}\right]_0^1 = \frac{e^2 - 1}{2}\pi\text{ unit}^3$.
  • (c) Example 11: $\displaystyle V = \pi\int_0^1 \left(x^{1/3} + x^{4/3}\right)^2 dx = \pi\int_0^1 \left(x^{2/3} + 2x^{5/3} + x^{8/3}\right)dx = \pi\left[\frac{3}{5}x^{5/3} + \frac{3}{4}x^{8/3} + \frac{3}{11}x^{11/3}\right]_0^1 = 1\frac{137}{220}\pi\text{ unit}^3$.

Answer: (a) $\frac{9}{2}\pi\text{ unit}^3$; (b) $\frac{e^2-1}{2}\pi\text{ unit}^3$; (c) $1\frac{137}{220}\pi\text{ unit}^3$.

Example 12 (Textbook p. 210 — Washer Method): Find the volume of revolution generated by revolving the region between $y = x^2$ and $y = \sqrt{x}$ about the $x$-axis.

Show step-by-step solution

Solving $\sqrt{x} = x^2$ gives $x = 0, 1$. On $[0, 1]$, $y = \sqrt{x}$ is on top of $y = x^2$. By the washer method:

$$V = \pi\int_0^1 \left[(\sqrt{x})^2 - (x^2)^2\right]dx = \pi\int_0^1 \left(x - x^4\right)dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = \frac{3}{10}\pi\text{ unit}^3$$

Answer: $\dfrac{3}{10}\pi\text{ unit}^3$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Writing $\pi\int_a^b [f(x) - g(x)]^2\,dx$ Instead of $\pi\int_a^b \bigl([f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2\bigr)\,dx$: In the washer method, always square the outer radius and inner radius separately before subtracting!
  • Forgetting the Factor $\pi$ in Volumes of Revolution: Cross-sections of solids of revolution are circles/washers, so their areas always carry a factor of $\pi$.
Key Takeaways
  • Volume by slicing: $V = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx$.
  • Disc method around the $x$-axis: $V = \pi\int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx$.
  • Washer method around the $x$-axis: $V = \pi\int_a^b \bigl([f(x)]^2 - [g(x)]^2\bigr)\,dx$.

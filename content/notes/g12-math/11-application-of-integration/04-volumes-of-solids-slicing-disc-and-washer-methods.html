Chapter 11: Application of Integration

Volumes Using Cross-Sections, Disc and Washer Methods

Just as integrating slice lengths gives the area of a plane region, integrating cross-sectional areas $A(x)$ gives the volume of a three-dimensional solid—including solids of revolution formed by rotating curves through $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis.

1. Volume by Method of Slicing

A cross-section of a solid is the plane region formed by intersecting the solid with a plane perpendicular to the $x$-axis.

Volume by Cross-Sectional Slicing

Suppose $A(x)$ is the integrable cross-sectional area of a solid at position $x$ from $x = a$ to $x = b$. Then the volume of the solid is: $$\text{Volume} = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx$$

2. Volumes of Revolution: Disc Method and Washer Method

When a plane region is rotated through $360^\circ$ ($2\pi$ radians) about the $x$-axis, it sweeps out a three-dimensional solid of revolution whose cross-sections are either solid circular discs or hollow circular washers.

Disc Method (Solid Circular Slices)

Rotating the region under $y = f(x)$ ($a \le x \le b$) about the $x$-axis produces circular discs of radius $r = y = f(x)$ and area $A(x) = \pi y^2$: $$V = \pi \int_a^b y^2\,dx = \pi \int_a^b \bigl[f(x)\bigr]^2\,dx$$

Washer Method (Hollow Ring Slices)

Rotating the region bounded above by $y = f(x)$ (outer radius) and below by $y = g(x) \ge 0$ (inner radius) on $[a, b]$ about the $x$-axis gives: $$V = \pi \int_a^b \left(\bigl[f(x)\bigr]^2 - \bigl[g(x)\bigr]^2\right)dx$$

4. Worked Textbook Examples

Example 9 (Textbook p. 207) — Volume of a Pyramid by Slicing

A pyramid of height $4\text{ m}$ has rectangular cross-sections of area $A(x) = 2x^2$ at distance $x$ from the apex ($0 \le x \le 4$). Find the volume of the pyramid.

Solution:

$$\text{Volume} = \int_0^4 A(x)\,dx = \int_0^4 2x^2\,dx = \left[\frac{2}{3}x^3\right]_0^4 = \frac{128}{3} = 42\frac{2}{3}\text{ m}^3$$

Example 10 & Example 11 (Textbook pp. 208–209) — Volume by the Disc Method

(a) Example 10(a): Rotate $y = \sqrt{x}$ for $0 \le x \le 3$ through $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis: $$V = \pi \int_0^3 (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^3 x\,dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^3 = \frac{9}{2}\pi\text{ unit}^3$$

(b) Example 10(b): Rotate $y = e^x$ for $0 \le x \le 1$ through $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis: $$V = \pi \int_0^1 (e^x)^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^1 e^{2x}\,dx = \pi\left[\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}\right]_0^1 = \frac{e^2 - 1}{2}\pi\text{ unit}^3$$

(c) Example 11: Rotate $y = x^{1/3} + x^{4/3}$ for $0 \le x \le 1$ through $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis: $$V = \pi \int_0^1 \left(x^{1/3} + x^{4/3}\right)^2 dx = \pi \int_0^1 \left(x^{2/3} + 2x^{5/3} + x^{8/3}\right)dx$$ $$= \pi\left[\frac{3}{5}x^{5/3} + \frac{3}{4}x^{8/3} + \frac{3}{11}x^{11/3}\right]_0^1 = \pi\left(\frac{3}{5} + \frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{11}\right) = \frac{357}{220}\pi = 1\frac{137}{220}\pi\text{ unit}^3$$

Example 12 (Textbook p. 210) — Volume by the Washer Method

Find the volume of revolution generated by revolving the region bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = \sqrt{x}$ about the $x$-axis.

Solution:

Setting $\sqrt{x} = x^2$ gives $x = x^4 \implies x(x^3 - 1) = 0 \implies x = 0, 1$. On $[0, 1]$, the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ lies above $y = x^2$. By the washer method: $$V = \pi \int_0^1 \left[(\sqrt{x})^2 - (x^2)^2\right]dx = \pi \int_0^1 \left(x - x^4\right)dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = \pi\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{5}\right) = \frac{3}{10}\pi\text{ unit}^3$$

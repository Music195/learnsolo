Chapter 11: Application of Integration

Area Between Two Curves

To find the area of a region trapped between two curves $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$, we integrate the vertical height difference $\bigl[y_{\text{top}}(x) - y_{\text{bottom}}(x)\bigr]$ between their points of intersection.

1. Area Bounded by Two Curves

Suppose $f$ and $g$ are continuous functions on $[a, b]$ with $f(x) \ge g(x)$ (so $y = f(x)$ lies on top of $y = g(x)$) throughout $[a, b]$. Then the area of the region bounded above by $y = f(x)$, below by $y = g(x)$, and on the sides by $x = a$ and $x = b$ is: $$\text{Area} = \int_a^b \bigl[f(x) - g(x)\bigr]\,dx$$

3-Step Procedure to Find the Area Between $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$
  1. Step 1: Find the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points of $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ by solving $f(x) = g(x)$.
  2. Step 2: Divide the interval $[a, b]$ at any interior intersection points and determine which function is on top on each subinterval.
  3. Step 3: Integrate $\bigl(f(x) - g(x)\bigr)$ where $f$ is on top, and $\bigl(g(x) - f(x)\bigr)$ where $g$ is on top, then sum the results.

3. Worked Textbook Examples

Example 7 (Textbook p. 203) — Area Between a Parabola and a Line

Find the area enclosed between $y = x^2 - 2x + 2$ and $y = 2x - 1$.

Solution:

First, find the $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection by setting the two equations equal: $$x^2 - 2x + 2 = 2x - 1 \implies x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0 \implies (x - 1)(x - 3) = 0 \implies x = 1, 3$$ On $[1, 3]$, the line $y = 2x - 1$ lies above the parabola $y = x^2 - 2x + 2$. Therefore: $$\text{Area} = \int_1^3 \bigl[(2x - 1) - (x^2 - 2x + 2)\bigr]\,dx = \int_1^3 \left(-x^2 + 4x - 3\right)dx$$ $$= \left[-\frac{x^3}{3} + 2x^2 - 3x\right]_1^3 = (-9 + 18 - 9) - \left(-\frac{1}{3} + 2 - 3\right) = 0 - \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = \frac{4}{3} = 1\frac{1}{3}\text{ unit}^2$$

Example 8 (Textbook pp. 203–204) — Area Between $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$

Find the area between $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ for $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \le x \le \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

Solution:

For $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \le x \le \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, we have $\sin x \ge \cos x$. Thus: $$\text{Area} = \int_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4} (\sin x - \cos x)\,dx = \bigl[-\cos x - \sin x\bigr]_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4}$$ $$= \left(-\cos\frac{5\pi}{4} - \sin\frac{5\pi}{4}\right) - \left(-\cos\frac{\pi}{4} - \sin\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) - \left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) = 2\sqrt{2}\text{ unit}^2$$

Exercise 11.2 — Problem 5: Area Enclosed by $f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 2x$ and $g(x) = x^2$

Solution: Set $f(x) = g(x)$ to find the intersection points: $$x^3 - 2x^2 + 2x = x^2 \implies x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x = 0 \implies x(x - 1)(x - 2) = 0 \implies x = 0, 1, 2$$

  • On $[0, 1]$: $f(x) \ge g(x)$, so $\displaystyle \int_0^1 (x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^4}{4} - x^3 + x^2\right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{4}$.
  • On $[1, 2]$: $g(x) \ge f(x)$, so $\displaystyle \int_1^2 (3x^2 - x^3 - 2x)\,dx = \left[x^3 - \frac{x^4}{4} - x^2\right]_1^2 = 0 - \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{4}$.

Hence, the total enclosed area is $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}\text{ unit}^2$.

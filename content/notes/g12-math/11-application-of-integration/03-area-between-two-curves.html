Area Between Two Curves

Concept — Area Bounded by Two Curves $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$

To find the area of a plane region bounded between two curves $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ from $x = a$ to $x = b$, we slice the region into vertical strips of height $\Delta y = y_{\text{top}}(x) - y_{\text{bottom}}(x)$ and integrate:

  • When $f(x) \ge g(x)$ on $[a, b]$: The curve $y = f(x)$ lies on top of $y = g(x)$, so: $$\text{Area} = \int_a^b \bigl[f(x) - g(x)\bigr]\,dx$$
  • 3-Step Procedure When Curves Intersect (Textbook p. 204):
    1. Step 1: Find the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points of $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ by solving $f(x) = g(x)$.
    2. Step 2: Divide the interval $[a, b]$ at the intersection points and determine which function is on top over each subinterval.
    3. Step 3: Integrate $\bigl(f(x) - g(x)\bigr)$ where $f$ is on top, or $\bigl(g(x) - f(x)\bigr)$ where $g$ is on top, and add the results.
Core Formulas — Area Between Two Curves
$$\text{Area} = \int_a^b \bigl|f(x) - g(x)\bigr|\,dx = \int_a^b \bigl(y_{\text{top}} - y_{\text{bottom}}\bigr)\,dx$$
  • Why Vertical Position Relative to the $x$-Axis Does Not Matter: Even if one or both curves dip below the $x$-axis, the vertical distance $f(x) - g(x)$ is always positive whenever $f(x) > g(x)$.
Why $\int_a^b [f(x) - g(x)]\,dx$ Works Even When $g(x) < 0$

Suppose $f(x) \ge g(x)$ on $[a, b]$, and choose a positive constant $K$ large enough that both $f(x) + K \ge 0$ and $g(x) + K \ge 0$ lie entirely above the $x$-axis. Subtracting the area under the lower shifted curve from the area under the upper shifted curve gives:

$$\text{Area} = \int_a^b \bigl(f(x) + K\bigr)\,dx - \int_a^b \bigl(g(x) + K\bigr)\,dx = \int_a^b \bigl[f(x) - g(x)\bigr]\,dx$$

Because $+K$ and $-K$ cancel identically, the formula $\int_a^b \bigl[f(x) - g(x)\bigr]\,dx$ holds regardless of whether the region lies above, below, or across the $x$-axis.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 7 & 8 and Exercise 11.2

Example 7 (Textbook p. 203): Find the area between $y = x^2 - 2x + 2$ and $y = 2x - 1$.

Show step-by-step solution

To find the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points, set $x^2 - 2x + 2 = 2x - 1$:

$$x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0 \implies (x - 1)(x - 3) = 0 \implies x = 1, 3$$

On $[1, 3]$, the line $y = 2x - 1$ lies above the parabola $y = x^2 - 2x + 2$. Therefore:

$$\text{Area} = \int_1^3 \bigl[(2x - 1) - (x^2 - 2x + 2)\bigr]\,dx = \int_1^3 \left(-x^2 + 4x - 3\right)dx$$ $$= \left[-\frac{x^3}{3} + 2x^2 - 3x\right]_1^3 = 0 - \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = 1\frac{1}{3}\text{ unit}^2$$

Answer: $1\dfrac{1}{3}\text{ unit}^2$.

Example 8 (Textbook pp. 203–204): Find the area between $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ for $\dfrac{\pi}{4} \le x \le \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

Show step-by-step solution

On $\left[\dfrac{\pi}{4}, \dfrac{5\pi}{4}\right]$, $\sin x \ge \cos x$. Therefore:

$$\text{Area} = \int_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4} (\sin x - \cos x)\,dx = \bigl[-\cos x - \sin x\bigr]_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4} = \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) - \left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) = 2\sqrt{2}\text{ unit}^2$$

Answer: $2\sqrt{2}\text{ unit}^2$.

Exercise 11.2 — Problem 5 (Textbook p. 206): Find the area enclosed by the graphs of $f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 2x$ and $g(x) = x^2$.

Show step-by-step solution

Solve $f(x) = g(x) \implies x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x = x(x - 1)(x - 2) = 0 \implies x = 0, 1, 2$:

  • On $[0, 1]$, $f(x) \ge g(x)$: $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \left(x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x\right)dx = \left[\frac{x^4}{4} - x^3 + x^2\right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{4}$.
  • On $[1, 2]$, $g(x) \ge f(x)$: $\displaystyle \int_1^2 \left(3x^2 - x^3 - 2x\right)dx = \left[x^3 - \frac{x^4}{4} - x^2\right]_1^2 = 0 - \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{4}$.

Total enclosed area $= \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}\text{ unit}^2$.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{2}\text{ unit}^2$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Integrating $(f - g)$ Across an Interior Intersection Point where the Curves Cross: If $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ cross inside $(a, b)$ (as in Exercise 11.2 #5 at $x = 1$), you must split the integral at the crossing point so that $(y_{\text{top}} - y_{\text{bottom}})$ stays non-negative on each subinterval.
Key Takeaways
  • To find the area between two curves, solve $f(x) = g(x)$ for the intersection limits and integrate $\bigl(y_{\text{top}} - y_{\text{bottom}}\bigr)$ over each subinterval.

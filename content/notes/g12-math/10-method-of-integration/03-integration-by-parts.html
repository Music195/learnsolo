Chapter 10: Method of Integration

Integration by Parts

When an integrand is a product of two functions of different types—such as $x e^x$, $x \sin x$, $\ln x$, or $e^x \sin x$—integration by parts reverses the product rule of differentiation.

1. The Integration by Parts Formula

Let $u = u(x)$ and $v = v(x)$ be differentiable functions of $x$. By the product rule for differentiation: $$\frac{d}{dx}[u(x)v(x)] = u'(x)v(x) + u(x)v'(x)$$ Integrating both sides with respect to $x$ gives: $$u(x)v(x) = \int u'(x)v(x)\,dx + \int u(x)v'(x)\,dx$$ Rearranging and writing $du = u'(x)\,dx$ and $dv = v'(x)\,dx$, we obtain the integration by parts formula.

Integration by Parts Formula

$$\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$$ equivalently, $$\int u(x)v'(x)\,dx = u(x)v(x) - \int v(x)u'(x)\,dx$$

Basic Steps for Integration by Parts:

  1. Step 1: Choose $u$ (a factor that simplifies when differentiated, such as $\ln x$ or a polynomial $x^n$) and $dv$ (the remaining factor $v'(x)\,dx$ that can be readily integrated).
  2. Step 2: Differentiate $u$ to find $du = u'(x)\,dx$, and integrate $dv$ to find $v = \int v'(x)\,dx$.
  3. Step 3: Substitute $u, v, du, dv$ into $\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$.
  4. Step 4: Evaluate the remaining integral $\int v\,du$ and simplify.

3. Worked Textbook Examples (Example 8, pp. 185–187)

Example 8(a) & 8(b) — $\int x e^x\,dx$ and $\int \ln x\,dx$

(a) Evaluate $\displaystyle \int x e^x\,dx$:
Let $u = x$ and $dv = e^x\,dx$. Then $du = dx$ and $v = \int e^x\,dx = e^x$. $$\int x e^x\,dx = x e^x - \int e^x\,dx = x e^x - e^x + C = (x - 1)e^x + C$$

(b) Evaluate $\displaystyle \int \ln x\,dx$:
Let $u = \ln x$ and $dv = 1\,dx$. Then $du = \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$ and $v = \int 1\,dx = x$. $$\int \ln x\,dx = (\ln x)(x) - \int x \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = x\ln x - \int 1\,dx = x\ln x - x + C$$

Example 8(c) & 8(d) — $\int x\cos x\,dx$ and $\int x\sin x\,dx$

(c) Evaluate $\displaystyle \int x\cos x\,dx$:
Let $u = x$ and $dv = \cos x\,dx$. Then $du = dx$ and $v = \sin x$. $$\int x\cos x\,dx = x\sin x - \int \sin x\,dx = x\sin x + \cos x + C$$

(d) Evaluate $\displaystyle \int x\sin x\,dx$:
Let $u = x$ and $dv = \sin x\,dx$. Then $du = dx$ and $v = -\cos x$. $$\int x\sin x\,dx = -x\cos x - \int (-\cos x)\,dx = -x\cos x + \sin x + C$$

Example 8(e) & 8(f) — Cyclic Integration by Parts & $\int x^2 \ln x\,dx$

(e) Evaluate $\displaystyle I = \int e^x \sin x\,dx$:
First integration by parts: let $u = e^x$ and $dv = \sin x\,dx \implies du = e^x\,dx$ and $v = -\cos x$: $$I = -e^x\cos x - \int (-\cos x)e^x\,dx = -e^x\cos x + \int e^x\cos x\,dx \qquad (1)$$ Second integration by parts on $\int e^x\cos x\,dx$: let $u = e^x$ and $dv = \cos x\,dx \implies du = e^x\,dx$ and $v = \sin x$: $$\int e^x\cos x\,dx = e^x\sin x - \int e^x\sin x\,dx = e^x\sin x - I \qquad (2)$$ Substituting $(2)$ into $(1)$ gives: $$I = -e^x\cos x + e^x\sin x - I \implies 2I = e^x(\sin x - \cos x) + C' \implies I = \frac{1}{2}e^x(\sin x - \cos x) + C$$

(f) Evaluate $\displaystyle \int x^2 \ln x\,dx$:
Let $u = \ln x$ and $dv = x^2\,dx$. Then $du = \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx$ and $v = \dfrac{x^3}{3}$: $$\int x^2 \ln x\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3}\ln x - \int \frac{x^3}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3}\ln x - \frac{1}{3}\int x^2\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3}\ln x - \frac{x^3}{9} + C$$

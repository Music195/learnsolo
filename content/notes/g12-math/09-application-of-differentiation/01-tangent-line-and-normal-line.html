Tangent Line and Normal Line

Concept — Inclination, Slope, Tangent Line & Normal Line

Differentiation provides the exact instantaneous slope of a curve $y = f(x)$ at any point $P(x_1, y_1)$ where $f$ is differentiable:

  • Inclination of a Line ($\theta$): The smallest counter-clockwise angle $\theta$ ($0^\circ \le \theta < 180^\circ$, or $0 \le \theta < \pi$) measured from the positive direction of the $x$-axis to the line $L$. The inclination of a horizontal line is $0^\circ$.
  • Slope (Gradient) of a Line ($m$): The tangent of its angle of inclination, $m = \tan\theta$ ($\theta \neq 90^\circ$). For a line passing through $P(x_1, y_1)$ and $Q(x_2, y_2)$ with $x_1 \neq x_2$: $$m = \tan\theta = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
  • Tangent Line (ဝန်းထိမျဉ်း) at $P(x_1, y_1)$: The line passing through $P(x_1, y_1)$ with slope equal to the derivative $m_T = y'(x_1) = f'(x_1)$.
  • Normal Line (ထောင့်မှန်မျဉ်း) at $P(x_1, y_1)$: The line passing through $P(x_1, y_1)$ perpendicular to the tangent line at $P$, with slope $m_N = -\dfrac{1}{y'(x_1)}$ (provided $y'(x_1) \neq 0$).
Core Formulas — Equations of the Tangent and Normal Lines at $(x_1, y_1)$
$$\text{Tangent Line: } y - y_1 = y'(x_1)(x - x_1) \qquad\qquad \text{Normal Line: } y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{y'(x_1)}(x - x_1)$$
  • Parallel Lines: Two non-vertical lines are parallel if and only if $m_1 = m_2$.
  • Perpendicular Lines: Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if $m_1 m_2 = -1$, so $m_N = -\dfrac{1}{m_T}$.
  • Horizontal Tangent ($y'(x_1) = 0$): The tangent line is horizontal ($y = y_1$) and the normal line is vertical ($x = x_1$).
  • Vertical Tangent ($\lim_{x \to x_1} |y'(x)| = \infty$): The tangent line is vertical ($x = x_1$) and the normal line is horizontal ($y = y_1$).
Why the Tangent Slope Equals $y'(x_1)$

Let $P(x_1, y_1)$ be a fixed point on $y = f(x)$ and let $Q(x_2, y_2)$ be a nearby point on the curve. The secant line $PQ$ has slope:

$$m_{PQ} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1}$$

As $Q$ approaches $P$ along the curve ($x_2 \to x_1$), the secant line $PQ$ rotates into its limiting position — the tangent line at $P$ — and its slope approaches the derivative:

$$m_T = \lim_{x_2 \to x_1} \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1} = f'(x_1) = y'(x_1)$$

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 1, 2 & 3

Example 1 (Textbook p. 146): Find the equations of the tangent line and normal line to the curve $y = \ln x$ at the point $\left(\dfrac{1}{e}, -1\right)$.

Show step-by-step solution

Differentiating $y = \ln x$ with respect to $x$ gives $y' = \dfrac{1}{x}$. At $x = \dfrac{1}{e}$, the slope of the tangent line is:

$$y'\left(\frac{1}{e}\right) = \frac{1}{1/e} = e$$

Equation of the tangent line at $\left(\frac{1}{e}, -1\right)$:

$$y - (-1) = e\left(x - \frac{1}{e}\right) \implies y + 1 = ex - 1 \implies ex - y = 2$$

Since the normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line, its slope is $m_N = -\dfrac{1}{e}$. Equation of the normal line:

$$y - (-1) = -\frac{1}{e}\left(x - \frac{1}{e}\right) \implies y + 1 = -\frac{1}{e}x + \frac{1}{e^2} \implies x + ey = \frac{1}{e} - e$$

Answer: Tangent line: $ex - y = 2$; Normal line: $x + ey = \frac{1}{e} - e$.

Example 2 (Textbook p. 146): Find the equations of the tangent line and normal line to the curve $y = \sin x$ at the origin $(0, 0)$.

Show step-by-step solution

Differentiating $y = \sin x$ gives $y' = \cos x$. At $(0, 0)$, the tangent slope is $y'(0) = \cos 0 = 1$, and the normal slope is $-1$:

  • Tangent line: $y - 0 = 1(x - 0) \implies x - y = 0$ (or $y = x$).
  • Normal line: $y - 0 = -1(x - 0) \implies x + y = 0$ (or $y = -x$).

Answer: Tangent line: $x - y = 0$; Normal line: $x + y = 0$.

Example 3 (Textbook p. 147): Consider the function $f(x) = x^2 + ax + b$. If the tangent line to the curve at $x = 2$ is $y = 2x + 1$, find the values of $a$ and $b$.

Show step-by-step solution

We have $f'(x) = 2x + a$. Since the tangent line at $x = 2$ is $y = 2x + 1$, its slope is $m = 2$:

$$f'(2) = 2 \implies 2(2) + a = 2 \implies a = -2$$

At $x = 2$, the point of tangency lies on $y = 2x + 1$, so $y = 2(2) + 1 = 5$. Since $(2, 5)$ also lies on $f(x) = x^2 - 2x + b$:

$$f(2) = 5 \implies 2^2 - 2(2) + b = 5 \implies b = 5$$

Answer: $a = -2$ and $b = 5$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Using $f'(x)$ Instead of the Constant Number $f'(x_1)$: In the tangent equation $y - y_1 = y'(x_1)(x - x_1)$, you must evaluate $y'(x_1)$ at the specific number $x = x_1$ so the equation is linear in $x$ and $y$.
  • Forgetting the Negative Sign in the Normal Slope: The normal line slope is the negative reciprocal $-\dfrac{1}{y'(x_1)}$, not $+\dfrac{1}{y'(x_1)}$.
Key Takeaways
  • The slope of a line with inclination $\theta$ is $m = \tan\theta = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
  • At $P(x_1, y_1)$ on $y = f(x)$, the tangent line is $y - y_1 = y'(x_1)(x - x_1)$ and the normal line is $y - y_1 = -\dfrac{1}{y'(x_1)}(x - x_1)$.
  • Horizontal tangents occur where $y'(x_1) = 0$; there the tangent line is $y = y_1$ and the normal line is $x = x_1$.

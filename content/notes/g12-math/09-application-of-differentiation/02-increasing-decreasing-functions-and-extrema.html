Increasing, Decreasing Functions, Critical Points and Extrema

Concept — Monotonicity, Critical Points & Local vs. Global Extrema

The first derivative $f'(x)$ tells us whether a function is rising or falling on an interval and identifies the candidate points where maximum and minimum values occur:

  • Increasing on an Interval $I$: $x_1 < x_2 \implies f(x_1) < f(x_2)$ for all $x_1, x_2 \in I$. If $f'(x) > 0$ on $(a, b)$, then $f$ is increasing on $[a, b]$.
  • Decreasing on an Interval $I$: $x_1 < x_2 \implies f(x_1) > f(x_2)$ for all $x_1, x_2 \in I$. If $f'(x) < 0$ on $(a, b)$, then $f$ is decreasing on $[a, b]$.
  • Critical Point (အခြေပြောင်းမှတ်): A point $(c, f(c))$ on the graph of $y = f(x)$ where $c$ belongs to the domain of $f$ and either $f'(c) = 0$ (a stationary point) or $f'(c)$ does not exist. The number $c$ is called a critical number.
  • Local vs. Global Extrema:
    • $f(c)$ is a local maximum (or local minimum) if $f(c) \ge f(x)$ (or $f(c) \le f(x)$) for all $x$ in some open interval containing $c$.
    • $f(c)$ is the global (absolute) maximum (or global minimum) on a domain $D$ if $f(c) \ge f(x)$ (or $f(c) \le f(x)$) for all $x \in D$.
Core Formulas — First Derivative Test & Closed Interval Method
  • First Derivative Test for Local Extrema (at a critical number $c$ of continuous $f$):
    • If $f'(x)$ changes sign from $+$ to $-$ as $x$ increases through $c$, then $f(c)$ is a local maximum value.
    • If $f'(x)$ changes sign from $-$ to $+$ as $x$ increases through $c$, then $f(c)$ is a local minimum value.
    • If $f'(x)$ does not change sign at $c$, then $f$ has no local extremum at $c$.
  • Closed Interval Method for Global Extrema of Continuous $f$ on $[a, b]$:
    1. Evaluate $f(c)$ at all critical numbers $c$ in the open interval $(a, b)$.
    2. Evaluate $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ at the endpoints.
    3. The largest of these values is the global maximum; the smallest is the global minimum.
Why Domain Membership Matters for Critical Points (Examples 6 & 7)

Compare $f(x) = (x - 1)^{1/3}$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$:

  • For $f(x) = (x - 1)^{1/3}$, the domain is $\mathbb{R}$ and $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{3(x - 1)^{2/3}}$ does not exist at $x = 1$. Since $1 \in \text{dom}(f)$ and $f(1) = 0$, the point $(1, 0)$ is a critical point (vertical tangent), even though $f'(x) > 0$ for all $x \neq 1$ so $f$ has no local extremum.
  • For $g(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$, $g'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ does not exist at $x = 0$, but $0 \notin \text{dom}(g)$. Therefore, $g(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ has no critical point.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 5, 8, 9 & 10

Example 5 & Example 8 (Textbook pp. 149, 154): Find the critical points, intervals of increase/decrease, and local extrema of:
(a) $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 1$   (b) $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 5$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 1$: $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x + 1)(x - 1) = 0 \implies x = -1, 1$.

  • Critical points: $(-1, 3)$ and $(1, -1)$.
  • $f'(x) > 0$ on $(-\infty, -1)$ and $(1, \infty)$ ($f$ increasing); $f'(x) < 0$ on $(-1, 1)$ ($f$ decreasing).
  • Local maximum value is $f(-1) = 3$; local minimum value is $f(1) = -1$.

(b) $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 5$: $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x = 3x(x - 2) = 0 \implies x = 0, 2$.

  • At $x = 0$, $f'(x)$ changes from $+$ to $-$, giving local maximum value $f(0) = 5$.
  • At $x = 2$, $f'(x)$ changes from $-$ to $+$, giving local minimum value $f(2) = 1$.

Answer: (a) Local max $3$ at $x = -1$, local min $-1$ at $x = 1$; (b) Local max $5$ at $x = 0$, local min $1$ at $x = 2$.

Example 9 (Textbook p. 155): Find the local extrema of $f(x) = (x^2 - 9)^{2/3}$.

Show step-by-step solution

Differentiating gives:

$$f'(x) = \frac{2}{3}(x^2 - 9)^{-1/3}(2x) = \frac{4x}{3(x^2 - 9)^{1/3}}$$

Here $f'(x) = 0$ at $x = 0$, and $f'(x)$ does not exist at $x = -3$ and $x = 3$. Testing signs on $(-\infty, -3)$, $(-3, 0)$, $(0, 3)$, and $(3, \infty)$ gives $-, +, -, +$:

  • Local minimum value $0$ at $x = -3$ and at $x = 3$.
  • Local maximum value $f(0) = (-9)^{2/3} = 3\sqrt[3]{9}$ at $x = 0$.

Answer: Local minima $f(-3) = 0$ and $f(3) = 0$; local maximum $f(0) = 3\sqrt[3]{9}$.

Example 10 (Textbook pp. 155–156): Find the global maximum and minimum values of:
(a) $f(x) = x^2 + 5x - 3$ on $[-4, 5]$   (b) $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}$ on $[-3, 3]$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) $f(x) = x^2 + 5x - 3$ on $[-4, 5]$: $f'(x) = 2x + 5 = 0 \implies x = -\dfrac{5}{2} \in (-4, 5)$. Comparing values:

$$f\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right) = -\frac{37}{4} = -9\frac{1}{4}, \qquad f(-4) = -7, \qquad f(5) = 47$$

(b) $f(x) = \dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1}$ on $[-3, 3]$: $f'(x) = \dfrac{1 - x^2}{(x^2 + 1)^2} = 0 \implies x = -1, 1 \in (-3, 3)$. Comparing values:

$$f(-1) = -\frac{1}{2}, \qquad f(1) = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad f(-3) = -\frac{3}{10}, \qquad f(3) = \frac{3}{10}$$

Answer: (a) Global max $47$ at $x = 5$, global min $-9\frac{1}{4}$ at $x = -\frac{5}{2}$; (b) Global max $\frac{1}{2}$ at $x = 1$, global min $-\frac{1}{2}$ at $x = -1$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Forgetting Points Where $f'(c)$ Does Not Exist: Critical numbers include both solutions of $f'(c) = 0$ and points in $\text{dom}(f)$ where $f'(c)$ is undefined (such as $x = \pm 3$ in Example 9).
  • Forgetting Endpoints in Global Extrema Problems: On a closed interval $[a, b]$, always compare $f(c)$ at interior critical numbers with the endpoint values $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.
Key Takeaways
  • $f'(x) > 0 \implies f$ is increasing; $f'(x) < 0 \implies f$ is decreasing.
  • A critical point $(c, f(c))$ requires $c \in \text{dom}(f)$ and either $f'(c) = 0$ or $f'(c)$ undefined.
  • By the First Derivative Test, $f'$ changing from $+$ to $-$ gives a local maximum, while $-$ to $+$ gives a local minimum.

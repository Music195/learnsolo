Concavity, Points of Inflection and Optimization

Concept — Concavity, Points of Inflection & The Second Derivative

While the first derivative $f'(x)$ measures the slope of $y = f(x)$, the second derivative $f''(x)$ measures how the slope itself is changing — determining how the curve bends:

  • Concave Up (အခွက်အပေါ်လှည့်): The graph of $f$ is concave up on an open interval $I$ if $f'$ is increasing on $I$ (the curve lies above its tangent lines). If $f''(x) > 0$ for all $x \in I$, then $f$ is concave up on $I$.
  • Concave Down (အခွက်အောက်လှည့်): The graph of $f$ is concave down on an open interval $I$ if $f'$ is decreasing on $I$ (the curve lies below its tangent lines). If $f''(x) < 0$ for all $x \in I$, then $f$ is concave down on $I$.
  • Point of Inflection (အခွက်ပြောင်းမှတ်): A point $P(c, f(c))$ on a continuous curve $y = f(x)$ where the concavity changes from concave up to concave down, or from concave down to concave up.
Core Formulas — Concavity Test, Inflection Points & Second Derivative Test
  • Concavity Criterion: $$f''(x) > 0 \text{ on } I \implies \text{Concave Up on } I \qquad\qquad f''(x) < 0 \text{ on } I \implies \text{Concave Down on } I$$
  • Point of Inflection Candidate Condition: If $(c, f(c))$ is a point of inflection of $f$, then either $f''(c) = 0$ or $f''(c)$ does not exist, and $f''(x)$ must change sign across $x = c$.
  • Second Derivative Test for Local Extrema (when $f'(c) = 0$):
    • If $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) < 0$, then $f$ has a local maximum at $x = c$.
    • If $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) > 0$, then $f$ has a local minimum at $x = c$.
    • If $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) = 0$, the test fails (is inconclusive; use the First Derivative Test).
Why $f''(c) = 0$ Alone Does Not Guarantee an Inflection Point (Example 13)

Consider $f(x) = x^6$. Differentiating twice gives $f'(x) = 6x^5$ and $f''(x) = 30x^4$.

At $x = 0$, we have $f''(0) = 0$. However, for every $x \neq 0$, $x^4 > 0 \implies f''(x) = 30x^4 > 0$ on both $(-\infty, 0)$ and $(0, \infty)$. Since the graph is concave up on both sides of $x = 0$ and concavity does not change, $(0, 0)$ is not a point of inflection (it is a local and global minimum).

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 11, 14, 15 & 16

Example 11 (Textbook p. 158): Determine the intervals on which the graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + 1}$ is concave up or concave down, and find its points of inflection.

Show step-by-step solution

Differentiating twice gives:

$$f'(x) = -\frac{2x}{(x^2 + 1)^2}, \qquad f''(x) = \frac{2(3x^2 - 1)}{(x^2 + 1)^3}$$

Setting $f''(x) = 0$ gives $3x^2 - 1 = 0 \implies x = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ or $x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$:

  • On $\left(-\infty, -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \infty\right)$: $f''(x) > 0 \implies$ concave up.
  • On $\left(-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$: $f''(x) < 0 \implies$ concave down.
  • Points of inflection: $\left(-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \dfrac{3}{4}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \dfrac{3}{4}\right)$.

Answer: Concave up on $(-\infty, -1/\sqrt{3}) \cup (1/\sqrt{3}, \infty)$, concave down on $(-1/\sqrt{3}, 1/\sqrt{3})$, inflection points $(\pm 1/\sqrt{3}, 3/4)$.

Example 14 (Textbook p. 160): Find the critical points, inflection points, and local extrema of $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3 + 5$.

Show step-by-step solution

We compute $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2(x - 3)$ and $f''(x) = 12x^2 - 24x = 12x(x - 2)$:

  • Critical points: $f'(x) = 0 \implies x = 0, 3$, giving $(0, 5)$ and $(3, -22)$.
  • Second Derivative Test: At $x = 3$, $f''(3) = 36 > 0 \implies f(3) = -22$ is a local minimum value. At $x = 0$, $f''(0) = 0$ (test fails), and since $f'(x) < 0$ on both sides of $0$, there is no local extremum at $x = 0$.
  • Points of inflection: $f''(x) = 0 \implies x = 0, 2$. Since $f''(x)$ changes sign at both $x = 0$ and $x = 2$, $(0, 5)$ and $(2, -11)$ are points of inflection.

Answer: Critical points $(0,5), (3,-22)$; local minimum $-22$ at $x = 3$; inflection points $(0,5)$ and $(2,-11)$.

Example 15 & Example 16 (Textbook pp. 161–162):
(a) Find the range of $f(x) = \ln x - x$.
(b) An open cylindrical vessel is to hold $400\pi\text{ cm}^3$ of water. Find the radius $r$ and minimum surface area $A$ of sheet metal required.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Range of $f(x) = \ln x - x$ ($x > 0$): $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x} - 1 = 0 \implies x = 1$. Since $f''(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x^2} < 0$ for all $x > 0$, $f(1) = \ln 1 - 1 = -1$ is the global maximum on $(0, \infty)$. Thus the range is $\{y \in \mathbb{R} \mid y \le -1\}$.

(b) Open Cylindrical Vessel: From $V = \pi r^2 h = 400\pi$, we get $h = \dfrac{400}{r^2}$. The surface area (circular base + curved side) is:

$$A(r) = \pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = \pi r^2 + \frac{800\pi}{r} \implies A'(r) = 2\pi r - \frac{800\pi}{r^2} = 0 \implies r^3 = 400 \implies r = \sqrt[3]{400}\text{ cm}$$

Since $A''(r) = 2\pi + \dfrac{1600\pi}{r^3} > 0$ for all $r > 0$, this gives the absolute minimum area $A_{\min} = 3\pi(400)^{2/3}\text{ cm}^2$.

Answer: (a) Range $y \le -1$; (b) $r = \sqrt[3]{400}\text{ cm}$, $A_{\min} = 3\pi(400)^{2/3}\text{ cm}^2$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Concluding $(c, f(c))$ is an Inflection Point Without Checking a Sign Change of $f''(x)$: Always verify that $f''(x)$ changes sign across $x = c$ (remember $f(x) = x^6$ at $x = 0$).
  • Misinterpreting $f''(c) = 0$ in the Second Derivative Test: When $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) = 0$, the Second Derivative Test gives no information — switch to the First Derivative Test.
Key Takeaways
  • $f''(x) > 0 \implies$ concave up ($f'$ increasing); $f''(x) < 0 \implies$ concave down ($f'$ decreasing).
  • A point of inflection occurs where $f$ is continuous and concavity reverses sign.
  • At a stationary point $f'(c) = 0$, $f''(c) > 0$ implies a local minimum and $f''(c) < 0$ implies a local maximum.

Translation of Axes and Parabolas with Vertex (h, k)

Concept — Translation of Axes & Shifted Parabolas

When the coordinate axes are shifted (translated) without rotation so that the new origin $O'$ is placed at $(h, k)$ in the original $xy$-plane, a new $x'y'$-coordinate system is formed. Every point $P$ has both original coordinates $(x, y)$ and translated coordinates $(x', y')$, related by the translation equations (ဝင်ရိုးရွှေ့ပြောင်းခြင်း ညီမျှခြင်းများ):

$$x = x' + h, \quad y = y' + k \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad x' = x - h, \quad y' = y - k$$

Shifting a standard parabola so that its vertex moves from $(0, 0)$ to $V(h, k)$ simply replaces $x'$ by $(x - h)$ and $y'$ by $(y - k)$ in the standard equations.

Core Formulas — Standard Equations of a Parabola with Vertex $V(h, k)$ ($p > 0$)
  • 1. Axis Parallel to the $x$-Axis (Horizontal Axis $y = k$):
    • Opens Right: $(y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h)$ — Focus $F(h + p, k)$, Directrix $x = h - p$, Latus Rectum Endpoints $(h + p, k \pm 2p)$.
    • Opens Left: $(y - k)^2 = -4p(x - h)$ — Focus $F(h - p, k)$, Directrix $x = h + p$, Latus Rectum Endpoints $(h - p, k \pm 2p)$.
  • 2. Axis Parallel to the $y$-Axis (Vertical Axis $x = h$):
    • Opens Up: $(x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)$ — Focus $F(h, k + p)$, Directrix $y = k - p$, Latus Rectum Endpoints $(h \pm 2p, k + p)$.
    • Opens Down: $(x - h)^2 = -4p(y - k)$ — Focus $F(h, k - p)$, Directrix $y = k + p$, Latus Rectum Endpoints $(h \pm 2p, k - p)$.
  • General Forms of a Parabola ($B = 0$, and either $C = 0$ or $A = 0$): $$Ax^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 \quad (\text{vertical axis}) \qquad \text{or} \qquad Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 \quad (\text{horizontal axis})$$
Derivation — From $x'y'$-Coordinates to $(x - h, y - k)$ Form

Suppose a parabola has vertex $V(h, k)$ and axis of symmetry parallel to the $y$-axis. Translate the coordinate axes so that the new origin $O'$ sits at $V(h, k)$. Relative to the $x'y'$-axes, the vertex is at $(0, 0)$ and the parabola has the simple equation:

$$(x')^2 = \pm 4py'$$

Substituting the translation equations $x' = x - h$ and $y' = y - k$ immediately yields the $xy$-equation:

$$(x - h)^2 = \pm 4p(y - k)$$

Moreover, since every geometric feature shifts by $+h$ horizontally and $+k$ vertically, the focus $(0, \pm p)$ in $x'y'$-coordinates becomes $(h, k \pm p)$ in $xy$-coordinates, and the directrix $y' = \mp p$ becomes $y - k = \mp p \implies y = k \mp p$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 8, 9, 10 & 11

Example 8 (Completing the Square on a Horizontal-Axis Parabola): Write the parabola equation $y^2 - 12x - 6y - 3 = 0$ in standard form and find its vertex, focus, directrix, and endpoints of the latus rectum.

Show step-by-step solution

Isolate the $y$-terms on the left and complete the square by adding $3^2 = 9$ to both sides:

$$y^2 - 6y = 12x + 3 \implies y^2 - 6y + 3^2 = 12x + 3 + 9 \implies (y - 3)^2 = 12(x + 1)$$

Comparing with $(y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h)$ gives $h = -1, k = 3,$ and $4p = 12 \implies p = 3$ (opens to the right):

  • Vertex: $V(h, k) = (-1, 3)$
  • Focus: $F(h + p, k) = (-1 + 3, 3) = (2, 3)$
  • Directrix: $x = h - p = -1 - 3 \implies x = -4$
  • Endpoints of Latus Rectum: $(h + p, k \pm 2p) = (2, 3 \pm 6) \implies (2, 9)$ and $(2, -3)$.

Answer: $(y - 3)^2 = 12(x + 1)$; $V(-1, 3)$, $F(2, 3)$, directrix $x = -4$, latus rectum endpoints $(2, 9)$ and $(2, -3)$.

Example 9 (Completing the Square on a Vertical-Axis Parabola): Write $x^2 - 2x + 8y - 23 = 0$ in standard form and find its vertex, focus, directrix, and endpoints of the latus rectum.

Show step-by-step solution

Isolate the $x$-terms on the left and complete the square by adding $1^2 = 1$ to both sides:

$$x^2 - 2x = -8y + 23 \implies x^2 - 2x + 1 = -8y + 24 \implies (x - 1)^2 = -8(y - 3)$$

Comparing with $(x - h)^2 = -4p(y - k)$ gives $h = 1, k = 3,$ and $4p = 8 \implies p = 2$ (opens downward):

  • Vertex: $V(1, 3)$
  • Focus: $F(h, k - p) = (1, 3 - 2) = (1, 1)$
  • Directrix: $y = k + p = 3 + 2 \implies y = 5$
  • Endpoints of Latus Rectum: $(h \pm 2p, k - p) = (1 \pm 4, 1) \implies (-3, 1)$ and $(5, 1)$.

Answer: $(x - 1)^2 = -8(y - 3)$; $V(1, 3)$, $F(1, 1)$, directrix $y = 5$, latus rectum endpoints $(-3, 1)$ and $(5, 1)$.

Example 10 & 11 (Finding Parabola Equations from Vertex and Focus):
(a) Find the standard form of the parabola with vertex $(1, 2)$ and focus $(4, 2)$.
(b) Find the general form of the parabola with vertex $(2, 1)$ and focus $(2, 4)$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Since $V(1, 2)$ and $F(4, 2)$ share the same $y$-coordinate ($y = 2$) and the focus lies to the right of the vertex, the parabola opens to the right with $h = 1, k = 2$, and $p = 4 - 1 = 3$:

$$(y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h) \implies (y - 2)^2 = 4(3)(x - 1) \implies (y - 2)^2 = 12(x - 1)$$

(b) Since $V(2, 1)$ and $F(2, 4)$ share the same $x$-coordinate ($x = 2$) and the focus lies above the vertex, the parabola opens upward with $h = 2, k = 1$, and $p = 4 - 1 = 3$:

$$(x - 2)^2 = 4(3)(y - 1) \implies x^2 - 4x + 4 = 12y - 12 \implies x^2 - 4x - 12y + 16 = 0$$

Answer: (a) $(y - 2)^2 = 12(x - 1)$; (b) $x^2 - 4x - 12y + 16 = 0$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Factoring Out the Negative Coefficient Completely: In Example 9, when factoring $-8y + 24$, remember that factoring out $-8$ reverses the sign of $+24$: $-8y + 24 = -8(y - 3)$, NOT $-8(y + 3)$!
  • Given Focus and Directrix Only (Exercise 6.3 #11, 12): When given the focus $F$ and directrix (without the vertex), remember that the vertex $V(h, k)$ is the midpoint between the focus and the directrix, and $2p$ is the total distance from focus to directrix!
Exam Tip — Instant Sanity Check on Focus vs. Directrix

A parabola always wraps around its focus and turns its back on its directrix. After computing $V$, $F$, and the directrix, check that $V$ is the exact midpoint between $F$ and the directrix line!

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Translation Equations (ဝင်ရိုးရွှေ့ပြောင်းခြင်း): $x' = x - h$ and $y' = y - k$.
  • Horizontal Parabola at $V(h, k)$: $(y - k)^2 = \pm 4p(x - h)$, Focus $(h \pm p, k)$, Directrix $x = h \mp p$.
  • Vertical Parabola at $V(h, k)$: $(x - h)^2 = \pm 4p(y - k)$, Focus $(h, k \pm p)$, Directrix $y = k \mp p$.

Parabolas with Vertex at the Origin

Concept — Definition & Anatomy of a Parabola

A parabola (ပါရာဘိုလာ) is the locus of all points $P(x, y)$ in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed line called the directrix (လမ်းညွှန်မျဉ်း) and a fixed point not on the line called the focus $F$ (ဆုံချက်): $PF = PD$.

  • Axis of Symmetry (အချိုးညီဝင်ရိုး): The line passing through the focus $F$ and perpendicular to the directrix.
  • Vertex $V$ (ထိပ်မှတ်): The point where the parabola intersects its axis of symmetry, located midway between the focus and the directrix. If the distance from $V$ to $F$ is $p > 0$, then the distance from $F$ to the directrix is $2p$.
  • Latus Rectum (ဆုံချက်ဖြတ်မျဉ်း): The chord through the focus $F$ parallel to the directrix with endpoints on the parabola. Its total length is $4p$ ($2p$ on each side of the axis of symmetry).
Core Formulas — The Four Standard Positions with Vertex at $V(0, 0)$ ($p > 0$)
  • 1. Opens to the Right ($y^2 = 4px$): Vertex $V(0, 0)$, Focus $F(p, 0)$, Directrix $x = -p$, Axis $y = 0$, Endpoints of Latus Rectum $(p, -2p)$ and $(p, 2p)$.
  • 2. Opens to the Left ($y^2 = -4px$): Vertex $V(0, 0)$, Focus $F(-p, 0)$, Directrix $x = p$, Axis $y = 0$, Endpoints of Latus Rectum $(-p, -2p)$ and $(-p, 2p)$.
  • 3. Opens Upward ($x^2 = 4py$): Vertex $V(0, 0)$, Focus $F(0, p)$, Directrix $y = -p$, Axis $x = 0$, Endpoints of Latus Rectum $(-2p, p)$ and $(2p, p)$.
  • 4. Opens Downward ($x^2 = -4py$): Vertex $V(0, 0)$, Focus $F(0, -p)$, Directrix $y = p$, Axis $x = 0$, Endpoints of Latus Rectum $(-2p, -p)$ and $(2p, -p)$.
Derivation — Deriving $y^2 = 4px$ from $PF = PD$

Place the vertex at the origin $V(0, 0)$, the focus at $F(p, 0)$ ($p > 0$), and the vertical directrix along the line $x = -p$. For any point $P(x, y)$ on the parabola, the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to the directrix is $D(-p, y)$.

By the geometric definition $PF = PD$, apply the distance formula:

$$\sqrt{(p - x)^2 + (0 - y)^2} = \sqrt{(-p - x)^2 + (y - y)^2}$$

Squaring both sides and expanding $(p - x)^2$ and $(-p - x)^2 = (p + x)^2$:

$$p^2 - 2px + x^2 + y^2 = p^2 + 2px + x^2 \implies y^2 = 4px \quad \blacksquare$$

Also, setting $x = p$ (along the line through the focus parallel to the directrix) gives $y^2 = 4p^2 \implies y = \pm 2p$, confirming that the endpoints of the latus rectum are $(p, \pm 2p)$ and its length is $4p$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 6 & 7

Example 6 (Finding Focus, Directrix & Latus Rectum): Sketch the graphs of the parabolas, showing the vertex, focus, directrix, and endpoints of the latus rectum:
(a) $x^2 = 16y$   (b) $y^2 + 8x = 0$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) $x^2 = 16y$: Since the equation has an $x^2$ term and a positive coefficient of $y$, the axis of symmetry is along the $y$-axis and the parabola opens upward. Comparing with $x^2 = 4py$ gives $4p = 16 \implies p = 4$:

  • Vertex: $V = (0, 0)$
  • Focus: $F = (0, p) = (0, 4)$
  • Directrix: $y = -p \implies y = -4$
  • Endpoints of Latus Rectum: $(\pm 2p, p) = (-8, 4)$ and $(8, 4)$.

(b) $y^2 + 8x = 0 \implies y^2 = -8x$: Since the equation has a $y^2$ term and a negative coefficient of $x$, the axis of symmetry is along the $x$-axis and the parabola opens to the left. Comparing with $y^2 = -4px$ gives $4p = 8 \implies p = 2$:

  • Vertex: $V = (0, 0)$
  • Focus: $F = (-p, 0) = (-2, 0)$
  • Directrix: $x = p \implies x = 2$
  • Endpoints of Latus Rectum: $(-p, \pm 2p) = (-2, -4)$ and $(-2, 4)$.

Answer: (a) $V(0,0), F(0,4), y=-4, (\pm 8, 4)$; (b) $V(0,0), F(-2,0), x=2, (-2, \pm 4)$.

Example 7 (Equation of a Parabola Passing Through a Given Point): Find an equation of the parabola that is symmetric about the $y$-axis with its vertex at the origin, and passes through $(5, 2)$.

Show step-by-step solution

Since the parabola has vertex $V(0, 0)$, is symmetric about the $y$-axis, and passes through $(5, 2)$ in the first quadrant ($y = 2 > 0$), it opens upward with equation $x^2 = 4py$. Substituting $(x, y) = (5, 2)$:

$$5^2 = 4p(2) \implies 25 = 8p \implies 4p = \frac{25}{2}$$

Substituting $4p = \frac{25}{2}$ back into $x^2 = 4py$ gives:

$$x^2 = \frac{25}{2}y$$

Answer: $x^2 = \dfrac{25}{2}y$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Keep $p > 0$ Always: In the Myanmar Grade 12 textbook convention, $p$ is a positive distance ($p > 0$), and the direction of opening is shown explicitly by the $+$ or $-$ sign in $y^2 = \pm 4px$ and $x^2 = \pm 4py$. Thus for $y^2 = -8x$, write $4p = 8 \implies p = 2$ (not $p = -2$).
  • Isolating the Squared Term First: Given $y = \frac{1}{4}x^2$ (Exercise 6.2 #5), rewrite it in standard form $x^2 = 4y$ before identifying $4p = 4 \implies p = 1$!
Exam Tip — The Textbook 4-Step Box Method for Sketching Parabolas

To sketch any parabola accurately on exam graph paper: (1) plot the vertex $V(0,0)$ and focus $F$ at distance $p$ along the axis; (2) go $2p$ units perpendicular to the axis on both sides of $F$ to mark the two latus rectum endpoints; (3) draw the smooth curve through $V$ and those two endpoints; (4) draw the dashed directrix at distance $p$ behind the vertex.

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Parabola Locus Definition (ပါရာဘိုလာ အဓိပ္ပာယ်ဖွင့်ဆိုချက်): Distance to focus equals perpendicular distance to directrix ($PF = PD$).
  • Horizontal Axis ($y = 0$): $y^2 = \pm 4px$, Focus $(\pm p, 0)$, Directrix $x = \mp p$, Latus rectum endpoints $(\pm p, \pm 2p)$.
  • Vertical Axis ($x = 0$): $x^2 = \pm 4py$, Focus $(0, \pm p)$, Directrix $y = \mp p$, Latus rectum endpoints $(\pm 2p, \pm p)$.

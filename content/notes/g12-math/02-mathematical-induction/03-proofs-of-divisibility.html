Proofs of Divisibility

Proving Multiples and Factors for Every Exponent $n$

Beyond summation series, mathematical induction is widely used in number theory to prove that an algebraic expression is always divisible by a fixed integer for every power $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

For example, is $3^n - 1$ always an even number (a multiple of $2$)?

  • $n = 1: \quad 3^1 - 1 = 2 = 2 \times 1$
  • $n = 2: \quad 3^2 - 1 = 8 = 2 \times 4$
  • $n = 3: \quad 3^3 - 1 = 26 = 2 \times 13$
  • $n = 4: \quad 3^4 - 1 = 80 = 2 \times 40$

The Key Algebraic Trick: In the inductive step, we rewrite the $(k+1)$-th expression to split off the known term from our hypothesis: $$3^{k+1} - 1 = 3 \cdot 3^k - 1 = (2 + 1)3^k - 1 = 2 \cdot 3^k + (3^k - 1)$$ Since $2 \cdot 3^k$ is obviously a multiple of $2$, and $(3^k - 1)$ is a multiple of $2$ by hypothesis, their sum is guaranteed to be a multiple of $2$!

Divisibility & General Base Cases

1. The Splitting Technique

$$a^{k+1} = a \cdot a^k = (d + c) a^k = d \cdot a^k + c \cdot a^k$$

Split the base $a$ into a multiple of the divisor $d$ plus remainder $c$ to reveal the hypothesis term.

2. Statements with Base Case $n \ge m$

When a theorem is only true for numbers starting from $m$ (e.g. $n \ge 4$), the initial step starts at $n = m$ instead of $n = 1$.

Example 1 (Textbook Page 17, Example 3)

Use the mathematical induction principle to prove that $3^n - 1$ is a multiple of $2$ for all natural numbers $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Show solution
  1. Initial Step ($n = 1$):
    $3^1 - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2$, which is a multiple of $2$ ($2 = 2 \times 1$).
    Therefore, the statement is true for $n = 1$.
  2. Inductive Step: Assume the statement is true for $n = k$, that is:
    $3^k - 1$ is a multiple of $2$ (i.e. $3^k - 1 = 2m$ for some integer $m$).
    We will show that $3^{k+1} - 1$ is also a multiple of $2$.
  3. Rewrite $3^{k+1} - 1$: $$3^{k+1} - 1 = 3 \cdot 3^k - 1 = (2 + 1)3^k - 1 = 2 \cdot 3^k + (3^k - 1)$$
  4. Since $2 \cdot 3^k$ has a factor of $2$, and $(3^k - 1) = 2m$ by hypothesis: $$2 \cdot 3^k + 2m = 2(3^k + m)$$ which is a multiple of $2$.
    Therefore, the statement is true for $n = k + 1$.
  5. Conclusion: Hence, by the principle of mathematical induction, $3^n - 1$ is a multiple of $2$ for all natural numbers $n$.

Proved true for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$

Example 2 (Divisibility by 4)

Prove that $5^n - 1$ is divisible by $4$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Show solution
  1. For $n = 1$: $5^1 - 1 = 4$, which is divisible by $4$. True for $n = 1$.
  2. Inductive Step: Assume $5^k - 1$ is divisible by $4$ (i.e. $5^k - 1 = 4M$).
    We must prove $5^{k+1} - 1$ is divisible by $4$.
  3. Algebraic manipulation: $$5^{k+1} - 1 = 5 \cdot 5^k - 1 = (4 + 1)5^k - 1 = 4 \cdot 5^k + (5^k - 1)$$ Substitute the hypothesis: $$= 4 \cdot 5^k + 4M = 4(5^k + M)$$ Since $5^k + M$ is an integer, $5^{k+1} - 1$ is divisible by $4$. True for $n = k + 1$.
  4. Conclusion: By mathematical induction, $5^n - 1$ is divisible by $4$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Proved true for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$

Express as an exact integer multiple: Always state clearly that the quotient $(3^k + m)$ is an integer. Divisibility means the ratio is an integer with no remainder.
General Formula to Remember: For any positive integers $a$ and $b$, $a^n - b^n$ is always divisible by $a - b$ because: $$a^n - b^n = (a - b)(a^{n-1} + a^{n-2}b + \dots + b^{n-1})$$ Induction provides a rigorous proof of this fact without expanding terms.

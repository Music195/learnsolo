Graphs of Logarithmic Functions

Concept — Logarithmic Functions $y = \log_b x$ ($b > 0, b \neq 1$)

For any positive base $b > 0$ ($b \neq 1$), the logarithmic function (လော့ဂရစ်သမ်ဖန်ရှင်) $y = \log_b x$ is defined for all $x > 0$ by the equivalence $y = \log_b x \iff x = b^y$:

  • Domain: $\{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid x > 0\} = (0, \infty)$   Range: $\mathbb{R} = (-\infty, \infty)$
  • Key Anchor Points: Every parent logarithmic graph $y = \log_b x$ passes through $\left(\frac{1}{b}, -1\right)$, the common $x$-intercept $(1, 0)$, and $(b, 1)$, and has the $y$-axis ($x = 0$) as its vertical asymptote.
  • Monotonicity by Base $b$:
    • For $b > 1$ (e.g., $y = \log_2 x$): strictly increasing ($x_1 > x_2 \implies \log_b x_1 > \log_b x_2$). Here $\log_b x < 0$ for $0 < x < 1$, and $\log_b x > 0$ for $x > 1$.
    • For $0 < b < 1$ (e.g., $y = \log_{1/2} x$): strictly decreasing ($x_1 > x_2 \implies \log_b x_1 < \log_b x_2$). Here $\log_b x > 0$ for $0 < x < 1$, and $\log_b x < 0$ for $x > 1$.
Core Formulas — Scaling, Reflection & Translation of Logarithmic Graphs
  • Reciprocal-Base Reflection Identity: $$\log_{\frac{1}{b}} x = -\log_b x \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{The graph of } y = \log_{\frac{1}{b}} x \text{ is the reflection of } y = \log_b x \text{ across the } x\text{-axis.}$$
  • Change-of-Base Vertical Scaling: For $b > 1$, $\log_b x = \dfrac{1}{\log_2 b}\log_2 x$, so $y = \log_b x$ is a vertical scaling of $y = \log_2 x$ by scale factor $\dfrac{1}{\log_2 b}$.
  • Translated & Scaled Graph $y = a\log_b(x - h) + k$:
    • Domain: $x > h$   Vertical Asymptote: $x = h$   Range: $\mathbb{R}$
    • Coordinate Mapping from $y = \log_b x$: $(x, y) \mapsto (x + h,\; ay + k)$. In particular, $(1, 0) \mapsto (1 + h,\, k)$ and $(b, 1) \mapsto (b + h,\, a + k)$.
  • Domain Distinction: $y = 2\log_b x$ vs. $y = \log_b(x^2)$ (Example 2):
    • $y = 2\log_b x$ has domain $x > 0$ (single branch in the right half-plane).
    • $y = \log_b(x^2) = 2\log_b|x|$ has domain $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ ($x \neq 0$) and is an even function with two symmetric branches on both sides of the vertical asymptote $x = 0$!
Proof — Why All Logarithmic Graphs Are Vertical Scalings of $y = \log_2 x$

By the change-of-base formula for logarithms, for any base $b > 0$ ($b \neq 1$) and any $x > 0$:

$$\log_b x = \frac{\log_2 x}{\log_2 b} = \left(\frac{1}{\log_2 b}\right)\log_2 x$$

If $b > 1$, then $\log_2 b > 0$, so every ordinate $y$ of $y = \log_2 x$ is multiplied by the positive constant $\frac{1}{\log_2 b}$. If $0 < b < 1$, writing $\frac{1}{b} > 1$ gives $\log_b x = -\log_{1/b} x = \left(\frac{1}{\log_{1/2} b}\right)\log_{1/2} x$. Thus every logarithmic curve is obtained from $y = \log_2 x$ by vertical scaling (and reflection across the $x$-axis when $0 < b < 1$).

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 1 & 2

Example 1 (Step-by-Step Transformation of $y = \log_2 x$): From the graph of $y = \log_2 x$, describe the step-by-step transformations to obtain the graphs of:
(a) $y = \log_2(x - 1) + 2$   (b) $y = \log_{\frac{1}{2}}(x + 2) - 1$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) $y = \log_2(x - 1) + 2$:

  1. Start with $y = \log_2 x$ (passes through $(0.5, -1), (1, 0), (2, 1)$ with vertical asymptote $x = 0$).
  2. Apply a horizontal translation of $+1$ unit and a vertical translation of $+2$ units: $(x, y) \mapsto (x + 1, y + 2)$.
  3. The vertical asymptote shifts to $x = 1$, and the key points become $(1.5, 1),\; (2, 2),\; (3, 3)$.

(b) $y = \log_{\frac{1}{2}}(x + 2) - 1$:

  1. First reflect $y = \log_2 x$ across the $x$-axis to get $y = -\log_2 x = \log_{\frac{1}{2}} x$ (passes through $(0.5, 1), (1, 0), (2, -1)$).
  2. Apply a horizontal translation of $-2$ units and a vertical translation of $-1$ unit: $(x, y) \mapsto (x - 2, y - 1)$.
  3. The vertical asymptote shifts to $x = -2$, and the key points become $(-1.5, 0),\; (-1, -1),\; (0, -2)$.

Answer: (a) Asymptote $x = 1$, key points $(1.5,1), (2,2), (3,3)$; (b) Asymptote $x = -2$, key points $(-1.5,0), (-1,-1), (0,-2)$.

Example 2 (Comparing $y = 2\log_3 x$ and $y = \log_3(x^2)$): Draw and compare the graphs of $y = 2\log_3 x$ and $y = \log_3(x^2)$.

Show step-by-step solution
  • Graph of $y = 2\log_3 x$: Obtained by vertically scaling $y = \log_3 x$ by scale factor $2$. Its domain is strictly $x > 0$, vertical asymptote is $x = 0$, and it passes through $\left(\frac{1}{3}, -2\right), (1, 0), (3, 2)$.
  • Graph of $y = \log_3(x^2)$: Since $x^2 > 0$ for every nonzero real number $x$, its domain is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$. Because $(-x)^2 = x^2$, $y = \log_3(x^2)$ is an even function symmetric about the $y$-axis: for $x > 0$ it coincides with $y = 2\log_3 x$, and for $x < 0$ it adds the reflected branch passing through $\left(-\frac{1}{3}, -2\right), (-1, 0), (-3, 2)$.

Answer: $y = 2\log_3 x$ has one branch ($x > 0$); $y = \log_3(x^2)$ has two symmetric branches ($x \neq 0$, even function).

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Assuming $\log_b(x^2) = 2\log_b x$ for All $x \neq 0$: The identity $\log_b(x^2) = 2\log_b x$ holds only for $x > 0$! For all $x \neq 0$, the correct identity is $\log_b(x^2) = 2\log_b|x|$.
  • Forgetting to Draw and Label the Vertical Asymptote: Every logarithmic graph $y = a\log_b(x - h) + k$ must clearly show the dashed vertical asymptote $x = h$.
Exam Tip — Three Points Are Enough to Sketch Any $y = a\log_b(x - h) + k$

To sketch $y = a\log_b(x - h) + k$ quickly, choose the three values of $x$ that make the inner argument $x - h$ equal to $\frac{1}{b}$, $1$, and $b$: namely $x = h + \frac{1}{b}$ (where $y = -a + k$), $x = h + 1$ (where $y = k$), and $x = h + b$ (where $y = a + k$).

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Base Monotonicity: $y = \log_b x$ is increasing for $b > 1$ and decreasing for $0 < b < 1$.
  • Reciprocal Base Reflection: $\log_{1/b} x = -\log_b x$ (reflection across the $x$-axis).
  • Translated Graph $y = a\log_b(x - h) + k$: Domain $x > h$, Range $\mathbb{R}$, Vertical asymptote $x = h$.

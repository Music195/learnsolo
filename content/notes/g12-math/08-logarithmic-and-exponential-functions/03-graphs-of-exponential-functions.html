Graphs of Exponential Functions

Concept — Exponential Functions $y = b^x$ ($b > 0, b \neq 1$)

A function of the form $y = b^x$, where the base $b > 0$ ($b \neq 1$) is constant and the variable $x$ appears in the exponent, is called an exponential function (ထပ်ညွှန်းဖန်ရှင်). It is the inverse function of $y = \log_b x$, so their graphs are reflections of each other across the diagonal line $y = x$:

  • Domain: $\mathbb{R} = (-\infty, \infty)$   Range: $\{y \in \mathbb{R} \mid y > 0\} = (0, \infty)$
  • Horizontal Asymptote: The $x$-axis ($y = 0$).
  • Key Anchor Points: Every parent exponential graph $y = b^x$ passes through $(-1, \frac{1}{b})$, the common $y$-intercept $(0, 1)$, and $(1, b)$.
  • Exponential Growth vs. Exponential Decay:
    • For $b > 1$ (e.g., $y = 2^x, 3^x, e^x$): strictly increasing exponential growth function.
    • For $0 < b < 1$ (e.g., $y = 2^{-x} = (\frac{1}{2})^x$): strictly decreasing exponential decay function, obtained by reflecting $y = b^x$ across the $y$-axis.
Core Formulas — Transformations of $y = ab^{x - h} + k$ ($a \neq 0$)
  • Reflection Across Coordinate Axes: $$y = b^x \;\xrightarrow{\text{Reflection across } y\text{-axis}}\; y = b^{-x} = \left(\frac{1}{b}\right)^x, \qquad y = b^x \;\xrightarrow{\text{Reflection across } x\text{-axis}}\; y = -b^x$$
  • Transformation Pathway from $y = b^x$ to $y = ab^{x - h} + k$: $$(x, y) \;\xrightarrow{\text{Vertical scale } a}\; (x, ay) \;\xrightarrow{\text{Translate } (h, k)}\; (x + h,\; ay + k)$$
    • Anchor point $(0, 1)$ on $y = b^x$ maps to $(0, a)$ on $y = ab^x$, and then to $(h,\; a + k)$ on $y = ab^{x - h} + k$.
    • Anchor point $(1, b)$ on $y = b^x$ maps to $(1, ab)$ on $y = ab^x$, and then to $(1 + h,\; ab + k)$ on $y = ab^{x - h} + k$.
  • Asymptote & Range of $y = ab^{x - h} + k$:
    • Horizontal Asymptote: $y = k$
    • Range if $a > 0$: $\{y \in \mathbb{R} \mid y > k\} = (k, \infty)$ (all values of $y$ above the asymptote $y = k$).
    • Range if $a < 0$: $\{y \in \mathbb{R} \mid y < k\} = (-\infty, k)$ (all values of $y$ below the asymptote $y = k$).
Justification — Why the Horizontal Asymptote Shifts to $y = k$

For any base $b > 0$ ($b \neq 1$) and any real input $x - h$, the exponential term $b^{x - h}$ is strictly positive ($b^{x - h} > 0$) and approaches $0$ at one end of the real line (as $x \to -\infty$ when $b > 1$, or as $x \to +\infty$ when $0 < b < 1$).

Consequently, $ab^{x - h}$ is never zero and approaches $0$, which means $y = ab^{x - h} + k$ approaches $k$ without ever reaching $k$. If $a > 0$, then $ab^{x - h} > 0 \implies y > k$; if $a < 0$, then $ab^{x - h} < 0 \implies y < k$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 6 & 7 and Exercise 8.3

Example 6 (Transforming Exponential Graphs Step-by-Step): Draw the graphs of:
(a) $y = 1.5 \cdot 2^{x - 1} - 1$ from $y = 2^x$   (b) $y = -2^{-(x + 1)} + 2$ from $y = -2^{-x}$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) $y = 1.5 \cdot 2^{x - 1} - 1$:

  1. Vertically scale $y = 2^x$ by factor $1.5$ to obtain $y = 1.5 \cdot 2^x$ (mapping $(0, 1) \to (0, 1.5)$ and $(1, 2) \to (1, 3)$).
  2. Translate horizontally by $+1$ unit and vertically by $-1$ unit to get $y = 1.5 \cdot 2^{x - 1} - 1$.
  3. Horizontal Asymptote: $y = -1$;   Range: $\{y \mid y > -1\}$;   Key points: $(1, 0.5),\; (2, 2),\; (3, 5)$.

(b) $y = -2^{-(x + 1)} + 2$:

  1. Start with $y = -2^{-x}$ (passing through $(-1, -2), (0, -1), (1, -0.5)$ with asymptote $y = 0$).
  2. Translate horizontally by $-1$ unit and vertically by $+2$ units to get $y = -2^{-(x + 1)} + 2$.
  3. Horizontal Asymptote: $y = 2$;   Range: $\{y \mid y < 2\}$;   Key points: $(-2, 0),\; (-1, 1),\; (0, 1.5)$.

Answer: (a) Asymptote $y = -1$, Range $y > -1$; (b) Asymptote $y = 2$, Range $y < 2$.

Example 7 & Exercise 8.3 #3 (Mapping Anchor Points, $y$-Intercept, Asymptote & Range):
(a) The points $(0, 1)$ and $(1, b)$ lie on $y = b^x$. Find the corresponding points on $y = ab^x$ and $y = ab^{x - h} + k$, and state its asymptote and range.
(b) Find the $y$-intercept, asymptote, and range of $y = 3e^{x - 1} + 2$ and $y = -2e^{-x + 1} + 3$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Under vertical scaling by $a$, $(0, 1) \mapsto (0, a)$ and $(1, b) \mapsto (1, ab)$ on $y = ab^x$. Under translation by $(h, k)$, they map to $(h,\; a + k)$ and $(1 + h,\; ab + k)$ on $y = ab^{x - h} + k$. The asymptote is $y = k$; the range is $\{y \mid y > k\}$ if $a > 0$, and $\{y \mid y < k\}$ if $a < 0$.

(b)

  • For $y = 3e^{x - 1} + 2$: Setting $x = 0$ gives the $y$-intercept $\left(0,\; \frac{3}{e} + 2\right)$; the horizontal asymptote is $y = 2$; since $a = 3 > 0$, the range is $\{y \mid y > 2\}$.
  • For $y = -2e^{-x + 1} + 3$: Setting $x = 0$ gives the $y$-intercept $(0,\; -2e + 3)$; the horizontal asymptote is $y = 3$; since $a = -2 < 0$, the range is $\{y \mid y < 3\}$.

Answer: (a) $(h, a+k)$ and $(1+h, ab+k)$, asymptote $y = k$; (b) $(0, \frac{3}{e}+2), y=2, y>2$ and $(0, -2e+3), y=3, y<3$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Do Not Combine Base $b$ and Multiplier $a$: In $y = 2 \cdot 3^{x + 1} - 2$, NEVER multiply $2 \times 3$ to write $6^{x + 1}$! By order of operations, the exponent $x + 1$ applies only to the base $3$, while $a = 2$ is the vertical scaling factor.
  • Sign of Exponent vs. Sign of $a$ for Range: Whether the range is $y > k$ or $y < k$ depends only on the sign of $a$ (not on whether the exponent is $+x$ or $-x$, since $b^{\text{anything}}$ is always positive!).
Exam Tip — Rewriting Negative Exponents Cleanly

When graphing $y = -2^{-x + 1} + 3$, always factor the minus sign out of the exponent first: $-x + 1 = -(x - 1)$, so $y = -2^{-(x - 1)} + 3 = -\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x - 1} + 3$. This immediately shows $h = 1$ and $k = 3$ without sign confusion!

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Parent Exponential Function: $y = b^x$ has Domain $\mathbb{R}$, Range $(0, \infty)$, Horizontal asymptote $y = 0$, and passes through $(0, 1)$ and $(1, b)$.
  • Growth vs. Decay: $b > 1$ gives exponential growth; $0 < b < 1$ (or $b^{-x}$ with $b > 1$) gives exponential decay.
  • Transformed Graph $y = ab^{x - h} + k$: Horizontal asymptote $y = k$; Range is $y > k$ (for $a > 0$) or $y < k$ (for $a < 0$).

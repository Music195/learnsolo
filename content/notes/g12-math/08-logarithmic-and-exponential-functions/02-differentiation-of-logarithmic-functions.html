Differentiation of Logarithmic Functions

Concept — Euler's Number $e$, Natural Logarithms & Logarithmic Derivatives

When differentiating $y = \log_b x$ from first principles, the limit of the difference quotient naturally introduces the fundamental constant $e$ (Euler's number, အွိုင်လာကိန်းသေ), defined by:

$$e = \lim_{t \to 0}(1 + t)^{\frac{1}{t}} \approx 2.7182818\dots$$

The logarithm with base $e$ is called the natural logarithm (သဘာဝ လော့ဂရစ်သမ်) and is denoted by $\ln x = \log_e x$. Because $\ln e = \log_e e = 1$, choosing base $b = e$ makes the derivative formula extraordinarily clean: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

Core Formulas — Derivatives of $\ln u$ and $\log_b u$ & Logarithmic Differentiation
  • Derivative of Natural Logarithm ($b = e$): For $x > 0$ and any positive differentiable function $u = u(x) > 0$: $$\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \frac{1}{x}, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\big(\ln u(x)\big) = \frac{1}{u(x)} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} = \frac{u'(x)}{u(x)}$$ (More generally, for $u(x) \neq 0$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln|u(x)| = \dfrac{1}{u(x)}\dfrac{du}{dx}$.)
  • Derivative of General Base-$b$ Logarithm ($b > 0, b \neq 1$): $$\frac{d}{dx}(\log_b x) = \frac{1}{x}\log_b e = \frac{1}{x\ln b}, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\big(\log_b u(x)\big) = \frac{1}{u(x)}\log_b e \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$$
  • Logarithmic Differentiation Procedure: To differentiate a complicated positive function $y = f(x)$ involving products, quotients, powers, or radicals:
    1. Take the natural logarithm $\ln$ of both sides: $\ln y = \ln f(x)$.
    2. Expand the right-hand side using log laws ($\ln(AB) = \ln A + \ln B$, $\ln(A/B) = \ln A - \ln B$, $\ln(A^p) = p\ln A$).
    3. Differentiate both sides implicitly with respect to $x$: $\dfrac{1}{y}\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{d}{dx}[\ln f(x)]$, and multiply through by $y$.
Proof — First-Principles Derivation of $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\log_b x) = \dfrac{1}{x}\log_b e$

Let $y = \log_b x$ ($x > 0$) and let $\delta x$ be a small increment in $x$. Using the logarithm quotient and power rules:

$$\delta y = \log_b(x + \delta x) - \log_b x = \log_b\!\left(\frac{x + \delta x}{x}\right) = \log_b\!\left(1 + \frac{\delta x}{x}\right)$$

Set $t = \dfrac{\delta x}{x}$, so that $\delta x = xt$ and $t \to 0$ as $\delta x \to 0$. Then the difference quotient becomes:

$$\frac{\delta y}{\delta x} = \frac{1}{xt}\log_b(1 + t) = \frac{1}{x} \cdot \frac{1}{t}\log_b(1 + t) = \frac{1}{x}\log_b\big((1 + t)^{1/t}\big)$$

Taking the limit as $\delta x \to 0$ (so $t \to 0$) and using the continuity of $\log_b$ together with $\lim_{t \to 0}(1 + t)^{1/t} = e$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \lim_{t \to 0}\frac{1}{x}\log_b\big((1 + t)^{1/t}\big) = \frac{1}{x}\log_b\!\left(\lim_{t \to 0}(1 + t)^{1/t}\right) = \frac{1}{x}\log_b e \quad \blacksquare$$

In particular, when $b = e$, $\log_e e = \ln e = 1$, which gives $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 3, 4 & 5

Example 3 (Differentiating Natural and Base-$b$ Logarithms): Differentiate with respect to $x$:
(a) $\log_3(3x^2 + 1)$   (b) $\ln\sqrt{4x^2 + 1}$   (c) $\ln x \cdot \log_{10} x$   (d) $\ln\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 2}}$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Using $\dfrac{d}{dx}\log_b u = \dfrac{1}{u}\log_b e \cdot \dfrac{du}{dx}$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\log_3(3x^2 + 1) = \frac{1}{3x^2 + 1}\log_3 e \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(3x^2 + 1) = \frac{6x}{3x^2 + 1}\log_3 e$$

(b) First simplify using $\ln\sqrt{4x^2 + 1} = \frac{1}{2}\ln(4x^2 + 1)$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{1}{2}\ln(4x^2 + 1)\right] = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4x^2 + 1} \cdot (8x) = \frac{4x}{4x^2 + 1}$$

(c) By the product rule:

$$\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x \cdot \log_{10} x) = \ln x \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x}\log_{10} e\right) + \log_{10} x \cdot \left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \frac{\ln x\,\log_{10} e + \log_{10} x}{x}$$

(d) Using the textbook chain/quotient method (or expanding $\ln x - \frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2 + 2)$ first):

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\ln x - \frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2 + 2)\right] = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2x}{x^2 + 2} = \frac{(x^2 + 2) - x^2}{x(x^2 + 2)} = \frac{2}{x(x^2 + 2)}$$

Answer: (a) $\dfrac{6x}{3x^2 + 1}\log_3 e$, (b) $\dfrac{4x}{4x^2 + 1}$, (c) $\dfrac{\ln x\log_{10}e + \log_{10}x}{x}$, (d) $\dfrac{2}{x(x^2 + 2)}$.

Example 4 (Logarithmic Differentiation of a Radical Product): Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \sqrt{(x^2 + 1)(x - 1)^3}$.

Show step-by-step solution

Take the natural logarithm of both sides and expand using logarithm laws:

$$\ln y = \ln\big[(x^2 + 1)(x - 1)^3\big]^{1/2} = \frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2 + 1) + \frac{3}{2}\ln(x - 1)$$

Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:

$$\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} + \frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{x - 1} = \frac{x}{x^2 + 1} + \frac{3}{2(x - 1)}$$

Multiply both sides by $y = \sqrt{(x^2 + 1)(x - 1)^3}$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \sqrt{(x^2 + 1)(x - 1)^3}\left(\frac{x}{x^2 + 1} + \frac{3}{2(x - 1)}\right)$$

Answer: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \sqrt{(x^2 + 1)(x - 1)^3}\left(\dfrac{x}{x^2 + 1} + \dfrac{3}{2(x - 1)}\right)$.

Example 5 (Logarithmic Differentiation with Trigonometric Factors): Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ if $y = \dfrac{x\sin x}{\sqrt{\sec x}}$ for $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Show step-by-step solution

Take $\ln$ of both sides and expand:

$$\ln y = \ln\!\left(\frac{x\sin x}{\sqrt{\sec x}}\right) = \ln x + \ln(\sin x) - \frac{1}{2}\ln(\sec x)$$

Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:

$$\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{x} + \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} - \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\sec x\tan x}{\sec x} = \frac{1}{x} + \cot x - \frac{1}{2}\tan x$$

Multiply by $y$:

Answer: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{x\sin x}{\sqrt{\sec x}}\left(\dfrac{1}{x} + \cot x - \dfrac{1}{2}\tan x\right)$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Forgetting the Factor $\log_b e$ When $b \neq e$: For $\log_{10} x$ or $\log_3 x$, the derivative is $\frac{1}{x}\log_{10} e$ or $\frac{1}{x}\log_3 e$, NOT simply $\frac{1}{x}$! Only the natural logarithm $\ln x$ has derivative $\frac{1}{x}$.
  • Forgetting to Multiply Back by $y$ in Logarithmic Differentiation: Differentiating $\ln y$ gives $\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}$ on the left side; always multiply the right side by the original expression for $y$ at the final step!
Exam Tip — Expand Logarithms BEFORE Differentiating!

Whenever you need to differentiate $\ln\!\left(\frac{A^p B^q}{C^r}\right)$, always expand it into $p\ln A + q\ln B - r\ln C$ before taking any derivatives. This completely avoids messy quotient-rule and radical-chain-rule algebra!

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Euler's Number: $e = \displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0}(1 + t)^{1/t} \approx 2.71828$, and $\ln x = \log_e x$.
  • Natural Log Derivative: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\ln u) = \dfrac{1}{u}\dfrac{du}{dx}$.
  • General Base-$b$ Log Derivative: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\log_b u) = \dfrac{1}{u}\log_b e \cdot \dfrac{du}{dx} = \dfrac{1}{u\ln b}\dfrac{du}{dx}$.

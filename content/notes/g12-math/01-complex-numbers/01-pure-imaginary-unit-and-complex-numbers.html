Pure Imaginary Unit and Complex Numbers

Extending the Real Numbers Beyond $x^2 \ge 0$

For every real number $x$, the square is always non-negative ($x^2 \ge 0$). Because of this, simple equations like $x^2 = -4$ have no real solution.

To overcome this limitation, mathematicians introduced a pure imaginary unit denoted by $i$, defined by: $$i^2 = -1 \quad (\text{or } i = \sqrt{-1})$$

With this unit, $x^2 = -4$ has two solutions: $x = 2i$ and $x = -2i$, since $(2i)^2 = 4i^2 = 4(-1) = -4$. Combining real numbers with imaginary numbers gives the system of complex numbers (ကွန်ပလက်စ်ကိန်းများ).

Complex Numbers & Ordered Pairs

$$z = x + yi = (x, y) \qquad (x, y \in \mathbb{R})$$

$x$ is the real part ($\text{Re}(z)$) and $y$ is the imaginary part ($\text{Im}(z)$). When $y = 0$, $z$ is purely real; when $x = 0$ and $y \ne 0$, $z$ is pure imaginary.

1. Equality of Two Complex Numbers

$(x_1, y_1) = (x_2, y_2) \iff x_1 = x_2 \quad\text{and}\quad y_1 = y_2$

2. Addition and Multiplication

$$(x_1, y_1) + (x_2, y_2) = (x_1 + x_2, \; y_1 + y_2)$$ $$(x_1, y_1)(x_2, y_2) = (x_1 x_2 - y_1 y_2, \; x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)$$

3. Cyclic Powers of $i$

$i^1 = i, \quad i^2 = -1, \quad i^3 = -i, \quad i^4 = 1, \quad \dots \quad i^{4k+r} = i^r$

Interactive Complex Number: Change $x$ and $y$

Example 1 (Textbook Page 1)

Solve $x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0$.

Show solution
  1. Complete the square: $x^2 + 2x + 1 = -5 + 1 \implies (x + 1)^2 = -4$.
  2. Take square roots on both sides: $x + 1 = \pm \sqrt{-4} = \pm 2i$.
  3. Isolate $x$: $x = -1 \pm 2i$.

Two solutions: $x = -1 + 2i$ and $x = -1 - 2i$

Example 2 (Textbook Page 2)

Solve $x^2 + 2x + 3 = 0$ and check your answers.

Show solution
  1. Rewrite as: $x^2 + 2x + 1 = -2 \implies (x + 1)^2 = -2$.
  2. $x + 1 = \pm \sqrt{2}i \implies x = -1 \pm \sqrt{2}i$.
  3. Check for $x = -1 + \sqrt{2}i$:
    $(-1 + \sqrt{2}i)^2 + 2(-1 + \sqrt{2}i) + 3 = (1 - 2\sqrt{2}i - 2) - 2 + 2\sqrt{2}i + 3 = 0$. ✓

$x = -1 + \sqrt{2}i$ and $x = -1 - \sqrt{2}i$

Example 3 (Textbook Page 4)

Compute $(-2, 3)(1, -2) + (1, 1)(0, 1)$ using both ordered pairs and $x + yi$ notation.

Show solution
  1. Method 1 (Ordered pairs):
    $(-2, 3)(1, -2) = ((-2)(1) - (3)(-2), \; (-2)(-2) + (3)(1)) = (-2 + 6, \; 4 + 3) = (4, 7)$.
    $(1, 1)(0, 1) = ((1)(0) - (1)(1), \; (1)(1) + (1)(0)) = (-1, 1)$.
    Sum: $(4, 7) + (-1, 1) = (4 - 1, \; 7 + 1) = (3, 8)$.
  2. Method 2 ($x + yi$ notation):
    $(-2 + 3i)(1 - 2i) + (1 + i)(i) = (-2 + 4i + 3i - 6i^2) + (i + i^2)$
    $= (-2 + 7i + 6) + (i - 1) = (4 + 7i) + (-1 + i) = 3 + 8i$.

$(3, 8)$ or $3 + 8i$

Common Mistake: $\sqrt{-a} \cdot \sqrt{-b} \ne \sqrt{ab}$ when $a, b > 0$.
Always write imaginary units first: $\sqrt{-4} \cdot \sqrt{-9} = (2i)(3i) = 6i^2 = -6$, NOT $\sqrt{(-4)(-9)} = \sqrt{36} = 6$.
Quick Power Rule for $i^n$: Divide the exponent $n$ by $4$. The remainder gives the power:
Remainder $0 \implies i^0 = 1$ | Remainder $1 \implies i^1 = i$ | Remainder $2 \implies i^2 = -1$ | Remainder $3 \implies i^3 = -i$.
Example: $i^{35} = i^{4 \times 8 + 3} = i^3 = -i$.
  • $i$ is the pure imaginary unit: $i^2 = -1$ (စိတ်ကူးယဉ်ယူနစ် $i$).
  • Every complex number can be written as $z = x + yi$ or as an ordered pair $(x, y)$.
  • Real numbers are complex numbers with $y = 0$; purely imaginary numbers have $x = 0$.
  • Multiplication rule: $(x_1 + y_1 i)(x_2 + y_2 i) = (x_1 x_2 - y_1 y_2) + (x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1)i$.

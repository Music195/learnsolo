Vectors in Three Dimensions

Concept — Vectors in Three-Dimensional Space ($\mathbb{R}^3$)

A vector (ဗက်တာ) in three-dimensional space is a directed quantity possessing both magnitude (ပမာဏ) and direction (ဦးတည်ချက်). We define the three standard unit vectors along the positive coordinate axes as:

$$\hat{i} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat{j} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat{k} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

For any point $P(x, y, z)$ in space, the directed segment $\overrightarrow{OP}$ from the origin $O(0,0,0)$ to $P$ is called the position vector (တည်နေရာဗက်တာ) of $P$, written in column vector or unit-vector component form as:

$$\overrightarrow{OP} = \vec{p} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$$
Core Formulas — Displacement, Magnitude, Unit Vectors & Parallelism
  • Displacement Vector Between Two Points: Given $A(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2, z_2)$: $$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} x_2 \\ y_2 \\ z_2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ y_1 \\ z_1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x_2 - x_1 \\ y_2 - y_1 \\ z_2 - z_1 \end{pmatrix}$$
  • Magnitude (Modulus) of a Vector: For $\vec{a} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$: $$|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$$
  • Unit Vector in the Direction of $\vec{a}$: For any nonzero vector $\vec{a} \neq \vec{0}$, the unit vector $\hat{a}$ (with $|\hat{a}| = 1$) in the same direction as $\vec{a}$ is: $$\hat{a} = \frac{1}{|\vec{a}|}\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{a}}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}}$$
  • Parallel Vectors & Collinear Points: Two nonzero vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are parallel ($\vec{a} \parallel \vec{b}$) if and only if $\vec{a} = k\vec{b}$ for some nonzero scalar $k \in \mathbb{R}$ ($k > 0$: same direction; $k < 0$: opposite direction). Three points $A, B, C$ are collinear if $\overrightarrow{BC} = k\overrightarrow{AB}$ (since they share point $B$).
Derivation — Triangle Rule for $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA}$ & Parallelogram Rule

1. Position-to-Displacement Identity: By the triangle law of vector addition on $\triangle OAB$, moving from $O$ to $A$ and then from $A$ to $B$ yields the same resultant displacement as moving directly from $O$ to $B$:

$$\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} \implies \overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA}$$

2. Parallelogram Vertex Rule: In a parallelogram $ABCD$ (with vertices labeled consecutively in order around the perimeter), opposite sides are equal in magnitude and direction:

$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{DC} \iff \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \overrightarrow{OC} - \overrightarrow{OD}$$

This gives the rapid coordinate identity $\vec{a} + \vec{c} = \vec{b} + \vec{d}$, allowing any missing fourth vertex of a parallelogram to be found immediately.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 4, 5, 6 & 7

Example 4 (Solving a Vector Equation): Given $\vec{p} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\vec{q} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$, and $\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$, find the vector $\vec{x}$ such that $2\vec{p} - \vec{x} = \vec{q} - 3\vec{r}$.

Show step-by-step solution

Rearranging the equation for $\vec{x}$ gives $\vec{x} = 2\vec{p} - \vec{q} + 3\vec{r}$:

$$\vec{x} = 2\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} + 3\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 - 3 + 15 \\ 4 - 0 - 12 \\ 2 - (-2) + 6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ -8 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}$$

Answer: $\vec{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ -8 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}$.

Example 5 (Fourth Vertex of a Parallelogram): Find the coordinates of vertex $D$ if $A(1, -1, -3)$, $B(4, -3, 1)$, and $C(3, 1, 2)$ are the first three vertices of parallelogram $ABCD$.

Show step-by-step solution

Let $D(x, y, z)$ be the fourth vertex. Since $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{DC}$:

$$\overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \overrightarrow{OC} - \overrightarrow{OD} \implies \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix}$$ $$\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 - x \\ 1 - y \\ 2 - z \end{pmatrix} \implies \begin{cases} 3 - x = 3 \implies x = 0 \\ 1 - y = -2 \implies y = 3 \\ 2 - z = 4 \implies z = -2 \end{cases}$$

Answer: $D(0, 3, -2)$.

Example 6 (Scaling Using Unit Vectors): Given $\vec{a} = -2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, find:
(a) the unit vector $\hat{a}$ in the direction of $\vec{a}$,
(b) a vector of magnitude $6$ in the direction of $\vec{a}$, and
(c) a vector of magnitude $6$ in the direction opposite to $\vec{a}$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) First compute the magnitude $|\vec{a}|$:

$$|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + 1^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = \sqrt{9} = 3$$ $$\hat{a} = \frac{1}{|\vec{a}|}\vec{a} = \frac{1}{3}(-2\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}) = -\frac{2}{3}\hat{i} + \frac{1}{3}\hat{j} + \frac{2}{3}\hat{k}$$

(b) A vector of magnitude $6$ in the same direction is $6\hat{a}$:

$$6\hat{a} = 6\left(-\frac{2}{3}\hat{i} + \frac{1}{3}\hat{j} + \frac{2}{3}\hat{k}\right) = -4\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$$

(c) A vector of magnitude $6$ in the opposite direction is $-6\hat{a}$:

$$-6\hat{a} = 4\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$$

Answer: (a) $\hat{a} = -\frac{2}{3}\hat{i} + \frac{1}{3}\hat{j} + \frac{2}{3}\hat{k}$, (b) $-4\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$, (c) $4\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$.

Example 7 (Proving Collinearity of Three Points): Prove that the points $A(0, -2, 4)$, $B(1, -3, 2)$, and $C(3, -5, -2)$ are collinear.

Show step-by-step solution

Compute the displacement vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}$:

$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$$ $$\overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{OC} - \overrightarrow{OB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -5 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix} = 2\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = 2\overrightarrow{AB}$$

Since $\overrightarrow{BC} = 2\overrightarrow{AB}$, the vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}$ are parallel and share the common point $B$.

Answer: Therefore $A, B,$ and $C$ are collinear.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Order of Subtraction in $\overrightarrow{AB}$: Always subtract the initial point from the terminal point ($\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA}$, read as "head minus tail"). Reversing the subtraction gives $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB}$.
  • Vertex Order in Parallelogram $ABCD$: In parallelogram $ABCD$, $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{DC}$ (not $\overrightarrow{CD}$!). Drawing a quick sketch of the loop $A \to B \to C \to D$ prevents sign errors.
  • Proving Collinearity vs. Parallelism: Showing $\overrightarrow{BC} = k\overrightarrow{AB}$ is not complete until you explicitly state that the two parallel segments share the common point $B$.
Exam Tip — Rapid Unit-Vector Scaling

Whenever a problem asks for "a vector of magnitude $L$ in the direction of $\vec{a}$", compute the unit vector $\hat{a} = \frac{\vec{a}}{|\vec{a}|}$ first, then multiply by $\pm L$: $\vec{v} = \pm L\hat{a} = \pm \frac{L}{|\vec{a}|}\vec{a}$.

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Position & Displacement Vector (တည်နေရာနှင့် ရွေ့လျားမှုဗက်တာ): $\overrightarrow{OP} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA}$.
  • Magnitude & Unit Vector (ပမာဏနှင့် ယူနစ်ဗက်တာ): $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$ and $\hat{a} = \dfrac{\vec{a}}{|\vec{a}|}$.
  • Parallel & Collinear Condition (အပြိုင်နှင့် တစ်ဖြောင့်တည်းဖြစ်ခြင်း): $\vec{a} \parallel \vec{b} \iff \vec{a} = k\vec{b}$ ($k \neq 0$); points $A, B, C$ are collinear if $\overrightarrow{BC} = k\overrightarrow{AB}$.

Concept — Angle Between Vectors & The Scalar (Dot) Product

The angle $\theta$ between two nonzero vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is the interior angle ($0 \le \theta \le \pi$, or $0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ$) formed when both vectors are placed tail-to-tail at the same initial point $O$.

The scalar product (or dot product, စကေလာမြှောက်လဒ်) of two vectors $\vec{a} = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ y_1 \\ z_1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\vec{b} = \begin{pmatrix} x_2 \\ y_2 \\ z_2 \end{pmatrix}$ produces a real scalar number (not a vector) defined component-wise and geometrically by:

$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 + z_1z_2 = |\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta$$
Core Formulas — Scalar Product, Angle $\theta$ & Algebraic/Geometric Properties
  • Cosine of the Angle Between Two Vectors: For nonzero $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$: $$\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|} = \frac{x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 + z_1z_2}{\sqrt{x_1^2 + y_1^2 + z_1^2}\sqrt{x_2^2 + y_2^2 + z_2^2}}$$
  • Perpendicularity (Orthogonality) Condition: For nonzero vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$: $$\vec{a} \perp \vec{b} \iff \theta = 90^\circ \iff \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$$
  • Parallel & Self-Dot Properties: $$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{a} = |\vec{a}|^2, \qquad \vec{a} \parallel \vec{b} \text{ (same direction)} \implies \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|, \qquad \vec{a} \parallel \vec{b} \text{ (opposite)} \implies \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = -|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|$$
  • Algebraic Properties: For any vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}$ and scalar $k \in \mathbb{R}$: $$\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = \vec{b}\cdot\vec{a}, \qquad (k\vec{a})\cdot\vec{b} = k(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}) = \vec{a}\cdot(k\vec{b}), \qquad \vec{a}\cdot(\vec{b} + \vec{c}) = \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} + \vec{a}\cdot\vec{c}$$
Proof — Cosine Rule Derivation of $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta$

Let $\overrightarrow{OA} = \vec{a} = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ y_1 \\ z_1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \vec{b} = \begin{pmatrix} x_2 \\ y_2 \\ z_2 \end{pmatrix}$ meet at angle $\angle AOB = \theta$. By the triangle rule, the third side of $\triangle OAB$ is $\overrightarrow{AB} = \vec{b} - \vec{a} = \begin{pmatrix} x_2 - x_1 \\ y_2 - y_1 \\ z_2 - z_1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Applying the Law of Cosines to $\triangle OAB$:

$$|\overrightarrow{AB}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 - 2|\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta$$

Expanding the squared lengths in coordinates:

$$(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2 = (x_1^2 + y_1^2 + z_1^2) + (x_2^2 + y_2^2 + z_2^2) - 2|\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta$$

Canceling $x_1^2 + y_1^2 + z_1^2 + x_2^2 + y_2^2 + z_2^2$ from both sides leaves:

$$-2(x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 + z_1z_2) = -2|\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta \implies \vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 + z_1z_2 = |\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta \quad \blacksquare$$

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 8, 9, 10 & 11

Example 8 (Angle Between Two Vectors): Find the angle between $\vec{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\vec{b} = \hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$.

Show step-by-step solution

Write $\vec{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Compute the dot product and magnitudes:

$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 2(1) + 1(-3) + 3(2) = 2 - 3 + 6 = 5$$ $$|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{2^2 + 1^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{14}, \qquad |\vec{b}| = \sqrt{1^2 + (-3)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{14}$$ $$\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{14}\sqrt{14}} = \frac{5}{14} \approx 0.3571 \implies \theta \approx 69.1^\circ$$

Answer: $\theta = \arccos\!\left(\frac{5}{14}\right) \approx 69.1^\circ$.

Example 9 (Angle Between Line Segments $AB$ and $AC$): Given points $A(2, -1, 3)$, $B(0, 1, -1)$, and $C(1, 3, -2)$, find the angle between the segments $AB$ and $AC$.

Show step-by-step solution

First form the displacement vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ starting from vertex $A$:

$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 - 2 \\ 1 - (-1) \\ -1 - 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 2 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \overrightarrow{AC} = \overrightarrow{OC} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2 \\ 3 - (-1) \\ -2 - 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}$$

Now compute $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC}$ and the two magnitudes:

$$\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC} = (-2)(-1) + (2)(4) + (-4)(-5) = 2 + 8 + 20 = 30$$ $$|\overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + 2^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{24}, \qquad |\overrightarrow{AC}| = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 4^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{42}$$ $$\cos\theta = \frac{30}{\sqrt{24}\sqrt{42}} = \frac{30}{\sqrt{1008}} \approx 0.9449 \implies \theta \approx 19.1^\circ$$

Answer: $\theta \approx 19.1^\circ$.

Example 10 (Evaluating Dot Products Using Algebraic Laws): Given $|\vec{a}| = 3$, $|\vec{b}| = 4$, and that $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are parallel in opposite directions, evaluate $(2\vec{a} - \vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} + 3\vec{b})$.

Show step-by-step solution

Since $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ point in opposite directions, $\theta = 180^\circ$ and $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = -|\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}| = -(3)(4) = -12$. Expanding by the distributive law:

$$(2\vec{a} - \vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} + 3\vec{b}) = 2(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{a}) + 6(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}) - (\vec{b}\cdot\vec{a}) - 3(\vec{b}\cdot\vec{b})$$ $$= 2|\vec{a}|^2 + 5(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}) - 3|\vec{b}|^2 = 2(3^2) + 5(-12) - 3(4^2) = 18 - 60 - 48 = -90$$

Answer: $-90$.

Example 11 (Finding Unknown Parameter $k$ for Perpendicularity): Consider the points $A(2, 1, -2)$, $B(0, 3, -4)$, and $C(1, -2, 1)$, and let $D$ be the point $(2k, -1 - k, 3k)$. Find:
(a) $\overrightarrow{BC}$,   (b) $\overrightarrow{AD}$ in terms of $k$,   and (c) the value of $k$ for which $\overrightarrow{AD} \perp \overrightarrow{BC}$.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) $\overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{OC} - \overrightarrow{OB} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$.

(b) $\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{OD} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \begin{pmatrix} 2k \\ -1 - k \\ 3k \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2k - 2 \\ -2 - k \\ 3k + 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

(c) Since $\overrightarrow{AD} \perp \overrightarrow{BC}$, their scalar product equals zero:

$$\overrightarrow{AD} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC} = 0 \implies 1(2k - 2) + (-5)(-2 - k) + 5(3k + 2) = 0$$ $$2k - 2 + 10 + 5k + 15k + 10 = 0 \implies 22k + 18 = 0 \implies k = -\frac{18}{22} = -\frac{9}{11}$$

Answer: (a) $\overrightarrow{BC} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$, (b) $\overrightarrow{AD} = \begin{pmatrix} 2k - 2 \\ -2 - k \\ 3k + 2 \end{pmatrix}$, (c) $k = -\dfrac{9}{11}$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Writing a Vector Instead of a Number for $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}$: The dot product $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 + z_1z_2$ is a single scalar number, NOT $\begin{pmatrix} x_1x_2 \\ y_1y_2 \\ z_1z_2 \end{pmatrix}$.
  • Tail-to-Tail Requirement for Interior Angles: To find $\angle BAC$ of a triangle, both vectors must start at vertex $A$: use $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ (not $\overrightarrow{BA}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$).
Exam Tip — Proving Rhombus Diagonals Are Perpendicular (Exercise 4.2 #6)

In a parallelogram $ABCD$ with $\overrightarrow{AB} = \vec{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD} = \vec{b}$, the diagonals are $\overrightarrow{AC} = \vec{a} + \vec{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{BD} = \vec{b} - \vec{a}$. Their dot product is $(\vec{a} + \vec{b})\cdot(\vec{b} - \vec{a}) = |\vec{b}|^2 - |\vec{a}|^2$. In a rhombus, $|\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}|$, so $\overrightarrow{AC}\cdot\overrightarrow{BD} = 0$, proving immediately that the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular!

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Scalar Product Formula (စကေလာမြှောက်လဒ်): $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 + z_1z_2 = |\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta$.
  • Perpendicularity Test (ထောင့်မှန်ကျခြင်း စစ်ဆေးချက်): Nonzero vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are perpendicular ($\vec{a} \perp \vec{b}$) if and only if $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = 0$.
  • Self-Dot Identity: $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{a} = |\vec{a}|^2$.

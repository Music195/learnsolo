Concept — Vector Equations of Lines and Planes in Space

Vectors provide an elegant, unified language for describing straight lines (မျဉ်းဖြောင့်များ) and planes (ပြင်ညီများ) in three-dimensional space:

  • Line in 3D Space: A line passing through a fixed point $A(a_1, a_2, a_3)$ with position vector $\vec{a} = \overrightarrow{OA}$ and parallel to a direction vector $\vec{b} = \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ b_3 \end{pmatrix}$ consists of all points $R(x, y, z)$ with position vector $\vec{r} = \overrightarrow{OR}$ such that $\overrightarrow{AR} = t\vec{b}$, giving $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + t\vec{b}$ ($t \in \mathbb{R}$).
  • Plane in 3D Space: A plane passing through $A(\vec{a})$ can be specified either by two nonparallel direction vectors $\vec{d}_1, \vec{d}_2$ lying in the plane ($\vec{r} = \vec{a} + t_1\vec{d}_1 + t_2\vec{d}_2$) or by a single normal vector $\vec{n}$ perpendicular to the plane ($(\vec{r} - \vec{a})\cdot\vec{n} = 0$).
Core Formulas — Vector, Parametric & Cartesian Forms
  • Equations of a Line Through $A(a_1, a_2, a_3)$ Parallel to $\vec{b} = (b_1, b_2, b_3)^T$: $$\textbf{Vector: } \vec{r} = \vec{a} + t\vec{b} \iff \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \\ a_3 \end{pmatrix} + t\begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ b_3 \end{pmatrix}$$ $$\textbf{Parametric: } \begin{cases} x = a_1 + tb_1 \\ y = a_2 + tb_2 \\ z = a_3 + tb_3 \end{cases} \qquad \textbf{Cartesian: } \frac{x - a_1}{b_1} = \frac{y - a_2}{b_2} = \frac{z - a_3}{b_3} \; (= t)$$
  • Parametric Vector Equation of a Plane (Two Direction Vectors $\vec{d}_1, \vec{d}_2$): $$\vec{r} = \vec{a} + t_1\vec{d}_1 + t_2\vec{d}_2 \quad (t_1, t_2 \in \mathbb{R})$$ If the plane passes through three noncollinear points $A, B, C$, take $\vec{a} = \overrightarrow{OA}$, $\vec{d}_1 = \overrightarrow{AB}$, and $\vec{d}_2 = \overrightarrow{AC}$.
  • Normal & Cartesian Equation of a Plane with Normal $\vec{n} = (n_1, n_2, n_3)^T$: $$(\vec{r} - \vec{a}) \cdot \vec{n} = 0 \iff \vec{r} \cdot \vec{n} = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{n} = d \iff n_1x + n_2y + n_3z = d$$ Note: Given two spanning vectors $\vec{d}_1$ and $\vec{d}_2$ in the plane, a normal vector is $\vec{n} = \vec{d}_1 \times \vec{d}_2$.
Derivation — Equivalence of Spanning-Vector Form and Normal Form of a Plane

Suppose $R(x, y, z)$ lies on the plane through $A(\vec{a})$ with normal vector $\vec{n} = \begin{pmatrix} n_1 \\ n_2 \\ n_3 \end{pmatrix}$. Since the displacement vector $\overrightarrow{AR} = \vec{r} - \vec{a}$ lies completely inside the plane and $\vec{n}$ is perpendicular to every line in the plane:

$$\overrightarrow{AR} \perp \vec{n} \implies (\vec{r} - \vec{a}) \cdot \vec{n} = 0 \implies \vec{r} \cdot \vec{n} = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{n}$$

Writing $\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix}$ and setting the constant scalar $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{n} = d$ yields the Cartesian equation $n_1x + n_2y + n_3z = d$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 16, 18, 19, 20 & 22

Example 16 (Vector to Cartesian Equation of a Line): Find the Cartesian equation of the line with vector equation $\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} + t\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$.

Show step-by-step solution

Writing $\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix}$ gives the parametric equations:

$$\begin{cases} x = 1 + 3t \implies t = \dfrac{x - 1}{3} \\[4pt] y = 4 + 2t \implies t = \dfrac{y - 4}{2} \\[4pt] z = -1 + 5t \implies t = \dfrac{z + 1}{5} \end{cases}$$

Equating the three expressions for $t$ gives the Cartesian equation:

Answer: $\dfrac{x - 1}{3} = \dfrac{y - 4}{2} = \dfrac{z + 1}{5}$.

Example 18 & 19 (Vector & Cartesian Equation of a Plane Through Three Points): Find the vector equation and Cartesian equation of the plane containing points $M(2, 2, -2)$, $N(1, -1, 3)$, and $P(4, 0, 2)$.

Show step-by-step solution

Step 1 (Vector Equation): Choose $\vec{a} = \overrightarrow{OM} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ and form two in-plane direction vectors from $M$:

$$\vec{d}_1 = \overrightarrow{MN} = \overrightarrow{ON} - \overrightarrow{OM} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2 \\ -1 - 2 \\ 3 - (-2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \vec{d}_2 = \overrightarrow{MP} = \overrightarrow{OP} - \overrightarrow{OM} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 - 2 \\ 0 - 2 \\ 2 - (-2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$$

Thus the parametric vector equation of the plane is:

$$\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} + t_1\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix} + t_2\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 2 (Cartesian Equation by Eliminating $t_1, t_2$): From components:

$$(1)\; x = 2 - t_1 + 2t_2, \qquad (2)\; y = 2 - 3t_1 - 2t_2, \qquad (3)\; z = -2 + 5t_1 + 4t_2$$

Adding $(1) + (2)$ eliminates $t_2$: $x + y = 4 - 4t_1$ $(4)$.
Multiplying $(2)$ by $2$ and adding $(3)$ also eliminates $t_2$: $2y + z = 2 - t_1$ $(5)$.
Eliminating $t_1$ between $(4)$ and $4 \times (5)$: $(x + y) - 4(2y + z) = 4 - 8 \implies x - 7y - 4z = -4$.
(Alternative check via normal vector: $\vec{n} = \vec{d}_1 \times \vec{d}_2 = (-2, 14, 8)^T = -2(1, -7, -4)^T$, so $x - 7y - 4z = 2 - 7(2) - 4(-2) = -4$.)

Answer: Vector form $\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} + t_1\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix} + t_2\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$; Cartesian form $x - 7y - 4z = -4$.

Example 20 (Testing Whether Four Points Are Coplanar): Determine whether the points $A(3, -1, 4)$, $B(2, 1, 1)$, $C(4, 3, 1)$, and $D(-3, 1, 4)$ lie in the same plane.

Show step-by-step solution

First form the plane containing $A, B, C$ using $\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$:

$$\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} + t_1\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} + t_2\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$$

Test if $D(-3, 1, 4)$ satisfies this equation:

$$(1)\; 3 - t_1 + t_2 = -3 \implies -t_1 + t_2 = -6$$ $$(2)\; -1 + 2t_1 + 4t_2 = 1 \implies 2t_1 + 4t_2 = 2$$ $$(3)\; 4 - 3t_1 - 3t_2 = 4 \implies -3t_1 - 3t_2 = 0$$

Solving $(1)$ and $(2)$ gives $2(-t_1 + t_2) + (2t_1 + 4t_2) = -12 + 2 \implies 6t_2 = -10 \implies t_2 = -\frac{5}{3}$ and $t_1 = \frac{13}{3}$. Substituting into the left side of $(3)$ gives $4 - 3\left(\frac{13}{3}\right) - 3\left(-\frac{5}{3}\right) = -4 \neq 4$.

Answer: Since equation $(3)$ is not satisfied, $D$ does not lie on the same plane as $A, B,$ and $C$.

Example 22 (Plane from Normal Vector and Point): Vector $\vec{n} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ is perpendicular to the plane containing $A(1, -3, 2)$.
(a) Write an equation of the plane in the form $\vec{r}\cdot\vec{n} = d$.   (b) Find the Cartesian equation of the plane.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Compute $d = \vec{a}\cdot\vec{n} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = 1(2) + (-3)(4) + 2(-2) = 2 - 12 - 4 = -14$. Thus:

$$\vec{r} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = -14$$

(b) Substituting $\vec{r} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix}$ gives $2x + 4y - 2z = -14$ (or $x + 2y - z = -7$).

Answer: (a) $\vec{r}\cdot\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = -14$, (b) $2x + 4y - 2z = -14$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Confusing Line Direction Vector with Plane Normal Vector: A line in 3D is defined by a single parallel direction vector $\vec{b}$ ($\vec{r} = \vec{a} + t\vec{b}$), whereas the single-vector equation of a plane uses a perpendicular normal vector $\vec{n}$ ($\vec{r}\cdot\vec{n} = \vec{a}\cdot\vec{n}$).
  • Reading $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ from Non-Standard Cartesian Line Equations: In $\frac{2 - x}{3} = \frac{y + 4}{2} = \frac{z - 1}{5}$, rewrite $2 - x = -(x - 2)$ as $\frac{x - 2}{-3}$ first so the direction vector is $(-3, 2, 5)^T$, not $(3, 2, 5)^T$!
Exam Tip — Instant Normal Vector via Cross Product

Even when solving for the Cartesian equation of a plane $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + t_1\vec{d}_1 + t_2\vec{d}_2$ by eliminating $t_1$ and $t_2$, always compute $\vec{n} = \vec{d}_1 \times \vec{d}_2$ on the side as a 10-second check: the coefficients of $x, y, z$ in your final Cartesian equation must be proportional to the components of $\vec{d}_1 \times \vec{d}_2$!

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Equation of a Line (မျဉ်းဖြောင့်ညီမျှခြင်း): $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + t\vec{b} \iff \dfrac{x - a_1}{b_1} = \dfrac{y - a_2}{b_2} = \dfrac{z - a_3}{b_3}$.
  • Parametric Plane Equation (ပြင်ညီ၏ ဗက်တာညီမျှခြင်း): $\vec{r} = \vec{a} + t_1\vec{d}_1 + t_2\vec{d}_2$.
  • Normal & Cartesian Plane Equation (နော်မယ်ဗက်တာသုံး ပြင်ညီညီမျှခြင်း): $\vec{r}\cdot\vec{n} = \vec{a}\cdot\vec{n} = d \iff n_1x + n_2y + n_3z = d$.

Concept — Combinations (Unordered Selections)

A combination (တွဲဖက်မှု / ရွေးချယ်မှု) is a selection of objects in which the order of selection does not matter. For example, choosing a 3-person committee $\{A, B, C\}$ from 4 people $\{A, B, C, D\}$ is a single combination, even though those same 3 people can be lined up in $3! = 6$ different permutations ($ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA$).

The number of combinations of $n$ distinct objects taken $r$ at a time ($0 \le r \le n$) is denoted by ${}^nC_r$.

Core Formulas — The Combination Formula ${}^nC_r$ & Symmetry Identities
  • Relation Between Permutations and Combinations: Every combination of $r$ objects can be ordered internally in $r!$ ways, so: $${}^nC_r \cdot r! = {}^nP_r \implies {}^nC_r = \frac{{}^nP_r}{r!}$$
  • Factorial & Practical Calculation Forms ($0 \le r \le n$): $${}^nC_r = \frac{n!}{r!\,(n - r)!} = \frac{n(n - 1)(n - 2)\cdots(n - r + 1)}{r(r - 1)\cdots 2 \cdot 1}$$
  • Fundamental Identities & Symmetry Law: $${}^nC_0 = 1, \qquad {}^nC_n = 1, \qquad {}^nC_1 = n, \qquad {}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n - r}$$
Proof — Why ${}^nC_r = \dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n - r)!}$ and ${}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n - r}$

1. Quotient Proof: To form an ordered permutation of $r$ objects from $n$ distinct objects (${}^nP_r$ ways), we can first select an unordered subset of $r$ objects (${}^nC_r$ ways) and then arrange those $r$ chosen objects in order ($r!$ ways). By the Multiplication Principle:

$${}^nP_r = {}^nC_r \times r! \implies {}^nC_r = \frac{{}^nP_r}{r!} = \frac{n!}{r!\,(n - r)!}$$

2. Symmetry Proof (${}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n - r}$): Choosing $r$ objects to include in a group automatically leaves behind $n - r$ objects to exclude. Algebraically, replacing $r$ by $n - r$ gives:

$${}^nC_{n - r} = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!\,[n - (n - r)]!} = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!\,r!} = {}^nC_r \quad \blacksquare$$
Interactive Demo — Combinations ${}^nC_r$ vs. Permutations ${}^nP_r$ & Symmetry ${}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n-r}$

Select $n$ and $r$ to inspect the $n$-row of combination values ${}^nC_0, {}^nC_1, \dots, {}^nC_n$. Notice how ${}^nC_r$ and its mirror twin ${}^nC_{n-r}$ are always equal, and how multiplying ${}^nC_r$ by $r!$ recovers ${}^nP_r$.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 15, 16, 17, 18 & 19

Example 15 (Using Symmetry ${}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n-r}$ to Compute Large Combinations): Evaluate ${}^{21}C_{19}$.

Show step-by-step solution

Using ${}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n - r}$ with $n = 21$ and $r = 19$:

$${}^{21}C_{19} = {}^{21}C_{21 - 19} = {}^{21}C_2 = \frac{21 \cdot 20}{2 \cdot 1} = 210$$

Answer: $210$.

Example 16 (Multi-Group Selection — Multiplication of Combinations): A music class consists of $5$ piano players, $7$ guitarists, and $4$ violinists. A band of $1$ piano player, $3$ guitarists, and $2$ violinists must be chosen to play at a school concert. In how many ways can the band be chosen?

Show step-by-step solution
  • Choose $1$ piano player from $5$: ${}^5C_1 = 5$ ways.
  • Choose $3$ guitarists from $7$: ${}^7C_3 = \dfrac{7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 35$ ways.
  • Choose $2$ violinists from $4$: ${}^4C_2 = \dfrac{4 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 1} = 6$ ways.

By the Multiplication Principle, total ways $= {}^5C_1 \cdot {}^7C_3 \cdot {}^4C_2 = 5 \times 35 \times 6 = 1050$.

Answer: $1050$ ways.

Example 17 (Same-Color Selection — Addition of Combinations): Suppose there are $4$ black cars and $7$ white cars, all distinguishable. In how many ways can $3$ cars of the same color be chosen?

Show step-by-step solution

To be of the same color, the $3$ cars must be either all black OR all white:

$$\text{Total ways} = {}^4C_3 + {}^7C_3 = \frac{4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} + \frac{7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 4 + 35 = 39$$

Answer: $39$ ways.

Example 18 (Distributing Books Among Three Children): There are $6$ different books. In how many ways can the books be distributed among three children if the youngest receives $3$ books, the middle child receives $1$ book, and the eldest receives $2$ books?

Show step-by-step solution
  • For the youngest child, choose $3$ books out of $6$: ${}^6C_3 = \dfrac{6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 20$ ways.
  • For the middle child, choose $1$ book from the remaining $3$ books: ${}^3C_1 = 3$ ways.
  • For the eldest child, choose $2$ books from the remaining $2$ books: ${}^2C_2 = 1$ way.

By the Multiplication Principle, total ways $= 20 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 = 60$.

Answer: $60$ ways.

Example 19 (Inclusion / Exclusion of a Specific Item): In how many ways can $4$ fruits be selected out of $9$ distinct fruits so as always to:
(a) include the largest fruit?   (b) exclude the smallest fruit?

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Always include the largest fruit: Since the largest fruit is already chosen ($1$ way), we only need to select the remaining $4 - 1 = 3$ fruits from the other $9 - 1 = 8$ fruits:

$${}^8C_3 = \frac{8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 56\text{ ways}$$

(b) Always exclude the smallest fruit: Set the smallest fruit aside completely and select all $4$ fruits from the remaining $8$ fruits:

$${}^8C_4 = \frac{8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5}{4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 70\text{ ways}$$

Answer: (a) $56$ ways, (b) $70$ ways.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Confusing ${}^nP_r$ and ${}^nC_r$: If the selected items are given distinct roles/positions (e.g., President, Treasurer, Secretary, or a 4-digit PIN), order matters $\implies$ use ${}^nP_r$. If they form an unranked group (e.g., a committee, a band of guitarists, a subset of fruits, or vertices of a triangle), order does not matter $\implies$ use ${}^nC_r$.
  • Forgetting to Reduce Both $n$ and $r$ When an Item Is Pre-Included: When $k$ specific items must be included, reduce the pool size to $n - k$ AND reduce the number of items still to be chosen to $r - k$, giving ${}^{n-k}C_{r-k}$.
Exam Tip — Geometric Counting with Combinations (Exercise 5.3 #4 & #5)

Given $n$ points in a plane with no $3$ collinear: every pair of points determines a unique line (${}^nC_2$ lines), and every triple of points determines a unique triangle (${}^nC_3$ triangles). Similarly, in an $n$-team league where every team plays every other team once, there are ${}^nC_2 = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ games!

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Combination Formula (တွဲဖက်မှုပုံသေနည်း): ${}^nC_r = \dfrac{{}^nP_r}{r!} = \dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n - r)!}$ counts unordered selections of $r$ objects from $n$.
  • Symmetry Identity (အချိုးညီဂုဏ်သတ္တိ): ${}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n - r}$, and ${}^nC_0 = {}^nC_n = 1$.
  • Mandatory Inclusion vs. Exclusion: Including $1$ fixed item gives ${}^{n-1}C_{r-1}$; excluding $1$ fixed item gives ${}^{n-1}C_r$.

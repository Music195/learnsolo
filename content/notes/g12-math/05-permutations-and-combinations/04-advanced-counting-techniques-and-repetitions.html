Concept — Advanced Counting Techniques: Repetitions, Exclusion & Subsets

Section 5.4 equips us with four powerful techniques for solving complex counting problems:

  1. Permutations with Repetitions (ထပ်နေသော အရာဝတ္ထုများပါဝင်သည့် အစီအစဉ်): Arranging $n$ objects when some of them are identical (indistinguishable), such as the letters in KEENNESS or EXCELLENCE.
  2. The Exclusion Principle (ဖယ်ထုတ်ခြင်း နိယာမ — Complementary Counting): Counting arrangements where a property is prohibited by subtracting the unwanted arrangements from the unrestricted total.
  3. Counting Subsets of a Finite Set (အစုပိုင်းများ ရေတွက်ခြင်း): Using the binary "Include / Exclude" decision for each element to show that an $n$-element set has $2^n$ subsets.
  4. Block ("Tie-Together") & Gap/Template Methods: Grouping items that must stay together into a single super-unit, or setting up fixed spacing templates.
Core Formulas — Multinomial Permutations, Exclusion & Subset Sum
  • Permutations of $n$ Objects with Repetitions: Given $n$ objects where $n_1$ are identical of the $1^{\text{st}}$ kind, $n_2$ of the $2^{\text{nd}}$ kind, $\dots$, and $n_r$ of the $r^{\text{th}}$ kind ($n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_r = n$), the number of distinct permutations of all $n$ objects is: $$\frac{n!}{n_1!\,n_2!\,\cdots\,n_r!}$$
  • The Exclusion Principle: $$\text{Count}(\text{Allowed}) = \text{Count}(\text{Total Unrestricted}) - \text{Count}(\text{Prohibited / Unwanted})$$
  • Number of Subsets of an $n$-Element Set: For any finite set $X$ with $n \ge 0$ elements: $${}^nC_0 + {}^nC_1 + {}^nC_2 + \cdots + {}^nC_n = 2^n$$
Proof — Two Derivations: Repeated-Letter Permutations & The $2^n$ Subset Identity

1. Why Repeated Letters Divide by $n_1!n_2!\cdots n_r!$: Consider the $8$-letter word KEENNESS ($1$ K, $3$ E's, $2$ N's, $2$ S's). To place these letters into $8$ positions:

  • Choose $1$ position out of $8$ for K in ${}^8C_1$ ways;
  • Choose $3$ positions out of the remaining $7$ for the three E's in ${}^7C_3$ ways;
  • Choose $2$ positions out of the remaining $4$ for the two N's in ${}^4C_2$ ways;
  • Choose $2$ positions out of the last $2$ for the two S's in ${}^2C_2$ ways.

Multiplying these combinations gives:

$${}^8C_1 \cdot {}^7C_3 \cdot {}^4C_2 \cdot {}^2C_2 = \frac{8!}{1!\,7!} \cdot \frac{7!}{3!\,4!} \cdot \frac{4!}{2!\,2!} \cdot \frac{2!}{2!\,0!} = \frac{8!}{1!\,3!\,2!\,2!}$$

2. Double Counting Proof of ${}^nC_0 + {}^nC_1 + \cdots + {}^nC_n = 2^n$: Every subset of an $n$-element set $X$ has size $r \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}$. Since there are ${}^nC_r$ subsets of size $r$, the Addition Principle gives ${}^nC_0 + {}^nC_1 + \cdots + {}^nC_n$ total subsets. On the other hand, when constructing a subset element-by-element, each of the $n$ elements has exactly $2$ choices (Include or Exclude). By the Multiplication Principle, there are $\underbrace{2 \cdot 2 \cdots 2}_{n \text{ times}} = 2^n$ subsets.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 & 26

Example 20 & 21 (Permutations with Repetitions & The Exclusion Principle):
(a) In how many ways can all the letters of the word EXCELLENCE be arranged?
(b) How many permutations of the letters of PROGRAM do not end in (i) two R's? (ii) MAP?

Show step-by-step solution

(a) EXCELLENCE has $10$ letters: four E's, two C's, two L's, one X, and one N. Thus:

$$\text{Number of permutations} = \frac{10!}{4!\,1!\,2!\,2!\,1!} = \frac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4!}{4! \cdot 2 \cdot 2} = 37800$$

(b) PROGRAM has $7$ letters with two R's and all other letters distinct, so the total unrestricted number of permutations is $\dfrac{7!}{2!} = 2520$.

  • (i) Not ending in RR: If the last two positions are fixed as RR, the remaining $5$ distinct letters (P, O, G, A, M) can be arranged in $5! = 120$ ways. By the Exclusion Principle, $2520 - 120 = 2400$.
  • (ii) Not ending in MAP: If the last three positions are fixed as MAP, the remaining $4$ letters (R, R, O, G, which contain two R's) can be arranged in $\dfrac{4!}{2!} = 12$ ways. By the Exclusion Principle, $2520 - 12 = 2508$.

Answer: (a) $37800$; (b)(i) $2400$, (b)(ii) $2508$.

Example 22 (Counting Subsets with "At Most" and "At Least"): If $A$ is a set containing $9$ distinct elements, how many subsets of $A$ contain:
(a) at most $2$ elements?   (b) at least $3$ elements?

Show step-by-step solution

(a) At most $2$ elements ($0, 1,$ or $2$ elements):

$${}^9C_0 + {}^9C_1 + {}^9C_2 = 1 + 9 + \frac{9 \cdot 8}{2 \cdot 1} = 1 + 9 + 36 = 46$$

(b) At least $3$ elements: The total number of subsets of $A$ is $2^9 = 512$. By the Exclusion Principle, subtract the subsets with at most $2$ elements:

$$2^9 - 46 = 512 - 46 = 466$$

Answer: (a) $46$ subsets, (b) $466$ subsets.

Example 23 (4-Digit Even Numbers Greater Than $4000$): How many $4$-digit even numbers greater than $4000$ can be formed using the digits $1, 2, 3, 4,$ and $5$ without repeating any digit?

Show step-by-step solution

To be greater than $4000$, the first digit must be $4$ or $5$; to be even, the last digit must be $2$ or $4$. Because $4$ satisfies both conditions, we split by the choice of the first digit:

  • Case 1 (First digit is $4$): Since $4$ is already used as the first digit, the last (even) digit must be $2$ ($1$ way). The $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $3^{\text{rd}}$ digits are filled from the remaining $3$ digits in $3 \times 2 = 6$ ways $\implies 1 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 6$ numbers.
  • Case 2 (First digit is $5$): The last (even) digit can be either $2$ or $4$ ($2$ ways). The $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $3^{\text{rd}}$ digits are filled from the remaining $3$ digits in $3 \times 2 = 6$ ways $\implies 1 \times 3 \times 2 \times 2 = 12$ numbers.

Total numbers $= 6 + 12 = 18$.

Answer: $18$ numbers.

Example 24, 25 & 26 (Grouping Blocks & Spacing Templates):
(a) (Ex 24) In how many ways can $2$ different chemistry books, $4$ different mathematics books, and $3$ different physics books be arranged on a shelf if books of the same subject must stay together?
(b) (Ex 25) How many permutations of the letters $S, U, N, D, A, Y$ have the two vowels ($U, A$) placed together?
(c) (Ex 26) Find the number of permutations of all letters of INTERNET such that there are exactly $4$ letters between the two T's.

Show step-by-step solution

(a) Subject Blocks Together: Within each subject block, the books can be arranged in $2!\cdot 4!\cdot 3! = 2 \cdot 24 \cdot 6 = 288$ ways. The $3$ subject blocks themselves can be ordered in $3! = 6$ ways. Total $= 6 \times 288 = 1728$ ways.

(b) Vowels Together in SUNDAY: Tie $U$ and $A$ into a single block $[UA]$. Together with $S, N, D, Y$, there are $5$ units, which can be arranged in $5! = 120$ ways. Inside the vowel block, $U$ and $A$ can be ordered in $2! = 2$ ways ($UA$ or $AU$). Total $= 120 \times 2 = 240$ ways.

(c) Exactly $4$ Letters Between the Two T's in INTERNET: The $8$-letter pattern must take one of $3$ template positions for the two T's:

$$\text{Form 1: }\; \mathbf{T}\;*\;*\;*\;*\;\mathbf{T}\;*\;* \qquad \text{Form 2: }\; *\;\mathbf{T}\;*\;*\;*\;*\;\mathbf{T}\;* \qquad \text{Form 3: }\; *\;*\;\mathbf{T}\;*\;*\;*\;*\;\mathbf{T}$$

In each form, the $6$ starred positions ($*$) are filled by the remaining $6$ letters ($2$ N's, $2$ E's, $1$ I, $1$ R) in $\dfrac{6!}{2!\,2!} = \dfrac{720}{4} = 180$ ways. Across all $3$ forms, total permutations $= 3 \times 180 = 540$.

Answer: (a) $1728$, (b) $240$, (c) $540$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Overlapping Digit Restrictions (Example 23): When both the first digit ($4$ or $5$) and the last digit ($2$ or $4$) can use the digit $4$, you cannot simply multiply $2 \times 3 \times 2 \times 2$! Always split into disjoint cases depending on whether the shared digit ($4$) is used in the first slot or not.
  • Forgetting Internal Permutations of Tied Blocks: When $k$ distinct items are tied together as a single block, remember to multiply by $k!$ for the arrangements inside the block (unless the items inside the block are identical!).
Exam Tip — Spotting Exclusion Principle Keywords

Phrases like "at least one", "not ending in...", "have repeated digits", or "not all vowels together" are classic signals to use the Exclusion Principle: compute the unrestricted total first, then subtract the single complementary case.

Key Takeaways (မှတ်သားရန် အချက်များ)
  • Permutations with Repetitions (ထပ်နေသော အရာများစီစဉ်ခြင်း): $\dfrac{n!}{n_1!\,n_2!\,\cdots\,n_r!}$.
  • Exclusion Principle (ဖယ်ထုတ်ခြင်း နိယာမ): $\text{Valid} = \text{Total} - \text{Unwanted}$.
  • Subsets of an $n$-Element Set (အစုပိုင်း အရေအတွက်): ${}^nC_0 + {}^nC_1 + \cdots + {}^nC_n = 2^n$.

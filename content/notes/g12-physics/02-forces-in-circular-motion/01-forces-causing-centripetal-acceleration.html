Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Concept — Why a Net Force Is Required for Circular Motion

According to Newton's first law of motion, an object naturally continues in a straight line at constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external force. To make an object deviate from a straight-line path and move in a circle, a net force must continuously pull it toward the center of curvature:

  • Centripetal Force ($F_c$): By Newton's second law ($F_{\text{net}} = ma$), the inward centripetal acceleration $a_c$ requires an inward net force of magnitude $F_c = m a_c$. Centripetal force is not a new kind of physical force; it is the role played by real physical forces present in the system.
  • Physical Sources of Centripetal Force:
    • Tension force ($T$): A string whirling a ball exerts tension directed along the string toward the center.
    • Gravitational force ($F_G$): Gravitational attraction by the Sun on orbiting planets, or by the Earth on the Moon and satellites.
    • Electric force ($F_e$): Electrostatic Coulomb attraction exerted by the positive nucleus on revolving electrons.
    • Friction force ($F_f$): Static friction between tires and the road surface allows a car to steer around a flat curve.
    • Normal force component ($F_N \sin\theta$): On a banked road, the horizontal component of the road's normal reaction supplies the required inward force.
    • Aerodynamic lift component ($F_L \sin\theta$): When an airplane banks its wings at angle $\theta$, the horizontal component of lift steers the plane in a horizontal circle.
Core Formulas — Centripetal Force and Banking Angle
$$F_c = m a_c = \frac{m v^2}{r} = m r \omega^2 \qquad\qquad \tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{r g}$$
  • $F_c$ = centripetal force ($\mathrm{N}$), always directed radially inward toward the center
  • $m$ = mass of the revolving body ($\mathrm{kg}$)
  • $v$ = linear speed ($\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$); $\omega$ = angular velocity ($\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$); $r$ = radius of circular path ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $\theta$ = angle of banking (or airplane wing tilt) measured from the horizontal ($\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg}$)

Vertical Circular Motion (e.g. whirling a bucket on a string of length $r$):

  • At lowest point (bottom): Tension pulls up, gravity pulls down $\implies F_{\text{net}} = T - mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \implies T = mg + \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ (maximum tension).
  • At highest point (top): Both tension and gravity pull downward $\implies F_{\text{net}} = T + mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \implies T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} - mg$ (minimum tension).
Derivation — Ideal Banking Angle for a Road Curve

Consider a vehicle of mass $m$ rounding a curve of radius $r$ on a road banked at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal without relying on friction:

The normal reaction force $F_N$ acts perpendicular to the inclined road surface. Resolving $F_N$ into vertical and horizontal components:

  1. Vertical equilibrium (no vertical acceleration): $$F_N \cos\theta - mg = 0 \implies F_N \cos\theta = mg \quad (1)$$
  2. Horizontal circular motion (horizontal component provides centripetal force): $$F_N \sin\theta = F_c = \frac{m v^2}{r} \quad (2)$$

Dividing equation (2) by equation (1):

$$\frac{F_N \sin\theta}{F_N \cos\theta} = \frac{mv^2 / r}{mg} \implies \tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{r g}$$

At this designed design speed $v = \sqrt{r g \tan\theta}$, the vehicle rounds the curve smoothly with zero lateral tire scrub or reliance on road friction.

Worked Examples — Textbook Problems

Example 2.1 (Textbook p. 16): A jet plane is flying around an airport with a speed of $800\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$ along a circular path with a radius of $2\ \mathrm{km}$. At what angle must the wings of the plane be banked with the vertical?

Show step-by-step solution

Convert units to SI:

$$v = 800\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}} = 800 \times \frac{1000\ \mathrm{m}}{3600\ \mathrm{s}} = 222.22\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$ $$r = 2\ \mathrm{km} = 2000\ \mathrm{m}, \quad g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$$

The banking angle $\theta$ satisfies:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg} = \frac{(222.22)^2}{2000 \times 9.8} = \frac{49381.7}{19600} \approx 2.5195$$ $$\theta = \arctan(2.5195) \approx 68.35^\circ$$

Answer: The wings must be banked at an angle of $68.35^\circ$ with the horizontal.

Example 2.2 (Textbook p. 17): A $60\ \mathrm{cm}$ rope is tied to the handle of a bucket which is then whirled in a vertical circle. The mass of the bucket is $3\ \mathrm{kg}$. If the tension of the rope at the lowest point in its path is $50\ \mathrm{N}$, find the speed of the bucket at that point ($g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$).

Show step-by-step solution

Given: $m = 3\ \mathrm{kg}$, $r = 60\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.6\ \mathrm{m}$, $T = 50\ \mathrm{N}$.

At the lowest point, tension $T$ acts upward toward the center and weight $w = mg$ acts downward:

$$w = mg = 3 \times 9.8 = 29.4\ \mathrm{N}$$

The net inward force provides the centripetal acceleration:

$$F_{\text{net}} = T - w = 50 - 29.4 = 20.6\ \mathrm{N}$$

Set $F_{\text{net}} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$:

$$20.6 = \frac{3 v^2}{0.6} = 5 v^2 \implies v^2 = \frac{20.6}{5} = 4.12 \implies v = \sqrt{4.12} \approx 2.03\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

Answer: $v \approx 2.03\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

Reviewed Exercise 1 (Textbook p. 17): What is the banking angle for an expressway off-ramp curve of radius $50\ \mathrm{m}$ at a limiting speed of $50\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$ ($g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$)?

Show step-by-step solution

Convert speed to SI:

$$v = 50\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}} = 50 \times \frac{1000}{3600} = 13.89\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

Compute the banking angle:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg} = \frac{(13.89)^2}{50 \times 9.8} = \frac{192.9}{490} \approx 0.3937$$ $$\theta = \arctan(0.3937) \approx 21.49^\circ$$

Answer: The banking angle is approximately $21.5^\circ$.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Treating Centripetal Force as a Separate Physical Force: Never draw an extra force called "$F_c$" on a free-body diagram! Instead, identify the actual physical force (e.g. friction, normal force, tension, or gravity) whose component points toward the center.
  • Inverting the Vertical Circle Net Force: At the bottom of a vertical circle, the net force is $T - mg$, not $mg - T$. Tension must be strictly greater than weight to accelerate the object upward into its curved path.
  • Forgetting to Convert $\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$: Always multiply $\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$ by $\dfrac{1000}{3600} = \dfrac{5}{18}$ before inserting into $v^2 / (rg)$.
Key Takeaways
  • An object moving in a circle of radius $r$ at speed $v$ requires an inward net force $F_c = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} = mr\omega^2$.
  • Without this centripetal force, the object would fly off along the straight-line tangent by Newton's first law.
  • On a road banked at angle $\theta$, the horizontal component of the normal reaction provides the centripetal force: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{rg}$.

The Compound Microscope

Concept — Two-Stage Optical Magnification

A compound microscope uses a combination of two short-focal-length convex lenses to achieve much higher magnification than a single magnifying glass:

  • 1. Objective Lens ($L_o$): A lens of very short focal length ($f_o \sim \text{a few millimeters}$) and small aperture placed very close to the tiny specimen. The specimen is positioned just outside the principal focus ($u_o > f_o$). The objective forms a real, inverted, and magnified intermediate image ($I_1$) inside the microscope tube.
  • 2. Eyepiece Lens ($L_e$): A lens of relatively larger focal length ($f_e > f_o$) and larger aperture that acts as a simple magnifier. The tube is adjusted so that the intermediate image $I_1$ falls just within the eyepiece's focal point ($u_e \le f_e$). The eyepiece produces a final virtual, inverted (relative to the original specimen), and highly enlarged image ($I_2$).
  • Diffraction Limit and Electron Microscopes: Optical microscopes cannot resolve details smaller than approximately half the wavelength of visible light ($\sim 200\mathrm{nm}$). To overcome this barrier, electron microscopes utilize accelerated electron beams ($\lambda_e \sim 0.005\mathrm{nm}$) focused by electromagnetic coils:
    • Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM): Electrons transmit through ultra-thin slices of specimen, resolving atomic-scale cellular ultrastructure in 2D up to $1\,000\,000\times$.
    • Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM): An electron beam raster-scans the metal-coated specimen surface; detected secondary electrons produce dramatic, three-dimensional topographical surface images.
Core Formulas — Magnifying Power Derivations
$$M = m_o \times m_e = -\frac{v_o}{u_o} \left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right) \qquad\text{or}\qquad M \approx -\frac{L}{f_o} \cdot \frac{D}{f_e}$$
  • $M$ = total angular magnifying power (dimensionless; negative sign indicates final image is inverted)
  • $m_o = -\frac{v_o}{u_o}$ = linear magnification of the objective lens
  • $m_e = 1 + \frac{D}{f_e}$ = angular magnification of eyepiece when final image is at near point ($D = 25\mathrm{cm}$)
  • $m_e = \frac{D}{f_e}$ = angular magnification when viewed with relaxed eye at infinity ($\infty$)
  • $L = v_o + u_e$ = physical distance between objective and eyepiece lenses (tube length)
  • $D = 25\mathrm{cm} = 0.25\mathrm{m}$ = standard least distance of distinct vision

Textbook Worked Examples — Microscope Magnification

Example 7.3: A compound microscope consists of two thin converging lenses. The focal length of the objective is $0.8\text{ cm}$ and that of the eyepiece is $2.5\text{ cm}$. An object is placed $0.9\text{ cm}$ in front of the objective.
(i) Find the position of the intermediate image formed by the objective and calculate its linear magnification.
(ii) Find the total magnifying power of the microscope if the final image is formed at the near point ($D = 25\text{ cm}$).
(iii) Find the distance between the two lenses.

Solution:
Given: $f_o = 0.8\text{ cm}$, $u_o = 0.9\text{ cm}$, $f_e = 2.5\text{ cm}$, $D = 25\text{ cm}$.

(i) For the objective lens: $$\frac{1}{v_o} = \frac{1}{f_o} - \frac{1}{u_o} = \frac{1}{0.8} - \frac{1}{0.9} = \frac{0.9 - 0.8}{0.72} = \frac{0.1}{0.72} = \frac{1}{7.2}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies v_o = 7.2\text{ cm}$$ Linear magnification of objective: $$m_o = -\frac{v_o}{u_o} = -\frac{7.2\text{ cm}}{0.9\text{ cm}} = -8$$ (ii) Angular magnification of the eyepiece when final image is at near point: $$m_e = 1 + \frac{D}{f_e} = 1 + \frac{25\text{ cm}}{2.5\text{ cm}} = 1 + 10 = 11$$ Total magnifying power: $$M = m_o \times m_e = (-8) \times 11 = -88$$ (iii) Finding distance between lenses $L = v_o + u_e$:
For the eyepiece, the final image is virtual at $v_e = -25\text{ cm}$: $$\frac{1}{u_e} = \frac{1}{f_e} - \frac{1}{v_e} = \frac{1}{2.5} - \frac{1}{-25} = \frac{1}{2.5} + \frac{1}{25} = \frac{11}{25}\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies u_e = \frac{25}{11} \approx 2.27\text{ cm}$$ Total separation: $$L = v_o + u_e = 7.2\text{ cm} + 2.27\text{ cm} = 9.47\text{ cm}$$

Reviewed Exercises:

1. Why can the final image produced by a compound microscope not be projected directly onto a screen?
The final image formed by the eyepiece is a virtual image formed by diverging rays that appear to diverge from a point behind the eyepiece. Virtual images cannot be projected onto physical screens because real light rays do not actually intersect at the image location.

2. A compound microscope of total length $17\text{ cm}$ has an eyepiece of focal length $2.5\text{ cm}$ and an objective of focal length $0.5\text{ cm}$. Assuming the final image is at infinity, estimate the magnifying power.
When the final image is at infinity, $u_e = f_e = 2.5\text{ cm}$.
Intermediate image distance: $v_o = L - f_e = 17\text{ cm} - 2.5\text{ cm} = 14.5\text{ cm}$.
Since $f_o = 0.5\text{ cm}$ is small, $u_o \approx f_o = 0.5\text{ cm}$.
Magnifying power: $$M = -\frac{v_o}{u_o} \cdot \frac{D}{f_e} \approx -\frac{14.5\text{ cm}}{0.5\text{ cm}} \times \frac{25\text{ cm}}{2.5\text{ cm}} = -29 \times 10 = -290\times$$

Key Takeaways
  • The objective lens forms a real, magnified, inverted intermediate image.
  • The eyepiece acts as a simple magnifier to produce a final virtual, highly enlarged image.
  • Magnifying power at near point: $M = -\frac{v_o}{u_o}(1 + \frac{D}{f_e})$.
  • Electron microscopes (TEM and SEM) use short de Broglie wavelength electron beams to achieve magnifications up to $1\,000\,000\times$, overcoming optical diffraction limits.

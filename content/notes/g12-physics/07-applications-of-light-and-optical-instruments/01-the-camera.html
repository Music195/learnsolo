The Camera

Concept — Optical Principles of Photographic Recording

A camera is an optical instrument that captures and records optical images of objects onto photosensitive film or a solid-state digital sensor:

  • Image Formation: A converging (convex) lens gathers light rays from an external illuminated object and refracts them to form a real, inverted, and diminished image on the focal plane.
  • Essential Subsystems:
    • Convex Lens System: Focuses incident light rays onto the film or digital sensor array.
    • Aperture and Diaphragm: An adjustable iris diaphragm controls the diameter $D$ of the circular aperture. The relative aperture is quantified by the f-number: $$\text{f-number } (N) = \frac{f}{D}$$ A smaller f-number (e.g., $f/2.8$) corresponds to a wider aperture, admitting more light for dim conditions. A larger f-number (e.g., $f/16$) restricts light for bright scenes and yields greater depth of field.
    • Shutter: A mechanical or electronic curtain that opens for a calibrated exposure time (e.g., $1/1000\mathrm{s}$ to $1\mathrm{s}$). Shorter exposure times freeze fast motion.
    • Focusing Ring: Adjusts the image distance $v$ between lens and image plane to satisfy the thin lens equation for objects at varying distances $u$.
    • Recording Medium: Photographic silver halide emulsion film, or modern digital image sensors (CMOS/CCD arrays comprising millions of light-sensitive photodiode pixels).
Core Formulas — Camera Optics
$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v} \qquad\qquad m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{v}{u} \qquad\qquad N = \frac{f}{D}$$
  • $f$ = focal length of camera lens ($\mathrm{m}$ or $\mathrm{mm}$)
  • $u$ = object distance from lens optical center ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $v$ = image distance from lens to sensor plane ($\mathrm{m}$ or $\mathrm{mm}$)
  • $h_o, h_i$ = object height and image height ($\mathrm{m}$ or $\mathrm{mm}$)
  • $m$ = linear magnification ($|m| < 1$ for ordinary cameras)
  • $N$ = f-number; $D$ = entrance pupil aperture diameter ($\mathrm{mm}$)

Textbook Worked Examples — Focal Length and Exposure

Example 7.1: A nature photographer wishes to photograph a $28\text{ m}$ tall tree from a distance of $58\text{ m}$. What focal length lens should be used if the image is to completely fill the $24\text{ mm}$ height of the camera film?

Solution:
Given:
Object height $h_o = 28\text{ m}$, object distance $u = 58\text{ m}$.
Because the image formed by a camera is real and inverted: $h_i = -24\text{ mm} = -0.024\text{ m}$.

1. Finding image distance $v$ using magnification: $$m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{v}{u}$$ $$\frac{-0.024\text{ m}}{28\text{ m}} = -\frac{v}{58\text{ m}} \implies v = \frac{0.024 \times 58}{28} \approx 0.0497\text{ m} \approx 50\text{ mm}$$ 2. Applying the thin lens equation: $$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{58\text{ m}} + \frac{1}{0.0497\text{ m}} \approx 0.0172 + 20.12 = 20.137\text{ m}^{-1}$$ $$f = \frac{1}{20.137} \approx 0.0497\text{ m} \approx 50\text{ mm}$$ Observation: For distant objects ($u \gg f$), the image forms essentially at the principal focus ($v \approx f$). Thus, a standard $50\text{ mm}$ focal length lens is chosen.

Reviewed Exercise:

Why must a camera lens be moved further from the film to focus on a closer object?
From the thin lens equation rearranged for image distance: $$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u}$$ As an object approaches the camera, $u$ decreases, which causes the term $\frac{1}{u}$ to become larger, thereby making $\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u}$ smaller. A smaller $\frac{1}{v}$ implies a larger image distance $v$. Therefore, the lens must be racked forward away from the film or sensor to capture the sharp image plane.

Key Takeaways
  • A camera lens forms a real, inverted, diminished image on the sensor or film plane.
  • The aperture's f-number is $N = f/D$; smaller f-numbers admit more light.
  • Focusing on closer objects requires increasing the lens-to-sensor distance $v$.
  • For distant subjects ($u \gg f$), image distance approximately equals focal length ($v \approx f$).

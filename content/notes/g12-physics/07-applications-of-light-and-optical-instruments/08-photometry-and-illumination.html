Photometry and Illumination

Concept — Measuring Visible Light Energy

Photometry is the branch of optics that measures the power of electromagnetic radiation in the visible spectrum ($380\mathrm{nm}\text{ to }750\mathrm{nm}$) weighted according to the human eye's spectral sensitivity (which peaks at $555\mathrm{nm}$ in bright daylight):

  • The Four Fundamental Photometric Quantities:
    • 1. Solid Angle ($\omega$ or $\Omega$): The three-dimensional angular cone subtended at the center of a sphere of radius $r$ by a surface area $A$ on the sphere: $$\omega = \frac{A}{r^2} \quad (\text{SI unit: steradian, }\mathrm{sr})$$ The total solid angle enclosing any point in three-dimensional space is $\omega_{\text{total}} = \frac{4\pi r^2}{r^2} = 4\pi\mathrm{sr} \approx 12.57\mathrm{sr}$.
    • 2. Luminous Flux ($\Phi$ or $F$): The total rate of luminous energy emitted in all directions by a light source as perceived by the eye: $$\text{SI unit: lumen }(\mathrm{lm})$$ For a uniform isotropic point source of luminous intensity $I$: $\Phi = 4\pi I$.
    • 3. Luminous Intensity ($I$): The luminous flux radiated by a point source per unit solid angle in a specified direction: $$I = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta \omega} \quad (\text{SI unit: candela, }\mathrm{cd} = \mathrm{lm\,sr^{-1}})$$
    • 4. Illuminance ($E$ or $E_v$): The areal density of luminous flux incident upon a receiving surface: $$E = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta A} \quad (\text{SI unit: lux, }\mathrm{lx} = \mathrm{lm\,m^{-2}})$$
  • Laws of Illumination:
    • Inverse Square Law: Illuminance on a normal surface decreases inversely with the square of the distance from the point source: $E = \frac{I}{r^2}$.
    • Lambert's Cosine Law: If light strikes a surface whose normal is inclined at an angle $\theta$ to the incident ray, the illuminance is reduced by $\cos\theta$: $$E = \frac{I \cos\theta}{r^2}$$
Core Formulas — Photometry Equations
$$I = \frac{\Phi}{\omega} \qquad\qquad \Phi = 4\pi I \qquad\qquad E = \frac{I \cos\theta}{r^2} \qquad\qquad \text{Efficacy} = \frac{\Phi}{P_{\text{electric}}}\ (\mathrm{lm\,W^{-1}})$$
  • $\Phi$ = total luminous flux ($\mathrm{lm}$)
  • $I$ = luminous intensity in candelas ($\mathrm{cd}$)
  • $\omega$ = solid angle ($\mathrm{sr}$)
  • $E$ = surface illuminance ($\mathrm{lx} = \mathrm{lm\,m^{-2}}$)
  • $r$ = distance from source to surface point ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $\theta$ = angle between incident light ray and surface normal vector
  • Luminous efficacy = ratio of luminous flux output to electric power consumed ($\mathrm{lm\,W^{-1}}$)

Textbook Worked Examples — Luminous Flux and Illuminance

Example 7.7: A $10\text{ W}$ energy-efficient fluorescent tube emits light uniformly in all directions with a luminous intensity of $35\text{ cd}$.
(i) Find the total luminous flux emitted by the lamp.
(ii) Calculate the luminous efficacy of the lamp in lumens per watt.

Solution:
Given: Luminous intensity $I = 35\text{ cd}$, Electric power $P = 10\text{ W}$.

(i) Total luminous flux radiated across all $4\pi\text{ steradians}$: $$\Phi = 4\pi I = 4 \times 3.1416 \times 35\text{ cd} \approx 439.8\text{ lm} \approx 440\text{ lm}$$ (ii) Luminous efficacy: $$\text{Efficacy} = \frac{\Phi}{P} = \frac{440\text{ lm}}{10\text{ W}} = 44\text{ lm\,W^{-1}}$$

Example 7.8: A street lamp of luminous intensity $200\text{ cd}$ is mounted at a height of $4\text{ m}$ above the ground. Determine the illuminance at:
(i) A point $A$ directly underneath the lamp on the ground.
(ii) A point $B$ on the ground located $3\text{ m}$ horizontally away from point $A$.

Solution:
Given: $I = 200\text{ cd}$, $h = 4\text{ m}$.

(i) At point $A$ directly beneath the lamp ($\theta = 0^\circ, \cos\theta = 1, r = h = 4\text{ m}$): $$E_A = \frac{I \cos 0^\circ}{h^2} = \frac{200\text{ cd}}{(4\text{ m})^2} = \frac{200}{16} = 12.5\text{ lx}$$ (ii) At point $B$ ($3\text{ m}$ horizontal offset):
Slant distance from lamp to point $B$: $$r = \sqrt{h^2 + x^2} = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ m}$$ Angle of incidence with the surface normal: $$\cos\theta = \frac{h}{r} = \frac{4\text{ m}}{5\text{ m}} = 0.80$$ Applying Lambert's cosine law: $$E_B = \frac{I \cos\theta}{r^2} = \frac{200\text{ cd} \times 0.80}{(5\text{ m})^2} = \frac{160}{25} = 6.4\text{ lx}$$

Key Takeaways
  • Solid angle: $\omega = A / r^2$ in steradians ($\mathrm{sr}$); total sphere is $4\pi\mathrm{sr}$.
  • Luminous intensity $I = \Phi / \omega$ in candelas ($\mathrm{cd}$); isotropic source emits $\Phi = 4\pi I$.
  • Illuminance $E = \Phi / A$ in lux ($\mathrm{lx} = \mathrm{lm\,m^{-2}}$).
  • Lambert's cosine law: $E = \frac{I\cos\theta}{r^2}$.

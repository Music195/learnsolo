Telescopes and Binoculars

Concept — Observing Distant Celestial and Terrestrial Objects

Telescopes are optical instruments designed to increase the visual angle subtended by distant objects, collecting light to form bright, magnified images:

  • 1. Astronomical Refracting Telescope: Consists of two convex lenses arranged coaxially:
    • Objective Lens ($L_o$): Features a very long focal length ($f_o$) and a large aperture to gather maximum light from faint distant celestial bodies. It forms a real, inverted, and diminished image ($I_1$) at its focal plane ($F_o$).
    • Eyepiece Lens ($L_e$): Has a short focal length ($f_e$) and smaller aperture. Positioned so its focal point $F_e$ coincides with $F_o$ in normal adjustment, collimating rays so the relaxed eye views a final inverted image at infinity ($\infty$).
    • Tube Length in Normal Adjustment: The physical separation between lenses equals the sum of their focal lengths: $$L = f_o + f_e$$
  • 2. Reflecting Astronomical Telescope: Replaces the heavy objective glass lens with a large concave paraboloidal mirror:
    • Zero Chromatic Aberration: Reflection reflects all wavelengths of light identically, completely avoiding the rainbow color fringing inherent in lenses.
    • Structural Rigidity: Massive glass mirrors can be structurally supported across their entire rear surface, avoiding the gravitational sagging and distortion that limits refracting lenses to diameters under $1\mathrm{meter}$.
  • 3. Terrestrial Telescopes and Prismatic Binoculars: For viewing earthbound objects, an inverted image is undesirable:
    • Terrestrial Telescope: Inserts an intermediate erecting convex lens ($m = +1$) at distance $2f$, which reinverts the intermediate image to produce an erect final view, at the cost of significantly lengthening the physical tube ($L = f_o + 4f + f_e$).
    • Binoculars: Compacts the optical path using two pairs of right-angle Porro prisms. By exploiting Total Internal Reflection (TIR) at $45^\circ$, the prisms fold the optical beam into a compact chassis while erecting the image both vertically and laterally, providing comfortable, three-dimensional stereoscopic vision.
Core Formulas — Angular Magnification
$$\text{Normal Adjustment (Image at }\infty\text{): } M = \frac{f_o}{f_e} \qquad\qquad L = f_o + f_e$$ $$\text{Image at Near Point (}D = 25\mathrm{cm}\text{): } M = \frac{f_o}{f_e}\left(1 + \frac{f_e}{D}\right) \qquad\qquad L = f_o + u_e$$
  • $M$ = angular magnifying power ($\beta / \alpha$)
  • $f_o$ = focal length of objective lens ($\mathrm{m}$ or $\mathrm{mm}$) — must be large for high magnification
  • $f_e$ = focal length of eyepiece lens ($\mathrm{m}$ or $\mathrm{mm}$) — must be small for high magnification
  • $L$ = barrel separation distance between objective and eyepiece
  • $D = 25\mathrm{cm} = 250\mathrm{mm}$ = least distance of distinct vision
Interactive Visualization — Astronomical Refracting Telescope Ray Optics

Light from a distant star arrives as parallel rays at a small angle $\alpha$. The objective brings them to a point on its focal plane (the image $I_1$); in normal adjustment the eyepiece's focus is at the same place, so the rays leave parallel again, at a bigger angle $\beta$. Change $f_o$, $f_e$ and $\alpha$ and check that $M = \beta/\alpha = f_o/f_e$ and $L = f_o + f_e$. (Heights are stretched so the small angles can be seen.)

Textbook Worked Examples — Telescope Calculations

Example 7.4: An astronomical telescope consists of two converging lenses: an objective of focal length $500\text{ mm}$ and an eyepiece of focal length $20\text{ mm}$.
(i) In normal adjustment (final image at infinity), find the separation between the lenses and the magnifying power.
(ii) If the final image is formed at the observer's near point ($D = 250\text{ mm}$), calculate the required lens separation and the new magnifying power.

Solution:
Given: $f_o = 500\text{ mm}$, $f_e = 20\text{ mm}$, $D = 250\text{ mm}$.

(i) Normal Adjustment (image at infinity): $$\text{Separation } L = f_o + f_e = 500\text{ mm} + 20\text{ mm} = 520\text{ mm} \quad (52\text{ cm})$$ $$\text{Magnifying power } M = \frac{f_o}{f_e} = \frac{500\text{ mm}}{20\text{ mm}} = 25\times$$ (ii) Final image at near point ($v_e = -250\text{ mm}$):
For the eyepiece: $$\frac{1}{u_e} = \frac{1}{f_e} - \frac{1}{v_e} = \frac{1}{20} - \frac{1}{-250} = \frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{250} = \frac{27}{500}\text{ mm}^{-1} \implies u_e = \frac{500}{27} \approx 18.52\text{ mm}$$ New lens separation: $$L = f_o + u_e = 500\text{ mm} + 18.52\text{ mm} = 518.52\text{ mm}$$ Magnifying power at near point: $$M = \frac{f_o}{f_e}\left(1 + \frac{f_e}{D}\right) = 25 \times \left(1 + \frac{20}{250}\right) = 25 \times (1 + 0.08) = 25 \times 1.08 = 27\times$$

Reviewed Exercises:

1. Why is the objective of a telescope made large, whereas that of a microscope is very small?
A telescope views distant celestial objects (stars, nebulae) that emit or reflect tiny amounts of light. A large objective lens aperture gathers as much incoming light energy as possible, producing bright, crisp images with high resolving power. In contrast, a microscope examines microscopic specimens placed millimeters from the lens illuminated by an intense condenser lamp, requiring a tiny focal length and small aperture to minimize optical spherical aberrations.

2. What are the key advantages of reflecting telescopes over refracting telescopes?
Reflecting telescopes eliminate chromatic aberration entirely, can be supported mechanically across their entire rear parabolic mirror surface without sagging under gravity, and are vastly lighter, more compact, and cheaper to build at apertures exceeding $1\mathrm{meter}$.

Key Takeaways
  • In normal adjustment, an astronomical telescope has magnifying power $M = f_o / f_e$ and length $L = f_o + f_e$.
  • High magnification requires an objective with very long focal length ($f_o$) and an eyepiece with short focal length ($f_e$).
  • Reflecting telescopes use concave parabolic mirrors to eliminate chromatic aberration.
  • Binoculars use Porro prisms operating via Total Internal Reflection to fold barrel length and produce erect stereoscopic images.

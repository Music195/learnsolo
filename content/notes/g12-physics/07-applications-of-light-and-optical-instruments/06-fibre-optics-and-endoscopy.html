Fibre Optics and Endoscopy

Concept — Guiding Light via Total Internal Reflection

Fibre optics is the technology of transmitting light and optical signals through thin, flexible strands of high-purity glass or plastic via repeated internal reflections:

  • Coaxial Fiber Architecture:
    • Core ($n_1$): The central cylindrical glass core through which light propagates, possessing a higher refractive index ($n_1$).
    • Cladding ($n_2$): The concentric outer optical layer having a slightly lower refractive index ($n_2 < n_1$), creating the necessary boundary for reflection.
    • Protective Buffer Jacket: An exterior polymer jacket that mechanically reinforces the fragile glass strand and shields it against moisture and physical abrasion.
  • Mechanism of Total Internal Reflection (TIR): Light launched into the core strikes the core-cladding boundary. Because the core is optically denser than the cladding ($n_1 > n_2$), any ray incident at an angle $i$ greater than the critical angle ($\theta_c$) cannot refract into the cladding and undergoes $100\%$ reflection back into the core, zigzagging losslessly down the entire length of the cable even when bent or looped.
  • Medical Application — The Endoscope: A flexible medical diagnostic instrument inserted into body orifices to examine internal gastrointestinal organs (stomach, colon, esophagus):
    • Illumination Bundle: An incoherent bundle of optical fibers that conveys bright light from an external lamp into the dark organ cavity.
    • Imaging Bundle: A strictly aligned (coherent) bundle of thousands of microscopic optical fibers that transmits the illuminated spatial image back out to a camera and video display.
Core Formulas — Critical Angle and Acceptance Cone
$$\sin\theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1} \qquad\qquad \text{Numerical Aperture (NA)} = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} = \sin\alpha_{\max}$$
  • $n_1$ = refractive index of optical core ($n_1 > n_2$)
  • $n_2$ = refractive index of optical cladding
  • $\theta_c$ = critical angle at the core-cladding interface ($\theta_c = \arcsin(n_2/n_1)$)
  • $\alpha_{\max}$ = maximum acceptance angle in air for light entering the fiber face
  • $\text{NA}$ = numerical aperture (light-gathering capacity of the fiber)

Textbook Worked Examples — Critical Angle Calculation

Example 7.6: The core of an optical fiber has a diameter of $2\ \mu\text{m}$ and a refractive index of $1.63$. The surrounding cladding has a refractive index of $1.48$. Calculate the critical angle at the core-cladding boundary.

Solution:
Given: Core index $n_1 = 1.63$, Cladding index $n_2 = 1.48$.
Using the critical angle relation: $$\sin\theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{1.48}{1.63} \approx 0.907975$$ $$\theta_c = \arcsin(0.907975) \approx 65.23^\circ$$ Implication: Any light ray traveling in the core that strikes the cladding boundary at an angle of incidence $i > 65.23^\circ$ will undergo total internal reflection and remain securely confined within the core.

Key Takeaways
  • Optical fibers transmit signals through Total Internal Reflection (TIR) within a high-index core ($n_1 > n_2$).
  • Critical angle condition: $\sin\theta_c = n_2 / n_1$.
  • Medical endoscopes combine illumination fiber bundles with coherent imaging fiber bundles to visually explore internal anatomy.
  • Optical fibers deliver immense communication bandwidth, zero electromagnetic interference, and low signal loss.

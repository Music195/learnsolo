Diffraction of Light and Single-Slit Diffraction

Concept — Wave Bending and Single-Slit Interference

Diffraction of light is defined as the bending of light waves around the edges of an obstacle or through an aperture into the region of geometrical shadow:

  • Criterion for Noticeable Diffraction: Diffraction is prominent only when the aperture or obstacle dimension $a$ is comparable to the wavelength $\lambda$ of the light ($a \sim \lambda$). For visible light ($\lambda \approx 400\text{--}700\mathrm{\,nm}$), macroscopic openings ($a \gg \lambda$) cast sharp geometric shadows with negligible diffraction.
  • Fresnel vs. Fraunhofer Diffraction:
    • Fresnel Diffraction: The source of light and the observation screen are at finite distances from the aperture. The incident and emerging wavefronts are spherical or cylindrical.
    • Fraunhofer Diffraction: The source and the observation screen are effectively at infinity (or placed at the focal planes of convex collimating and converging lenses). The incident and diffracted wavefronts are strictly planar, simplifying mathematical analysis.
  • Fraunhofer Single-Slit Diffraction: Consider a slit of narrow width $a$ illuminated by parallel rays of monochromatic light:
    • According to Huygens' principle, every infinitesimal element of the open aperture emits secondary wavelets in phase.
    • Central Maximum ($m = 0$): Along the forward axis ($\theta = 0$), all wavelets travel equal optical distances to the screen and arrive in phase, producing an intense, broad central bright band.
    • Condition for Minima (Dark Fringes): Dividing the slit into two halves of width $a/2$, the path difference between wavelets from corresponding points in the top and bottom halves is $\frac{a}{2}\sin\theta$. When this equals $\frac{\lambda}{2}$, pairs of wavelets arrive with a phase difference of $\pi$ and destructively cancel: $$a \sin\theta = m\lambda \qquad (m = \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \dots)$$ Note: $m \ne 0$ because $\theta = 0$ is the central maximum, not a minimum.
    • Position of Dark Fringes on Screen: For small angles $\theta \approx \frac{y}{D}$: $$y_m = \frac{m\lambda D}{a} \qquad (m = \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots)$$
    • Width of Central Maximum: The angular and linear width spanning between the first dark fringes on either side ($m = -1$ to $m = +1$): $$W_{\text{central}} = 2y_1 = \frac{2\lambda D}{a}$$ The central bright fringe is twice as wide as any secondary bright fringe. Furthermore, narrowing the slit width $a$ causes the diffraction pattern to widen!
Core Formulas — Single-Slit Fraunhofer Diffraction
$$a \sin\theta = m\lambda \quad (m = \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots) \qquad\qquad y_m = \frac{m\lambda D}{a} \qquad\qquad W_{\text{central}} = \frac{2\lambda D}{a}$$
  • $a$ = slit width ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $\theta$ = diffraction angle to the $m$-th minimum ($\mathrm{rad}$ or degrees)
  • $\lambda$ = wavelength of monochromatic light ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $m$ = order number of diffraction minimum ($m = \pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \dots$)
  • $D$ = distance from slit to observation screen ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $y_m$ = linear distance of $m$-th dark fringe from screen center ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $W_{\text{central}}$ = linear width of central maximum ($2y_1$) ($\mathrm{m}$)

Worked Example 8.3 — Calculating Laser Wavelength from Diffraction Width
Textbook Example 8.3 (Page 132):
A beam of green light from a laser light source is diffracted by a slit of width $0.55\mathrm{\,mm}$. The diffraction pattern forms on a wall $2.06\mathrm{\,m}$ beyond the slit. The distance between the positions of the first minima on both sides of the central bright fringe is $4.1\mathrm{\,mm}$. Calculate the wavelength of the laser light.
Given Data:
  • Slit width $a = 0.55\mathrm{\,mm} = 0.55 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m}$
  • Screen distance $D = 2.06\mathrm{\,m}$
  • Distance between first minima $2y_1 = 4.1\mathrm{\,mm} = 4.1 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m}$
Formula & Calculation:
The width of the central maximum spanning from $m = -1$ to $m = +1$ is: $$2y_1 = \frac{2\lambda D}{a}$$ Rearranging to solve for the wavelength $\lambda$: $$\lambda = \frac{a \cdot (2y_1)}{2D}$$ Substitute the numerical values: $$\lambda = \frac{(0.55 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m}) \times (4.1 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m})}{2 \times 2.06\mathrm{\,m}}$$ $$\lambda = \frac{2.255 \times 10^{-6}}{4.12} \approx 5.47 \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,m} = 547\mathrm{\,nm}$$ Conclusion:
The wavelength of the green laser light is $547\mathrm{\,nm}$.
Textbook Exercise — Positions of Dark Fringes
Chapter 8 Exercise 10 (Textbook Page 136):
Light of wavelength $580\mathrm{\,nm}$ is incident on a slit of width $0.30\mathrm{\,mm}$. The observing screen is placed $2.0\mathrm{\,m}$ from the slit. Find the positions of the first dark fringes and the total width of the central bright fringe.
Given Data:
  • $\lambda = 580\mathrm{\,nm} = 5.80 \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,m}$
  • Slit width $a = 0.30\mathrm{\,mm} = 3.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,m}$
  • Screen distance $D = 2.0\mathrm{\,m}$
Step 1: First Dark Fringe Position ($m = 1$): $$y_1 = \frac{\lambda D}{a} = \frac{(5.80 \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,m})(2.0\mathrm{\,m})}{3.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,m}} = \frac{1.16 \times 10^{-6}}{3.0 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 3.87 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m} = 3.87\mathrm{\,mm}$$ The first dark fringes appear symmetrically at $y = \pm 3.87\mathrm{\,mm}$ from the central line. Step 2: Total Width of Central Bright Fringe: $$W_{\text{central}} = 2y_1 = 2 \times 3.87\mathrm{\,mm} = 7.73\mathrm{\,mm}$$
Key Takeaways
  • Diffraction is wave bending around obstacles into geometrical shadows, significant when slit width $a \sim \lambda$.
  • Fraunhofer condition assumes plane wavefronts (parallel incident and diffracted rays).
  • Single-slit minima condition: $a \sin\theta = m\lambda$ ($m = \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$).
  • Central maximum width: $W_{\text{central}} = \frac{2\lambda D}{a}$. A narrower slit paradoxically produces a wider central diffraction band.

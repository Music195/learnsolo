Diffraction Grating and Optical Spectra

Concept — Multiple-Slit Dispersion and Spectrometry

A diffraction grating is an optical device consisting of a large number of equidistant, parallel narrow slits of identical width separated by equal opaque intervals:

  • Types of Gratings:
    • Transmission Grating: Light passes through transparent slits etched onto a glass or quartz plate.
    • Reflection Grating: Light reflects from fine, closely spaced parallel grooves ruled on a polished metallic or reflective plastic surface (e.g., the concentric spiral data track of a Compact Disc acts as a reflection grating, yielding iridescent rainbow spectra).
  • Grating Constant ($N$) and Slit Spacing ($d$): The ruling density $N$ is expressed in lines (slits) per unit length (e.g., $\text{lines/cm}$ or $\text{lines/mm}$). The spacing between consecutive slit centers is: $$d = \frac{1}{N}$$
  • Grating Equation & Maxima: When a plane wave is incident normally on the grating, wavelets from all $N$ slits interfere constructively along angles $\theta$ where path differences between adjacent slits are integer multiples of $\lambda$: $$d \sin\theta = m\lambda \qquad (m = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots)$$ Because thousands of slits contribute, destructive cancellation occurs almost everywhere except at these precise angles. Consequently, diffraction grating maxima form extremely sharp, intense spectral lines.
  • Dispersion of White Light:
    • Central Maximum ($m = 0$): At $\theta = 0$, path difference is zero for all wavelengths; white light produces an undispersed central white image.
    • Higher Orders ($m \ge 1$): Because $\sin\theta = \frac{m\lambda}{d}$, angle $\theta$ is directly proportional to wavelength $\lambda$. Red light ($\lambda \approx 700\mathrm{\,nm}$) is diffracted at a greater angle than violet light ($\lambda \approx 400\mathrm{\,nm}$).
  • Comparison: Diffraction Grating vs. Prism:
    Feature Diffraction Grating Prism
    Operating Principle Diffraction & multi-wave interference Refraction & dispersion ($n(\lambda)$)
    Bending Order Red deviates most, violet least Violet deviates most, red least
    Spectra Produced Multiple orders ($m = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2$) symmetric Single spectrum only
Core Formulas — Diffraction Grating
$$d \sin\theta = m\lambda \qquad\qquad d = \frac{1}{N} \qquad\qquad m_{\max} \le \frac{d}{\lambda}$$
  • $d$ = slit spacing / grating pitch ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $N$ = grating line density ($\text{lines/m}$ or $\text{lines/cm}$)
  • $\theta$ = diffraction angle for the $m$-th order principal maximum ($\mathrm{rad}$ or degrees)
  • $m$ = spectrum order integer ($m = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$)
  • $\lambda$ = wavelength of incident light ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $m_{\max}$ = highest observable order ($\sin\theta \le 1$)

Worked Example 8.4 — Diffraction Angle for Second-Order Maximum
Textbook Example 8.4 (Page 134):
Light of wavelength $457\mathrm{\,nm}$ illuminates a diffraction grating ruled with $5000\mathrm{\,lines\,cm^{-1}}$. What is the diffraction angle for the second-order maximum?
Given Data:
  • Wavelength $\lambda = 457\mathrm{\,nm} = 457 \times 10^{-9}\mathrm{\,m}$
  • Grating constant $N = 5000\mathrm{\,lines\,cm^{-1}} = 5.0 \times 10^5\mathrm{\,lines\,m^{-1}}$
  • Diffraction order $m = 2$
Step 1: Calculate slit spacing $d$: $$d = \frac{1}{N} = \frac{1}{5000\mathrm{\,cm^{-1}}} = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,cm} = 2.0 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,m}$$ Step 2: Apply the grating equation: $$d \sin\theta = m\lambda$$ $$\sin\theta = \frac{m\lambda}{d} = \frac{2 \times (457 \times 10^{-9}\mathrm{\,m})}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,m}} = \frac{9.14 \times 10^{-7}}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.457$$ Step 3: Calculate angle $\theta$: $$\theta = \arcsin(0.457) \approx 27.19^\circ \approx 27.2^\circ$$ Conclusion:
The second-order diffracted spectral line appears at an angle of $27.19^\circ$ relative to the central normal axis.
Textbook Exercises — Grating Line Calculation and Angle
Chapter 8 Exercise 11 & 12 (Textbook Page 136):
(a) A grating containing $4000\mathrm{\,slits\,cm^{-1}}$ is illuminated with monochromatic light and produces a second-order bright line at a $30^\circ$ angle. What is the wavelength of the light used?
(b) A diffraction grating has $2000\mathrm{\,lines\,cm^{-1}}$. At what angle will the first-order maximum be for $520\mathrm{\,nm}$ green light?
Part (a):
  • $d = \frac{1}{4000}\mathrm{\,cm} = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,cm} = 2.5 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,m}$
  • $m = 2$, $\theta = 30^\circ \implies \sin(30^\circ) = 0.5$
  • $\lambda = \frac{d \sin\theta}{m} = \frac{(2.5 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,m})(0.5)}{2} = \frac{1.25 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,m}}{2} = 6.25 \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,m} = 625\mathrm{\,nm}$ (orange-red light).
Part (b):
  • $d = \frac{1}{2000}\mathrm{\,cm} = 5.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,cm} = 5.0 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,m}$
  • $\lambda = 520\mathrm{\,nm} = 5.20 \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,m}$, $m = 1$
  • $\sin\theta = \frac{m\lambda}{d} = \frac{1 \times 5.20 \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,m}}{5.0 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,m}} = 0.104$
  • $\theta = \arcsin(0.104) \approx 5.97^\circ \approx 6.0^\circ$.
Key Takeaways
  • A diffraction grating has thousands of parallel slits per cm, producing extremely narrow and intense interference-diffraction peaks.
  • Principal maxima satisfy $d \sin\theta = m\lambda$, where $d = 1/N$.
  • White light splits into spectral components with red bending the most and violet bending the least (the exact inverse of prism dispersion).
  • Widely used in optical spectrometers to measure elemental emission spectra and determine wavelengths.

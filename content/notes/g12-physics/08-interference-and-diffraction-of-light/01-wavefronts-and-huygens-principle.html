Wavefronts and Huygens' Principle

Concept — Wave Propagation and Huygens' Model

In physical optics (wave optics), the ray approximation of geometrical optics is replaced by a wave description. Wave propagation is characterized by wavefronts, phases, and secondary wavelets:

  • Wavefront: A surface associated with a propagating wave which is the locus of all adjacent points vibrating with the same phase:
    • Rays (the direction of wave energy propagation) are always perpendicular to the wavefront.
    • Spherical Wavefront: Produced by an isotropic point source in a uniform medium. As distance from the source increases, the radius of curvature becomes very large.
    • Plane Wavefront: At large distances from a source, a small section of a spherical wavefront can be treated as planar.
  • Phase and Phase Angle: The phase angle $\phi$ specifies the instantaneous fractional state of a periodic oscillation on a reference phasor circle ($0 \le \phi < 2\pi\mathrm{\,rad}$).
  • Phase Difference ($\Delta\phi$) vs. Path Difference ($\Delta x$):
    • In Phase (Same Phase): When $\Delta\phi = 0, 2\pi, 4\pi, \dots = 2m\pi$ (where $m$ is an integer), corresponding to a path difference $\Delta x = 0, \lambda, 2\lambda, \dots = m\lambda$. Crest meets crest.
    • Completely Out of Phase: When $\Delta\phi = \pi, 3\pi, 5\pi, \dots = (2m+1)\pi$, corresponding to a path difference $\Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{2}, \frac{3\lambda}{2}, \dots = (m + \frac{1}{2})\lambda$. Crest meets trough.
  • Huygens' Principle (1678): Every point on a primary wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets that spread outward in all directions with the wave speed $v$ in that medium. The new position of the wavefront at time $t + \Delta t$ is the surface envelope tangent to all these secondary wavelets.
Core Formulas — Phase Difference and Huygens' Wavelets
$$\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x \qquad\qquad r_{\text{wavelet}} = v\,\Delta t \qquad\qquad \text{In Phase: } \Delta x = m\lambda \quad (m \in \mathbb{Z})$$
  • $\Delta\phi$ = phase difference between two points or waves ($\mathrm{rad}$)
  • $\Delta x$ = path difference along the direction of propagation ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $\lambda$ = wavelength of the propagating wave ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $v$ = propagation speed of wave in the medium ($\mathrm{m/s}$)
  • $r_{\text{wavelet}}$ = radius of secondary Huygens wavelets after elapsed time $\Delta t$ ($\mathrm{m}$)
Interactive Demo — Huygens' Wavelets, and Path Difference to Phase Difference

How does a wavefront "know" where to go next? And when two waves meet, how do we tell whether they add up or cancel?

Huygens: every point on a wavefront (dots) sends out a small wavelet; after a time $\Delta t$ each wavelet has radius $v\Delta t$, and the line touching all of them is the new wavefront. Try a gap (the wave spreads out) and a glass block (wavelets are smaller in glass, so the front bends). Phase: set the two path lengths; a path difference of a whole number of wavelengths gives crest on crest, a half-number gives crest on trough.

Worked Example 8.1 — Path Difference to Phase Difference
Textbook Example 8.1 (Page 125):
What is the phase difference for two waves of the same frequency arriving at a point with respective path lengths of $6\lambda$ and $4.5\lambda$? Determine whether they are in phase or out of phase.
Step 1: Calculate the path difference $\Delta x$: $$\Delta x = 6\lambda - 4.5\lambda = 1.5\lambda$$ Step 2: Relate phase difference $\Delta\phi$ to path difference: $$\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(1.5\lambda) = 3\pi\mathrm{\,rad}$$ Step 3: State the phase relationship: $$\Delta\phi = 3\pi = (2 \times 1 + 1)\pi$$ Since the phase difference is an odd integral multiple of $\pi$ (and the path difference is an odd half-multiple of wavelength $1.5\lambda$), the two waves are completely out of phase. When superimposing with equal amplitudes, destructive interference occurs.
Reviewed Exercise — Phase and Wavefront Concepts
Reviewed Exercise (Textbook Page 125):
How is the phase difference of two waves related to their path difference? What are the phase relationships when path difference is $2\lambda$ versus $2.5\lambda$?
Answer:
  • General Formula: $$\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x$$ One full wavelength $\lambda$ in space corresponds to a full cycle phase angle of $2\pi\mathrm{\,rad}$ ($360^\circ$).
  • For $\Delta x = 2\lambda$: $$\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(2\lambda) = 4\pi\mathrm{\,rad}$$ The waves are in phase (crest meets crest).
  • For $\Delta x = 2.5\lambda$: $$\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(2.5\lambda) = 5\pi\mathrm{\,rad}$$ The waves are completely out of phase (crest meets trough).
Key Takeaways
  • A wavefront is the locus of all points vibrating in the same phase. Propagation rays are perpendicular to wavefronts.
  • Huygens' Principle: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets; the forward envelope tangent to all wavelets forms the new wavefront.
  • Phase difference: $\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x$. Integral multiples of $\lambda$ yield constructive reinforcement ($\Delta\phi = 2m\pi$); odd half-multiples yield cancellation ($\Delta\phi = (2m+1)\pi$).

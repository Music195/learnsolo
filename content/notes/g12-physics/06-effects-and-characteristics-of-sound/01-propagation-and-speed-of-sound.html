Propagation and Speed of Sound

Concept — Nature of Acoustic Pressure Waves

Sound is a mechanical, longitudinal wave composed of alternating compressions and rarefactions that propagates through an elastic, deformable medium (solid, liquid, or gas):

  • Mechanical Nature: Sound requires a material medium to propagate; it cannot travel across a vacuum because there are no particles to transmit collisions.
  • Factors Influencing Speed ($v$):
    • Elasticity of Medium: The stiffer the intermolecular bonds (higher elastic modulus), the faster restoring forces transmit the disturbance. Solids are much stiffer than liquids, and liquids are stiffer than compressible gases ($v_{\text{solid}} > v_{\text{liquid}} > v_{\text{gas}}$).
    • Density of Medium ($\rho$): Greater inertial density slightly retards wave acceleration ($v \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}$).
    • Temperature of Gases ($T$): Sound speed in gases increases with temperature because thermal agitation increases average molecular velocity ($v \propto \sqrt{T}$).
    • Humidity: Sound travels slightly faster in humid air than in dry air because water vapor ($\mathrm{H_2O}$, molar mass $18\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$) is less dense than diatomic nitrogen and oxygen ($\sim 29\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$).
  • Sound Intensity ($I$) and Intensity Level ($\beta$): Sound intensity is the physical acoustic power transported per unit area perpendicular to the direction of propagation ($\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$). Because the human ear responds logarithmically rather than linearly to intensity, sound levels are quantified on a logarithmic decibel ($\mathrm{dB}$) scale referenced to the threshold of hearing ($I_0 = 10^{-12}\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$).
Core Formulas — Speed of Sound and Decibel Level
$$v_{\text{solid}} = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}} \qquad v_{\text{liquid}} = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}} \qquad v_{\text{gas}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma p}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}} \qquad \beta = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)$$
  • $Y$ = Young's modulus of solid ($\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$ or $\mathrm{Pa}$)
  • $B$ = bulk modulus of liquid or gas ($\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$)
  • $\rho$ = density of medium ($\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$)
  • $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$ = adiabatic index of gas ($1.40$ for diatomic air); $p$ = static pressure ($\mathrm{Pa}$)
  • $T$ = absolute temperature ($\mathrm{K}$); $M$ = molar mass ($\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$)
  • Temperature dependence in air: $v(T) = v_0 \sqrt{\frac{T}{273}} \approx 331 + 0.6 \, T_C\ (\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})$
  • $\beta$ = sound intensity level in decibels ($\mathrm{dB}$)
  • $I_0 = 10^{-12}\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ = standardized threshold of human hearing at $1000\mathrm{Hz}$ ($0\mathrm{dB}$)

Textbook Worked Examples — Elasticity and Decibel Calculations

Example 6.1: What is the speed of sound in: (i) a solid steel rod, and (ii) water?
Young's modulus of steel is $Y = 20 \times 10^{10}\text{ N\,m}^{-2}$, density of steel is $\rho = 7.8 \times 10^3\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$.
The bulk modulus of water is $B = 0.22 \times 10^{10}\text{ N\,m}^{-2}$, density of water is $\rho = 10^3\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$.

Solution:
(i) Speed of sound in a steel rod: $$v_{\text{steel}} = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{20 \times 10^{10}\text{ N\,m}^{-2}}{7.8 \times 10^3\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}}} = \sqrt{2.564 \times 10^7} \approx 5.06 \times 10^3\text{ m\,s}^{-1} \quad (5060\text{ m\,s}^{-1})$$ (ii) Speed of sound in water: $$v_{\text{water}} = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.22 \times 10^{10}\text{ N\,m}^{-2}}{10^3\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}}} = \sqrt{2.2 \times 10^6} \approx 1.48 \times 10^3\text{ m\,s}^{-1} \quad (1480\text{ m\,s}^{-1})$$

Example 6.2: Calculate the velocity of sound in air at STP ($T = 273\text{ K}$, $p = 1.01 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$, $\rho = 1.29\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$, $\gamma = 1.4$). Also calculate the velocity of sound in air at $27^\circ\text{C}$.

Solution:
1. At STP ($T_1 = 273\text{ K}$): $$v_{\text{STP}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma p}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.4 \times (1.01 \times 10^5\text{ Pa})}{1.29\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}}} = \sqrt{\frac{141\,400}{1.29}} = \sqrt{109\,612} \approx 331.07\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$$ 2. At $27^\circ\text{C}$ ($T_2 = 27 + 273 = 300\text{ K}$):
Since velocity is directly proportional to $\sqrt{T}$: $$\frac{v_2}{v_1} = \sqrt{\frac{T_2}{T_1}} \implies v_{300\text{ K}} = 331.07 \times \sqrt{\frac{300}{273}} = 331.07 \times \sqrt{1.0989} \approx 331.07 \times 1.0483 \approx 347.06\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$$

Example 6.3 & 6.4:
(a) At a busy street intersection, the sound intensity level is $70\text{ dB}$. What is the physical sound intensity in $\text{W\,m}^{-2}$?
(b) By how many decibels does the sound level increase if the intensity increases by a factor of $1000$?

Solution:
(a) Using $\beta = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)$ with $I_0 = 10^{-12}\text{ W\,m}^{-2}$:
$$70 = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{10^{-12}}\right) \implies 7 = \log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{10^{-12}}\right)$$ $$\frac{I}{10^{-12}} = 10^7 \implies I = 10^7 \times 10^{-12} = 10^{-5}\text{ W\,m}^{-2}$$ (b) If intensity increases by factor $1000$ ($\frac{I_2}{I_1} = 1000 = 10^3$):
$$\Delta \beta = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{I_2}{I_1}\right) = 10 \log_{10}(10^3) = 10 \times 3 = 30\text{ dB}$$

Reviewed Exercises:

1. Why is the speed of sound higher in solids than in liquids and gases?
Sound velocity depends on the ratio of elastic modulus to density ($v = \sqrt{E/\rho}$). Although solids are denser than liquids and gases, their elastic moduli (Young's modulus $Y$) are thousands of times larger due to strong, rigid crystalline intermolecular chemical bonds. The enormous elasticity far outweighs the higher density, producing much higher wave speeds in solids.

2. If the intensity of sound is doubled ($I_2 / I_1 = 2$), by how many decibels does the sound level increase?
$$\Delta \beta = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{I_2}{I_1}\right) = 10 \log_{10}(2) \approx 10 \times 0.3010 \approx 3.01\text{ dB}$$ Doubling the acoustic intensity increases the sound level by approximately $3\text{ dB}$.

Key Takeaways
  • Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave requiring a deformable medium: $v_{\text{solid}} > v_{\text{liquid}} > v_{\text{gas}}$.
  • Speed formulas: $v_{\text{solid}} = \sqrt{Y/\rho}$, $v_{\text{liquid}} = \sqrt{B/\rho}$, $v_{\text{gas}} = \sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}$.
  • In gases, sound speed is independent of pressure at constant temperature, but scales with absolute temperature: $v \propto \sqrt{T}$.
  • Sound level is measured in decibels: $\beta = 10 \log_{10}(I/I_0)$ where $I_0 = 10^{-12}\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$.
  • Every $10\mathrm{dB}$ increase corresponds to a $10$-fold increase in physical sound intensity; doubling intensity adds $+3.01\mathrm{dB}$.

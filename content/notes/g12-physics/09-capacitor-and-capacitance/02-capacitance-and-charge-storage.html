Capacitance and Charge Storage

Concept — Definition and Physical Nature of Capacitance

The ability of an electrical capacitor to store electric charge per unit potential difference is quantified by its capacitance ($C$):

  • Definition: The capacitance $C$ of a capacitor is defined as the ratio of the magnitude of electric charge $Q$ on either conductor plate to the potential difference $V$ across the two conductors: $$C = \frac{Q}{V}$$ Note: When a capacitor is charged, one conductor carries $+Q$ and the opposite conductor carries $-Q$. The total net charge of the capacitor as a whole is zero ($+Q - Q = 0$). By convention, the "charge on a capacitor" refers exclusively to the magnitude $Q$.
  • SI Unit & Submultiples:
    • The SI unit of capacitance is the Farad ($\mathrm{F}$), named in honour of Michael Faraday: $$1\mathrm{\,Farad} = 1\mathrm{\,Coulomb\ per\ Volt} \quad (1\mathrm{\,F} = 1\mathrm{\,C/V})$$
    • Definition of One Farad: A capacitor has a capacitance of one farad if a potential difference of one volt between its conductors establishes a charge of one coulomb on each conductor.
    • One farad is an extraordinarily large capacitance for conventional geometric devices. Common practical submultiples include: $$\text{Microfarad } (\mu\mathrm{F}) = 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,F} \qquad \text{Nanofarad } (\mathrm{nF}) = 10^{-9}\mathrm{\,F} \qquad \text{Picofarad } (\mathrm{pF}) = 10^{-12}\mathrm{\,F}$$
  • Independence from Voltage and Charge: A fundamental principle of electrostatics is that $C$ is a geometric constant of the physical device. It depends only on the physical geometry (plate area $A$, separation distance $d$, and shape) and the dielectric material between the plates. It is strictly independent of the applied voltage $V$ or accumulated charge $Q$:
    • Increasing the applied voltage $V$ causes a proportional influx of charge $Q$ ($Q \propto V$), keeping the ratio $Q/V = C$ constant.
Core Formulas — Capacitance Definition
$$C = \frac{Q}{V} \qquad\qquad Q = C V \qquad\qquad V = \frac{Q}{C}$$
  • $C$ = capacitance ($\mathrm{F}$, $\mu\mathrm{F}$, $\mathrm{nF}$, $\mathrm{pF}$)
  • $Q$ = magnitude of charge stored on either plate ($\mathrm{C}$, $\mu\mathrm{C}$)
  • $V$ = electrostatic potential difference across the plates ($\mathrm{V}$)

Worked Example 9.1 — Evaluating Capacitance and Voltage Doubling
Textbook Example 9.1 (Page 141):
When a parallel-plate capacitor is connected to a $50\mathrm{\,V}$ battery, each plate receives a charge of magnitude $0.002\mathrm{\,C}$.
(a) Find its capacitance.
(b) If the capacitor is connected to a $100\mathrm{\,V}$ battery, does the capacitance change?
(c) Does the charge still remain the same?
Given Data:
  • $V_1 = 50\mathrm{\,V}$
  • $Q_1 = 0.002\mathrm{\,C} = 2 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,C}$
Part (a): Calculate Capacitance $C$: $$C = \frac{Q_1}{V_1} = \frac{0.002\mathrm{\,C}}{50\mathrm{\,V}} = 4.0 \times 10^{-5}\mathrm{\,F} = 40\,\mu\mathrm{F}$$ Part (b): Voltage increased to $100\mathrm{\,V}$ — Does $C$ change?
No. Capacitance depends purely on physical factors (plate area, spacing, dielectric material). It does not depend on applied voltage. Hence, $C = 40\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ remains identical. Part (c): Does the charge remain the same?
No. The accumulated charge is directly proportional to voltage: $$Q_2 = C V_2 = (4.0 \times 10^{-5}\mathrm{\,F}) \times (100\mathrm{\,V}) = 4.0 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,C} = 0.004\mathrm{\,C}$$ Doubling the potential difference from $50\mathrm{\,V}$ to $100\mathrm{\,V}$ doubles the stored charge to $0.004\mathrm{\,C}$.
Reviewed Exercise — True or False Analysis
Textbook Exercises 1 & 2 (Page 151):
State whether the following statements are True (T) or False (F):
(i) If one conductor of a capacitor has a charge $+Q$, the other has a charge $-Q$.
(ii) The charge of the capacitor is the magnitude of the charge on each conductor.
(iii) The potential difference of the capacitor is twice the potential difference between two conductors.
Answers:
  • (i) True: Electric induction and charge conservation ensure equal and opposite surface charges on facing plates.
  • (ii) True: By standard definition, the stored charge $Q$ is defined as the absolute magnitude on either plate ($Q = |+Q| = |-Q|$).
  • (iii) False: The potential difference of the capacitor is simply the electric potential difference $V = V_+ - V_-$ between the two conductors, not twice that value.
Key Takeaways
  • Capacitance $C = \frac{Q}{V}$ measures the charge stored per volt of electric potential difference.
  • $1\mathrm{\,Farad} = 1\mathrm{\,Coulomb / Volt}$. Common practical units: $\mu\mathrm{F}, \mathrm{nF}, \mathrm{pF}$.
  • Capacitance is an intrinsic geometric and dielectric property; varying $V$ alters $Q$ proportionally, leaving $C$ unchanged.

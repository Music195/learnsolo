Energy Stored and RC Circuits

Concept — Electrostatic Field Energy and Transient Dynamics

Charging a capacitor requires electrical work to push electrons against the opposing Coulomb repulsive force of previously accumulated charge. This work is stored as electrostatic potential energy within the electric field:

  • Derivation of Stored Energy: When intermediate charge $q$ resides on the plates, the potential difference across them is $v = \frac{q}{C}$. The incremental work $dW$ required to transfer an additional charge $dq$ is: $$dW = v\,dq = \frac{q}{C}\,dq$$ Integrating from uncharged ($q = 0$) to final charge $Q$: $$W = \int_0^Q \frac{q}{C}\,dq = \left[\frac{q^2}{2C}\right]_0^Q = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{1}{2}QV = \frac{1}{2}CV^2$$ Physical Significance: During charging by a battery of voltage $V_0$, total energy supplied is $W_{\text{battery}} = Q V_0 = C V_0^2$. Exactly half ($50\%$) is stored in the capacitor's electric field; the other half is dissipated as Joule heat in connecting wire resistance.
  • RC Charging Transient: When an uncharged capacitor $C$ is connected in series with a resistor $R$ and DC source $V_0$: $$V_c(t) = V_0\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right) \qquad q(t) = Q_0\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$ The charging current begins at $I_0 = \frac{V_0}{R}$ and decays exponentially: $$I(t) = \frac{V_0}{R}e^{-t/RC}$$
  • RC Discharging Transient: When a fully charged capacitor discharges across resistor $R$: $$V_c(t) = V_0 e^{-t/RC} \qquad q(t) = Q_0 e^{-t/RC}$$
  • Time Constant ($\tau = RC$): The product $\tau = RC$ has units of seconds ($\Omega \cdot \mathrm{F} = \mathrm{s}$) and governs the rate of exponential response:
    • At $t = \tau$ during charging: $V_c = V_0(1 - e^{-1}) \approx 0.632 V_0$ ($63.2\%$ of maximum voltage).
    • At $t = \tau$ during discharging: $V_c = V_0 e^{-1} \approx 0.368 V_0$ ($36.8\%$ of initial voltage).
    • After $5\tau$, the capacitor is considered virtually fully charged or discharged ($> 99.3\%$).
Core Formulas — Energy and RC Transients
$$W = \frac{1}{2}QV = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C} \qquad\qquad \tau = RC \qquad\qquad V_c(t) = V_0\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$
  • $W$ = electrostatic energy stored in the capacitor ($\mathrm{J}$)
  • $C$ = capacitance ($\mathrm{F}$)
  • $V$ or $V_0$ = supply or capacitor potential difference ($\mathrm{V}$)
  • $Q$ = stored charge magnitude ($\mathrm{C}$)
  • $R$ = circuit series resistance ($\Omega$)
  • $\tau = RC$ = capacitive time constant ($\mathrm{s}$)
  • $t$ = elapsed time ($\mathrm{s}$)

Worked Example 9.3 — Required Voltage for Target Stored Energy
Textbook Example 9.3 (Page 147):
What potential difference must be applied across a $10\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor if it is to store an energy content of $0.2\mathrm{\,J}$?
Given Data:
  • Capacitance $C = 10\,\mu\mathrm{F} = 10 \times 10^{-6}\mathrm{\,F} = 1.0 \times 10^{-5}\mathrm{\,F}$
  • Stored energy $W = 0.2\mathrm{\,J}$
Calculation:
The energy stored in terms of capacitance and voltage is: $$W = \frac{1}{2}CV^2$$ Rearranging to solve for potential difference $V$: $$V^2 = \frac{2W}{C} = \frac{2 \times 0.2\mathrm{\,J}}{1.0 \times 10^{-5}\mathrm{\,F}} = \frac{0.4}{1.0 \times 10^{-5}} = 40{,}000\mathrm{\,V^2}$$ Taking the square root: $$V = \sqrt{40{,}000\mathrm{\,V^2}} = 200\mathrm{\,V}$$ Conclusion:
A potential difference of $200\mathrm{\,V}$ must be applied across the capacitor.
Worked Example 9.4 — RC Charging Resistance Value
Textbook Example 9.4 (Page 147):
In a capacitor charging $RC$ circuit, $C = 50\,\mu\mathrm{F}$. What is the value of resistance $R$ that would produce a voltage rise to $20\%$ of supply voltage after $1\mathrm{\,s}$?
Given Data:
  • Capacitance $C = 50\,\mu\mathrm{F} = 5.0 \times 10^{-5}\mathrm{\,F}$
  • Elapsed time $t = 1\mathrm{\,s}$
  • Capacitor voltage $V_c = 0.20\,V_0$ ($20\%$ of supply $V_0$)
Step 1: Write the charging equation: $$V_c(t) = V_0\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$ $$0.20\,V_0 = V_0\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right) \implies 0.20 = 1 - e^{-t/RC}$$ Step 2: Solve for the exponential term: $$e^{-t/RC} = 1 - 0.20 = 0.80$$ Taking natural logarithms of both sides: $$-\frac{t}{RC} = \ln(0.80) \approx -0.2231$$ $$\frac{t}{RC} = 0.2231 \implies R = \frac{t}{0.2231 \cdot C}$$ Step 3: Substitute numerical values: $$R = \frac{1\mathrm{\,s}}{0.2231 \times (5.0 \times 10^{-5}\mathrm{\,F})} = \frac{1}{1.1155 \times 10^{-5}} \approx 89{,}640\,\Omega \approx 89.6\text{ k}\Omega$$ Conclusion:
The required circuit resistance is $R \approx 89.6\text{ k}\Omega$.
Key Takeaways
  • Electrostatic energy stored in a capacitor: $W = \frac{1}{2}QV = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$.
  • In an $RC$ charging circuit, voltage climbs according to $V_c(t) = V_0(1 - e^{-t/RC})$.
  • The time constant $\tau = RC$ measures the charging response time ($63.2\%$ voltage achieved at $t = \tau$).

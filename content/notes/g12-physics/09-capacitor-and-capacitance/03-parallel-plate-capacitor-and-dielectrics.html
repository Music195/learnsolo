Parallel-Plate Capacitor and Dielectrics

Concept — Plate Geometry and Dielectric Polarization

The parallel-plate capacitor is the archetype of capacitive components, consisting of two parallel planar metal plates of identical area $A$ separated by a uniform gap $d$ filled with an insulating dielectric:

  • Capacitance with Vacuum (or Air): When the intervening space is a vacuum (or air, $\kappa \approx 1.0006$), the electric field $E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A}$ is uniform between the plates. Since potential difference $V = E d = \frac{Q d}{\varepsilon_0 A}$: $$C_0 = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$ where $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\mathrm{\,F/m}$ ($\mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}$) is the electric permittivity of free space.
  • Geometrical Dependencies:
    • $C$ is directly proportional to plate surface area $A$ ($C \propto A$). Doubling the overlapping plate area doubles capacitance.
    • $C$ is inversely proportional to plate separation distance $d$ ($C \propto \frac{1}{d}$). Halving the spacing doubles capacitance.
  • Role of the Dielectric Medium: When an insulating material of dielectric constant $\kappa$ (also known as relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$) fills the gap: $$C = \kappa C_0 = \frac{\kappa \varepsilon_0 A}{d} = \frac{\varepsilon A}{d}$$ where $\varepsilon = \kappa \varepsilon_0$ is the absolute permittivity of the dielectric material.
  • Dielectric Constant ($\kappa$): The dimensionless ratio: $$\kappa = \frac{C}{C_0} = \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_0}$$ Because dielectric molecules polarize under the external electric field, their induced dipoles set up an opposing internal field, reducing the net electric field ($E = E_0 / \kappa$) and net voltage ($V = V_0 / \kappa$) for a fixed charge $Q$, thereby increasing capacitance by a factor of $\kappa$.
  • Benefits of Dielectrics:
    1. Multiplies Capacitance: Increases $C$ by $\kappa$ without increasing physical dimensions.
    2. Elevates Maximum Operating Voltage: Dielectric materials exhibit much higher dielectric breakdown strength ($E_{\text{breakdown}}$) than air, allowing the capacitor to withstand higher working voltages without arcing.
    3. Mechanical Separation: Provides solid physical support keeping plates from touching.
Core Formulas — Parallel-Plate Capacitance & Dielectrics
$$C_0 = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} \qquad\qquad C = \frac{\kappa \varepsilon_0 A}{d} = \frac{\varepsilon A}{d} \qquad\qquad \kappa = \frac{C}{C_0} = \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_0}$$
  • $A$ = overlapping surface area of each plate ($\mathrm{m^2}$)
  • $d$ = separation distance between plates ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\mathrm{\,F/m}$ = permittivity of free space
  • $\kappa$ = dielectric constant of the insulating material ($\kappa \ge 1$, dimensionless)
  • $\varepsilon = \kappa \varepsilon_0$ = absolute permittivity of dielectric ($\mathrm{F/m}$)
  • $C_0$ = capacitance with vacuum or air ($\mathrm{F}$ or $\mathrm{pF}$)
  • $C$ = capacitance with dielectric inserted ($\mathrm{F}$ or $\mathrm{pF}$)

Worked Example 9.2 — Capacitance and Charge of Parallel Plates
Textbook Example 9.2 (Page 144):
The plates of a parallel-plate capacitor are $50\mathrm{\,cm^2}$ in area and $1\mathrm{\,mm}$ apart.
(i) What is its capacitance in air?
(ii) When the capacitor is connected to a $24\mathrm{\,V}$ battery, what is the charge on each plate?
Given Data:
  • Area $A = 50\mathrm{\,cm^2} = 50 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,m^2} = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m^2}$
  • Separation $d = 1\mathrm{\,mm} = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m}$
  • $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\mathrm{\,F/m}$
  • Battery voltage $V = 24\mathrm{\,V}$
Part (i): Capacitance $C$: $$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} = \frac{(8.85 \times 10^{-12}\mathrm{\,F/m})(50 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,m^2})}{1.0 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m}}$$ $$C = \frac{4.425 \times 10^{-14}}{1.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 4.425 \times 10^{-11}\mathrm{\,F} = 44.25\mathrm{\,pF}$$ Part (ii): Charge $Q$ on each plate: $$Q = C V = (4.425 \times 10^{-11}\mathrm{\,F})(24\mathrm{\,V}) = 1.062 \times 10^{-9}\mathrm{\,C} = 1.062\mathrm{\,nC}$$ The positive plate holds $+1.062\mathrm{\,nC}$ and the negative plate holds $-1.062\mathrm{\,nC}$.
Textbook Exercise — Determining Dielectric Constant
Chapter 9 Exercise 7 (Textbook Page 151):
The capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor is increased from $8\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ to $50\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ when a sheet of glass is inserted between its plates. What is the dielectric constant of the glass?
Given Data:
  • Original capacitance (without glass) $C_0 = 8\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
  • New capacitance (with glass inserted) $C = 50\,\mu\mathrm{F}$
Calculation:
By the definition of dielectric constant $\kappa$: $$\kappa = \frac{C}{C_0} = \frac{50\,\mu\mathrm{F}}{8\,\mu\mathrm{F}} = 6.25$$ Conclusion:
The dielectric constant of the glass sheet is $\kappa = 6.25$ (dimensionless).
Key Takeaways
  • Parallel-plate capacitance is $C = \frac{\kappa \varepsilon_0 A}{d}$. It increases with larger plate area $A$ and smaller plate spacing $d$.
  • Dielectric constant $\kappa = C / C_0 = \varepsilon / \varepsilon_0 \ge 1$.
  • Inserting a dielectric reduces the internal electric field, increases capacitance by a factor of $\kappa$, and provides higher breakdown voltage resistance.

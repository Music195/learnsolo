Relations between Linear and Angular Quantities

Concept — How Linear and Angular Quantities Connect

When a rigid body rotates about a fixed axis, all of its particles undergo circular motion about that same axis:

  • Common Angular Quantities: Every point on the rigid body turns through the exact same angle $\theta$ in any given time interval, so every point shares the same angular velocity $\omega$ and angular acceleration $\alpha$.
  • Different Linear Quantities: Particles farther from the axis of rotation travel a longer circular arc in the same time. Therefore, linear displacement $s$, linear (tangential) speed $v$, and tangential acceleration $a_T$ increase proportionally with distance $r$ from the axis.
  • Tangential Acceleration ($a_T$): Tangential acceleration is directed tangent to the circular path. It is a direct measure of how fast the tangential speed changes.
Core Formulas — Conversion between Angular and Linear Measures
$$s = r\theta \qquad\qquad v = r\omega \qquad\qquad a_T = r\alpha$$
  • $r$ = perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation to the particle ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $\theta$ = angular displacement ($\mathrm{rad}$, must be in radians)
  • $v$ = linear / tangential speed ($\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$), directed tangent to the circle
  • $\omega$ = angular velocity ($\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$)
  • $a_T$ = tangential acceleration ($\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$), along the tangent
  • $\alpha$ = angular acceleration ($\mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$)

Direction of the Angular Velocity Vector ($\vec{\omega}$): Determined by the Right-Hand Rule. Curl the fingers of your right hand in the direction of rotation; your extended thumb points along the rotation axis in the direction of $\vec{\omega}$.

Direction of $\vec{\alpha}$: If the rotation speeds up, $\vec{\alpha}$ points in the same direction as $\vec{\omega}$. If the rotation slows down, $\vec{\alpha}$ points in the opposite direction.

Worked Examples — Textbook Problems

Example 1.2 (Textbook p. 5): A boy steps on a merry-go-round which has a radius of $5\ \mathrm{m}$ and is at rest. It starts accelerating at a constant rate up to an angular velocity of $5\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ in $20\ \mathrm{s}$. What is the linear distance traversed by the boy?

Show step-by-step solution

Given: $r = 5\ \mathrm{m}$, $\omega_0 = 0$, $\omega = 5\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, $t = 20\ \mathrm{s}$.

First calculate the angular displacement $\theta$:

$$\bar{\omega} = \frac{\omega + \omega_0}{2} = \frac{5 + 0}{2} = 2.5\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$$ $$\theta = \bar{\omega} t = 2.5 \times 20 = 50\ \mathrm{rad}$$

Then find the linear distance $s$ along the arc:

$$s = r\theta = 5\ \mathrm{m} \times 50\ \mathrm{rad} = 250\ \mathrm{m}$$

Answer: The boy traverses a distance of $250\ \mathrm{m}$.

Example 1.3 (Textbook p. 6): The wheel of a car with radius $20\ \mathrm{cm}$ starts moving. The angular acceleration provided by the engine is $12\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$. What is the tangential acceleration of the rim of the wheel?

Show step-by-step solution

Given: $r = 20\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.2\ \mathrm{m}$, $\alpha = 12\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$.

The tangential acceleration of the rim is:

$$a_T = r\alpha = 0.2\ \mathrm{m} \times 12\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}} = 2.4\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$$

The direction of tangential acceleration is tangent to the rim of the wheel.

Answer: $a_T = 2.4\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

Reviewed Exercise 2 (Textbook p. 6): On a rotating carousel, a child sits on a horse near the outer edge and another child sits on a lion halfway out from the centre. (i) Which child has the greater linear velocity? (ii) Which child has the greater angular velocity?

Show step-by-step solution

(i) Linear velocity is given by $v = r\omega$. Since both children rotate on the same carousel, their angular velocity $\omega$ is identical. Because the horse is near the outer edge, $r_{\text{horse}} > r_{\text{lion}}$, so the child on the horse has the greater linear velocity.

(ii) Both children rotate together on the rigid platform, turning through the same angle in any time interval. Therefore, both children have the same angular velocity.

Answer: (i) Child on the horse; (ii) Both have the same angular velocity.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Angles in Degrees: The formulas $s = r\theta$, $v = r\omega$, and $a_T = r\alpha$ are only valid when angles are in radians. If $\theta$ is in degrees or revolutions, convert to radians first ($1\ \text{rev} = 2\pi\ \text{rad}$, $180^\circ = \pi\ \text{rad}$).
  • Confusing Linear Speed with Angular Speed: Two points on the same spinning turntable have the same $\omega$, but their linear speeds $v = r\omega$ are different unless they are at the exact same radius.
  • Tangential vs Centripetal: Tangential acceleration $a_T = r\alpha$ changes the magnitude of velocity (speed), whereas centripetal acceleration $a_c$ changes the direction.
Key Takeaways
  • Every particle in a rigid rotating body shares the exact same angular displacement $\theta$, angular velocity $\omega$, and angular acceleration $\alpha$.
  • Linear counterparts scale directly with distance $r$ from the axis: $s = r\theta$, $v = r\omega$, and $a_T = r\alpha$.
  • The vector $\vec{\omega}$ lies along the axis of rotation following the right-hand grip rule.

Principles of House Wiring and Earthing

Concept — Domestic Distribution, Wire Standards, and Chassis Earthing

Electrical power enters domestic buildings from public distribution transformers through a multi-conductor cable entering a consumer unit (service panel), supplying power to separate parallel circuits designed for safety and efficiency:

  • Standard Three-Core Cable Color Coding:
    Conductor Current Modern Color Old UK Color Electric Potential & Function
    Live (L) Brown Red Oscillates at $220\mathrm{\,V}$ RMS ($+311\mathrm{\,V}$ to $-311\mathrm{\,V}$); carries supply current
    Neutral (N) Blue Black Ground-referenced at substation; nominally at $0\mathrm{\,V}$; completes circuit
    Earth (E) Green & Yellow Green Directly bonded to soil via earth rod ($0\mathrm{\,V}$); carries current only during faults
  • Subcircuit Architecture: All appliances inside a house are connected in parallel across the $220\mathrm{\,V}$ supply so that each operates independently at its rated voltage without being affected when other devices switch on or off:
    1. Lighting Circuit ($5\mathrm{\,A}$ fuse / MCB): Uses lighter gauge wire to power ceiling lamps and fixed luminaires.
    2. Ring Main Power Circuit ($30\mathrm{\,A}$ MCB): A continuous closed loop originating and returning to the consumer unit, supplying $13\mathrm{\,A}$ wall socket outlets.
    3. Dedicated Radial Heating Circuit ($30\text{--}45\mathrm{\,A}$ MCB): Direct heavy cables feeding high-draw devices (electric cookers, geysers, air conditioners).
  • Critical Rule — Switches and Fuses in the Live Wire: Switches, fuses, and circuit breakers must always be placed in the Live (Brown) wire, never the Neutral:
    • When a switch in the Live wire is opened, the entire appliance downstream is completely disconnected from high potential ($V = 0\mathrm{\,V}$ relative to ground) and is safe to service.
    • If a switch were mistakenly wired in the Neutral wire, opening the switch stops the current, but the appliance remains energized at $220\mathrm{\,V}$ live potential. Any grounded user touching internal components would establish a shock path to earth!
  • The Life-Saving Function of Earthing: The conductive metallic outer body (chassis) of Class I appliances (e.g., metal kettles, washing machines, electric stoves) is bonded directly to the Earth pin:
    • Normal Operation: No current flows through the earth conductor.
    • Fault Scenario (Loose Live Wire Touching Metal Case): If the insulation chafes and the live wire makes contact with the chassis:
      1. Because the earth path has negligible electrical resistance ($R_{\text{earth}} \approx 0$), a massive fault current surges instantly from live through the chassis directly to ground.
      2. This high current immediately melts the fuse or trips the Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB) within milliseconds.
      3. The chassis voltage is clamped to near ground potential, completely protecting users from lethal electrocution.
Summary — Earth Fault Clearance Action
$$I_{\text{fault}} = \frac{V_{\text{mains}}}{R_{\text{earth}} + R_{\text{wire}}} \gg I_{\text{fuse}} \qquad (\implies \text{Instant Fuse Blow / MCB Trip in } < 0.1\mathrm{\,s})$$
  • $V_{\text{mains}} = 220\mathrm{\,V}$ = standard domestic alternating supply voltage
  • $R_{\text{earth}}$ = ground loop path resistance (typically $< 1\text{--}2\,\Omega$)
  • $I_{\text{fault}}$ = high short-circuit current triggering instantaneous overcurrent protection
  • $I_{\text{fuse}}$ = calibrated trip threshold rating of the protective breaker

If the lamp is switched off, is it safe to touch? And why does a metal kettle need a third, earth wire?

Wires are coloured by their potential: red = 220 V, blue = 0 V (the same as the ground you stand on). A person gets a shock when their hand is at a different potential from their feet. Switch position: put the switch in the live or the neutral wire, turn the lamp off, and touch the lamp holder. Earth fault: make the live wire touch the metal case, with and without the earth wire.

Conceptual Analysis — Switch Placement Safety
Domestic Safety Evaluation:
Explain why all control switches and protective fuses in domestic wiring must be installed in the Live wire rather than the Neutral wire.
Physical Reasoning:
  • Switch in Live Wire (Correct Design): When the switch is turned OFF, it interrupts the circuit before the high-potential electrical charge can reach the appliance. The internal wiring and heating elements of the appliance sit at $0\mathrm{\,V}$ potential relative to earth, ensuring safety during routine maintenance or bulb replacement.
  • Switch in Neutral Wire (Hazardous Fault): When turned OFF, current ceases to flow because the closed return loop is broken. However, the appliance remains directly connected to the $220\mathrm{\,V}$ Live conductor. If an individual standing on the ground touches the internal element or socket, their body establishes an alternate conductive path to earth ($220\mathrm{\,V} \to \text{Person} \to \text{Earth}$), resulting in a severe electric shock even though the switch is in the "OFF" position.
Key Takeaways
  • Three-core color codes: Live = Brown, Neutral = Blue, Earth = Green & Yellow.
  • Switches and fuses are always placed in the Live wire to isolate appliances from high potential.
  • Earthing: Connects metal appliance bodies to ground; diverts fault currents to instantly trip fuses, eliminating shock risks.

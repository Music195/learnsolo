Alternating Current and AC Waveforms

Concept — Sinusoidal Time-Varying Voltages and Currents

Electrical power in modern utility grids is transmitted almost exclusively via alternating current (AC) rather than direct current (DC):

  • Direct Current (DC): Electric charge carriers flow steadily in a single unidirectional sense throughout the conductor (e.g., electrochemical batteries, fuel cells, DC dynamos).
  • Alternating Current (AC): The magnitude and polarity of electric potential difference oscillate periodically with time, causing mobile electrons to vibrate back and forth about fixed mean positions at a characteristic frequency $f$:
    • Standard Mains Voltages: In Myanmar, Europe, and most Commonwealth nations, domestic supply is standardized at $220\text{--}240\mathrm{\,V}$ at $f = 50\mathrm{\,Hz}$ (period $T = 0.02\mathrm{\,s}$). In North America and parts of Japan, mains voltage is $100\text{--}120\mathrm{\,V}$ at $60\mathrm{\,Hz}$ (or $50\mathrm{\,Hz}$).
  • Sinusoidal Generation in a Rotating Armature: When a plane coil rotates uniformly with angular velocity $\omega$ in a uniform magnetic field, the instantaneous electromotive force and resultant current through a linear resistor follow pure sine functions: $$E(t) = E_0 \sin(\omega t) = E_0 \sin(2\pi f t)$$ $$I(t) = I_0 \sin(\omega t) = I_0 \sin(2\pi f t)$$
  • Waveform Parameters:
    • Peak Value ($E_0, I_0$): The maximum instantaneous amplitude reached in either the positive or negative half-cycle.
    • Angular Frequency ($\omega$): The rotational phase rate in radians per second ($\omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T}$).
    • Frequency ($f$): The number of complete alternating cycles executed per second ($\mathrm{Hz} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}$).
    • Period ($T$): The elapsed time for one complete oscillation cycle ($T = \frac{1}{f}$).
  • Strategic Advantages of AC over DC:
    1. Transformer Capability: AC voltages can be stepped up to hundreds of kilovolts for ultra-low-loss transmission and stepped down safely for consumer use.
    2. Minimal Long-Distance Joule Loss: Transmitting power at high voltage $V$ drastically reduces required transmission current $I = P/V$, cutting line resistive heating $I^2 R$ by orders of magnitude.
    3. Simplicity of AC Machinery: Induction motors and alternator generators have no fragile commutators or mechanical brushes.
Core Formulas — Sinusoidal AC Waveforms
$$E(t) = E_0 \sin(\omega t) \qquad\qquad I(t) = I_0 \sin(\omega t) \qquad\qquad \omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T} \qquad\qquad I_0 = \frac{E_0}{R}$$
  • $E(t), I(t)$ = instantaneous emf ($\mathrm{V}$) and current ($\mathrm{A}$) at time $t$ ($\mathrm{s}$)
  • $E_0, I_0$ = peak (maximum) amplitude of voltage ($\mathrm{V}$) and current ($\mathrm{A}$)
  • $\omega$ = angular frequency ($\mathrm{rad/s}$)
  • $f$ = cyclical frequency ($\mathrm{Hz}$)
  • $T$ = period of one complete cycle ($\mathrm{s}$)
  • $R$ = connected pure resistive load ($\Omega$)

Worked Example 11.1 — AC Generator Parameter Analysis
Textbook Example 11.1 (Page 174):
An AC generator produces an instantaneous electromotive force given by the equation: $$E(t) = 150 \sin(377 t)\mathrm{\quad(Volts)}$$ (i) Find the maximum EMF output, the cyclical frequency $f$, and the instantaneous EMF at $t = \frac{1}{120}\mathrm{\,s}$.
(ii) If the generator is connected across a $50\,\Omega$ pure resistor, find the maximum circuit current.
Part (i): Comparison with General Equation $E = E_0 \sin(\omega t)$:
  • Maximum EMF Output: $E_0 = 150\mathrm{\,V}$.
  • Angular Frequency: $\omega = 377\mathrm{\,rad/s}$. Since $\omega = 2\pi f$: $$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{377\mathrm{\,rad/s}}{2 \times 3.1416} \approx 60.0\mathrm{\,Hz}$$
  • Instantaneous EMF at $t = \frac{1}{120}\mathrm{\,s}$: $$\omega t = 377 \times \frac{1}{120} = \frac{377}{120} \approx 3.1416\mathrm{\,rad} = \pi\mathrm{\,rad}$$ $$E\left(\frac{1}{120}\right) = 150 \sin(\pi) = 150 \times 0 = 0\mathrm{\,V}$$
Part (ii): Maximum Circuit Current $I_0$: $$I_0 = \frac{E_0}{R} = \frac{150\mathrm{\,V}}{50\,\Omega} = 3.0\mathrm{\,A}$$ The current waveform is $I(t) = 3.0 \sin(377 t)\mathrm{\,A}$.
Worked Example 11.2 — Instantaneous Current from Sinusoidal Expression
Textbook Example 11.2 (Page 175):
The current in an AC circuit at any time $t$ seconds is given by $I = 20 \sin(100\pi t)\mathrm{\,A}$. Find:
(i) The peak current, the frequency, and the period of the alternating current.
(ii) The instantaneous current at $t = \frac{1}{600}\mathrm{\,s}$.
Part (i): Parameters:
  • Peak Current: $I_0 = 20\mathrm{\,A}$
  • Angular Frequency: $\omega = 100\pi\mathrm{\,rad/s}$
  • Frequency: $f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{100\pi}{2\pi} = 50\mathrm{\,Hz}$
  • Period: $T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{50\mathrm{\,Hz}} = 0.02\mathrm{\,s} = 20\mathrm{\,ms}$
Part (ii): Instantaneous Current at $t = \frac{1}{600}\mathrm{\,s}$: $$I = 20 \sin\left(100\pi \times \frac{1}{600}\right) = 20 \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right) = 20 \sin(30^\circ)$$ $$I = 20 \times 0.5 = 10.0\mathrm{\,A}$$
Key Takeaways
  • AC voltages and currents vary sinusoidally: $E = E_0 \sin(\omega t)$ and $I = I_0 \sin(\omega t)$, with $\omega = 2\pi f$.
  • Myanmar mains standard: $220\text{--}240\mathrm{\,V}$ at $50\mathrm{\,Hz}$ ($T = 0.02\mathrm{\,s}$).
  • AC is globally chosen for power distribution because it enables low-loss high-voltage transmission via transformers.

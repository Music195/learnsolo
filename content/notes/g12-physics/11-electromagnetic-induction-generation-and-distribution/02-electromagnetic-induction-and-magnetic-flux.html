Electromagnetic Induction and Magnetic Flux

Concept — Magnetic Flux and the Discovery of Induction

In 1831, Michael Faraday in England and Joseph Henry in the United States independently discovered that electricity could be generated from magnetism. This phenomenon is known as electromagnetic induction:

  • The Induction Phenomenon: Whenever the magnetic lines of force threading through a closed conducting circuit change over time, an electromotive force (and hence an electric current) is momentarily induced in the circuit. The effect persists only while the magnetic field or loop geometry is in active transition.
  • Magnetic Flux Density ($B$) and Permeability ($\mu$): The magnetic flux density $\mathbf{B}$ within a medium is related to magnetic field intensity $\mathbf{H}$ by: $$B = \mu H$$ where $\mu = \mu_r \mu_0$ is the magnetic permeability ($\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,T\cdot m/A}$ in vacuum). The SI unit of $B$ is the Tesla ($\mathrm{T}$) or $\mathrm{Wb/m^2}$.
  • Definition of Magnetic Flux ($\Phi$): Magnetic flux quantifies the total number of magnetic field lines penetrating a surface of area $A$: $$\Phi = \mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{A} = B A \cos\theta$$ where $\theta$ is the angle between the magnetic field vector $\mathbf{B}$ and the unit normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ perpendicular to the surface.
    • SI Unit: Weber ($\mathrm{Wb}$). $1\mathrm{\,Wb} = 1\mathrm{\,T\cdot m^2} = 1\mathrm{\,V\cdot s}$.
    • Maximum Flux ($\Phi_{\max} = B A$): Occurs when the magnetic field is directed perpendicular to the plane of the loop ($\theta = 0^\circ, \cos 0^\circ = 1$).
    • Zero Flux ($\Phi = 0$): Occurs when the magnetic field lies parallel to the plane of the loop ($\theta = 90^\circ, \cos 90^\circ = 0$). No field lines pierce the surface.
  • Ways to Change Magnetic Flux: Since $\Phi = B A \cos\theta$, an induced emf can be created by altering any of three factors:
    1. Changing the magnetic field magnitude $B$ (e.g., moving a permanent magnet or ramping electromagnet current).
    2. Changing the effective loop area $A$ inside the field (e.g., squeezing or stretching a flexible loop).
    3. Changing the orientation angle $\theta$ over time (e.g., rotating an armature coil in a generator).
Core Formulas — Magnetic Flux and Flux Density
$$\Phi = B A \cos\theta \qquad\qquad \Phi_{\max} = B A \qquad\qquad B = \mu H \qquad\qquad 1\mathrm{\,Wb} = 1\mathrm{\,T\cdot m^2}$$
  • $\Phi$ = magnetic flux through the closed loop surface ($\mathrm{Wb}$)
  • $B$ = magnetic flux density ($\mathrm{T}$ or $\mathrm{Wb/m^2}$)
  • $A$ = surface area enclosed by the loop ($\mathrm{m^2}$)
  • $\theta$ = angle between magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ and surface normal $\mathbf{n}$
  • $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,T\cdot m/A}$ = permeability of free space

Worked Example 11.3 — Maximum and Minimum Magnetic Flux
Textbook Example 11.3 (Page 178):
A square loop of wire $10\mathrm{\,cm}$ on a side is immersed in a uniform magnetic field of $1.25\mathrm{\,T}$. What are the maximum and minimum magnetic fluxes through the loop?
Given Data:
  • Side of square $s = 10\mathrm{\,cm} = 0.10\mathrm{\,m}$
  • Loop surface area $A = s^2 = (0.10\mathrm{\,m})^2 = 0.01\mathrm{\,m^2} = 1.0 \times 10^{-2}\mathrm{\,m^2}$
  • Magnetic field $B = 1.25\mathrm{\,T}$
Part 1: Maximum Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_{\max}$):
Occurs when the magnetic field is perpendicular to the loop plane ($\theta = 0^\circ$ relative to normal): $$\Phi_{\max} = B A \cos(0^\circ) = (1.25\mathrm{\,T}) \times (0.01\mathrm{\,m^2}) \times 1 = 0.0125\mathrm{\,Wb} = 1.25 \times 10^{-2}\mathrm{\,Wb}$$ Part 2: Minimum Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_{\min}$):
Occurs when the magnetic field is parallel to the loop surface plane ($\theta = 90^\circ$ to normal): $$\Phi_{\min} = B A \cos(90^\circ) = (1.25\mathrm{\,T}) \times (0.01\mathrm{\,m^2}) \times 0 = 0\mathrm{\,Wb}$$
Textbook Exercise — Tilted Loop Magnetic Flux
Chapter 11 Exercise 9 (Textbook Page 193):
A square loop of wire of side $5.0\mathrm{\,cm}$ is placed in a uniform magnetic field of $0.16\mathrm{\,T}$. What is the magnetic flux in the loop when:
(i) the magnetic field is perpendicular to the surface of the loop?
(ii) the magnetic field is inclined at an angle of $30^\circ$ to the surface of the loop?
Given Data:
  • $s = 5.0\mathrm{\,cm} = 0.05\mathrm{\,m} \implies A = (0.05\mathrm{\,m})^2 = 2.5 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m^2}$
  • $B = 0.16\mathrm{\,T}$
Part (i): Field Perpendicular to Surface:
Here, the angle between field $\mathbf{B}$ and surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ is $\theta = 0^\circ$: $$\Phi = B A \cos(0^\circ) = (0.16\mathrm{\,T}) \times (2.5 \times 10^{-3}\mathrm{\,m^2}) \times 1 = 4.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,Wb}$$ Part (ii): Field at $30^\circ$ to Surface Plane:
The angle with the surface normal vector is $\theta = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$: $$\Phi = B A \cos(60^\circ) = (4.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,Wb}) \times 0.5 = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,Wb}$$
Key Takeaways
  • Electromagnetic induction produces an electromotive force whenever magnetic flux changes through a circuit.
  • Magnetic flux is $\Phi = B A \cos\theta$ ($\mathrm{Wb}$).
  • $\Phi$ is maximum when $\mathbf{B}$ is perpendicular to loop plane ($\theta = 0^\circ$); zero when parallel ($\theta = 90^\circ$).

Applications of Heating Effect and Electrical Safety

Concept — Domestic Heating Appliances and Overcurrent Protection

The controlled conversion of electrical work into heat via Joule dissipation ($W = I^2 R t$) is exploited in domestic and industrial appliances, while uncontrolled heating poses severe fire hazards that necessitate safety devices:

  • Incandescent Filament Lamps:
    • Tungsten Filament ($W$): Chosen for its exceptionally high melting point ($3400^\circ\mathrm{C}$), high electrical resistivity, and mechanical ductility enabling fine coiling.
    • Inert Gas Atmosphere: The sealed glass bulb is filled with a mixture of inactive gases (argon and nitrogen at low pressure). This prevents atmospheric oxygen from burning the white-hot filament and suppresses tungsten evaporation, extending lamp life.
    • Efficiency: Only $\approx 5\text{--}10\%$ of electrical energy is converted into visible light; the remaining $90\text{--}95\%$ is dissipated as infrared radiation (heat).
  • Electric Heating Elements (Irons, Stoves, Toasters, Geysers):
    • Nichrome Alloy: Composed of $80\%$ Nickel and $20\%$ Chromium.
    • Key Properties of Nichrome:
      1. High Resistivity: Generates concentrated Joule heat across compact element lengths.
      2. High Melting Point ($\approx 1400^\circ\mathrm{C}$): Operates safely at glowing red-hot temperatures without softening.
      3. Superior Oxidation Resistance: Forms an adherent self-passivating surface layer of chromium oxide ($\mathrm{Cr_2O_3}$) that prevents ongoing oxidation and burnout in air.
    • Insulation: Wound around or encased in pure mica or compacted magnesium oxide ($\mathrm{MgO}$), which are excellent electrical insulators but effective heat conductors.
  • Electric Fuse and Circuit Safety:
    • Construction: A short, thin length of wire made of a tin-lead alloy ($63\%\text{ Sn}, 37\%\text{ Pb}$) possessing high electrical resistivity and a low melting point ($\approx 183\text{--}230^\circ\mathrm{C}$).
    • Placement: Must always be wired in series with the live (phase) wire upstream of all protected branch circuits.
    • Operating Principle: During an overcurrent or short-circuit event ($I > I_{\text{rated}}$), excessive Joule heating ($I^2 R t$) swiftly raises the wire temperature beyond its melting point. The fuse wire melts, creating an open circuit that immediately interrupts current flow and safeguards appliances and wiring against fire.
    • Why Copper, Iron, or Silver Cannot Be Used as Fuse Wire: Silver and copper have excessively high conductivity (low resistance, so they do not produce sufficient heat to melt promptly) and high melting points ($> 1000^\circ\mathrm{C}$). Iron oxidizes readily and has an excessively high melting point ($1538^\circ\mathrm{C}$), failing to protect sensitive circuitry.

Worked Problem — Fuse Rating and Safe Additional Load
Chapter 10 Exercise 23 (Textbook Page 171):
An electrical installation in an office contains a $10\mathrm{\,A}$ fuse on a $220\mathrm{\,V}$ mains supply. Currently, ten $100\mathrm{\,W}$ electric lamps and two $150\mathrm{\,W}$ refrigerators are operated simultaneously.
(i) Find the total cost of operating all lamps and the two refrigerators for $10\mathrm{\,h}$ if electricity costs $50\mathrm{\,kyats}$ per unit.
(ii) Find the maximum number of additional $60\mathrm{\,W}$ electric lamps that can be safely switched on without blowing the fuse.
Given Data:
  • $V = 220\mathrm{\,V}$, Fuse rating $I_{\text{fuse}} = 10\mathrm{\,A}$
  • Existing load: $10 \times 100\mathrm{\,W} + 2 \times 150\mathrm{\,W} = 1000\mathrm{\,W} + 300\mathrm{\,W} = 1300\mathrm{\,W}$
  • Tariff = $50\mathrm{\,kyats/unit}$
Part (i): Energy Consumption and Cost for $10\mathrm{\,h}$: $$\text{Units Consumed} = \frac{\text{Total Power (W)} \times \text{Time (h)}}{1000} = \frac{1300\mathrm{\,W} \times 10\mathrm{\,h}}{1000} = 13.0\mathrm{\,kWh} = 13\mathrm{\,units}$$ $$\text{Total Cost} = 13\mathrm{\,units} \times 50\mathrm{\,kyats/unit} = 650\mathrm{\,kyats}$$ Part (ii): Additional $60\mathrm{\,W}$ Lamps:
  • Maximum safe power capacity of the $10\mathrm{\,A}$ fuse: $$P_{\max} = V \times I_{\text{fuse}} = 220\mathrm{\,V} \times 10\mathrm{\,A} = 2200\mathrm{\,W}$$
  • Spare electrical power available: $$P_{\text{available}} = P_{\max} - P_{\text{current}} = 2200\mathrm{\,W} - 1300\mathrm{\,W} = 900\mathrm{\,W}$$
  • Maximum number of $60\mathrm{\,W}$ lamps: $$N = \frac{P_{\text{available}}}{60\mathrm{\,W}} = \frac{900}{60} = 15\mathrm{\,lamps}$$
Conclusion: Up to 15 additional $60\mathrm{\,W}$ lamps can be safely operated without tripping the $10\mathrm{\,A}$ fuse.
Key Takeaways
  • Tungsten filament: Melting point $3400^\circ\mathrm{C}$, inert gas prevents oxidation and reduces vaporization.
  • Nichrome elements: High resistivity, melting point $1400^\circ\mathrm{C}$, durable protective oxide film.
  • Electric fuse: Low melting point tin-lead alloy placed in series with the Live wire to automatically disconnect circuits during overload or short-circuit.
  • Maximum safe connected power is $P_{\max} = V_{\text{mains}} \times I_{\text{fuse}}$.

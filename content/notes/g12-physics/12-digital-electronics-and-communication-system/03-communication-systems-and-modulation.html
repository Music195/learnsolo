Communication Systems and Modulation

Concept — Telecommunication Architecture, Carrier Modulation, and Tuning

A communication system transfers information (audio, video, or digital binary data) from a transmission source to a distant destination across guided or unguided channels:

  • Universal Block Architecture of Communication:
    1. Information Source & Input Transducer: Produces baseband audio/video signals (e.g., microphone converting acoustic air compressions into a $20\mathrm{\,Hz}\text{--}20\mathrm{\,kHz}$ electrical waveform).
    2. Transmitter: Comprises an RF oscillator generating a high-frequency carrier wave, a modulator that encodes the baseband audio onto the carrier, a power amplifier, and a transmitting antenna.
    3. Transmission Channel (Medium):
      • Guided Channels: Coaxial cables, twisted copper pairs, and optical fibers (total internal reflection of laser pulses).
      • Unguided Channels: Free space / atmosphere via electromagnetic waves (ground waves, ionospheric sky waves, line-of-sight space waves, and geostationary satellite transponders).
    4. Receiver: Intercepts weak microvolt electromagnetic waves via receiving antenna, selects the desired station frequency via an $LC$ resonant tuning circuit, amplifies the RF signal, extracts the original baseband signal via a demodulator (detector), and delivers it to an output transducer (loudspeaker).
  • Why Modulation is Necessary: Baseband acoustic signals ($20\mathrm{\,Hz}\text{--}20\mathrm{\,kHz}$) cannot be directly radiated into space as radio waves:
    1. Antenna Length Constraint: An efficient dipole antenna must have a physical length comparable to a quarter wavelength ($L \approx \frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{c}{4f}$). For a $15\mathrm{\,kHz}$ audio wave, $\lambda = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{15{,}000} = 20{,}000\mathrm{\,m} = 20\mathrm{\,km}$, demanding an impossible $5\mathrm{\,km}$ tall antenna tower! Modulating onto a $100\mathrm{\,MHz}$ FM carrier shrinks the required antenna length to just $75\mathrm{\,cm}$.
    2. Excessive Signal Attenuation: Low-frequency electromagnetic waves possess low photon energy and dissipate completely over short distances.
    3. Channel Cross-Talk Interference: If multiple radio stations radiated baseband audio simultaneously, all broadcasts would overlap into an unintelligible acoustic cacophony. Modulation shifts each broadcast to a unique carrier frequency band (Frequency Division Multiplexing).
  • Types of Modulation:
    • Amplitude Modulation (AM): The instantaneous amplitude of the high-frequency carrier wave is modulated in direct proportion to the modulating audio signal, while carrier frequency $f_c$ and phase remain fixed. Susceptible to static atmospheric lightning noise.
    • Frequency Modulation (FM): The instantaneous frequency of the carrier wave deviates around $f_c$ in proportion to the modulating signal, while carrier amplitude remains strictly constant. Because atmospheric noise alters amplitude rather than frequency, FM provides crystal-clear, high-fidelity audio reception.
    • Phase Modulation (PM): The phase angle of the carrier wave is shifted proportionally to the modulating signal.
  • Tuning Circuit Resonance: A parallel inductor-capacitor ($LC$) tank circuit selects the desired broadcast station frequency while rejecting all adjacent carrier channels when its natural resonant frequency matches the incoming carrier: $$f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$$
Core Formulas — Resonant Tuning and Antenna Length
$$f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} \qquad\qquad \lambda = \frac{c}{f} \qquad\qquad L_{\text{antenna}} \approx \frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{c}{4f}$$
  • $f_0$ = resonant frequency of receiver tuning circuit ($\mathrm{Hz}$)
  • $L$ = tuning inductor inductance ($\mathrm{H}$ or $\mu\mathrm{H}$)
  • $C$ = tuning capacitor capacitance ($\mathrm{F}$ or $\mathrm{pF}$)
  • $\lambda$ = electromagnetic wavelength in free space ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\mathrm{\,m/s}$ = speed of light in vacuum
  • $L_{\text{antenna}}$ = theoretical quarter-wave resonant antenna length ($\mathrm{m}$)

Worked Problem — Resonant Frequency of Receiver Tuning Circuit
Tuning Circuit Design:
The tuning circuit of an AM broadcast receiver consists of a fixed inductor of $L = 200\,\mu\mathrm{H}$ and a variable capacitor $C$ adjustable between $20\mathrm{\,pF}$ and $200\mathrm{\,pF}$. Calculate the minimum and maximum frequencies of the radio stations that can be tuned by this circuit.
Given Data:
  • $L = 200\,\mu\mathrm{H} = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,H}$
  • $C_{\min} = 20\mathrm{\,pF} = 2.0 \times 10^{-11}\mathrm{\,F}$
  • $C_{\max} = 200\mathrm{\,pF} = 2.0 \times 10^{-10}\mathrm{\,F}$
Step 1: Calculate Minimum Tunable Frequency ($f_{\min}$ at $C_{\max}$): $$f_{\min} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L C_{\max}}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(2.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,H})(2.0 \times 10^{-10}\mathrm{\,F})}}$$ $$\sqrt{L C_{\max}} = \sqrt{4.0 \times 10^{-14}} = 2.0 \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,s}$$ $$f_{\min} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times (2.0 \times 10^{-7})} = \frac{1}{1.2566 \times 10^{-6}} \approx 7.96 \times 10^5\mathrm{\,Hz} = 796\mathrm{\,kHz}$$ Step 2: Calculate Maximum Tunable Frequency ($f_{\max}$ at $C_{\min}$): $$f_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L C_{\min}}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(2.0 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,H})(2.0 \times 10^{-11}\mathrm{\,F})}}$$ $$\sqrt{L C_{\min}} = \sqrt{4.0 \times 10^{-15}} \approx 6.3245 \times 10^{-8}\mathrm{\,s}$$ $$f_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times (6.3245 \times 10^{-8})} \approx 2.52 \times 10^6\mathrm{\,Hz} = 2520\mathrm{\,kHz} = 2.52\mathrm{\,MHz}$$ Conclusion:
The receiver tunes across the entire Medium Wave (MW) broadcast band from $796\mathrm{\,kHz}$ to $2520\mathrm{\,kHz}$.
Key Takeaways
  • Communication systems follow: Source → Transmitter → Channel → Receiver → Destination.
  • Modulation translates low-frequency audio to high-frequency RF carrier waves, shrinking antenna dimensions to practical size and preventing channel overlap.
  • AM: Carrier amplitude varies; simpler circuitry but noisy.
  • FM: Carrier frequency varies; constant amplitude grants high noise rejection.
  • Tuning frequency is given by Thomson's formula: $f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$.

Digital Electronics and Logic Gates

Concept — Binary Information Processing and Boolean Logic

Electronic circuits process information in either continuous or discrete domains, with digital architectures offering superior noise immunity and computational flexibility:

  • Analog vs. Digital Signals:
    • Analog Signal: A voltage or current waveform that varies continuously over time, taking on an infinite range of values (e.g., pure audio waves, natural temperature fluctuations). Highly vulnerable to cumulative thermal noise and transmission distortion.
    • Digital Signal: A signal that switches between two discrete voltage levels representing the binary states $1$ (HIGH, typically $+5\mathrm{\,V}$) and $0$ (LOW, typically $0\mathrm{\,V}$). Minor electrical noise is rejected during threshold regeneration.
  • Fundamental Logic Gates: Building blocks of digital combinational circuits that evaluate Boolean algebraic functions:
    1. NOT Gate (Inverter): Single-input gate that inverts binary logic: $$Y = \bar{A} \qquad (1 \to 0,\quad 0 \to 1)$$
    2. AND Gate: Output is HIGH ($1$) only if all inputs are HIGH ($1$): $$Y = A \cdot B$$
    3. OR Gate: Output is HIGH ($1$) if at least one input is HIGH ($1$): $$Y = A + B$$
    4. NAND Gate (NOT-AND): Inverted output of an AND gate. Output is LOW ($0$) only when both inputs are HIGH: $$Y = \overline{A \cdot B}$$
    5. NOR Gate (NOT-OR): Inverted output of an OR gate. Output is HIGH ($1$) only when both inputs are LOW ($0$): $$Y = \overline{A + B}$$
    6. XOR Gate (Exclusive OR): Output is HIGH ($1$) if the inputs are different ($0,1$ or $1,0$), and LOW ($0$) if inputs are identical: $$Y = A \oplus B = \bar{A}B + A\bar{B}$$
  • De Morgan's Theorems: Foundational rules in Boolean algebra simplifying complex digital logic expressions: $$\text{Theorem 1: } \overline{A \cdot B} = \bar{A} + \bar{B} \qquad (\text{Break the line, change the sign})$$ $$\text{Theorem 2: } \overline{A + B} = \bar{A} \cdot \bar{B}$$
  • Universal Gates: NAND and NOR gates are termed universal gates because any arbitrary Boolean logic function (NOT, AND, OR, XOR) can be constructed exclusively using only NAND gates or only NOR gates.

Worked Problem — Synthesizing an OR Gate from Universal NAND Gates
Chapter 12 Exercise 2 (Textbook Page 212):
A digital circuit consists of two inverters (NOT gates) whose outputs feed into a two-input NAND gate.
(a) Write the Boolean expression and truth table for the overall output $Y$.
(b) Identify which single elementary logic gate this composite circuit is equivalent to.
Step 1: Formulate Boolean Expression:
  • Input to first inverter is $A$, giving output $\bar{A}$.
  • Input to second inverter is $B$, giving output $\bar{B}$.
  • These inputs enter the NAND gate, yielding: $$Y = \overline{\bar{A} \cdot \bar{B}}$$
  • Applying De Morgan's First Theorem ($\overline{X \cdot Y} = \bar{X} + \bar{Y}$): $$Y = \bar{\bar{A}} + \bar{\bar{B}} = A + B$$
Step 2: Construct the Truth Table:
$A$ $B$ $\bar{A}$ $\bar{B}$ $Y = \overline{\bar{A} \cdot \bar{B}} = A + B$
00110
01101
10011
11001
Conclusion:
The circuit produces output $1$ whenever either input is $1$. It is completely equivalent to a standard two-input OR gate ($Y = A + B$).
Key Takeaways
  • Digital systems use binary voltage levels: $1$ (HIGH, $+5\mathrm{\,V}$) and $0$ (LOW, $0\mathrm{\,V}$), providing strong noise rejection.
  • Primary gate functions: NOT ($Y = \bar{A}$), AND ($Y = A \cdot B$), OR ($Y = A + B$), XOR ($Y = A \oplus B$).
  • De Morgan's Laws: $\overline{A \cdot B} = \bar{A} + \bar{B}$ and $\overline{A + B} = \bar{A} \cdot \bar{B}$.
  • NAND and NOR are universal gates capable of synthesizing any digital logic function.

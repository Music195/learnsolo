Environmental Impacts and Energy Transformations

Concept — Ecological Costs of Power Generation

Every method of harvesting, converting, and consuming energy leaves an ecological footprint on Earth's biosphere:

  • 1. Environmental Impacts of Conventional (Fossil & Nuclear) Sources:
    • Global Warming & Greenhouse Effect: Combusting fossil fuels releases vast volumes of carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO_2}$), methane ($\mathrm{CH_4}$), and nitrous oxide ($\mathrm{N_2O}$). These gases accumulate in the troposphere, trapping outgoing terrestrial infrared radiation and driving global climate change. Halocarbons (CFCs) additionally catalyze catalytic breakdown of the stratospheric ozone layer ($\mathrm{O_3}$).
    • Acid Rain: Burning high-sulfur coal and heavy oils emits sulfur dioxide ($\mathrm{SO_2}$) and nitrogen oxides ($\mathrm{NO_x}$). These react with atmospheric moisture and oxidants to produce sulfuric acid ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$) and nitric acid ($\mathrm{HNO_3}$). Acid rain leaches nutrients from soils, acidifies freshwater lakes, stunts forest growth, and corrodes architectural structures.
    • Leaded Gasoline Emissions: The historic addition of tetraethyl lead to petrol releases airborne lead particulates. According to the WHO, lead exposure causes hypertension and cardiovascular illness in adults, and produces irreversible cognitive impairment, reduced IQ, and behavioral disorders in children.
    • Oil Spills & Marine Destruction: Tanker collisions, pipeline ruptures, and offshore drilling blowouts discharge toxic petroleum slicks that destroy coastal wetlands, contaminate fisheries, and kill marine wildlife.
    • Coal Mining Degredation: Strip mining decimates landscapes, while runoff contaminated with pyritic sulfides causes severe acid mine drainage that sterilizes river systems.
  • 2. Environmental Impacts of Renewable (Non-Conventional) Sources:
    • Solar Power: Operates silently with zero emissions, but requires energy-intensive mining, toxic chemicals (cadmium, lead, silicon tetrachloride) during PV manufacturing, and large surface land footprints.
    • Wind Power: Lowest lifecycle greenhouse gas intensity, but generates localized aerodynamic noise, low-frequency infrasound, visual shadow flicker, and avian collision hazards for migrating birds and bats.
    • Hydroelectric Dams: Flooding behind large dams drowns vast forests and displaces indigenous communities; submerged decaying biomass generates anaerobic methane emissions; dams trap nutrient-rich river sediments, obstruct fish migration routes, and release thermally altered water that disrupts downstream ecosystems.
    • Geothermal Energy: Releases minimal greenhouse gases, but can bring trace hydrogen sulfide ($\mathrm{H_2S}$) and heavy metals to the surface and cause minor induced micro-seismicity.
    • Biomass Combustion: Carbon-neutral in principle if replanted at equal rates, but direct burning generates particulate smoke, carbon monoxide, and smog-forming nitrogen oxides.

Chapter Quantitative Exercises — Hydropower and Wind Energy

Exercise 10: An important hydroelectric plant has a head of $100\text{ m}$ and a water volumetric flow rate of $10\,000\text{ m}^3\,\text{s}^{-1}$. The turbine-generator efficiency is $60\%$. What is the maximum power that the plant can produce? (Density of water $= 1000\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m\,s}^{-2}$).

Solution:
Given: $h = 100\text{ m}$, $Q = 10\,000\text{ m}^3\,\text{s}^{-1}$, $\eta = 0.60$, $\rho = 1000\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m\,s}^{-2}$.
Using $P = \eta \rho Q g h$: $$P = 0.60 \times 1000 \times 10\,000 \times 9.8 \times 100$$ $$P = 6\,000\,000 \times 980 = 5\,880\,000\,000\text{ W} = 5880\text{ MW} = 5.88\text{ GW}$$

Exercise 11: A dam with a maximum head of $200\text{ m}$ produces $2000\text{ MW}$ of electrical power. What is the rate of falling water (mass flow rate) on the turbines if the turbine-generator efficiency is $50\%$? (Density of water $= 1000\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$).

Solution:
Given: $h = 200\text{ m}$, $P = 2000\text{ MW} = 2 \times 10^9\text{ W}$, $\eta = 50\% = 0.50$, $g = 9.8\text{ m\,s}^{-2}$.
Using $P = \eta \left(\frac{m}{t}\right) g h$: $$\frac{m}{t} = \frac{P}{\eta g h} = \frac{2 \times 10^9\text{ W}}{0.50 \times 9.8\text{ m\,s}^{-2} \times 200\text{ m}} = \frac{2 \times 10^9}{980} \approx 2.04 \times 10^6\text{ kg\,s}^{-1}$$ This corresponds to a volumetric discharge rate of: $$Q = \frac{m / t}{\rho} = \frac{2.04 \times 10^6\text{ kg\,s}^{-1}}{1000\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}} = 2041\text{ m}^3\,\text{s}^{-1}$$

Exercise 12: The wind is blowing at $10\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$. How much total kinetic power is incident on a wind turbine if its blades are $45\text{ m}$ long? The density of air is $1.3\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$.

Solution:
Rotor swept radius $r = 45\text{ m}$.
Swept area $A = \pi r^2 = 3.142 \times (45)^2 = 3.142 \times 2025 \approx 6362.5\text{ m}^2$.
Wind speed $v = 10\text{ m\,s}^{-1} \implies v^3 = 1000\text{ m}^3\,\text{s}^{-3}$.
Using $P = \frac{1}{2}\rho A v^3$: $$P = \frac{1}{2}(1.3)(6362.5)(1000) = 0.65 \times 6362.5 \times 1000 \approx 4.136 \times 10^6\text{ W} = 4.14\text{ MW}$$

Key Takeaways
  • Combusting fossil fuels emits greenhouse gases ($\mathrm{CO_2}$, $\mathrm{CH_4}$) driving global warming and $\mathrm{SO_2}/\mathrm{NO_x}$ generating destructive acid rain.
  • Leaded fuels release neurotoxic lead compounds that cause severe cognitive developmental impairment in children.
  • Hydropower dams cause upstream flooding, methane emissions from submerged foliage, downstream sediment loss, and aquatic habitat disruption.
  • Every energy production process follows a sequential energy transformation chain subject to thermodynamic efficiency limits.

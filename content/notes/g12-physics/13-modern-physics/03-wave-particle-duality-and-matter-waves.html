Wave-Particle Duality and Matter Waves

Concept — De Broglie Matter Waves and Standing Orbit Quantization

In classical mechanics, waves and particles were viewed as mutually exclusive physical entities. Quantum physics revealed that both radiation and material particles possess an intrinsic wave-particle dual nature:

  • De Broglie's Matter Wave Hypothesis (1924): Louis de Broglie reasoned that nature loves symmetry. If electromagnetic radiation behaves as waves in propagation (interference, diffraction) and as discrete particles/photons in absorption and emission (photoelectric effect), then material particles (electrons, protons, neutrons) in motion must likewise possess associated matter waves: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}$$ where $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\mathrm{\,J\cdot s}$ is Planck's constant, $m$ is particle mass, and $v$ is velocity.
  • Dependence on Kinetic Energy and Accelerating Voltage: For a non-relativistic particle of mass $m$ and kinetic energy $E_k = \frac{p^2}{2m}$: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m E_k}}$$ When a charge $q$ (such as an electron with charge $e$) is accelerated from rest through a potential difference $V$, its kinetic energy is $E_k = qV$: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m q V}}$$ For an electron ($m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}\mathrm{\,kg}$, $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\mathrm{\,C}$): $$\lambda_{\text{electron}} \approx \frac{1.227\mathrm{\,nm}}{\sqrt{V}} = \frac{1.227 \times 10^{-9}\mathrm{\,m}}{\sqrt{V}}$$ For $V = 100\mathrm{\,V}$, $\lambda \approx 0.123\mathrm{\,nm}$ ($1.23\text{ \AA}$), which is exactly comparable to interatomic spacing in crystalline solids.
  • Experimental Verification:
    1. Davisson-Germer Experiment (1927): Fired low-energy electrons onto a nickel single crystal, observing sharp diffraction maxima at angles strictly matching Bragg's diffraction law for matter waves.
    2. G.P. Thomson Experiment (1927): Demonstrated concentric circular diffraction rings by passing high-speed cathode rays through thin polycrystalline gold foil.
  • De Broglie's Derivation of Bohr's Angular Momentum Postulate: In 1913, Niels Bohr postulated ad hoc that orbital angular momentum in hydrogen is quantized ($L = n\frac{h}{2\pi}$). De Broglie provided the physical rationale:
    • An electron orbiting a nucleus forms a closed circular standing wave. To prevent destructive self-interference over successive revolutions, the circumference of the orbit must contain an integer number of de Broglie wavelengths: $$2\pi r = n\lambda \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$
    • Substituting $\lambda = \frac{h}{mv}$: $$2\pi r = n\left(\frac{h}{mv}\right) \implies m v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi} = n\hbar$$ Hence, Bohr's quantization condition is the natural consequence of standing matter waves!
Core Formulas — De Broglie Wavelength and Standing Orbits
$$\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m E_k}} \qquad\qquad \lambda_e = \frac{1.227\mathrm{\,nm}}{\sqrt{V}} \qquad\qquad 2\pi r_n = n\lambda$$
  • $\lambda$ = de Broglie matter wavelength ($\mathrm{m}$ or $\mathrm{nm}$)
  • $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\mathrm{\,J\cdot s}$ = Planck's constant
  • $p = mv$ = particle linear momentum ($\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$)
  • $E_k = qV$ = kinetic energy gained across potential difference $V$ ($\mathrm{J}$)
  • $r_n$ = radius of the $n$-th stationary circular orbit ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ = principal quantum number (number of standing wavelengths)

Worked Problem — Accelerated Electron De Broglie Wavelength
Chapter 13 Exercise 14 (Textbook Page 247):
In older radios, vacuum tubes generated and accelerated electrons across a potential difference of $100\mathrm{\,V}$. Find:
(a) The final speed attained by an electron accelerated from rest.
(b) The de Broglie wavelength of the electron ($m_e = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\mathrm{\,kg}$, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\mathrm{\,C}$, $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\mathrm{\,J\cdot s}$).
Given Data:
  • $V = 100\mathrm{\,V}$
  • $E_k = e V = (1.6 \times 10^{-19}\mathrm{\,C}) \times (100\mathrm{\,V}) = 1.6 \times 10^{-17}\mathrm{\,J}$
Part (a): Electron Speed $v$: $$E_k = \frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 \implies v = \sqrt{\frac{2 E_k}{m_e}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-17}\mathrm{\,J})}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}\mathrm{\,kg}}}$$ $$v = \sqrt{\frac{3.2 \times 10^{-17}}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}}} = \sqrt{3.516 \times 10^{13}} \approx 5.93 \times 10^6\mathrm{\,m/s}$$ (Note: $v / c \approx 2\%$, confirming non-relativistic treatment is valid). Part (b): De Broglie Wavelength $\lambda$: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{m_e v} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}\mathrm{\,J\cdot s}}{(9.1 \times 10^{-31}\mathrm{\,kg}) \times (5.93 \times 10^6\mathrm{\,m/s})}$$ $$\lambda = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{5.396 \times 10^{-24}} \approx 1.23 \times 10^{-10}\mathrm{\,m} = 0.123\mathrm{\,nm} = 1.23\text{ \AA}$$ Check with quick formula: $\lambda = \frac{1.227\mathrm{\,nm}}{\sqrt{100}} = \frac{1.227}{10} = 0.123\mathrm{\,nm}$.
Textbook Exercise — Comparing Electron and Proton Wavelengths
Chapter 13 Exercise 13 (Textbook Page 247):
A proton and an electron are each accelerated from rest through a potential difference of $1.0\mathrm{\,kV}$. Compare their kinetic energies and their de Broglie wavelengths ($m_p = 1840\,m_e$).
Kinetic Energy Comparison:
Both particles carry identical magnitude of charge $q = e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\mathrm{\,C}$: $$E_k = q V = 1.0\mathrm{\,keV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-16}\mathrm{\,J}$$ Their kinetic energies are identical ($E_{k, p} = E_{k, e} = 1.0\mathrm{\,keV}$). De Broglie Wavelength Ratio: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m E_k}}$$ Since $h$ and $E_k$ are identical for both: $$\frac{\lambda_e}{\lambda_p} = \frac{h / \sqrt{2 m_e E_k}}{h / \sqrt{2 m_p E_k}} = \sqrt{\frac{m_p}{m_e}} = \sqrt{1840} \approx 42.9$$ Conclusion: The electron's de Broglie wavelength is approximately $43\times$ longer than the proton's due to its much smaller inertial mass.
Key Takeaways
  • Every moving particle has a de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}$.
  • Electron matter wave diffraction confirms wave-particle duality (Davisson-Germer and Thomson experiments).
  • Bohr's quantization postulate is explained by standing circular matter waves: $2\pi r = n\lambda \implies mvr = n\hbar$.
  • Accelerated electrons have wavelengths comparable to interatomic spacing ($\sim 0.1\mathrm{\,nm}$), enabling transmission electron microscopy ($\mathrm{TEM}$).

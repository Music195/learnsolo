Bernoulli's Equation

Concept — Conservation of Mechanical Energy in Fluid Flow

Derived by Daniel Bernoulli in 1738, Bernoulli's equation applies the work-energy theorem to the steady, streamline flow of an ideal (incompressible, non-viscous) fluid:

  • Energy Forms per Unit Volume:
    • Static Pressure Energy ($p$): The potential energy associated with fluid pressure ($\mathrm{Pa}$ or $\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$).
    • Kinetic Energy per Unit Volume ($\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2$): The energy associated with bulk fluid motion ($\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$).
    • Gravitational Potential Energy per Unit Volume ($\rho g h$): The potential energy due to elevation $h$ above a reference datum ($\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$).
  • Bernoulli's Principle: In horizontal streamline flow ($h_1 = h_2$), where flow speed increases, the internal fluid pressure decreases simultaneously, and vice versa: $$p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 = p_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2 = \text{constant}$$ High speed implies low pressure; low speed implies high pressure.
Core Formulas — Bernoulli's Equation
$$p + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant}$$
  • $p$ = static fluid pressure at a given elevation ($\mathrm{Pa} = \mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$)
  • $\rho$ = fluid density ($\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$); for water, $\rho \approx 1000\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$
  • $v$ = fluid flow velocity ($\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$)
  • $g$ = acceleration due to gravity ($9.8\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$)
  • $h$ = vertical height above a chosen horizontal reference datum ($\mathrm{m}$)
  • Torricelli's Law of Efflux (Open Tank): $$v_{\text{efflux}} = \sqrt{2 g h}$$
Derivation — Applying the Work-Energy Theorem to a Fluid Element

Consider a section of fluid flowing through a non-uniform pipe from cross-section $1$ (area $A_1$, elevation $h_1$) to cross-section $2$ (area $A_2$, elevation $h_2$):

  1. In time $\Delta t$, volume $\Delta V = A_1 \Delta x_1 = A_2 \Delta x_2$ enters at section 1 and exits at section 2.
  2. The net external work done on the fluid by pressure forces is: $$\Delta W = F_1 \Delta x_1 - F_2 \Delta x_2 = p_1 A_1 \Delta x_1 - p_2 A_2 \Delta x_2 = (p_1 - p_2)\Delta V$$ (The force $F_2$ at the downstream end opposes the motion, giving a negative sign.)
  3. The mass displaced is $\Delta m = \rho \Delta V$. The change in kinetic energy is: $$\Delta KE = \frac{1}{2} \Delta m (v_2^2 - v_1^2) = \frac{1}{2}\rho \Delta V (v_2^2 - v_1^2)$$
  4. The change in gravitational potential energy is: $$\Delta PE = \Delta m g (h_2 - h_1) = \rho \Delta V g (h_2 - h_1)$$
  5. By the work-energy theorem ($\Delta W = \Delta KE + \Delta PE$): $$(p_1 - p_2)\Delta V = \frac{1}{2}\rho \Delta V (v_2^2 - v_1^2) + \rho \Delta V g (h_2 - h_1)$$
  6. Dividing both sides by $\Delta V$ and rearranging terms: $$p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 + \rho g h_1 = p_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2 + \rho g h_2$$

Textbook Worked Examples — Bernoulli Calculations

Example 3.5: Water flows through a horizontal pipe with a constriction. The radius of the pipe at the left end and right end are $6\text{ cm}$ and $2\text{ cm}$ respectively. If the velocity and pressure of water at the left end are $1\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$ and $200\text{ kPa}$, find the velocity and pressure of water at the right end of the pipe.

Solution:
Given:
$r_1 = 6\text{ cm} = 0.06\text{ m}$, $v_1 = 1\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$, $p_1 = 200\text{ kPa} = 200 \times 10^3\text{ Pa}$.
$r_2 = 2\text{ cm} = 0.02\text{ m}$, horizontal pipe $\implies h_1 = h_2$.

1. Finding velocity $v_2$ via continuity equation: $$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2 \implies \pi r_1^2 v_1 = \pi r_2^2 v_2$$ $$v_2 = v_1 \left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2 = 1 \times \left(\frac{6}{2}\right)^2 = 1 \times 3^2 = 9\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$$ 2. Finding pressure $p_2$ using Bernoulli's theorem ($h_1 = h_2$): $$p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 = p_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2$$ $$p_2 = p_1 - \frac{1}{2}\rho (v_2^2 - v_1^2)$$ $$p_2 = 200 \times 10^3 - \frac{1}{2}(1000)(9^2 - 1^2) = 200\,000 - 500(80) = 200\,000 - 40\,000$$ $$p_2 = 160\,000\text{ Pa} = 160\text{ kPa}$$

Example 3.6: Calculate the pressure and speed of water at points B and C. At point A, $A_A = 3\text{ m}^2$, $v_A = 10\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$, $p_A = 300\text{ kPa}$, and elevation $h_A = 20\text{ m}$. Point B is at ground level ($h_B = 0$) with area $A_B = 3\text{ m}^2$. Point C is also at ground level ($h_C = 0$) with expanded area $A_C = 6\text{ m}^2$. Take density of water $\rho = 1000\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$.

Solution:
1. For Point B:
Since $A_B = A_A = 3\text{ m}^2$, by continuity: $$v_B = v_A = 10\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$$ Using Bernoulli's theorem between A and B ($h_A = 20\text{ m}, h_B = 0$): $$p_A + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_A^2 + \rho g h_A = p_B + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_B^2 + \rho g h_B$$ Since $v_A = v_B$: $$p_B = p_A + \rho g h_A = 300\,000 + 1000 \times 9.8 \times 20 = 300\,000 + 196\,000 = 496\,000\text{ Pa} = 496\text{ kPa}$$ 2. For Point C:
By continuity between B and C: $$A_B v_B = A_C v_C \implies 3 \times 10 = 6 \times v_C \implies v_C = 5\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$$ Using Bernoulli's theorem between B and C ($h_B = h_C = 0$): $$p_B + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_B^2 = p_C + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_C^2$$ $$p_C = p_B + \frac{1}{2}\rho (v_B^2 - v_C^2) = 496\,000 + \frac{1}{2}(1000)(10^2 - 5^2)$$ $$p_C = 496\,000 + 500(100 - 25) = 496\,000 + 37\,500 = 533\,500\text{ Pa} = 533.5\text{ kPa}$$

Example 3.7: A sealed tank containing seawater to a height of $11\text{ m}$ contains pressurized air above the water at $4\text{ atm}$. Water flows out from the bottom through a small hole open to the atmosphere ($1\text{ atm} = 1.01 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$). How fast is this water moving? (Take $\rho = 1000\text{ kg\,m}^{-3}$ and $g = 9.8\text{ m\,s}^{-2}$).

Solution:
At top surface (A): $p_A = 4\text{ atm} = 4 \times 1.01 \times 10^5\text{ Pa} = 4.04 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$, $h_A = 11\text{ m}$.
Because tank area is huge compared to the small hole, $v_A \approx 0$.
At bottom hole (B): $p_B = 1\text{ atm} = 1.01 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$, $h_B = 0\text{ m}$.
Applying Bernoulli's theorem between A and B: $$p_A + \rho g h_A = p_B + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_B^2$$ $$4.04 \times 10^5 + 1000 \times 9.8 \times 11 = 1.01 \times 10^5 + \frac{1}{2}(1000) v_B^2$$ $$404\,000 + 107\,800 = 101\,000 + 500 v_B^2$$ $$511\,800 - 101\,000 = 500 v_B^2 \implies 410\,800 = 500 v_B^2$$ $$v_B^2 = \frac{410\,800}{500} = 821.6 \implies v_B = \sqrt{821.6} \approx 28.66\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$$

Reviewed Exercises:

1. As water in a level pipe passes from a narrower cross-sectional area to a wider cross-sectional area, does the pressure change? Explain.
Yes, the pressure increases. By the equation of continuity ($A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$), when the pipe widens ($A_2 > A_1$), the flow speed decreases ($v_2 < v_1$). By Bernoulli's equation for a horizontal pipe ($p + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = \text{constant}$), a decrease in kinetic energy per unit volume must be accompanied by an increase in static pressure energy ($p_2 > p_1$).

2. A small circular hole $6.00\text{ mm}$ in diameter is cut in the side of a large water tank, $14.0\text{ m}$ below the water level in the tank. The top of the tank is open to the air. Find (i) the speed of efflux of the water, and (ii) the volume discharged per second.
Given: $h = 14.0\text{ m}$, hole diameter $d = 6.00\text{ mm} = 6 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} \implies r = 3 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$.
(i) By Torricelli's theorem: $$v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 14.0} = \sqrt{274.4} \approx 16.56\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$$ (ii) Cross-sectional area of the hole: $$A = \pi r^2 = \pi (3 \times 10^{-3})^2 = 9\pi \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2 \approx 2.827 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$$ Volume flow rate discharged: $$\frac{V}{t} = A v = (2.827 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2)(16.56\text{ m\,s}^{-1}) \approx 4.68 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^3\,\text{s}^{-1} \quad (0.468\text{ L\,s}^{-1})$$

Key Takeaways
  • Bernoulli's equation states that $p + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant}$ along any streamline for an ideal fluid.
  • In horizontal flow ($h_1 = h_2$), speed and pressure are inversely related: high velocity corresponds to low static pressure.
  • For an open tank, efflux velocity through a small bottom hole is $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ (Torricelli's law).
  • Total head or total mechanical energy per unit volume is conserved in frictionless streamline flow.

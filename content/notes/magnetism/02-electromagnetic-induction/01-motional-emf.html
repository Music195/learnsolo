Motional EMF

A rod moving across a field becomes a battery of emf $vBL$; the current it drives brakes the rod

The induced emf is the emf made in a conductor moving in a magnetic field. "Magnetic field" gives $B$, "conductor" gives its length $L$, "moving" gives $v$:

$$V = vBL.$$

It is an emf, measured in volts, not a force. Three formulas look alike, so keep them apart:

  • Lorentz force $qvB$ (a charge moving in a field);
  • electromagnetic force $IBL$ (a current in a field);
  • induced emf $vBL$ (a conductor moving in a field).

Draw it as a battery. Moving the rod pushes its positive charges to one end (the Lorentz force), so that end becomes the + terminal. In a resistor, current flows from high to low potential. Inside a battery it is the other way round: from $-$ to $+$. Marking a battery symbol on the rod stops you from getting the potential the wrong way round.

Braking. The induced current flows through the rod, which is in the field, so the rod also feels $IBL$. That force always opposes the motion. If something keeps pulling the rod (a hanging mass, for example), it speeds up until the braking force balances the pull. Then it moves at a steady terminal speed.

Energy. At terminal speed, the pull feeds in energy at a rate (power) $Fv$, and the resistor turns energy into heat at the same rate, $I^2R$. The kinetic energy stays constant, like a bath with the tap and the drain running at the same rate. Cut the string (close the tap) and the kinetic energy drains away into heat until the rod stops.

A rod on rails with a resistor $R$

$$V = vBL, \qquad I = \frac{vBL}{R}, \qquad \text{braking force } IBL = \frac{vB^2L^2}{R}$$

$v$: rod's speed (m/s). $B$: field (T). $L$: rod length between the rails (m). With a steady pull $F$, the terminal speed is where $IBL = F$: $v = FR/(B^2L^2)$. Power: $Fv = I^2R$.

  1. Each positive charge in the moving rod feels the Lorentz force $qvB$ along the rod, so + charge builds up at one end and $-$ at the other.
  2. These charges make an electric field $E$ in the rod, which pushes back with $qE$. The build-up stops when $qE = qvB$, so $E = vB$.
  3. A uniform field $E$ along a length $L$ gives a potential difference $V = EL = vBL$.

B = 0.50 T, L = 0.40 m, R = 0.20 Ω; rod 0.020 kg, hanging mass 0.010 kg (slowed down 3 times)

Example 1

In the demo's circuit ($B = 0.50$ T, $L = 0.40$ m, $R = 0.20$ Ω), the rod moves at 0.30 m/s. Find the emf and the current.

Show solution
  1. $V = vBL = 0.30 \times 0.50 \times 0.40 = 0.060$ V.
  2. $I = V/R = 0.060/0.20 = 0.30$ A.

0.060 V; 0.30 A

Example 2

In the demo, the field points out of the page and the rod moves to the right. P is the rod's upper end and Q its lower end. Which end is at the higher potential?

Show solution
  1. Positive charges in the rod: fingers to the right, curl towards "out of the page", and the thumb points down the page. The charges are pushed towards Q.
  2. So the current goes from P to Q inside the rod. The rod is a battery, and inside a battery current flows from $-$ to $+$. Q is the + terminal.
  3. (Many people answer P because "current flows from high to low". That is only true in a resistor.)

Q

Example 3

A hanging mass of 0.010 kg pulls the rod through a string over a pulley. Find the terminal speed. ($g = 9.8$ m/s²)

Show solution
  1. At terminal speed there is no acceleration, so the braking force balances the pull: $\dfrac{vB^2L^2}{R} = mg$.
  2. $v = \dfrac{mgR}{B^2L^2} = \dfrac{0.010 \times 9.8 \times 0.20}{0.50^2 \times 0.40^2} = \dfrac{0.0196}{0.040} = 0.49$ m/s.

0.49 m/s

Example 4

At this terminal speed, compare the power supplied by gravity with the power turned into heat in the resistor.

Show solution
  1. Gravity: $mgv = 0.098 \times 0.49 = 0.048$ W.
  2. $I = vBL/R = 0.49 \times 0.50 \times 0.40/0.20 = 0.49$ A, so $I^2R = 0.49^2 \times 0.20 = 0.048$ W.
  3. They are equal: energy flows in and out at the same rate, so the kinetic energy does not change.

Both 0.048 W

Example 5

While the rod (mass 0.020 kg) moves at the terminal speed 0.49 m/s, the string is cut. Find the total heat produced in the resistor until the rod stops.

Show solution
  1. No more energy comes in, and the rod's kinetic energy drains away as heat until it is gone.
  2. $H = \tfrac12Mv^2 = \tfrac12 \times 0.020 \times 0.49^2 = 2.4\times10^{-3}$ J.

$2.4\times10^{-3}$ J

The moving rod is a battery, not a resistor. The current leaves it at the + end, which is the higher potential, even though inside the rod the current flows "uphill".
When a question shows a field and a rod, ask yourself in words: "a conductor moves in a field" means an emf ($vBL$); "a current flows in a field" means a force ($IBL$). Usually both happen, one after the other.
  • $V = vBL$; draw the rod as a battery.
  • The current makes a braking force $IBL$ against the motion.
  • Terminal speed: braking force = pull, and power in = heat out.

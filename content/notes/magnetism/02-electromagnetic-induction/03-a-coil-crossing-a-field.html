A Coil Crossing a Field

A coil pulled through a field has current only while one side is in the field; you must push it then

A rectangular coil (resistance $R$) is pulled at a constant speed $v$ through a region of field $B$. Split the motion into three stages and look at each side of the coil.

  1. Entering: only the front side is in the field. It cuts across the field, so it has emf $vBa$ ($a$ = length of that side). A current $I = vBa/R$ flows round the coil.
  2. Completely inside: both upright sides cut the field and make equal emfs. Round the loop these two emfs oppose each other, so they cancel: no current.
  3. Leaving: only the back side is in the field. Same size emf, but the current goes round the other way.

Which sides make an emf? Think of the field lines as stalks of grass and each side of the coil as a blade. A side moving across the stalks cuts them and makes an emf. A side moving along its own length (the top and bottom here) cuts nothing.

The force. Whenever a current flows, the side in the field feels $IBa$. This force always opposes the motion, both while entering and while leaving. To keep the speed constant you must push forward with the same force. So the push is in the same direction in both stages, while the current reverses.

While one side is in the field

$$V = vBa, \qquad I = \frac{vBa}{R}, \qquad F_\text{push} = IBa = \frac{vB^2a^2}{R}$$

$a$: length of the side that cuts the field (m). $v$: constant speed (m/s). $R$: the coil's resistance (Ω). Completely inside or completely outside: $I = 0$, $F = 0$. Each stage lasts (coil width $b$)/$v$.

  1. Fully inside, the front and back sides both move at $v$ in the same field, so each has emf $vBa$ pointing the same way in space (say, down the page).
  2. Going round the loop, you pass down one side and up the other. So one emf counts $+vBa$ and the other $-vBa$, and the total is 0.
  3. No current means no force, so no push is needed. (Lenz's law gives the same answer: the flux through the coil does not change.)

Coil 0.20 m tall, 0.30 m wide, R = 0.10 Ω, through a 0.60 m wide field of 0.50 T (out of the page)

Example 1

In the demo ($a = 0.20$ m, $B = 0.50$ T, $R = 0.10$ Ω, $v = 0.50$ m/s, field out of the page, coil moving right), find the current while the coil is entering, and its direction.

Show solution
  1. $V = vBa = 0.50 \times 0.50 \times 0.20 = 0.050$ V, so $I = 0.050/0.10 = 0.50$ A.
  2. Front side: fingers to the right, curl towards "out of the page", and the thumb points down the page. The current goes down the front side, which is clockwise round the coil.

0.50 A, clockwise (negative if anticlockwise counts as positive)

Example 2

What force must be applied to keep the coil moving at 0.50 m/s while it enters?

Show solution
  1. The front side carries 0.50 A in the field: $IBa = 0.50 \times 0.50 \times 0.20 = 0.050$ N. Current down the page in a field out of the page gives a force to the left.
  2. So push to the right with 0.050 N.

0.050 N forwards (in the direction of motion)

Example 3

While the coil is completely inside the field, find the current and the force needed.

Show solution
  1. Both upright sides make 0.050 V, and round the loop they cancel. The top and bottom sides cut no field lines.

No current, no force needed

Example 4

Find the current and the push while the coil is leaving the field.

Show solution
  1. Only the back side is in the field. Its emf points down the page as before, but the back side is on the other side of the loop, so the current goes anticlockwise: $+0.50$ A.
  2. The force on the back side still opposes the motion, so the push is again 0.050 N forwards.

+0.50 A (anticlockwise); 0.050 N forwards

Example 5

The coil is 0.30 m wide. Find the total work done by the push during the whole crossing, and the heat produced in the coil.

Show solution
  1. The push acts over 0.30 m entering and 0.30 m leaving: $W = 0.050 \times 0.60 = 0.030$ J.
  2. Heat: $I^2R \times$ time $= 0.50^2 \times 0.10 \times (0.60 + 0.60) = 0.030$ J. They are equal, because all the work turns into heat.

0.030 J of work, 0.030 J of heat

"Inside the field" does not mean "current flows". When the whole coil is in a uniform field, the emfs cancel and the current is zero. Current flows only while the amount of field inside the coil is changing.
The graphs have a typical shape. The push is a pulse, a gap, then the same pulse (same sign). The current is a pulse, a gap, then a pulse of the opposite sign. Recognising these shapes makes multiple-choice questions quick.
  • Only sides moving across the field lines make an emf.
  • Entering and leaving: $I = vBa/R$, in opposite directions.
  • The push $vB^2a^2/R$ is forwards in both stages; zero in the middle.

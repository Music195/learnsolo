The Lorentz Force

A charge moving across a magnetic field feels $f = qvB$ at right angles to its motion, so it goes round in a circle

Magnetic flux density. In most force formulas we use $B = \mu H$ instead of $H$. Here $\mu$ is the permeability of the material ($\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ N/A² in a vacuum). $B$ is measured in tesla (T). People often call $B$ "the magnetic field" too. It points the same way as $H$.

A field fills the whole space. An arrow labelled $B$ on a diagram means the field is like that everywhere in the region, not only along the arrow.

The Lorentz force is the force on a charged particle moving in a magnetic field. Read the words to build the formula: "in a magnetic field" gives $B$, "charged" gives $q$, "moving" gives $v$:

$$f = qvB.$$

A charge at rest feels nothing. Only the part of the velocity perpendicular to $B$ counts. Moving along the field gives no force.

Direction. Right hand: point your fingers along $v$, curl them towards $B$, and your thumb gives $f$. (Or use Fleming's left-hand rule: first finger = field, second finger = velocity, thumb = force.) For a negative charge, such as an electron, the force is the opposite way.

The motion. The force is always at right angles to the velocity, so it never speeds the particle up or slows it down. It only turns it, and the result is uniform circular motion. If the velocity is at an angle to $B$, the part along $B$ carries on unchanged, so the particle moves in a helix (a spiral).

Lorentz force and the circle it makes

$$f = qvB, \qquad \frac{mv^2}{r} = qvB \;\Rightarrow\; r = \frac{mv}{qB}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{qB}$$

$q$: charge (C). $v$: speed across the field (m/s). $B$: flux density (T). $m$: mass (kg). $r$: radius (m). $T$: period (s), the same for every speed. $B = \mu H$.

  1. The force $qvB$ is always perpendicular to $v$, so it does no work and the speed stays $v$.
  2. A constant-size force at right angles to the motion is exactly what circular motion needs. It supplies the centripetal force: $mv^2/r = qvB$.
  3. So $r = mv/(qB)$. One turn takes $T = 2\pi r/v = 2\pi m/(qB)$, and $v$ cancels: faster particles make bigger circles in the same time.
  4. At an angle θ to $B$: $v\cos\theta$ along $B$ (no force) and $v\sin\theta$ across $B$ (a circle). Together they make a helix of radius $mv\sin\theta/(qB)$. It moves forward $L = v\cos\theta \cdot T$ per turn (the pitch).

A particle at 1.0 × 10⁶ m/s in B = 0.10 T (slowed down). Left: looking along B. Right: from the side

Example 1

A proton ($q = 1.6\times10^{-19}$ C) moves at $1.0\times10^6$ m/s at right angles to a field $B = 0.10$ T. Find the force on it.

Show solution
  1. $f = qvB = 1.6\times10^{-19} \times 1.0\times10^6 \times 0.10$.

$1.6\times10^{-14}$ N

Example 2

For the proton in Example 1 ($m = 1.67\times10^{-27}$ kg), find the radius and the period of its circle.

Show solution
  1. $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB} = \dfrac{1.67\times10^{-27} \times 1.0\times10^6}{1.6\times10^{-19} \times 0.10} = 0.104$ m.
  2. $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{qB} = \dfrac{2\pi \times 1.67\times10^{-27}}{1.6\times10^{-20}} = 6.6\times10^{-7}$ s.

$r = 0.10$ m, $T = 6.6\times10^{-7}$ s

Example 3

A field $B$ points along the $+y$ axis. An electron is fired from the origin at speed $v_0$ along the $+y$ axis. Describe its motion.

Show solution
  1. Its velocity is parallel to $B$, so there is no part across the field and no Lorentz force.

Straight along the $y$-axis at constant speed $v_0$

Example 4

The same electron (mass $m$, charge $-e$) is now fired along the $+x$ axis. Describe its motion.

Show solution
  1. For a positive charge: fingers along $+x$, curl towards $+y$, so the thumb points along $+z$. The electron is negative, so its force is along $-z$.
  2. The force stays at right angles to $v$, so the electron goes round a circle in the $xz$-plane.
  3. $r = \dfrac{mv_0}{eB}$, centred at $z = -\dfrac{mv_0}{eB}$, with period $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{eB}$.

Uniform circular motion in the $xz$-plane, radius $mv_0/(eB)$, period $2\pi m/(eB)$

Example 5

The electron is fired at an angle θ (0° < θ < 90°) to the $+y$ axis. Describe its motion and find how far along the $y$-axis it moves in each turn.

Show solution
  1. Along $B$: $v_0\cos\theta$, which is unchanged. Across $B$: $v_0\sin\theta$, which makes a circle of radius $\dfrac{mv_0\sin\theta}{eB}$.
  2. Together: a helix. It crosses the $y$-axis once every period $T = 2\pi m/(eB)$.
  3. Distance per turn: $L = v_0\cos\theta \cdot \dfrac{2\pi m}{eB}$.

A helix along $B$; $L = \dfrac{2\pi m v_0\cos\theta}{eB}$

The hand rules give the force on a positive charge. For an electron, turn the answer round. It is the most common slip in these problems.
$T = 2\pi m/(qB)$ has no $v$ in it. Every particle of the same kind in the same field goes round in the same time, whatever its speed. (This is how a cyclotron keeps in step with its particles.)
  • $f = qvB$, using only the velocity across $B$; at right angles to both.
  • Circle: $r = mv/(qB)$, $T = 2\pi m/(qB)$.
  • At an angle to $B$: a helix with pitch $v\cos\theta \cdot T$.

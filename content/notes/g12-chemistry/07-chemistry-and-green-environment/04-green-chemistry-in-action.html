Green Chemistry in Action

Concept — Safer Products and Processes, Sector by Sector

Chemistry helps reach the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Green chemistry (sustainable chemistry) designs chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate hazardous substances.

  • Agriculture. Organic farming uses compost, manure, green manure and bone meal instead of chemical fertilisers. Plant-based pesticides (e.g. neem leaves) are safer than chlorinated hydrocarbons; formulations that form stable water-soluble complexes with nutrients improve uptake through leaves, cut fertiliser use and reduce runoff. Insecticidal soaps kill small soft-bodied insects, leave no residue, are non-toxic to animals and birds and spare useful insects.
  • Energy. Waste gasification (pyrolysis) turns municipal and non-hazardous waste into H₂ + CO, which a catalyst converts into ethanol, methanol and other liquid fuels. Renewable energy: solar, wind, hydroelectric, biomass, biorefineries, geothermal, tidal. Biofuels from sugar cane, rapeseed, corn, straw, wood and residues:
    • Bioethanol: yeast ferments sugars (also from corncob, cornstalk, bagasse) into ethanol and CO₂.
    • Biodiesel: made from plant oils (even used cooking oil) by removing the by-product glycerine, a raw material for soap. Unlike fossil diesel it gives no sulphur compounds.
  • Wastewater treatment removes suspended solids, organic carbon, nutrients, salts, heavy metals and pathogens, using green adsorbents (agricultural waste, activated carbon, rice-husk ceramics) and photocatalyst nanomaterials (TiO₂, ZnO, Fe₂O₃) that decompose pollutants.
  • Consumer goods. Unleaded petrol: tetraethyllead (TEL) anti-knock agent replaced by MTBE (methyl tert-butyl ether). Bioplastics from corn starch, vegetable oils and fats for packaging, pots and bowls. Dry cleaning: carcinogenic tetrachloroethene (Cl₂C=CCl₂), which pollutes groundwater, replaced by liquid CO₂, which is stored and reused.
  • Industry. Adipic acid HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH (for nylon, polyurethanes, lubricants, plasticisers) is made from carcinogenic benzene; the green route turns glucose into it with an enzyme from genetically modified bacteria. Chromium plating: hexavalent Cr(VI) is a carcinogen and gives hazardous waste (lead chromates, barium sulphate); trivalent Cr(III) plating from chromium sulphate or chloride is a less toxic alternative with the same scratch and corrosion resistance.
Fermentation
$$\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} \xrightarrow{\text{yeast}} 2\mathrm{C_2H_5OH} + 2\mathrm{CO_2} \qquad 180\ \mathrm{g} \to 92\ \mathrm{g} + 88\ \mathrm{g}$$
  • $M(\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}) = 180$, $M(\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}) = 46$, $M(\mathrm{CO_2}) = 44$ g mol⁻¹; 1 mol of gas ≈ 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Cr(III) vs Cr(VI): the hexavalent form is the more toxic, carcinogenic one.
  • Biodiesel by-product: it is glycerine (glycerol), used for soap, not ethanol.

Worked Examples — Review Questions (p. 123) and a Fermentation Calculation

Review Q1: What fuel products come from the waste gasification process?

Show answer

The waste gives a mixture of hydrogen and carbon monoxide, which a catalyst converts into ethanol, methanol and other liquid fuels or chemicals.

Review Q2: What are the renewable energy resources?

Show answer

Solar, wind, hydroelectric, biomass, biorefineries, geothermal and tidal energy.

Review Q3: Which by-product of biodiesel production is a raw material for soap?

Show answer

Glycerine (glycerol).

Extra example: What mass of ethanol, and what volume of CO₂ at r.t.p., come from fermenting 1.00 kg of glucose?

Show answer

$n(\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}) = 1000 / 180 = 5.56$ mol, so $n(\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}) = n(\mathrm{CO_2}) = 2 \times 5.56 = 11.1$ mol.

Ethanol: $11.1 \times 46 = \mathbf{511\ g}$. CO₂: $11.1 \times 44 = 489$ g, or $11.1 \times 24 = \mathbf{267\ dm^3}$ at r.t.p. (Mass check: 511 + 489 = 1000 g.)

(12) (i) Use liquid CO₂ instead of tetrachloroethene: CO₂ is non-toxic and reused, while Cl₂C=CCl₂ is carcinogenic and pollutes groundwater. Key Takeaways
  • Agriculture: organic fertilisers, plant-based pesticides, insecticidal soaps.
  • Energy: waste gasification, renewables, bioethanol (C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂), biodiesel (by-product glycerine).
  • Swaps: liquid CO₂ for Cl₂C=CCl₂, Cr(III) for Cr(VI), MTBE for TEL, glucose for benzene.

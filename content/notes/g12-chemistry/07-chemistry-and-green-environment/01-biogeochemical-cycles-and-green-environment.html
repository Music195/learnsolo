Biogeochemical Cycles and a Green Environment

Concept — Elements Move Round and Round

The environment is the hydrosphere, lithosphere, atmosphere and biosphere. Carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus and sulphur keep changing form and moving between them. Each pathway through living and non-living things is a biogeochemical cycle. The cycles are tied to the water cycle (runoff carries nitrogen and phosphate into rivers and oceans), and the ocean is a major reservoir.

  • Carbon cycle: most carbon is stored in rocks and sediments; the rest is in the ocean, the air and living things. Processes: photosynthesis (plants take in CO₂), respiration and decay (CO₂ returned), exchange with the ocean, fossilisation and sedimentation (coal and fossil fuels), and combustion (CO₂, H₂O and energy released).
  • Nitrogen cycle: nitrogen fixation, nitrification, assimilation, ammonification and denitrification, from the air to the soil to organisms and back (in detail in the Grade 11 book).
  • Phosphorus cycle: no gas phase; it moves only through rock, soil, water and sediments. Weathering releases phosphate (PO₄³⁻, HPO₄²⁻) that plants absorb; it is needed for DNA, bones and teeth. Decay returns it to soil and ocean, then to sediments for millions of years. Seabird guano brings phosphorus back from the ocean to land. It cycles fast through living things and very slowly through rocks.
  • Sulphur cycle: through rocks, water, air and living things (proteins and vitamins contain S). Volcanoes, bacteria, decay and plankton (dimethyl sulphide) put sulphur into the air; SO₂ is oxidised to SO₃ and forms H₂SO₄, which falls as acid deposition and is taken up by plants again.

Human effects: burning fossil fuels, deforestation, land-use change, livestock and making concrete push CO₂ (and CH₄) into the air; the ocean absorbs CO₂ and its pH falls (ocean acidification, harming shells of corals, crabs, snails). Fertiliser runoff and sewage add N and P, causing algal blooms: they block light, the plants die and decay uses up dissolved O₂, making dead zones and fish kills. Burning coal and smelting sulphide ores (Cu, Pb, Zn) release H₂S and SO₂ that cause acid rain, which lowers the pH of lakes and corrodes buildings.

Green environment: no pollution and a better quality of life, through informed consumption, conservation and renewable energy. Green chemistry designs products and processes that reduce or eliminate hazardous substances. The 7 R's: Rethink, Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repair, Regift, Recycle.

  • The last equilibrium is why extra CO₂ makes the ocean more acidic
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Phosphorus in the air: the phosphorus cycle is the one with no atmospheric step.
  • Algae make oxygen, so blooms are good? The problem is their death and decay, which uses up the dissolved oxygen.

Worked Examples — Review Questions (p. 115)

Review Q1: List the main biogeochemical cycles. Which one does not involve the atmosphere?

Show answer

Carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and sulphur cycles (linked to the water cycle). The phosphorus cycle has no atmospheric step.

Review Q2: (i) The processes in the carbon cycle. (ii) Which are most important for the survival of plants and animals? (iii) Which gases from burning coal cause environmental problems?

Show answer

(i) Photosynthesis, respiration, exchange, fossilisation, sedimentation, combustion.

(ii) Photosynthesis (plants make food and O₂) and respiration (energy for living things). (iii) CO₂ (greenhouse gas), and SO₂ and H₂S (acid rain).

Review Q3: What are the 7 R's of a green environment?

Show answer

Rethink, Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repair, Regift, Recycle.

Exercises 1(a), 1(e), 2(a), 5(a), 5(b), 6(a) (pp. 124–125): (1a) TRUE or FALSE: the atmosphere is the carbon reservoir most affected directly by human activity. (1e) Phosphorus can be found as a gas in its cycle. (2a) The guano of marine birds is rich in ___. (5a) and (5b) one word: the natural pathways that circulate the elements of living matter; lowering of ocean pH by CO₂ uptake. (6a) How does atmospheric CO₂ enter living organisms?

Show answer

(1a) TRUE: burning fuels and deforestation move carbon quickly into the air. (1e) FALSE: phosphorus has no gas phase; it cycles through rock, soil, water and sediments. (2a) phosphorus.

(5a) biogeochemical cycle; (5b) ocean acidification. (6a) By photosynthesis in plants; animals then take in the carbon by eating plants.

Key Takeaways
  • C, N, P, S cycle between air, water, land and life; P has no gas step.
  • People add CO₂ (warming, ocean acidification), N and P (algal blooms, dead zones), SO₂ (acid rain).
  • Green chemistry reduces hazardous substances; the 7 R's guide daily life.

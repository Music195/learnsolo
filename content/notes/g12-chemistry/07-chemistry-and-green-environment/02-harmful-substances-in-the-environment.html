Harmful Substances in the Environment

Concept — Pollutants That Stay and Build Up

Environmental pollution is the harmful release of substances that damage biodiversity, ecosystems and health. Four groups matter most here.

  • Heavy metals: dense metals that are toxic even at very low concentrations. People spread them through air, water, soil and food, and change their chemical form.
    • Arsenic (+3, +5; not found as the element): dissolves out of arsenic-rich rocks into groundwater and soil; pure As oxidises in humid air to toxic arsenic anhydride. Soluble arsenic compounds are used in green and yellow paint, glass, ceramics and semiconductors; sodium arsenate kills parasites on fruit and potato crops. Harmful at micrograms to milligrams per litre.
    • Cadmium (+2): released as Cd and CdO, also CdS (coal burning) and CdCl₂ (waste incineration); tobacco smoke is a major source. Used in cells, accumulators, porcelain, dental amalgam, plastics, metal coatings, photovoltaics.
    • Lead (+2, +4): mainly from engine exhaust, so highest in urban industrial air. Used in batteries, paint and alloys. Causes slower growth and reproduction, and nerve damage in vertebrates.
    • Mercury (0, +1, +2): microorganisms turn inorganic mercury into methylmercury, which bioaccumulates in fish and shellfish. People take it in mostly as vapour (gold panning, tooth fillings). It harms the nervous, digestive and immune systems, lungs, kidneys, skin, eyes and the unborn child.
  • Pesticides kill pests or reduce the damage they cause (insects, mice, weeds, fungi, bacteria, viruses). Families: organophosphates, organochlorines, carbamates and thiocarbamates, pyrethroids. In the environment they are adsorbed on soil (positive pesticide molecules bind to negative clay), transferred (volatilisation, spray drift, runoff, leaching, absorption, crop removal) and degraded after application by microbes, chemical reactions and photodegradation (sunlight), over hours to years. They also poison birds, fish, useful insects and other plants, with acute and chronic effects.
  • Persistent organic pollutants (POPs): carbon compounds that last a long time and resist breakdown, e.g. PCBs and DDT. They are lipophilic (fat-loving), so they collect in fatty tissue and build up along food chains.
  • Volatile organic compounds (VOCs): organic chemicals that vaporise, from paint thinners, dry-cleaning fluid, nail polish remover, grease solvents, fuels and plastics. In sunlight VOCs react with nitrogen oxides to form ozone and fine particles: smog. Burning plastics releases dioxins, furans, mercury and PCBs.
Key Ideas
$$\text{bioaccumulation: intake faster than removal} \qquad \text{biomagnification: } c_{\text{next level}} \approx F \times c_{\text{level}}$$ $$\mathrm{VOCs} + \mathrm{NO}_x \xrightarrow{\text{sunlight}} \mathrm{O_3} + \text{fine particles} \to \text{smog}$$
  • $F$ (the factor per step up the food chain) is large for fat-soluble, persistent pollutants (DDT, PCBs, methylmercury) and about 1 for water-soluble ones that are excreted
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • POPs vs VOCs: POPs (DDT, PCBs) persist and build up in fat; VOCs evaporate and make smog.
  • "Organic" means harmless: DDT, PCBs and methylmercury are organic and highly toxic.

Worked Examples — Review Questions (p. 118)

Review Q1: Classify as inorganic or organic: sodium arsenate, DDT, methylmercury, lead, cadmium sulphide, PCBs.

Show answer

Inorganic: sodium arsenate, lead, cadmium sulphide. Organic: DDT, methylmercury (CH₃Hg⁺ has a C–Hg bond), PCBs.

Review Q2: Which mercury compound accumulates in living things? Describe the toxic effects of mercury on humans.

Show answer

Methylmercury (in fish and shellfish). Mercury harms the nervous, digestive and immune systems, the lungs, kidneys, skin and eyes, and the development of the child in the womb and in early life.

Review Q3: (a) How are pesticides broken down? (b) One example each of a POP and a VOC.

Show answer

(a) By microbes, by chemical reactions and by photodegradation (sunlight). (b) POP: DDT (or PCBs). VOC: paint thinner (or dry-cleaning fluid, nail polish remover).

Key Takeaways
  • As, Cd, Pb, Hg are toxic at very low levels; methylmercury builds up in fish.
  • Pesticides are adsorbed, transferred and degraded; they also harm non-target life.
  • POPs (DDT, PCBs) persist and magnify in food chains; VOCs + NOₓ + sunlight → smog.

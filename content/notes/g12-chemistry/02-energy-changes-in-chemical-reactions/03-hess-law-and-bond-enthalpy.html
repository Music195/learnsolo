Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Concept — The Route Does Not Matter

Few enthalpy changes can be measured directly in a calorimeter; some reactions are too slow, too fast, or impossible to carry out on their own. Hess's law lets us calculate them from other, known values:

The enthalpy change for a chemical reaction is the same, whatever route is taken from reactants to products.

  • Enthalpy cycle: draw the reaction as Route I (A → B directly) and a second path, Route II, through an intermediate C (A → C → B). Then $\Delta H_1 = \Delta H_2 + \Delta H_3$. With more steps, add them all.
  • Building a cycle in four steps: (1) write the equation of the wanted reaction on top; (2) draw the other route from the data with the correct arrow directions; (3) write the enthalpy changes on the arrows; (4) set Route I = Route II and solve, multiplying by the number of moles. $\Delta H^\circ_f$ of an element in its standard state is zero.
  • From enthalpies of formation: put the elements at the bottom with arrows pointing up to the reactants and to the products.
  • From enthalpies of combustion: put the combustion products ($\mathrm{CO_2}$, $\mathrm{H_2O}$) at the bottom with arrows pointing down from the reactants and from the products; add oxygen to balance.
  • Bond enthalpy $E$: the energy to break one mole of a given bond, e.g. $E(\text{C–H})$. Breaking absorbs it ($+E$), making releases it ($-E$): $\mathrm{Br_2(g) \to 2Br(g)}$, $+193$; $\mathrm{2Br(g) \to Br_2(g)}$, $-193$ kJ mol−1. A larger bond enthalpy means a stronger bond. Using gaseous atoms as the bottom of the cycle gives approximate reaction enthalpies (bond enthalpies are averages).
Core Formulas
$$\text{Route I} = \text{Route II}$$ $$\Delta H_r^\circ = \sum \Delta H_f^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum \Delta H_f^\circ(\text{reactants})$$ $$\Delta H_r^\circ = \sum \Delta H_c^\circ(\text{reactants}) - \sum \Delta H_c^\circ(\text{products})$$ $$\Delta H_r \approx \sum E(\text{bonds broken}) - \sum E(\text{bonds formed})$$
  • Going against an arrow changes the sign of its $\Delta H$
  • Multiply each $\Delta H$ by the number of moles in the equation (e.g. $2\Delta H_c[\mathrm{H_2}]$)
  • $\Delta H_f^\circ$ of elements ($\mathrm{O_2}$, $\mathrm{N_2}$, Fe, C(graphite), …) $= 0$
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • The two sums are in opposite orders: formation uses products − reactants; combustion uses reactants − products. Drawing the cycle avoids mixing them up.
  • Forgetting coefficients: in $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3 + 3Ca \to 2Fe + 3CaO}$, CaO counts three times.
  • Counting bonds: ethanol $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}$ has 5 C–H, 1 C–C, 1 C–O and 1 O–H bond. Draw the full structure before counting.

Why must Route I and Route II give the same ΔH?

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 5–8 (pp. 37–42)

Worked cycle (p. 37): Find $\Delta H_r^\circ$ for $\mathrm{NO(g) + \tfrac12 O_2(g) \to NO_2(g)}$ given $\Delta H_f^\circ[\mathrm{NO}] = +90$, $\Delta H_f^\circ[\mathrm{NO_2}] = +33$ kJ mol−1.

Show step-by-step solution

Bottom of the cycle: the elements $\mathrm{\tfrac12 N_2 + O_2}$. Route I goes elements → NO (+90) then → $\mathrm{NO_2}$ ($\Delta H_r$). Route II goes elements → $\mathrm{NO_2}$ directly (+33).

$(+90) + \Delta H_r^\circ = (+33)$

$\Delta H_r^\circ = 33 - 90 = -57\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

Example 5: $\mathrm{CaCO_3(s) \to CaO(s) + CO_2(g)}$ with $\Delta H_f^\circ$: CaO −636, $\mathrm{CO_2}$ −394, $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$ −1207 kJ mol−1.

Show step-by-step solution

$\Delta H_f^\circ[\mathrm{CaCO_3}] + \Delta H_r^\circ = \Delta H_f^\circ[\mathrm{CaO}] + \Delta H_f^\circ[\mathrm{CO_2}]$

$(-1207) + \Delta H_r^\circ = (-636) + (-394) = -1030$

$\Delta H_r^\circ = -1030 + 1207 = +177\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ (endothermic: limestone must be heated).

Formation of methane (p. 39): $\mathrm{C(graphite) + 2H_2(g) \to CH_4(g)}$ from $\Delta H_c^\circ$: $\mathrm{CH_4}$ −891, C −394, $\mathrm{H_2}$ −286 kJ mol−1.

Show step-by-step solution

Bottom: $\mathrm{CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l)}$. Both routes start at C + 2H2 (+ 2O2).

$\Delta H_c[\mathrm{C}] + 2\Delta H_c[\mathrm{H_2}] = \Delta H_f^\circ + \Delta H_c[\mathrm{CH_4}]$

$(-394) + 2(-286) = \Delta H_f^\circ + (-891)$, so $-966 = \Delta H_f^\circ - 891$

$\Delta H_f^\circ[\mathrm{CH_4}] = -966 + 891 = -75\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

Example 6: $\Delta H_f^\circ$ of ethanoic acid, $\mathrm{2C(graphite) + 2H_2(g) + O_2(g) \to CH_3COOH(l)}$, with $\Delta H_c^\circ$: C −394, $\mathrm{H_2}$ −286, $\mathrm{CH_3COOH}$ −872 kJ mol−1.

Show step-by-step solution

$2(-394) + 2(-286) = \Delta H_f^\circ + (-872)$

$-788 - 572 = -1360 = \Delta H_f^\circ - 872$

$\Delta H_f^\circ[\mathrm{CH_3COOH}] = -1360 + 872 = -488\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

Example 7: Haber process $\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \to 2NH_3(g)}$ with $E(\text{N≡N}) = 945$, $E(\text{H–H}) = 436$, $E(\text{N–H}) = 391$ kJ mol−1.

Show step-by-step solution

Bonds broken: $945 + 3(436) = 945 + 1308 = +2253$ kJ.

Bonds formed: 2 NH3 × 3 N–H = 6 N–H: $-6(391) = -2346$ kJ.

$\Delta H_r^\circ = 2253 + (-2346) = -93\ \text{kJ}$ (for 2 mol NH3).

Example 8: Combustion of ethanol, $\mathrm{C_2H_5OH(l) + 3O_2(g) \to 2CO_2(g) + 3H_2O(l)}$, with E: C–C 347, C–H 410, C–O 336, O=O 496, C=O 805, O–H 465 kJ mol−1.

Show step-by-step solution

Ethanol H–C(H2)–C(H2)–O–H has 1 C–C, 5 C–H, 1 C–O, 1 O–H; plus 3 O=O.

Broken: $347 + 5(410) + 336 + 465 + 3(496) = 347 + 2050 + 336 + 465 + 1488 = +4686$ kJ.

Formed: 2 O=C=O have 4 C=O; 3 H2O have 6 O–H: $-[4(805) + 6(465)] = -(3220 + 2790) = -6010$ kJ.

$\Delta H_c = 4686 - 6010 = -1324\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ (the measured value is −1367; bond enthalpies are averages).

Worked Examples — Review Questions (p. 42) and Exercises (pp. 43–45)

Review Q1: $\Delta H_f^\circ$ of ethane, $\mathrm{2C + 3H_2 \to C_2H_6}$; $\Delta H_c$: C −394, $\mathrm{H_2}$ −286, $\mathrm{C_2H_6}$ −1560.

Show solution

$2(-394) + 3(-286) = \Delta H_f^\circ + (-1560)$; $-788 - 858 = -1646$.

$\Delta H_f^\circ = -1646 + 1560 = -86\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

Review Q2: $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3(s) + 3Ca(s) \to 2Fe(s) + 3CaO(s)}$; $\Delta H_f^\circ$: $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$ −824, CaO −636, Ca and Fe 0.

Show solution

$\Delta H = [2(0) + 3(-636)] - [(-824) + 3(0)] = -1908 + 824 = -1084\ \text{kJ}$

Review Q3: $\mathrm{CH_4 + 2O_2 \to CO_2 + 2H_2O}$ from bond enthalpies (C–H 410, O=O 496, C=O 805, O–H 465).

Show solution

Broken: $4(410) + 2(496) = 1640 + 992 = 2632$. Formed: $2(805) + 4(465) = 1610 + 1860 = 3470$.

$\Delta H = 2632 - 3470 = -838\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

Exercise 8: (a) $\Delta H_f^\circ$ of butane $\mathrm{C_4H_{10}}$ ($\Delta H_c$ = −2877). (b) $\mathrm{CO + 2H_2 \to CH_3OH}$ ($\Delta H_c$: CO −283, $\mathrm{H_2}$ −286, $\mathrm{CH_3OH}$ −726).

Show solution

(a) $\mathrm{4C + 5H_2 \to C_4H_{10}}$: $4(-394) + 5(-286) = -1576 - 1430 = -3006 = \Delta H_f^\circ - 2877$, so $\Delta H_f^\circ = -129$ kJ mol−1.

(b) $(-283) + 2(-286) = -855 = \Delta H + (-726)$, so $\Delta H = -129$ kJ mol−1.

Exercises 9 and 10: (9) $\mathrm{Al_2O_3 + 3Mg \to 3MgO + 2Al}$ ($\Delta H_f$: $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$ −1676, MgO −602). (10) $\mathrm{2Al + Fe_2O_3 \to 2Fe + Al_2O_3}$ ($\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$ −824).

Show solution

(9) $\Delta H = 3(-602) - (-1676) = -1806 + 1676 = \mathbf{-130}$ kJ.

(10) $\Delta H = (-1676) - (-824) = \mathbf{-852}$ kJ (the thermit reaction: very exothermic).

Exercise 12: Which fuel releases the most energy per gram: methanol (−726), ethanol (−1367), octane (−5471 kJ mol−1)?

Show solution

$M$: $\mathrm{CH_3OH} = 32$, $\mathrm{C_2H_5OH} = 46$, $\mathrm{C_8H_{18}} = 114$ g mol−1.

Per gram: methanol $726/32 = 22.7$; ethanol $1367/46 = 29.7$; octane $5471/114 = 48.0$ kJ g−1.

Octane releases the most energy per gram (48.0 kJ g−1).

Exercise 13(b): $\mathrm{C_2H_2(g) + 2HCl(g) \to C_2H_4Cl_2(g)}$ with E: C≡C 835, C–H 410, C–C 347, C–Cl 326, H–Cl 428.

Show solution

Broken: C≡C + 2 C–H + 2 H–Cl $= 835 + 820 + 856 = 2511$ kJ.

Formed: C–C + 4 C–H + 2 C–Cl $= 347 + 1640 + 652 = 2639$ kJ.

$\Delta H = 2511 - 2639 = -128$ kJ mol−1

Exercise 14: $\mathrm{B_2H_6(g) + 3O_2(g) \to B_2O_3(s) + 3H_2O(l)}$; $\Delta H_f$: $\mathrm{B_2H_6}$ −36, $\mathrm{H_2O}$ −286, $\mathrm{B_2O_3}$ −1274.

Show solution

$\Delta H_c = [(-1274) + 3(-286)] - [(-36) + 0] = -2132 + 36 = -2096\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

Key Takeaways
  • Hess's law: ΔH depends only on the start and end states, not on the route.
  • Formation data: elements at the bottom, $\Delta H = \sum \Delta H_f(\text{products}) - \sum \Delta H_f(\text{reactants})$.
  • Combustion data: $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $\mathrm{H_2O}$ at the bottom, $\Delta H = \sum \Delta H_c(\text{reactants}) - \sum \Delta H_c(\text{products})$.
  • Bond enthalpies: $\Delta H \approx \sum E(\text{broken}) - \sum E(\text{formed})$; count every bond.

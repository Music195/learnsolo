Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Concept — Similar Metals, Very Unlike Group I

Across a transition series the outer (4s) arrangement hardly changes, so neighbouring elements are much alike. Compared with the alkali metals (Table 6.4):

  • Physical: hard, strong, malleable, ductile, lustrous, good conductors; high melting points (Sc 1539 °C to Cu 1083 °C; Zn only 420 °C) and high densities (Fe 7.9 g cm⁻³: a 1 cm³ cube of iron is about 8 times heavier than one of sodium). All silvery-white except reddish-brown copper.
  • Chemical: much less reactive than Group I (Ni and Cu do not react with water or burn in air). They form alloys (iron + carbon = steel). Iron is the exception that corrodes (rusts) easily.

The incompletely filled d orbitals give four special characteristics:

  • Variable oxidation states. 4s electrons are lost first, then a varying number of 3d electrons. +3 is more stable at the start of the series (left of Mn), +2 towards the end. The middle elements have the most states; Mn reaches +7. High states (Cr₂O₇²⁻, MnO₄⁻, FeO₄²⁻) are oxidising agents; low states V²⁺ and Cr²⁺ are reducing agents. The highest states appear with O and F (VF₅, CrO₃, Mn₂O₇).
  • Coloured compounds and ions. Ions with incomplete d orbitals (unpaired electrons) absorb part of visible light and we see the complementary colour. Sc³⁺ (3d⁰) and Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰) are colourless. Colours help in qualitative analysis.
  • Magnetism. Unpaired d electrons make a substance paramagnetic (weakly attracted into a magnetic field); all paired makes it diamagnetic (not attracted, slightly repelled). Fe, Co and Ni are ferromagnetic: very strongly attracted and they stay magnetised for a while.
  • Catalysts. Fe in the Haber process (NH₃); V₂O₅ in the Contact process (H₂SO₄); Ni for hydrogenation of alkenes; TiCl₄ for high-density polyethene.
Core Rules
$$\text{3d electrons in } \mathrm{M}^{n+} = Z - 18 - n \qquad \text{unpaired} = \begin{cases} d & d \le 5 \\ 10 - d & d > 5 \end{cases}$$
  • unpaired > 0 → paramagnetic, and usually coloured; d⁰ or d¹⁰ → diamagnetic and colourless
  • Highest state of Sc to Mn = number of 4s + 3d electrons (Sc +3, Ti +4, V +5, Cr +6, Mn +7)
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Every transition compound is coloured": Sc³⁺ and Zn²⁺ compounds are white.
  • Paramagnetic vs ferromagnetic: paramagnetism is weak and needs unpaired electrons; only Fe, Co, Ni (as metals) are ferromagnetic.

Worked Examples — Examples 2–3 and Review Questions (pp. 104–105)

Example 2: Is copper (₂₉Cu) diamagnetic? Give a reason.

Show answer

₂₉Cu: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹. All ten 3d electrons are paired.

Therefore copper is a diamagnetic substance (judged, as the textbook does, by the (n−1)d electrons).

Example 3: Is vanadium (₂₃V) paramagnetic? Give a reason.

Show answer

₂₃V: [Ar] 3d³ 4s². The three 3d electrons sit one in each of three orbitals, so they are unpaired.

Therefore vanadium is a paramagnetic substance (3 unpaired electrons).

Review Q1: (a) Three properties of transition metals that differ from Group I. (b) Choose: soft/hard; high/low density; high/low melting point; reactive/unreactive with water.

Show answer

(a) Any three: high melting point, high density, strong and hard, coloured compounds, variable oxidation states, low reactivity. (b) hard; high density; high melting point; unreactive with water.

Review Q2: (a) Three characteristic properties. (b) Oxidation states of chromium, manganese, iron, nickel and copper.

Show answer

(a) Variable oxidation states, coloured compounds and ions, paramagnetism, catalytic activity (and forming complex ions).

(b) Cr: +2 to +6; Mn: +2 to +7; Fe: +2 to +6; Ni: +2, +3; Cu: +1, +2, +3 (Table 6.5).

Review Q3: (a) Colours of Ti³⁺, Cr³⁺, Mn²⁺, Fe²⁺, Zn²⁺. (b) Why are Cu and Zn diamagnetic? (c) Catalysts for the Haber and Contact processes.

Show answer

(a) Ti³⁺ purple, Cr³⁺ violet, Mn²⁺ pale pink, Fe²⁺ green, Zn²⁺ colourless.

(b) Cu is 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ and Zn is 3d¹⁰ 4s²: their 3d orbitals are completely filled, so all the d electrons are paired.

(c) Haber process: iron (Fe). Contact process: vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅).

(11) The 4s and 3d energies are very close, so after the 4s electrons, different numbers of 3d electrons can also be lost.

(13) (i) Cu²⁺ (+2 is stable at the right of the series) (ii) Ti⁴⁺ (Ti⁴⁺ is [Ar], with an empty 3d set) (iii) Fe³⁺ (3d⁵, half-filled) (iv) Co³⁺ (Co⁴⁺ is rare; +2 and +3 are cobalt's usual states).

Key Takeaways
  • Hard, dense, high-melting, less reactive than Group I.
  • Incomplete d orbitals → variable oxidation states, colour, paramagnetism, catalysis.
  • +3 is favoured on the left of Mn, +2 on the right; Mn has the most states (up to +7).
  • Fe, Co, Ni are ferromagnetic; Sc³⁺ and Zn²⁺ are colourless.

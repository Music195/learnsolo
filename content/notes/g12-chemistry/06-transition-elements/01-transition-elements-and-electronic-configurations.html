The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations

Concept — The d-Block Between Groups II and III

The transition elements sit between Group II (s-block) and Group III (p-block), in Groups 3 to 12. IUPAC defines a transition element as one whose atom has a partially filled d subshell, or which gives cations with an incomplete d subshell. They are also called d-block elements.

  • Three series: 3d (₂₁Sc to ₃₀Zn, in Period 4), 4d (₃₉Y to ₄₈Cd), 5d (₅₇La to ₈₀Hg, leaving out the 14 lanthanides ₅₈Ce to ₇₁Lu). The lanthanides and actinides are the inner transition elements.
  • Across the 3d series the nuclear charge rises, so the atomic radius falls slowly (162 pm for Sc to 124 pm for Ni) and the first ionisation energy rises slightly. The radii are so similar that these metals blend into alloys. Zn is larger again (138 pm) with a high IE (906 kJ mol⁻¹), because its full 3d shell adds electron–electron repulsion.
  • Filling: the 4s orbital is slightly lower in energy than 3d, so 4s fills first and the rest go into 3d, one per orbital before pairing (Hund's rule). ₂₂Ti: [Ar] 4s² 3d².
  • Two exceptions: a half-filled or completely filled 3d set is extra stable, so one 4s electron moves into 3d. ₂₄Cr: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵ (not 4s² 3d⁴); ₂₉Cu: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰ (not 4s² 3d⁹).
  • Ions: electrons are removed from 4s first, then from 3d. Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶ and Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵.
Core Patterns
$$\text{atoms: } (n-1)d^{1\text{–}10}\, ns^{1\text{–}2} \qquad \text{ions: } (n-1)d^{\,0\text{–}10} \qquad [\mathrm{Ar}] = 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6$$ $$\text{number of 3d electrons in } \mathrm{M}^{n+} = Z - 18 - n$$
  • The shortcut works for every 3d ion because all the 4s electrons go first
  • Cu is a transition element because Cu²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁹ (incomplete); Zn is not, because both Zn and Zn²⁺ have a full 3d¹⁰
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Removing 3d electrons first: Fe²⁺ is 3d⁶, not 4s² 3d⁴. The 4s fills first and empties first.
  • Forgetting Cr and Cu: they have 4s¹, so Cr³⁺ is 3d³ and Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹ (count with $Z - 18 - n$).

(8) Sc: +3, since Sc³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁰, the stable argon arrangement. Zn: +2, since Zn²⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹⁰, a completely filled 3d set.

(15) 3d and 4s are very close in energy, and a half-filled (3d⁵) or completely filled (3d¹⁰) set is extra stable, so one 4s electron moves into 3d: Cr 4s¹ 3d⁵ and Cu 4s¹ 3d¹⁰.

Key Takeaways
  • Transition element: partially filled d subshell in the atom or in a common ion; Zn is not one.
  • 4s fills before 3d but empties before 3d; Cr and Cu have 4s¹.
  • Across Sc → Cu, radius falls slowly and the first IE rises slightly.

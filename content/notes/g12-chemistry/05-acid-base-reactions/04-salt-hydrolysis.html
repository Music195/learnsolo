Salt Hydrolysis

Concept — Not Every Salt Solution Is Neutral

A neutralisation forms a salt and water. Salts are strong electrolytes and dissociate completely in water. But the ions of some salts then react with water, salt hydrolysis, and change the pH. It depends on the acid and base the salt came from:

  • Strong base + strong acid (NaCl, KNO3, CaCl2): neither Na⁺ nor Cl⁻ reacts with water (NaOH and HCl are strong, so they stay ionised). The water equilibrium is undisturbed: neutral, pH 7.
  • Strong base + weak acid (CH3COONa, Na2CO3, NaOCl): the anion is the conjugate base of a weak acid, so it takes H⁺ from water: $\mathrm{CH_3COO^- + H_2O \rightleftharpoons CH_3COOH + OH^-}$. Extra OH⁻: basic, pH > 7.
  • Weak base + strong acid (NH4Cl, NH4NO3, (NH4)2SO4): the cation NH4+ is the conjugate acid of a weak base, so it gives H⁺: $\mathrm{NH_4^+ + H_2O \rightleftharpoons NH_4OH + H^+}$. Extra H⁺: acidic, pH < 7.
  • Weak base + weak acid (CH3COONH4, NH4CN, NH4F): both ions hydrolyse; compare $K_b$ of the anion with $K_a$ of the cation:
    • $K_b(\text{anion}) > K_a(\text{cation})$: basic (e.g. NH4CN)
    • $K_b(\text{anion}) < K_a(\text{cation})$: acidic (e.g. NH4F)
    • $K_b \approx K_a$: nearly neutral (CH3COONH4: both are $5.6 \times 10^{-10}$)
Core Rules
$$K_a \times K_b = K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14} \quad\text{(for a conjugate pair)}$$ $$\text{salt of strong base + weak acid } (c):\ [\mathrm{OH^-}] \approx \sqrt{\tfrac{K_w}{K_a}\,c} \qquad \text{weak base + strong acid}:\ [\mathrm{H^+}] \approx \sqrt{\tfrac{K_w}{K_b}\,c}$$
  • "The stronger parent wins": strong acid → acidic salt; strong base → basic salt; both strong → neutral
  • $K_a(\mathrm{NH_4^+}) = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$, $K_b(\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}) = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$ (from $K_w/1.8 \times 10^{-5}$)
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "Salts are neutral": only salts of a strong acid with a strong base.
  • Which ion reacts: it is the ion from the weak parent; ions from strong parents (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, NO3−, SO42−) are spectators.

Worked Examples — Example 9 and Review Questions (pp. 90–91)

Example 9: Acidic, basic or nearly neutral? (i) (NH4)2SO4, (ii) KNO3, (iii) Na2CO3, (iv) NH4F ($K_a = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$ for NH4+, $K_b = 1.4 \times 10^{-11}$ for F⁻).

Show answer

(i) Weak base NH4OH + strong acid H2SO4: acidic. (ii) Strong base KOH + strong acid HNO3: neutral. (iii) Strong base NaOH + weak acid H2CO3: basic.

(iv) Both weak; $K_b(\mathrm{F^-}) = 1.4 \times 10^{-11} < K_a(\mathrm{NH_4^+}) = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$, so the cation hydrolyses more: acidic.

Review Q1: The four classes of salts, with an example of each.

Show answer

Strong base + strong acid: sodium chloride, NaCl (neutral). Strong base + weak acid: sodium ethanoate, CH3COONa (basic). Weak base + strong acid: ammonium chloride, NH4Cl (acidic). Weak base + weak acid: ammonium ethanoate, CH3COONH4 (depends on $K_a$ and $K_b$; this one is neutral).

Review Q2: An acid–base reaction producing each salt and the nature of its solution: (i) CaCl2, (ii) HCOONa, (iii) NH4I.

Show answer

(i) $\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2 + 2HCl \to CaCl_2 + 2H_2O}$: neutral. (ii) $\mathrm{HCOOH + NaOH \to HCOONa + H_2O}$: basic. (iii) $\mathrm{NH_4OH + HI \to NH_4I + H_2O}$ (or $\mathrm{NH_3 + HI \to NH_4I}$): acidic (HI is a strong acid).

Review Q3: Solutions of (i) ammonium nitrate, (ii) ammonium cyanide, (iii) potassium chloride ($K_a = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$ for NH4+, $K_b = 2.5 \times 10^{-5}$ for CN⁻).

Show answer

(i) Acidic (weak base + strong acid). (ii) $K_b(\mathrm{CN^-}) = 2.5 \times 10^{-5} > K_a(\mathrm{NH_4^+}) = 5.6 \times 10^{-10}$: basic. (iii) Neutral (KOH and HCl both strong).

Exercises 2(d) and 12: (2d) Why is NaCN solution basic? (12a) Why is NH4NO3 (a fertiliser) acidic? (12b) Why is NaOCl (bleach) basic?

Show answer

(2d) Hydrolysis of the anion CN⁻: $\mathrm{CN^- + H_2O \rightleftharpoons HCN + OH^-}$ (Na⁺ is a spectator).

(12a) NH4+ is the conjugate acid of the weak base NH3: $\mathrm{NH_4^+ + H_2O \rightleftharpoons NH_3 + H_3O^+}$; NO3− (from the strong acid HNO3) does not react. Extra H3O⁺: acidic.

(12b) OCl⁻ is the conjugate base of the weak acid HOCl: $\mathrm{OCl^- + H_2O \rightleftharpoons HOCl + OH^-}$; Na⁺ does not react. Extra OH⁻: basic (about pH 10 at 0.1 mol dm⁻³).

Key Takeaways
  • Strong + strong → neutral; strong base + weak acid → basic; weak base + strong acid → acidic.
  • Weak + weak: compare $K_b$ of the anion with $K_a$ of the cation.
  • The hydrolysing ion is the one from the weak parent.

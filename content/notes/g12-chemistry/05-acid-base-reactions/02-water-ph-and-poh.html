Ionic Product of Water, pH and pOH

Concept — Water Ionises a Little

Water can act as an acid or as a base; it is amphiprotic (it can both donate and accept protons). Even pure water reacts with itself very slightly, the autoionisation of water:

$\mathrm{H_2O(l) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)}$   or simply   $\mathrm{H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)}$

  • Ionic product of water: the concentration of water hardly changes, so it is included in the constant: $K_w = [\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]$. In pure water at 25 °C, $[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}] = 1.0 \times 10^{-7}$ mol dm−3, so $K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$ mol2 dm−6. This holds for every dilute aqueous solution at 25 °C.
  • Temperature: autoionisation is endothermic, so $K_w$ grows when water is heated (about $5.6 \times 10^{-13}$ at 100 °C, roughly 50 times larger). Pure water is still neutral ($[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}]$), but its pH is then below 7.
  • pH (Sørensen, 1909): $\mathrm{pH} = -\log[\mathrm{H^+}]$, a convenient 0–14 scale. pOH $= -\log[\mathrm{OH^-}]$. At 25 °C, $\mathrm{pH + pOH} = 14$.
  • Acidic: pH < 7 (the smaller the pH, the more H⁺); neutral: pH = 7; alkaline: pH > 7. Universal indicator shows the pH as a colour, from red (strong acid) through green (neutral) to violet (strong alkali).
  • One pH unit is a factor of 10 in [H⁺]: pH 3 has ten times more H⁺ than pH 4.
Core Formulas (at 25 °C)
$$K_w = [\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}\ \text{mol}^2\,\text{dm}^{-6}$$ $$\mathrm{pH} = -\log[\mathrm{H^+}] \qquad [\mathrm{H^+}] = 10^{-\mathrm{pH}}$$ $$\mathrm{pOH} = -\log[\mathrm{OH^-}] \qquad [\mathrm{OH^-}] = 10^{-\mathrm{pOH}}$$ $$\mathrm{p}K_w = \mathrm{pH} + \mathrm{pOH} = 14$$
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Sign of the log: $\log(6.5 \times 10^{-4}) = -3.19$, so the pH is $+3.19$.
  • "pH 7 is always neutral": only at 25 °C. Neutral means $[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}]$.
  • Linear thinking: pH 2 is not "twice as acidic" as pH 4; it has 100 times more H⁺.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 2–4 and Review Questions (pp. 82–83)

Example 2: $K_w = 2.4 \times 10^{-13}$ at 80 °C. Find [H⁺] and [OH⁻] in pure water.

Show solution

Pure water: $[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}]$, so $[\mathrm{H^+}]^2 = 2.4 \times 10^{-13}$.

$[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}] = \sqrt{2.4 \times 10^{-13}} = 4.9 \times 10^{-7}$ mol dm−3 (pH 6.31, yet neutral).

Example 3: pH of grapefruit juice with $[\mathrm{H^+}] = 6.5 \times 10^{-4}$ mol dm−3.

Show solution

$\mathrm{pH} = -\log(6.5 \times 10^{-4}) = -(0.81 - 4) = 3.19$

Example 4: [H⁺] and [OH⁻] in blood of pH 7.4.

Show solution

$[\mathrm{H^+}] = 10^{-7.4} = 4.0 \times 10^{-8}$ mol dm−3.

$[\mathrm{OH^-}] = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{4.0 \times 10^{-8}} = 2.5 \times 10^{-7}$ mol dm−3 (blood is slightly alkaline).

Review Q1: $K_w = 2.9 \times 10^{-14}$ at 40 °C. Find [H⁺], [OH⁻] and pH of pure water.

Show solution

$[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}] = \sqrt{2.9 \times 10^{-14}} = 1.7 \times 10^{-7}$ mol dm−3; pH $= -\log(1.7 \times 10^{-7}) = 6.77$.

Review Q2: [H⁺] in a lake of pH 4.5.

Show solution

$[\mathrm{H^+}] = 10^{-4.5} = 3.2 \times 10^{-5}$ mol dm−3

Review Q3: [OH⁻] of coal-mine runoff with $[\mathrm{H^+}] = 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$ mol dm−3. Acidic, basic or neutral?

Show solution

$[\mathrm{OH^-}] = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{1.2 \times 10^{-4}} = 8.3 \times 10^{-11}$ mol dm−3. Since $[\mathrm{H^+}] > [\mathrm{OH^-}]$ (pH 3.92), it is acidic.

Exercise 7 (p. 95): (a) pH of (i) seawater with $[\mathrm{OH^-}] = 1.58 \times 10^{-6}$, (ii) black coffee with $[\mathrm{H^+}] = 7.8 \times 10^{-6}$, (iii) fruit juice with $[\mathrm{H^+}] = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}$. (b) [H⁺] at pH (i) 8.3, (ii) 3.6, (iii) 12.4.

Show solution

(a)(i) pOH $= -\log(1.58 \times 10^{-6}) = 5.80$, so pH $= 14 - 5.80 = 8.20$. (ii) pH $= -\log(7.8 \times 10^{-6}) = 5.11$. (iii) pH $= -\log(2.5 \times 10^{-4}) = 3.60$.

(b)(i) $10^{-8.3} = 5.0 \times 10^{-9}$; (ii) $10^{-3.6} = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}$; (iii) $10^{-12.4} = 4.0 \times 10^{-13}$ mol dm−3.

Key Takeaways
  • $K_w = [\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$ at 25 °C; it increases with temperature.
  • pH $= -\log[\mathrm{H^+}]$; pOH $= -\log[\mathrm{OH^-}]$; pH + pOH = 14 at 25 °C.
  • Each pH unit is a factor of 10 in [H⁺].
  • Neutral means $[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}]$; at 25 °C that is pH 7.

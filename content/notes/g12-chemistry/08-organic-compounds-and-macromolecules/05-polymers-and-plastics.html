Polymers and Plastics

Concept — Long Chains from Small Repeat Units

A macromolecule contains thousands of atoms or more. A polymer is a long-chain macromolecule built from many small repeat units, the monomers; joining them is polymerisation. One kind of monomer gives a homopolymer; two or more give a copolymer. Natural polymers: proteins, DNA, starch, cellulose, wool, natural rubber, silk, hair. Synthetic: polyethene, nylon, terylene, teflon, PVC, PVA, polystyrene, polypropylene.

  • Addition polymerisation: unsaturated monomers (with C=C) join with nothing lost. The double bond opens and each monomer bonds to two others. The repeat unit is the monomer with C=C changed to C–C.
    • Polyethene (PE): $n\,\mathrm{H_2C{=}CH_2} \to \mathrm{{-}[CH_2{-}CH_2]_n{-}}$ (high temperature and pressure, catalyst), with n = 10,000 to 30,000. Easily moulded: toys, bags, buckets.
    • PVC (polychloroethene) from chloroethene H₂C=CHCl, repeat unit –CH₂–CHCl–: strong, light, durable: pipes, raincoats, thin gloves, flooring.
    • Teflon –[CF₂–CF₂]–: heat-resistant and non-stick (frying pans). Polystyrene –[CH₂–CH(C₆H₅)]–: hard, light, brittle (styrofoam, disposable containers).
    • Addition polymers (plastics) swell or dissolve in organic solvents, weather poorly and turn brittle at low temperatures.
  • Condensation polymerisation: monomers with two different functional groups (in one molecule or two) join and lose a small molecule, usually water.
    • Polyester: terylene (PET) from terephthalic acid HOOC–C₆H₄–COOH and ethane-1,2-diol HO–CH₂CH₂–OH; acid –OH + alcohol –H leave as H₂O, forming ester linkages. Resists stretching and shrinking, washes and dries quickly: clothing, carpets, curtains, sheets.
    • Polyamide: nylon 6,6 from adipic acid HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH and 1,6-diaminohexane H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂, joined by amide linkages –CO–NH–. Very strong: fishing line, shoes, toothbrush bristles, guitar strings, machine parts.
    • Proteins are natural polyamides: about 20 amino acids H₂N–CHR–COOH join by peptide bonds (amide links): dipeptide, tripeptide, … polypeptide (50 to 2000 amino acid residues).
    • Condensation polymers usually have lower molar masses than addition polymers, but polar groups and hydrogen bonds between chains raise crystallinity and tensile strength.
  • Plastics and the environment. Plastics are cheap, mouldable, light, tough, waterproof and resist decay, rust and chemicals, but are non-biodegradable, so they last for centuries. Air: burning gives poisonous gases (PVC gives HCl). Water: turtles choke on bags; plastics block drains, worsen floods and breed germs. Soil: landfill plastic stays for hundreds of years.
    • Recycling codes (lower number = easier to recycle): 1 PET, 2 HDPE, 3 V/PVC, 4 LDPE, 5 PP, 6 PS, 7 others.
    • Biodegradable plastics: contain starch granules, or are made from plant monomers such as polylactic acid (PLA, from fermented corn starch); microorganisms break them down. Photodegradable plastics: C=O groups along the chain absorb UV, the chain breaks into fragments that then biodegrade faster.
Counting Masses
$$\text{addition: } M_{\text{chain}} = n \times M_{\text{monomer}} \qquad \text{condensation: } M_{\text{chain}} = \sum M_{\text{monomers}} - 18 \times (\text{links formed})$$
  • Polyethene with n = 10,000 to 30,000: $M = 28n$ = 280,000 to 840,000 g mol⁻¹
  • Terylene repeat unit: 166 + 62 − 2 × 18 = 192; nylon 6,6 repeat unit: 146 + 116 − 2 × 18 = 226
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Repeat unit of an addition polymer: it has a C–C single bond and the two "open" bonds at its ends; no double bond remains.
  • Condensation needs two groups: a monomer like HO–□–OH can only make a condensation polymer with a partner such as HOOC–□–COOH.

Worked Examples — Review Questions (p. 151)

Review Q1: CH₂=CHCl polymerises. (a) Structural formula of the polymer. (b) Addition or condensation?

Show answer

(a) $\mathrm{{-}[CH_2{-}CHCl]_n{-}}$, polychloroethene (PVC). (b) Addition: the C=C opens and no small molecule is lost.

Review Q2: (a) Draw terylene showing two repeat units. (b) Name its linkage.

Show answer

(a) –O–CO–C₆H₄–CO–O–CH₂–CH₂–O–CO–C₆H₄–CO–O–CH₂–CH₂– (the repeat unit is –CO–C₆H₄–CO–O–CH₂–CH₂–O–). (b) The ester linkage –CO–O–.

Review Q3: The two functional groups in all amino acids.

Show answer

The amine group –NH₂ and the carboxyl group –COOH (H₂N–CHR–COOH).

Worked Examples — Exercises (pp. 152–154)

Short answers: 1(d) An amino acid is a protein monomer. 1(e) Polyethene can be decomposed by microorganisms. 2(c) H₂C=CH₂ is the monomer of ___. 2(d) Nylon is obtained by ___ polymerisation. 2(e) Polypeptides form when several ___ link. 3(d) Which cannot form a condensation polymer: Cl–□–Cl, HO–□–OH, HOOC–□–COOH? 3(e) Nylon is a (polyamide, polyester, polyethene). 4(a), 4(c), 4(e) matching. 5(c)–(f) one word.

Show answer

1(d) TRUE. 1(e) FALSE: polyethene is non-biodegradable. 2(c) polyethene. 2(d) condensation. 2(e) amino acids. 3(d) Cl–□–Cl (it has no –OH, –COOH or –NH₂ group to condense). 3(e) polyamide. 4: (a) polylactic acid – biodegradable plastic; (c) terylene – synthetic polyester; (e) proteins – build-up of amino acids. 5: (c) polymer; (d) condensation polymerisation; (e) ester linkage; (f) peptide bond.

Exercise 15: (a) Which of H₂NCH(CH₃)COOH, H₂C=CHCOOH, H₂NCH₂COOH, F₂C=CF₂ form addition and which condensation polymers? (b) Equation for polymerising propene and its repeat unit.

Show answer

(a) Addition: H₂C=CHCOOH and F₂C=CF₂ (they have C=C). Condensation: H₂NCH(CH₃)COOH and H₂NCH₂COOH (each has –NH₂ and –COOH, which join with loss of water).

(b) $n\,\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH{-}CH_3 \to {-}[CH_2{-}CH(CH_3)]_n{-}}$; repeat unit –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–.

Exercise 16: The amino acid H₂NCH(CH₂CH₂SCH₃)COOH. (a) Structural formula. (b) Formula of its polymer. (c) Does the polymer have peptide bonds?

Show answer

(a) H₂N–CH(R)–COOH with R = –CH₂–CH₂–S–CH₃ on the central carbon (this is methionine).

(b) $\mathrm{{-}[NH{-}CH(CH_2CH_2SCH_3){-}CO]_n{-}}$. (c) Yes: each –COOH has condensed with the –NH₂ of the next molecule, forming –CO–NH– peptide bonds.

Exercises 17–19: (17) Monomers of H–NH–CHR–CO–NH–CHR′–CO–NH–CHR″–CO–NH–CHR‴–COOH and the bond between them. (18) A chain from two molecules of A (H₂N–CHR–COOH) and one of B (H₂N–CHR′–COOH). (19) What is a photodegradable polymer and how is it designed to break down?

Show answer

(17) H₂N–CHR–COOH, H₂N–CHR′–COOH, H₂N–CHR″–COOH, H₂N–CHR‴–COOH; each has an amine group –NH₂ and a carboxyl group –COOH; they are joined by peptide bonds.

(18) One possible order (A–A–B): H₂N–CHR–CO–NH–CHR–CO–NH–CHR′–COOH, with two peptide bonds and two H₂O released.

(19) It breaks down when exposed to light. Carbonyl (C=O) groups are built into the chain at intervals; they absorb ultraviolet energy from sunlight, which weakens and breaks the chain into small fragments that then biodegrade much more quickly.

Key Takeaways
  • Addition: C=C monomers, nothing lost (PE, PVC, teflon, polystyrene).
  • Condensation: two functional groups, water lost: polyesters (terylene), polyamides (nylon, proteins).
  • Plastics are non-biodegradable; recycle by code, or use biodegradable (PLA) and photodegradable plastics.

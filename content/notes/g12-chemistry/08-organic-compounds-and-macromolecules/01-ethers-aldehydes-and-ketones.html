Ethers, Aldehydes and Ketones

Concept — Oxygen Between Carbons, and the Carbonyl Group

Hydrocarbons contain only C and H. Bonding carbon to oxygen gives alcohols, ethers, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids and esters; adding nitrogen gives amines and amides. Each family has a functional group that decides its reactions.

  • Ethers, R–O–R′ (general formula CnH2n+2O): an O atom bonded to two alkyl groups; symmetrical (R = R′) or unsymmetrical.
    • Names: common name = the two alkyl groups + "ether"; IUPAC = smaller group + O as an alkoxy prefix on the alkane: CH₃OCH₃ dimethyl ether = methoxymethane; C₂H₅OCH₃ ethyl methyl ether = methoxyethane; C₂H₅OC₂H₅ diethyl ether = ethoxyethane.
    • Preparation: dehydration of alcohol: 2C₂H₅OH → C₂H₅OC₂H₅ + H₂O (limited conc. H₂SO₄, 140 °C).
    • Properties: methoxymethane and methoxyethane are gases; the others are volatile liquids. No hydrogen bonding between ether molecules, so their boiling points are much lower than alcohols of similar mass. Up to 3 carbons they dissolve in water. Hot conc. HI breaks the C–O bond: CH₃OCH₃ + HI → CH₃I + CH₃OH.
    • Uses: fairly inert, so used as reaction solvents; ethoxyethane was a surgical anaesthetic.
  • Aldehydes and ketones (general formula CnH2nO) contain the carbonyl group >C=O. In an aldehyde it is at the end of the chain (bonded to at least one H, –CHO); in a ketone it is inside the chain.
    • Names: aldehydes take -al (methanal HCHO = formaldehyde, ethanal CH₃CHO = acetaldehyde, propanal, 2-methylpropanal); ketones take -one (propanone CH₃COCH₃ = acetone, butanone = ethyl methyl ketone, 3-methylbutan-2-one). Number from the end nearest the carbonyl. Common names use Greek letters: the α carbon is bonded to the functional group, β is next, and so on.
    • Preparation: oxidise alcohols with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ ([O]). A primary alcohol gives an aldehyde, which must be distilled off at once (it boils lower than the alcohol), or it is oxidised further to a carboxylic acid. A secondary alcohol gives a ketone, which is not oxidised further even under reflux. Hydration of alkynes (dil. H₂SO₄, HgSO₄, 60 °C): ethyne gives ethanal; other alkynes give ketones (propyne → propanone).
    • Reactions: aldehydes are easily oxidised to carboxylic acids (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇, HNO₃); ketones only under vigorous conditions. Both are reduced by LiAlH₄ in dry ether (then dil. H₂SO₄): aldehyde → primary alcohol, ketone → secondary alcohol. HCN adds across C=O (HCN made from NaCN + dil. H₂SO₄) to give hydroxynitriles.
    • Uses: aldehydes for resins, dyes, organic acids, perfumes, detergents; formalin (40 % methanal) as disinfectant and specimen preservative; cinnamaldehyde in perfumes. Ketones as solvents for waxes, plastics, paints, lacquers, varnishes and glues; propanone in nail polish remover.
Key Reactions
$$\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH + [O] \xrightarrow[\text{distil}]{K_2Cr_2O_7/H^+} CH_3CHO + H_2O} \qquad \mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_3 + [O] \to CH_3COCH_3 + H_2O}$$ $$\mathrm{CH_3CHO + 2[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} CH_3CH_2OH} \qquad \mathrm{CH_3CHO + HCN \to CH_3CH(OH)CN}\ \text{(2-hydroxypropanenitrile)}$$
  • [O] = oxygen from the oxidising agent; [H] = hydrogen from the reducing agent
  • Dry ether is needed because LiAlH₄ reacts violently with water
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Distil vs reflux: distilling stops at the aldehyde; refluxing a primary alcohol goes on to the carboxylic acid.
  • Ketone + oxidising agent: no easy reaction. This is how the two are told apart (Fehling's test, next notes).
  • Ether boiling points: lower than isomeric alcohols, because ethers have no O–H and cannot hydrogen bond with each other.

Exercises 1(a), 2(b), 3(a), 4(b), 5(a): (1a) TRUE or FALSE: dehydrogenation of secondary alcohols gives ketones. (2b) A ___ group in an aldehyde or ketone is detected with 2,4-DNP. (3a) Butanal contains a (carboxyl, carbonyl, hydroxyl) group. (4b) Diethyl ether is matched with? (5a) Common name of dimethyl ketone?

Show answer

(1a) TRUE: removing two H atoms (oxidation) from a secondary alcohol gives a ketone. (2b) carbonyl. (3a) carbonyl. (4b) an anaesthetic agent. (5a) acetone.

Exercise 11: An organic compound gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP but no change with Fehling's reagent. What type of compound is it?

Show answer

A ketone: the 2,4-DNP result shows a carbonyl group, and no reaction with Fehling's shows it is not an aldehyde (aldehydes are oxidised and give a reddish-brown precipitate).

Extra (from the text): Why must ethanal be distilled off as it forms, but propanone need not be?

Show answer

Ethanal boils lower than ethanol, so it can be removed at once; left in the hot acidified dichromate it would be oxidised on to ethanoic acid. Propanone is a ketone and is not oxidised further, even under reflux.

Key Takeaways
  • Ethers R–O–R′: alkoxyalkane names, made by dehydrating alcohols, low boiling points, split by HI.
  • Aldehydes (–CHO, -al) from primary alcohols (distil); ketones (C–CO–C, -one) from secondary alcohols.
  • Aldehydes oxidise easily to acids; both reduce with LiAlH₄ and add HCN.

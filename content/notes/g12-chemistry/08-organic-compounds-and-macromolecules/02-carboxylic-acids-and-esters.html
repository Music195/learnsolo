Carboxylic Acids and Esters

Concept — –COOH, and What Happens When It Meets an Alcohol
  • Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group –COOH (a carbonyl plus a hydroxyl); general formula CnH2nO₂.
    • Names: common names come from Latin sources: formic acid HCOOH (formica, ant), acetic acid CH₃COOH (acetum, vinegar). IUPAC: replace -e by -oic acid (methanoic, ethanoic acid). The COOH carbon is always C-1 and the carboxyl group takes priority: 2-chloro-3-methylbutanoic acid, 4-hydroxy-2-methylbutanoic acid.
    • Preparation: oxidise a primary alcohol under reflux with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (reflux keeps volatile material reacting to completion), or with air and Acetobacter bacteria (vinegar).
    • Properties: up to C₉ they are colourless liquids with an unpleasant smell; up to C₄ they dissolve in water. Strong hydrogen bonding between molecules gives high boiling points.
    • Reactions (weak acids): with reactive metals → salt + H₂; with carbonates → salt + H₂O + CO₂; with bases → salt + H₂O; reduced by LiAlH₄ to primary alcohols.
    • Uses: polymers, medicines (antibiotics, antihistamines; aspirin is the acetyl derivative of salicylic acid), food additives (ethanoic acid, sodium benzoate); methanoic acid in textiles, rubber and leather.
  • Esters are acid derivatives in which the –OH is replaced by an alkoxy group –OR′: R–COO–R′.
    • Names: alkyl group of the alcohol first, then the acid with -oic acid → -oate: HCOOCH₃ methyl methanoate (methyl formate), CH₃COOC₂H₅ ethyl ethanoate (ethyl acetate), C₂H₅COOCH₃ methyl propanoate.
    • Preparation (esterification): warm the acid and alcohol with a few drops of conc. H₂SO₄. The acid loses –OH and the alcohol loses –H, which leave as water.
    • Properties: small esters are colourless volatile liquids with pleasant, fruity smells, slightly soluble in water, with lower boiling points than their parent acids.
    • Reactions: acidic hydrolysis is the reverse of esterification (reversible); basic hydrolysis with NaOH under reflux goes to completion, giving the sodium salt and the alcohol; transesterification: ester + another alcohol (acid catalyst) → a different ester.
    • Uses: natural fruit flavours and flower scents, artificial flavourings, solvents for cosmetics, perfumes and glues; fats and oils (natural esters) + NaOH → soap.
Key Reactions

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Naming an ester backwards: CH₃COOC₂H₅ is ethyl ethanoate; the part bonded to the –O– (from the alcohol) is named first, but it is written last in the formula.
  • Boiling points: an ester boils lower than its parent acid; it has no O–H to hydrogen bond.

Worked Examples — Review Questions 1–3 (p. 139)

Review Q1 (part): IUPAC names of (CH₃)₃CCH₂COOH and CH₃COOCH₃.

Show answer

(CH₃)₃C–CH₂–COOH: the chain through COOH is C1 (COOH), C2 (CH₂), C3 (carrying two CH₃ groups as well as one in the chain), C4.

3,3-dimethylbutanoic acid; CH₃COOCH₃ is methyl ethanoate.

Review Q2: Identify Q, R, S, T: (a) Q + magnesium → magnesium methanoate + R (gas); (b) S + sodium hydroxide → sodium ethanoate + water; (c) ethanoic acid + T → methyl ethanoate + water.

Show answer

(a) $\mathrm{2HCOOH + Mg \to (HCOO)_2Mg + H_2}$; (b) $\mathrm{CH_3COOH + NaOH \to CH_3COONa + H_2O}$; (c) $\mathrm{CH_3COOH + CH_3OH \to CH_3COOCH_3 + H_2O}$.

Q = methanoic acid HCOOH, R = hydrogen H₂, S = ethanoic acid CH₃COOH, T = methanol CH₃OH.

Review Q3: Reactants for (a) CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ (smells like pears), (b) CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ (smells like bananas).

Show answer

(a) CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH (ethanoic acid + propan-1-ol: propyl ethanoate). (b) CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂OH (ethanoic acid + pentan-1-ol: pentyl ethanoate).

Worked Examples — Exercises 1(b), 2(a), 3(b), 6–10 (pp. 152–153)

Short answers: (1b) An ester has a higher boiling point than its acid: TRUE or FALSE? (2a) Heating ethanol and ethanoic acid with conc. H₂SO₄ gives a fruity compound; the reaction is called ___. (3b) Which ester is made from methanol and methanoic acid?

Show answer

(1b) FALSE: the ester has a lower boiling point (no hydrogen bonding). (2a) esterification. (3b) HCOOCH₃ (methyl methanoate).

Exercise 6: I CH₃CH₂OH, II CH₃CH(CH₃)COOH, III CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃. (i) Which comes from fermentation of starch or sugar? (ii) Their classes? (iii) IUPAC names of II and III.

Show answer

(i) I, ethanol. (ii) I alcohol, II carboxylic acid, III ester. (iii) II: 2-methylpropanoic acid; III: ethyl propanoate.

Exercise 7: Equations for (a) complete combustion and (b) reduction of butanoic acid.

Show answer

(a) Butanoic acid is C₃H₇COOH = C₄H₈O₂: $\mathrm{C_4H_8O_2 + 5O_2 \to 4CO_2 + 4H_2O}$ (O: 2 + 10 = 8 + 4 ✓).

(b) $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2COOH + 4[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4,\ dry\ ether;\ dil\ H_2SO_4} CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH + H_2O}$ (butan-1-ol).

Exercise 8: Alcohol X (C₂H₆O) is oxidised to Y; Y reacts with X to give sweet-smelling Z (C₄H₈O₂). Identify X, Y, Z.

Show answer

X = ethanol CH₃CH₂OH; Y = ethanoic acid CH₃COOH; Z = ethyl ethanoate CH₃COOC₂H₅ (C₄H₈O₂ ✓).

Exercise 9: Ethanoic acid and methanol are warmed with conc. H₂SO₄. Equation and IUPAC name of the product.

$\mathrm{CH_3{-}CO{-}OH + H{-}O{-}CH_3 \rightleftharpoons CH_3{-}CO{-}O{-}CH_3 + H_2O}$: methyl ethanoate.

Exercise 10: (a) Acid and alcohol for butyl methanoate, ethyl methanoate, propyl propanoate. (b) Esters from ethanoic acid + butan-1-ol, and propanoic acid + ethanol. (c) Structures of ethyl methanoate and propyl ethanoate. (d) Two uses of esters.

Show answer

(a) methanoic acid + butan-1-ol; methanoic acid + ethanol; propanoic acid + propan-1-ol. (b) butyl ethanoate CH₃COOC₄H₉; ethyl propanoate C₂H₅COOC₂H₅.

(c) H–CO–O–CH₂CH₃ (HCOOC₂H₅) and CH₃–CO–O–CH₂CH₂CH₃. (d) Artificial food flavourings; solvents for perfumes, cosmetics and glues (also soap from fats).

Key Takeaways
  • –COOH acids: -oic acid names, high boiling points, react like weak acids, reduce to primary alcohols.
  • Acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (conc. H₂SO₄); names "alkyl alkanoate".
  • Acid hydrolysis is reversible; NaOH hydrolysis is complete (soap).

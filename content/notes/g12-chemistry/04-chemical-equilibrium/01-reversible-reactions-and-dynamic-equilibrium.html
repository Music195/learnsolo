Reversible Reactions and Dynamic Equilibrium

Concept — Two Reactions Running at the Same Speed

Equilibrium means a state of balance. A book resting on a table is in static equilibrium: nothing moves. Chemical equilibrium is dynamic: in a sealed bottle of soda, $\mathrm{CO_2}$ keeps dissolving and keeps escaping, at the same rate, so the amounts stay the same. Equilibria also matter in blood (haemoglobin and oxygen) and in the carbon cycle.

  • Irreversible reactions go to completion: burning fuel stops when the fuel is used up, and the products cannot turn back.
  • Reversible reactions go both ways: the forward reaction (reactants → products) and the reverse reaction (products → reactants) happen at the same time. The symbol is $\rightleftharpoons$: $\mathrm{A + B \rightleftharpoons C}$.
  • Closed system: energy can enter or leave but matter cannot. A reversible reaction in a closed system eventually reaches equilibrium.
  • Reaching equilibrium: at the start there are only reactants, so the forward rate is high and the reverse rate is zero. As products build up, the forward rate falls and the reverse rate rises, until the two rates are equal. From then on the concentrations stay constant, but both reactions continue.
  • Homogeneous equilibrium: everything in one phase, e.g. $\mathrm{N_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO(g)}$.
  • Heterogeneous equilibrium: more than one phase, e.g. $\mathrm{H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_2O(g)}$ and $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3(s) + 3CO(g) \rightleftharpoons 2Fe(s) + 3CO_2(g)}$.
Signs of Dynamic Equilibrium
$$\text{reactants} \;\underset{\text{reverse}}{\overset{\text{forward}}{\rightleftharpoons}}\; \text{products} \qquad \text{at equilibrium:}\ \ \text{rate}_{\text{forward}} = \text{rate}_{\text{reverse}}$$
  • Concentrations constant (not necessarily equal!)
  • Both reactions still going on (dynamic)
  • Only in a closed system; the same equilibrium can be reached from either side
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • "At equilibrium the reaction stops": both reactions continue at equal rates.
  • "At equilibrium the concentrations are equal": they are constant; they can be very different from each other.
  • Phases: classify by the states written in the equation; one solid or liquid with gases makes it heterogeneous.

Worked Examples — Review Questions (p. 64)

Review Q1: Ammonium chloride breaks down on heating into ammonia and hydrogen chloride, which re-form it on cooling. Write (i) the breakdown, (ii) the formation, (iii) the overall reversible equation.

Show answer

(i) $\mathrm{NH_4Cl(s) \to NH_3(g) + HCl(g)}$   (ii) $\mathrm{NH_3(g) + HCl(g) \to NH_4Cl(s)}$   (iii) $\mathrm{NH_4Cl(s) \rightleftharpoons NH_3(g) + HCl(g)}$

Review Q2: Which statement is NOT true about $\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)}$: (i) it is at equilibrium, (ii) it is a heterogeneous equilibrium, (iii) it is a homogeneous equilibrium?

Show answer

(ii) is not true: all three substances are gases (one phase), so it is a homogeneous equilibrium.

Review Q3: Homogeneous or heterogeneous? (i) $\mathrm{2NaHCO_3(s) \rightleftharpoons Na_2CO_3(s) + H_2O(g) + CO_2(g)}$, (ii) $\mathrm{C(s) + 2H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons CH_4(g)}$, (iii) $\mathrm{C_2H_4(g) + H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons C_2H_6(g)}$, (iv) $\mathrm{2Hg(l) + O_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2HgO(s)}$.

Show answer

(i) heterogeneous (solids + gases); (ii) heterogeneous (solid + gases); (iii) homogeneous (all gases); (iv) heterogeneous (liquid, gas and solid).

Exercise 1 (True/False, p. 74): (a) Reversible reactions go forward only. (c) The reaction stops at equilibrium. (e) The equilibrium does not depend on the initial concentrations.

Show answer

(a) FALSE: they proceed in both directions. (c) FALSE: both reactions continue at equal rates. (e) TRUE in the sense that the same equilibrium (the same $K$) is reached from either side; the actual equilibrium concentrations do depend on how much was put in.

Key Takeaways
  • A reversible reaction runs forward and backward at the same time ($\rightleftharpoons$).
  • In a closed system it reaches dynamic equilibrium: equal forward and reverse rates, constant concentrations.
  • The same equilibrium is reached starting from reactants or from products.
  • Homogeneous: one phase. Heterogeneous: more than one phase.

Rate of Reaction

Concept — How Fast Reactants Turn into Products

Every reaction has a speed. An explosion of dynamite, striking a match or burning magnesium ribbon takes less than a second; spoiling of preserved food takes weeks; rusting of an iron pipe months to years; weathering of rocks many years. The study of reaction rates is chemical kinetics. It matters in drug design, pollution control, food processing and in making ammonia for fertiliser economically.

  • Rate of reaction = the change in the amount (mass, volume or concentration) of a reactant used up or a product formed, per unit time.
  • Following a reaction: for $\mathrm{Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) \to MgCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)}$ we can weigh the magnesium left, or collect the hydrogen in a gas syringe and read its volume at time intervals.
  • The shape of the graph: the reactant concentration falls and the product concentration rises, quickly at first and then more slowly, until the reaction stops when a reactant is used up. The steeper the graph, the faster the reaction.
  • Units: g s−1 (mass), cm3 min−1 or dm3 min−1 (gas volume), mol dm−3 s−1 (concentration in solution). Use whatever time unit suits the reaction.
  • Sign: reactants decrease, so we put a minus sign in front of $\Delta[\text{reactant}]$ to make the rate positive. A rate is always a positive quantity.
Core Formulas
$$\text{rate} = \frac{\text{change in amount}}{\text{time taken}} = -\frac{\Delta[\text{reactant}]}{\Delta t} = +\frac{\Delta[\text{product}]}{\Delta t}$$ $$a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \to c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}:\qquad \text{rate} = -\frac{1}{a}\frac{\Delta[\mathrm{A}]}{\Delta t} = -\frac{1}{b}\frac{\Delta[\mathrm{B}]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{c}\frac{\Delta[\mathrm{C}]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{d}\frac{\Delta[\mathrm{D}]}{\Delta t}$$
  • $[\ ]$ = molar concentration in mol dm−3; $\Delta$ = final − initial
  • Dividing by the coefficient makes the rate the same whichever substance we follow
  • Solids and pure liquids have no concentration term; follow their mass or moles instead
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • A negative rate: $\Delta[\text{reactant}]$ is negative; the minus sign in the formula makes the rate positive.
  • Forgetting the coefficient: in $\mathrm{2A \to 4B + C}$, the rate is $-\tfrac12 \Delta[\mathrm{A}]/\Delta t$, not $-\Delta[\mathrm{A}]/\Delta t$.
  • Mixed units: convert minutes to seconds (2 min = 120 s) when the answer is asked per second.

Worked Examples — Textbook Examples 1–4 (pp. 49–50)

Example 1: In $\mathrm{Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) \to ZnCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)}$, 30 g of zinc is used up in 2 min. Find the rate in mol s−1. (Zn = 65.38)

Show step-by-step solution

moles of Zn $= 30 / 65.38 = 0.459 \approx 0.46$ mol; time $= 2 \times 60 = 120$ s.

rate $= 0.46 / 120 = 3.8 \times 10^{-3} \approx 0.004$ mol s−1

Example 2: Write rate expressions for (a) $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6(s) \to 2C_2H_5OH(aq) + 2CO_2(g)}$, (b) $\mathrm{3O_2(g) \to 2O_3(g)}$.

Show answer

(a) $\text{rate} = -\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{CO_2}]}{\Delta t}$

(b) $\text{rate} = -\dfrac{1}{3}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{O_2}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{O_3}]}{\Delta t}$

Example 3: For $\mathrm{H_2(g) + 2ICl(g) \to I_2(g) + 2HCl(g)}$, $[\mathrm{H_2}] = 0.674$ mol dm−3 at 1 s and 0.526 mol dm−3 at 2 s. Find the rate.

Show step-by-step solution

rate $= -\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{H_2}]}{\Delta t} = -\dfrac{0.526 - 0.674}{2 - 1} = -\dfrac{-0.148}{1} = 0.148$ mol dm−3 s−1

Example 4: In $\mathrm{2A(aq) \to 4B(aq) + C(aq)}$, $[\mathrm{A}]$ falls from 2.00 to 1.76 mol dm−3 in 120 s. Find the rate of reaction.

Show step-by-step solution

$\Delta[\mathrm{A}] = 1.76 - 2.00 = -0.24$ mol dm−3

rate $= -\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{A}]}{\Delta t} = -\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{-0.24}{120} = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ mol dm−3 s−1

Worked Examples — Review Questions (p. 50) and Exercises (p. 59)

Review Q2: (a) How long do these take: rusting of an iron door, sodium with water, making grape wine, decay of buildings by acid rain, burning magnesium? (b) Choose units (dm3 min−1, g s−1, mol dm−3 s−1) for the rate at which (i) Mg is used up, (ii) $\mathrm{CO_2}$ is given off, (iii) sulphur is formed, (iv) [HCl] changes.

Show answer

(a) Rusting: months to years. Sodium + water: seconds. Wine: days to weeks. Acid-rain decay: years. Burning Mg: seconds.

(b) (i) g s−1, (ii) dm3 min−1, (iii) g s−1, (iv) mol dm−3 s−1.

Review Q3: Rate expressions for (a) $\mathrm{Zn + 2HCl \to ZnCl_2 + H_2}$, (b) $\mathrm{2N_2O_5(g) \to 4NO_2(g) + O_2(g)}$.

Show answer

(a) $\text{rate} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{HCl}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{ZnCl_2}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{H_2}]}{\Delta t}$ (zinc is a solid: follow its mass, $-\Delta m_{\mathrm{Zn}}/\Delta t$).

(b) $\text{rate} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{N_2O_5}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{NO_2}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{O_2}]}{\Delta t}$

Exercise 5: Moles used up and times: (1) 2 mol in 30 s, (2) 5 mol in 2 min, (3) 1 mol in 1.5 min, (4) 3.2 mol in 1.5 min, (5) 5.9 mol in 30 s. Find each rate, the fastest and the slowest.

Show step-by-step solution

(1) $2/30 = 0.067$ mol s−1; (2) $5/120 = 0.042$; (3) $1/90 = 0.011$; (4) $3.2/90 = 0.036$; (5) $5.9/30 = 0.197$ mol s−1.

Fastest: reaction 5. Slowest: reaction 3.

Exercise 6: Rate expressions for (a) $\mathrm{CaCO_3(s) + 2HCl(aq) \to CaCl_2(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)}$, (b) $\mathrm{4NH_3(g) + 5O_2(g) \to 4NO(g) + 6H_2O(g)}$, (c) $\mathrm{CH_4(g) + Br_2(g) \to CH_3Br(g) + HBr(g)}$.

Show answer

(a) $-\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{HCl}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{CaCl_2}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{CO_2}]}{\Delta t}$ (CaCO3 solid and water liquid: no concentration terms).

(b) $-\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{NH_3}]}{\Delta t} = -\dfrac{1}{5}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{O_2}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{NO}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{6}\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{H_2O}]}{\Delta t}$

(c) $-\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{CH_4}]}{\Delta t} = -\dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{Br_2}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{CH_3Br}]}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta[\mathrm{HBr}]}{\Delta t}$

Exercise 7: $\mathrm{Mg + Cl_2 \to MgCl_2}$ makes 2 g of $\mathrm{MgCl_2}$ in 1 min. (i) Moles of MgCl2? (ii) The rate? (iii) If $\mathrm{2Na + Cl_2 \to 2NaCl}$ goes at the same rate, how many moles of NaCl form in 1 min, and what mass of sodium is needed? (Mg 24, Na 23, Cl 35.5)

Show step-by-step solution

(i) $M(\mathrm{MgCl_2}) = 24 + 2(35.5) = 95$ g mol−1; $n = 2/95 = 0.0211$ mol.

(ii) rate $= 0.0211$ mol min−1 $= 3.5 \times 10^{-4}$ mol s−1.

(iii) The same rate of reaction means 0.0211 mol of $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ reacts per minute. The equation gives 2 NaCl per $\mathrm{Cl_2}$: $n(\mathrm{NaCl}) = 2 \times 0.0211 = 0.0421$ mol, and $n(\mathrm{Na}) = 0.0421$ mol.

mass of Na $= 0.0421 \times 23 = 0.97$ g. (If "same rate" is read as the same moles of product per minute, it would be 0.0211 mol NaCl and 0.48 g Na.)

Exercise 12: 5 g of $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$ powder with 20 cm3 of 0.1 mol dm−3 HCl; the gas is collected for the first minute. (a) Rate expressions. (b) What happens with (i) a lump of the same mass, (ii) 40 cm3 of the same acid?

Show answer

(a) rate $= -\dfrac{\Delta m(\mathrm{CaCO_3})}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta V(\mathrm{CO_2})}{\Delta t}$.

(b)(i) Slower: a lump has a smaller surface area, so fewer collisions per second. (ii) The concentration is the same, so the rate in the first minute is about the same. CaCO3 is in excess (0.05 mol, while the HCl is only 0.002 mol), so twice the acid gives about twice the final volume of gas.

Key Takeaways
  • Rate = change in amount of reactant or product ÷ time; it is always positive.
  • $\text{rate} = -\tfrac1a \Delta[\mathrm{A}]/\Delta t = \tfrac1c \Delta[\mathrm{C}]/\Delta t$ for $a\mathrm{A} \to c\mathrm{C}$.
  • On a volume–time or concentration–time graph, the slope is the rate; reactions are fastest at the start.
  • Units depend on what is measured: g s−1, cm3 min−1, mol dm−3 s−1.

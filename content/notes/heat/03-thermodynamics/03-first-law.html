The First Law of Thermodynamics

Heat put in = rise in internal energy + work the gas does

Give a gas some heat $Q$. Where does that energy go? Some of it raises the internal energy (the gas gets hotter), and the rest leaves as work, when the gas pushes a piston out. Energy is never lost, so:

$Q = \Delta U + W$: "money in = savings + spending".

  • $Q > 0$: heat goes into the gas. $Q < 0$: the gas gives heat out.
  • $\Delta U > 0$: the temperature rises. $\Delta U < 0$: it falls.
  • $W > 0$: the gas expands and does work. $W < 0$: it is compressed, so work is done on it.

Many textbooks write the same law as $\Delta U = Q + W_{\text{on}}$, where $W_{\text{on}}$ is the work done on the gas. Since $W_{\text{on}} = -W$, it is the same equation. In this course $W$ always means work done by the gas.

The first law (energy conservation for a gas)

$$Q = \Delta U + W$$

$Q$: heat into the gas. $\Delta U$: change in internal energy. $W$: work done by the gas. All in J, each with its sign.

  1. The gas's energy is its internal energy $U$. Only two things can change it: heat flowing in or out, and work done.
  2. Heat $Q$ in adds $Q$. Work $W$ done by the gas on the outside takes $W$ away.
  3. So $\Delta U = Q - W$, which rearranges to $Q = \Delta U + W$.
  4. To use it: find any two of $Q$, $\Delta U$, $W$ with the right signs; the third follows.

Where does the energy go? (bars: $Q$, $\Delta U$, $W$)

Example 1

A gas takes in 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work. Find the change in its internal energy.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta U = Q - W = 500 - 200$.

$\Delta U = +300\ \mathrm{J}$: the gas gets hotter

Example 2

A gas's internal energy falls by 400 J while it gives out 200 J of heat. Did its volume increase or decrease? How much work did it do?

Show solution
  1. Signs: $\Delta U = -400$ J, $Q = -200$ J (heat out).
  2. $W = Q - \Delta U = -200 - (-400) = +200$ J.
  3. $W > 0$ means the gas did work on the outside: it expanded.

The volume increased; the gas did 200 J of work

Example 3

200 J of work is done on a gas and its internal energy rises by 100 J. Was heat taken in or given out, and how much?

Show solution
  1. Work done on the gas is 200 J, so the work done by it is $W = -200$ J.
  2. $Q = \Delta U + W = 100 + (-200) = -100$ J.

100 J of heat was given out

Example 4

A gas in a rigid container is given 300 J of heat. Find $W$ and $\Delta U$.

Show solution
  1. Rigid: no volume change, so $W = 0$.
  2. $\Delta U = Q - W = 300$ J: all the heat goes into internal energy.

$W = 0$, $\Delta U = 300\ \mathrm{J}$

Example 5

A gas expands at constant temperature and does 250 J of work. How much heat did it take in?

Show solution
  1. Constant temperature means $\Delta U = 0$ for an ideal gas.
  2. $Q = 0 + 250$.

$Q = 250\ \mathrm{J}$: all the heat taken in comes out as work

Most mistakes are sign mistakes. "Work done on the gas" means $W$ is negative here; "heat released" means $Q$ is negative. Write each sign down before using the formula.
Read the situation for free information: rigid container → $W = 0$; constant temperature → $\Delta U = 0$; insulated (no heat in or out) → $Q = 0$.
  • $Q = \Delta U + W$, with $W$ the work done by the gas.
  • Signs: heat in $+$, temperature up $+$, expansion $+$.
  • Work done on the gas $= -W$.

Connecting and Mixing Gases

Two containers joined by a tube: count moles, balance pressures, keep the total energy

Many problems join two containers with a thin tube and a valve. Three facts solve almost all of them:

  • Moles are conserved. Gas moves between the containers but none is made or lost: $n_A + n_B$ stays the same.
  • Open valve → equal pressure. Gas flows until the pressure is the same everywhere (the temperatures can still differ if each container is held at its own temperature).
  • Insulated and rigid → total internal energy is conserved. No heat comes in ($Q = 0$) and the walls don't move, so no work is done on the outside ($W = 0$). So $U_A + U_B$ before = $U$ after.

A special case: gas rushing into an empty (vacuum) container. It pushes against nothing, so it does no work; no heat flows; so $\Delta U = 0$ and the temperature doesn't change. This is called free expansion.

Monatomic gases mixing in insulated, rigid containers

$$T = \frac{n_AT_A + n_BT_B}{n_A + n_B} \qquad P = \frac{P_AV_A + P_BV_B}{V_A + V_B}$$

Both come from $\frac32n_ART_A + \frac32n_BRT_B = \frac32(n_A + n_B)RT$, which is the same as $\frac32P_AV_A + \frac32P_BV_B = \frac32P(V_A + V_B)$.

  1. Insulated: $Q = 0$. Rigid: $W = 0$. First law: $\Delta U = 0$ for the whole gas.
  2. Before: $U = \tfrac32n_ART_A + \tfrac32n_BRT_B$. After: $U = \tfrac32(n_A + n_B)RT$.
  3. Set them equal and cancel $\tfrac32R$: $T = \dfrac{n_AT_A + n_BT_B}{n_A + n_B}$, a mole-weighted average.
  4. Using $U = \tfrac32PV$ instead: $P_AV_A + P_BV_B = P(V_A + V_B)$, which gives the final pressure without finding $n$ or $T$.

Example 1

Container A (volume $V$) is held at 500 K and container B (volume $2V$) at 600 K. They are joined by an open tube and hold 1.0 mol of gas in total. How many moles are in each?

Show solution
  1. Open tube, so the same pressure $P$: $n_A = \dfrac{PV}{500R}$, $n_B = \dfrac{P \cdot 2V}{600R} = \dfrac{PV}{300R}$.
  2. $\dfrac{n_B}{n_A} = \dfrac{500}{300} = \dfrac53$.
  3. $n_A + n_B = 1.0$, so $n_A = \tfrac38$ and $n_B = \tfrac58$.

$n_A = 0.375$ mol, $n_B = 0.625$ mol

Example 2

Insulated container A holds gas at 300 K and $3.0 \times 10^5$ Pa. Container B, the same size, is a vacuum. The valve is opened. Find the final temperature and pressure.

Show solution
  1. Free expansion: $Q = 0$, $W = 0$, so $\Delta U = 0$ and $T$ stays at 300 K.
  2. Same $n$ and $T$ in twice the volume: $P$ halves.

$300\ \mathrm{K}$ and $1.5 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$

Example 3

Insulated rigid containers: A has 1.0 mol of a monatomic gas at 300 K; B has 2.0 mol at 450 K. The valve is opened. Find the final temperature.

Show solution
  1. $T = \dfrac{1.0 \times 300 + 2.0 \times 450}{1.0 + 2.0} = \dfrac{1200}{3.0}$.

$T = 400\ \mathrm{K}$

Example 4

In Example 3, A has volume $V$ at $3.0 \times 10^5$ Pa and B has volume $2V$ at $4.5 \times 10^5$ Pa. Find the final pressure without using moles.

Show solution
  1. $P = \dfrac{P_AV_A + P_BV_B}{V_A + V_B} = \dfrac{3.0 \times V + 4.5 \times 2V}{3V} \times 10^5$.

$P = 4.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$

Example 5

Two equal insulated containers each hold $n$ mol of a monatomic gas at $T_0$, with the valve closed. The gas in A alone is heated to $3T_0$. Then the valve is opened. Find the final temperature.

Show solution
  1. Each container still holds $n$ mol (the valve was closed while heating).
  2. $T = \dfrac{n \cdot 3T_0 + n \cdot T_0}{2n}$.

$T = 2T_0$

The final temperature is the average weighted by moles, not the plain average of the two temperatures, and not weighted by volume. In Example 3, $(300 + 450)/2 = 375$ K is wrong.
Ask three questions: Is the valve open (equal pressure)? Is anything heated or held at a temperature (then energy isn't conserved)? Is it insulated and rigid (then $U$ total is conserved)? Moles are conserved in every case.
  • Joined containers: total moles conserved; an open valve means equal pressure.
  • Insulated and rigid: total $U$ conserved, so $T$ is the mole-weighted average.
  • Free expansion into a vacuum: no work, no heat, no temperature change.

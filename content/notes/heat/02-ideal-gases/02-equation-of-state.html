The Ideal Gas Equation

$PV/T$ is fixed, and its value is just "number of moles × $R$"

The combined law says $PV/T$ stays the same for a given amount of gas. But what is that constant? Twice as much gas at the same $P$ and $T$ takes up twice the volume, so the constant must be proportional to the number of moles $n$: $PV/T = nR$.

  • To find $R$, use one known state. At standard conditions (0 °C $= 273$ K, $1.013 \times 10^5$ Pa), 1 mol of any gas fills $22.4$ L.
  • That gives $R = 8.31$ J/(mol·K), the gas constant. It is the same for every gas.
  • A gas that follows $PV = nRT$ exactly is an ideal gas. Real gases are close to ideal unless they are very cold or very squeezed.

To find the pressure of a gas under a piston, look at the forces on the piston: the gas pushes up with $PS$, the air outside pushes down with $P_0S$, and the piston's weight is $Mg$.

The ideal gas equation (equation of state)

$$PV = nRT \qquad R = 8.31\ \mathrm{J/(mol\cdot K)}$$

$P$: pressure (Pa). $V$: volume (m³; 1 L $= 10^{-3}$ m³). $n$: amount (mol). $T$: temperature (K).

  1. $R = \dfrac{PV}{nT} = \dfrac{1.013 \times 10^5 \times 22.4 \times 10^{-3}}{1 \times 273} \approx 8.31$.
  2. Unit: $\mathrm{Pa \cdot m^3} = \dfrac{\mathrm{N}}{\mathrm{m^2}} \cdot \mathrm{m^3} = \mathrm{N \cdot m} = \mathrm{J}$. So $PV$ is an energy, and $R$ is in J/(mol·K).
  3. Gas under a piston (area $S$, mass $M$, outside pressure $P_0$). The piston is at rest, so the forces balance: $PS = P_0S + Mg$, giving $P = P_0 + \dfrac{Mg}{S}$.
  4. With gas column length $L$, $V = SL$, so $PV = nRT$ gives $T = \dfrac{(P_0S + Mg)L}{nR}$.

Gas under a piston ($S = 1.0 \times 10^{-2}$ m², $P_0 = 1.0 \times 10^5$ Pa)

Example 1

How many moles of gas are in $2.0 \times 10^{-2}$ m³ at $1.0 \times 10^5$ Pa and 300 K?

Show solution
  1. $n = \dfrac{PV}{RT} = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^5 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-2}}{8.31 \times 300} = \dfrac{2000}{2493}$.

$n \approx 0.80\ \mathrm{mol}$

Example 2

0.50 mol of gas is kept in a 10 L container at 27 °C. Find its pressure.

Show solution
  1. Units: $V = 10 \times 10^{-3} = 1.0 \times 10^{-2}$ m³, $T = 300$ K.
  2. $P = \dfrac{nRT}{V} = \dfrac{0.50 \times 8.31 \times 300}{1.0 \times 10^{-2}}$.

$P \approx 1.2 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$

Example 3

A cylinder stands upright with a 10 kg piston of area $1.0 \times 10^{-2}$ m². Outside pressure is $1.0 \times 10^5$ Pa. Find the gas pressure. ($g = 9.8$ m/s²)

Show solution
  1. Forces on the piston: $PS = P_0S + Mg$.
  2. $P = 1.0 \times 10^5 + \dfrac{10 \times 9.8}{1.0 \times 10^{-2}} = 1.0 \times 10^5 + 9800$.

$P \approx 1.1 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$

Example 4

The cylinder in Example 3 holds 0.10 mol of gas at 300 K. How long is the gas column?

Show solution
  1. $V = \dfrac{nRT}{P} = \dfrac{0.10 \times 8.31 \times 300}{1.098 \times 10^5} \approx 2.27 \times 10^{-3}$ m³.
  2. $L = V/S = \dfrac{2.27 \times 10^{-3}}{1.0 \times 10^{-2}}$.

$L \approx 0.23\ \mathrm{m}$

Example 5

The gas in Example 4 is heated slowly. The piston is free to rise. At what temperature is the column 0.30 m long?

Show solution
  1. The forces on the piston don't change, so $P$ stays the same, and $V \propto T$.
  2. $T = 300 \times \dfrac{0.30}{0.227}$ (or use $T = \dfrac{(P_0S + Mg)L}{nR} = \dfrac{1098 \times 0.30}{0.831}$).

$T \approx 4.0 \times 10^2\ \mathrm{K}$

With $R = 8.31$, every quantity must be in SI units: pressure in Pa, volume in m³ (not L or cm³), temperature in K. 1 L $= 10^{-3}$ m³ and 1 cm³ $= 10^{-6}$ m³.
A piston that can move freely means constant pressure (its force balance doesn't change). A piston held by stops, or a rigid container, means constant volume.
  • $PV = nRT$ with $R = 8.31$ J/(mol·K), from 1 mol filling 22.4 L at 273 K and $1.013 \times 10^5$ Pa.
  • $\mathrm{Pa \cdot m^3} = \mathrm{J}$: $PV$ is an energy.
  • Gas under a piston: balance the piston, $P = P_0 + Mg/S$.

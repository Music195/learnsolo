Kinetic Theory of Gases

Pressure is countless molecules hitting the wall; temperature is how fast they move

A gas is a huge number of tiny molecules flying around and bouncing off the walls. Each bounce gives the wall a small push. Add up all the pushes every second and you get a steady force: that is the gas pressure.

  • One molecule (mass $m$) hitting a wall with velocity $v_x$ bounces back with $-v_x$. Its momentum changes by $2mv_x$, so it gives the wall an impulse of $2mv_x$.
  • In a box of length $L$ it comes back to the same wall every $2L/v_x$ seconds. Faster molecules hit harder and more often, so the force goes as $v_x^2$.
  • Comparing the result with $PV = nRT$ shows that the average kinetic energy of a molecule depends only on temperature: $\tfrac12m\overline{v^2} = \tfrac32kT$.

So "hotter" literally means "molecules moving faster". At the same temperature, light molecules move faster than heavy ones, because they have the same average kinetic energy.

$N$ molecules in a volume $V$

$$P = \frac{Nm\overline{v^2}}{3V} \qquad \frac12m\overline{v^2} = \frac32kT \qquad \sqrt{\overline{v^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$$

$\overline{v^2}$: average of $v^2$ over all molecules. $k = R/N_A = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K (Boltzmann constant; $N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}$ /mol). $M$: molar mass in kg/mol (for O₂, $M = 0.032$). $\sqrt{\overline{v^2}}$ is the root-mean-square (rms) speed.

  1. A cube of side $L$. One molecule bounces off the right wall: impulse to the wall $2mv_x$.
  2. It goes to the far wall and back, $2L$, before the next hit, so in time $t$ it hits $\dfrac{v_xt}{2L}$ times.
  3. Total impulse in time $t$: $2mv_x \times \dfrac{v_xt}{2L} = \dfrac{mv_x^2}{L}\,t$. Impulse = force × time, so the average force is $\dfrac{mv_x^2}{L}$.
  4. For $N$ molecules, use the average $\overline{v_x^2}$: $F = \dfrac{Nm\overline{v_x^2}}{L}$. No direction is special, so $\overline{v_x^2} = \overline{v_y^2} = \overline{v_z^2}$, and since $v^2 = v_x^2 + v_y^2 + v_z^2$, $\overline{v_x^2} = \tfrac13\overline{v^2}$.
  5. Pressure = force ÷ area: $P = \dfrac{F}{L^2} = \dfrac{Nm\overline{v^2}}{3L^3} = \dfrac{Nm\overline{v^2}}{3V}$.
  6. Compare with $PV = nRT$ and $N = nN_A$: $\tfrac13Nm\overline{v^2} = nRT$, so $\tfrac12m\overline{v^2} = \tfrac32\dfrac{R}{N_A}T = \tfrac32kT$.
  7. Also $N_Am = M$ (molar mass), so $\overline{v^2} = \dfrac{3RT}{N_Am} = \dfrac{3RT}{M}$.

Example 1

Find the rms speed of nitrogen molecules ($M = 0.028$ kg/mol) at 300 K.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3 \times 8.31 \times 300}{0.028}} = \sqrt{2.67 \times 10^5}$.

about $5.2 \times 10^2\ \mathrm{m/s}$ (faster than sound)

Example 2

At the same temperature, how many times faster (rms) are hydrogen molecules ($M = 2$ g/mol) than oxygen molecules ($M = 32$ g/mol)?

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{\overline{v^2}} \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt M}$ at the same $T$.
  2. Ratio $= \sqrt{\dfrac{32}{2}} = \sqrt{16}$.

4 times

Example 3

Find the average kinetic energy of one gas molecule at 300 K. ($k = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K)

Show solution
  1. $\tfrac32kT = 1.5 \times 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \times 300$.

$\approx 6.2 \times 10^{-21}\ \mathrm{J}$, the same for every kind of gas

Example 4

The temperature of a gas goes from 300 K to 1200 K. By what factor does the rms speed change?

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{\overline{v^2}} \propto \sqrt T$, and $T$ is 4 times bigger.

2 times (not 4)

Example 5

In the derivation, a molecule with $v_x = 400$ m/s and mass $5.0 \times 10^{-26}$ kg moves in a box with $L = 0.20$ m. Find the impulse per hit, the number of hits on one wall per second, and its average force on that wall.

Show solution
  1. Impulse per hit: $2mv_x = 2 \times 5.0 \times 10^{-26} \times 400 = 4.0 \times 10^{-23}$ N·s.
  2. Hits per second: $\dfrac{v_x}{2L} = \dfrac{400}{0.40} = 1000$.
  3. Force: $4.0 \times 10^{-23} \times 1000 = \dfrac{mv_x^2}{L}$.

$4.0 \times 10^{-23}$ N·s, 1000 hits/s, $4.0 \times 10^{-20}$ N

In $\sqrt{3RT/M}$ the molar mass must be in kg/mol: oxygen is $0.032$, not $32$. Using 32 makes the speed $\sqrt{1000} \approx 32$ times too small.
The $\frac13$ comes only from "the three directions are equal". A wall only feels the motion that's at right angles to it, which on average is one third of $\overline{v^2}$.
  • Pressure comes from molecules hitting the walls: $P = \dfrac{Nm\overline{v^2}}{3V}$.
  • Average kinetic energy per molecule: $\tfrac32kT$, depending only on $T$.
  • rms speed $\sqrt{3RT/M}$: doubles when $T$ is ×4; lighter gases are faster.

Problem 1 I Q1 · Location of roots

Consider the quadratic equation $x^2 - 2ax + a + 6 = 0$, where $a$ is a constant. Let $f(x) = x^2 - 2ax + a + 6$.

  1. (1) Find the range of $a$ for which the equation has two different real solutions.
  2. (2) Find the range of $a$ for which both solutions are different and greater than $1$.
  3. (3) Find how many integers $a$ satisfy the condition in (2). (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions, Quadratic Graph and Intersection with x-axis

Show hint

For two roots greater than $1$, check three things on the graph of $y = f(x)$: the discriminant, the axis $x = a$, and the sign of $f(1)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{D}{4} = a^2 - a - 6 = (a + 2)(a - 3) > 0$: $a < -2$ or $a > 3$.
  2. (2) The axis $x = a$ must satisfy $a > 1$, and $f(1) = 1 - 2a + a + 6 = 7 - a > 0$, so $a < 7$.
  3. Together with (1): $3 < a < 7$.
  4. (3) The integers $4, 5, 6$: $3$ of them.

(1) $a < -2$ or $a > 3$ (2) $3 < a < 7$ (3) $3$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Drawing without replacement

A bag holds 3 red balls and 2 white balls. Balls are taken out one at a time, without putting them back, until a white ball appears.

  1. (1) Find the probability that the first white ball appears on the 2nd draw.
  2. (2) Find the probability that it appears on the 3rd draw, and on the 4th draw.
  3. (3) Find the expected number of draws. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Multiplication Rule of Probability, Expectation (Expected Value)

Show hint

Multiply the probabilities draw by draw: the bag changes after each draw. The process stops by the 4th draw at the latest.

Show solution
  1. (1) Red then white: $\dfrac{3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{3}{10}$.
  2. (2) 3rd draw: $\dfrac{3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{2}{4} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{1}{5}$. 4th draw: $\dfrac{3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{2}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 1 = \dfrac{1}{10}$.
  3. The 1st draw has probability $\dfrac{2}{5}$, and the four probabilities add up to $1$.
  4. (3) $E = 1 \cdot \dfrac{2}{5} + 2 \cdot \dfrac{3}{10} + 3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{5} + 4 \cdot \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{4 + 6 + 6 + 4}{10} = 2$.

(1) $\dfrac{3}{10}$ (2) $\dfrac{1}{5}$, $\dfrac{1}{10}$ (3) $2$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Double radicals

Let $x = \sqrt{7 + 4\sqrt{3}}$ and $y = \sqrt{7 - 4\sqrt{3}}$.

  1. (1) Simplify $x$ and $y$.
  2. (2) Find $x + y$ and $xy$.
  3. (3) Find $x^2 + y^2$ and $x^3 + y^3$. (Type $x^3 + y^3$ in the box.)

Uses: Double Radicals Expansion, Expansion Formulas

Show hint

$7 + 4\sqrt{3} = 7 + 2\sqrt{12}$: look for two numbers with sum $7$ and product $12$. Then $x^3 + y^3 = (x + y)^3 - 3xy(x + y)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $7 + 2\sqrt{12} = (\sqrt{4} + \sqrt{3})^2$, so $x = 2 + \sqrt{3}$; similarly $y = 2 - \sqrt{3}$ (positive, since $2 > \sqrt{3}$).
  2. (2) $x + y = 4$, $xy = 4 - 3 = 1$.
  3. (3) $x^2 + y^2 = 16 - 2 = 14$.
  4. $x^3 + y^3 = 4^3 - 3 \cdot 1 \cdot 4 = 52$.

(1) $x = 2 + \sqrt{3}$, $y = 2 - \sqrt{3}$ (2) $4$, $1$ (3) $14$, $52$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Systems of inequalities

Let $a$ be a constant. Consider $P$: $x^2 - x - 6 < 0$ and $Q$: $(x - 1)(x - a) \le 0$.

  1. (1) Solve $P$.
  2. (2) Solve $Q$ when $a > 1$ and when $a < 1$.
  3. (3) Find the range of $a$ for which every solution of $Q$ is also a solution of $P$, and how many integers $a$ are in this range. (Type the number of integers in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Inequality Solutions, Necessary and Sufficient Conditions

Show hint

The solution set of $Q$ is the closed interval between $1$ and $a$. It must fit inside the open interval from (1).

Show solution
  1. (1) $(x + 2)(x - 3) < 0$: $-2 < x < 3$.
  2. (2) $a > 1$: $1 \le x \le a$. $a < 1$: $a \le x \le 1$. ($a = 1$: only $x = 1$.)
  3. (3) Need $a < 3$ when $a \ge 1$, and $a > -2$ when $a < 1$. So $-2 < a < 3$.
  4. Integers: $-1, 0, 1, 2$, so $4$.

(1) $-2 < x < 3$ (3) $-2 < a < 3$; $4$ integers

Problem 5 III · Euclidean algorithm

Answer the following.

  1. (1) Use the Euclidean algorithm to find $\gcd(1001, 1573)$, and then $\operatorname{lcm}(1001, 1573)$.
  2. (2) For a positive integer $n$, show that $\gcd(n + 5,\ 3n + 1) = \gcd(n + 5,\ 14)$.
  3. (3) Find how many integers $n$ with $1 \le n \le 50$ satisfy $\gcd(n + 5,\ 3n + 1) = 7$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Euclidean Algorithm, Integer Division

Show hint

$\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a - qb)$. For (2), $3n + 1 = 3(n + 5) - 14$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $1573 = 1001 + 572$, $1001 = 572 + 429$, $572 = 429 + 143$, $429 = 3 \cdot 143$. So $\gcd = 143$.
  2. $\operatorname{lcm} = \dfrac{1001 \cdot 1573}{143} = 1001 \cdot 11 = 11011$.
  3. (2) $3n + 1 = 3(n + 5) - 14$, so $\gcd(n + 5, 3n + 1) = \gcd(n + 5, 14)$.
  4. (3) The gcd is $7$ when $n + 5$ is a multiple of $7$ but not of $2$: $n + 5 = 7, 21, 35, 49$.
  5. So $n = 2, 16, 30, 44$: $4$ values.

(1) $143$, $11011$ (3) $4$

Problem 6 IV · Trigonometric ratios

An angle $\theta$ with $90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ$ satisfies $\sin\theta + \cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

  1. (1) Find $\sin\theta\cos\theta$.
  2. (2) Find $\sin\theta - \cos\theta$.
  3. (3) Find $\sin\theta$. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) Find $\tan\theta + \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}$.

Uses: Trigonometric Ratio Identities, Trigonometric Ratios of 180°−θ

Show hint

Square both sides and use $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$. For $90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ$, $\sin\theta > 0 > \cos\theta$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $1 + 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{4}$, so $\sin\theta\cos\theta = -\dfrac{3}{8}$.
  2. (2) $(\sin\theta - \cos\theta)^2 = 1 + \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{7}{4}$, and $\sin\theta - \cos\theta > 0$, so it is $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$.
  3. (3) Adding: $2\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$, so $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{7}}{4}$.
  4. (4) $\tan\theta + \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta} = \dfrac{\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} = -\dfrac{8}{3}$.

(1) $-\dfrac{3}{8}$ (2) $\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$ (3) $\dfrac{1 + \sqrt{7}}{4}$ (4) $-\dfrac{8}{3}$

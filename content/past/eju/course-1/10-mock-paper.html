Problem 1 I Q1 · Graphs with absolute value

Consider $f(x) = x^2 - 2|x| - 3$.

  1. (1) Write $f(x)$ without absolute value signs for $x \ge 0$ and $x < 0$, and find the smallest value of $f(x)$.
  2. (2) Solve the inequality $f(x) < 0$.
  3. (3) Find the value of $k$ for which the equation $f(x) = k$ has exactly three real solutions. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Absolute Value, Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q, Quadratic Inequality Solutions

Show hint

The graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis, since $f(-x) = f(x)$. An odd number of solutions needs $x = 0$ to be one of them.

Show solution
  1. (1) $x \ge 0$: $(x - 1)^2 - 4$. $x < 0$: $(x + 1)^2 - 4$. The smallest value is $-4$ (at $x = \pm 1$).
  2. (2) With $t = |x|$: $t^2 - 2t - 3 = (t - 3)(t + 1) < 0$, so $0 \le t < 3$: $-3 < x < 3$.
  3. (3) The solutions come in pairs $\pm x$, so three solutions need $x = 0$: $k = f(0) = -3$.
  4. Check: $x^2 - 2|x| = 0$ gives $x = 0, \pm 2$, three solutions.

(1) smallest value $-4$ (2) $-3 < x < 3$ (3) $k = -3$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Expected value

Five cards are numbered $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$. Two cards are taken out at the same time; let $X$ be the larger of the two numbers.

  1. (1) Find the probability that the sum of the two numbers is odd.
  2. (2) Find the probability distribution of $X$.
  3. (3) Find the expected value of $X$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Counting Combinations, Expectation (Expected Value)

Show hint

There are $_5\mathrm{C}_2 = 10$ pairs. $X = k$ means one card is $k$ and the other is smaller.

Show solution
  1. (1) Odd sum: one odd and one even: $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ pairs, so $\dfrac{6}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.
  2. (2) $X = k$ for $k - 1$ pairs: $P(X = 2, 3, 4, 5) = \dfrac{1}{10}, \dfrac{2}{10}, \dfrac{3}{10}, \dfrac{4}{10}$.
  3. (3) $E[X] = \dfrac{2 + 6 + 12 + 20}{10} = 4$.

(1) $\dfrac{3}{5}$ (3) $4$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Counting with sets

Among the integers from $1$ to $100$, let $A$, $B$ and $C$ be the sets of multiples of $2$, $3$ and $5$.

  1. (1) Find $n(A)$, $n(B)$, $n(C)$, $n(A \cap B)$, $n(B \cap C)$, $n(C \cap A)$ and $n(A \cap B \cap C)$.
  2. (2) Find $n(A \cup B \cup C)$.
  3. (3) Find how many of the integers are divisible by none of $2$, $3$, $5$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Counting Elements in Unions and Complements, De Morgan's Laws

Show hint

$n(A \cup B \cup C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) - n(A \cap B) - n(B \cap C) - n(C \cap A) + n(A \cap B \cap C)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $50$, $33$, $20$; multiples of $6$: $16$, of $15$: $6$, of $10$: $10$; of $30$: $3$.
  2. (2) $50 + 33 + 20 - 16 - 6 - 10 + 3 = 74$.
  3. (3) $100 - 74 = 26$.

(2) $74$ (3) $26$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Signs of roots

Consider the quadratic equation $x^2 + (k - 3)x + k = 0$, where $k$ is a constant.

  1. (1) Find the range of $k$ for which the equation has real solutions.
  2. (2) Find the range of $k$ for which it has one positive and one negative solution.
  3. (3) Find the range of $k$ for which both solutions are negative (equal solutions allowed), and its smallest value. (Type the smallest $k$ in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions, Quadratic Graph and Intersection with x-axis

Show hint

With $f(x) = x^2 + (k - 3)x + k$: one positive and one negative root $\iff f(0) < 0$. Both negative: discriminant, axis left of $0$, and $f(0) > 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $D = (k - 3)^2 - 4k = k^2 - 10k + 9 = (k - 1)(k - 9) \ge 0$: $k \le 1$ or $k \ge 9$.
  2. (2) $f(0) = k < 0$.
  3. (3) Need $D \ge 0$, axis $x = \dfrac{3 - k}{2} < 0$ (so $k > 3$), and $f(0) = k > 0$.
  4. Together: $k \ge 9$. The smallest is $k = 9$ (double root $x = -3$).

(1) $k \le 1$ or $k \ge 9$ (2) $k < 0$ (3) $k \ge 9$; smallest $9$

Problem 5 III · Equations in integers

Find the pairs of positive integers $(x, y)$ with $xy - 2x - 3y = 0$.

  1. (1) Rewrite the equation in the form $(x - p)(y - q) = r$.
  2. (2) Find how many pairs of positive integers satisfy the equation. (Type this one in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the largest value of $x + y$ among them.

Uses: Number of Divisors, Factorization Formula

Show hint

$xy - 2x - 3y + 6 = (x - 3)(y - 2)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $(x - 3)(y - 2) = 6$.
  2. (2) $x - 3 \ge -2$ and $y - 2 \ge -1$. Negative factor pairs of $6$ would need $x - 3 \le -3$ or $y - 2 \le -2$, so both factors are positive.
  3. $(x - 3, y - 2) = (1, 6), (2, 3), (3, 2), (6, 1)$: $(4, 8), (5, 5), (6, 4), (9, 3)$. So $4$ pairs.
  4. (3) $x + y = 12, 10, 10, 12$: the largest is $12$.

(1) $(x - 3)(y - 2) = 6$ (2) $4$ (3) $12$

Problem 6 IV · Ratios in a triangle

In triangle $ABC$, $D$ is the point on $BC$ with $BD : DC = 2 : 1$, and $E$ is the point on $CA$ with $CE : EA = 1 : 2$. Segments $AD$ and $BE$ meet at $P$.

  1. (1) Find $AP : PD$.
  2. (2) Find $BP : PE$.
  3. (3) Find $\dfrac{\text{area of } \triangle ABP}{\text{area of } \triangle ABC}$. (Type it as a fraction in the box.)

Uses: Menelaus' Theorem (メネラウスの定理), Ceva's Theorem (チェバの定理)

Show hint

Use Menelaus' theorem for triangle $ADC$ and the line $BE$ (it meets $DC$ extended at $B$).

Show solution
  1. (1) Triangle $ADC$, line $B$–$P$–$E$: $\dfrac{AP}{PD} \cdot \dfrac{DB}{BC} \cdot \dfrac{CE}{EA} = 1$, so $\dfrac{AP}{PD} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$ and $AP : PD = 3 : 1$.
  2. (2) Triangle $BCE$, line $A$–$P$–$D$: $\dfrac{BP}{PE} \cdot \dfrac{EA}{AC} \cdot \dfrac{CD}{DB} = 1$, so $\dfrac{BP}{PE} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$ and $BP : PE = 3 : 1$.
  3. (3) $\triangle ABD = \dfrac{2}{3}\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ABP = \dfrac{3}{4}\triangle ABD$.
  4. So $\dfrac{\triangle ABP}{\triangle ABC} = \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

(1) $3 : 1$ (2) $3 : 1$ (3) $\dfrac{1}{2}$

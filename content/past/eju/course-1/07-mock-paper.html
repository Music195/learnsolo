Problem 1 I Q1 · Maximum and minimum

Let $a \ne 0$ and $b$ be constants. On $0 \le x \le 3$, the function $f(x) = ax^2 - 4ax + b$ has maximum value $7$ and minimum value $-1$.

  1. (1) Write $f(x)$ in the form $a(x - p)^2 + q$.
  2. (2) Find $a$ and $b$ when $a > 0$. (Type $b$ in the box.)
  3. (3) Find $a$ and $b$ when $a < 0$.

Uses: Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q, Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q

Show hint

The axis is $x = 2$, inside the interval. Of the two ends, $x = 0$ is further from the axis than $x = 3$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f(x) = a(x - 2)^2 + b - 4a$.
  2. (2) $a > 0$: minimum at $x = 2$: $b - 4a = -1$; maximum at $x = 0$: $b = 7$. So $a = 2$, $b = 7$.
  3. (3) $a < 0$: maximum at $x = 2$: $b - 4a = 7$; minimum at $x = 0$: $b = -1$. So $a = -2$, $b = -1$.

(1) $a(x - 2)^2 + b - 4a$ (2) $a = 2$, $b = 7$ (3) $a = -2$, $b = -1$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Circular permutations

Four boys and three girls sit at random around a round table with 7 seats. Arrangements that differ only by a rotation are counted as the same.

  1. (1) How many arrangements are there?
  2. (2) How many arrangements have two particular girls sitting next to each other?
  3. (3) Find the probability that no two girls sit next to each other. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Counting Circular Permutations, Counting Permutations, Probability of Event A

Show hint

For (3), seat the boys first; the girls then go into the gaps between boys, at most one per gap.

Show solution
  1. (1) $(7 - 1)! = 720$.
  2. (2) Treat the two girls as one block: $(6 - 1)! = 120$ circular arrangements, times $2$ for the order in the block: $240$.
  3. (3) Boys around the table: $(4 - 1)! = 6$. The 4 gaps between boys take the 3 girls: $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 24$. So $144$ arrangements.
  4. Probability $\dfrac{144}{720} = \dfrac{1}{5}$.

(1) $720$ (2) $240$ (3) $\dfrac{1}{5}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Repeating decimals

Consider the repeating decimals $p = 0.\dot{1}\dot{8}$ ($= 0.181818\ldots$) and $q = 0.2\dot{3}$ ($= 0.2333\ldots$).

  1. (1) Write $p$ as a fraction in lowest terms.
  2. (2) Write $q$ as a fraction in lowest terms.
  3. (3) Write $p + q$ as a fraction in lowest terms. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) Find the 100th digit after the decimal point of $p$.

Uses: Repeating Decimals

Show hint

$100p - p$ removes the repeating part. For $q$, use $100q - 10q$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $100p - p = 18$, so $p = \dfrac{18}{99} = \dfrac{2}{11}$.
  2. (2) $100q - 10q = 23.333\ldots - 2.333\ldots = 21$, so $q = \dfrac{21}{90} = \dfrac{7}{30}$.
  3. (3) $\dfrac{2}{11} + \dfrac{7}{30} = \dfrac{60 + 77}{330} = \dfrac{137}{330}$.
  4. (4) The digits repeat $1, 8, 1, 8, \dots$; even positions are $8$, so the 100th digit is $8$.

(1) $\dfrac{2}{11}$ (2) $\dfrac{7}{30}$ (3) $\dfrac{137}{330}$ (4) $8$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Quadratics in two variables

Real numbers $x$ and $y$ satisfy $x + 2y = 6$, $x \ge 0$ and $y \ge 0$.

  1. (1) Write $x^2 + y^2$ as a function of $y$, and give the range of $y$.
  2. (2) Find the largest value of $x^2 + y^2$ and the $x$, $y$ where it is taken.
  3. (3) Find the smallest value of $x^2 + y^2$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q

Show hint

Substitute $x = 6 - 2y$. The conditions $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$ give $0 \le y \le 3$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $x^2 + y^2 = (6 - 2y)^2 + y^2 = 5y^2 - 24y + 36$, with $0 \le y \le 3$.
  2. $= 5\left(y - \dfrac{12}{5}\right)^2 + \dfrac{36}{5}$.
  3. (2) The end further from $y = \dfrac{12}{5}$ is $y = 0$: largest value $36$ at $(x, y) = (6, 0)$.
  4. (3) Smallest value $\dfrac{36}{5}$ at $y = \dfrac{12}{5}$, $x = \dfrac{6}{5}$.

(1) $5y^2 - 24y + 36$, $0 \le y \le 3$ (2) $36$ at $(6, 0)$ (3) $\dfrac{36}{5}$

Problem 5 III · Prime factors of factorials

Answer the following about factorials.

  1. (1) Find the exponent of $2$ in the prime factorisation of $20!$.
  2. (2) Find how many zeros $50!$ ends with.
  3. (3) Find the largest integer $n$ for which $100!$ is divisible by $3^n$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Divisibility Rules, Number of Divisors

Show hint

Count multiples of $p$, then of $p^2$, of $p^3$, …: each adds one more factor $p$. Trailing zeros come from pairs $2 \cdot 5$, and there are fewer $5$s.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\left\lfloor\dfrac{20}{2}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\dfrac{20}{4}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\dfrac{20}{8}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\dfrac{20}{16}\right\rfloor = 10 + 5 + 2 + 1 = 18$.
  2. (2) Factors $5$ in $50!$: $10 + 2 = 12$, and there are more $2$s, so $12$ zeros.
  3. (3) $33 + 11 + 3 + 1 = 48$.

(1) $18$ (2) $12$ (3) $48$

Problem 6 IV · Incentre and angle bisector

In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 6$, $AC = 4$ and $BC = 5$. The bisector of $\angle A$ meets $BC$ at $D$, and $I$ is the incentre.

  1. (1) Find $BD$ and $DC$.
  2. (2) Find $\cos A$ and the length $AD$.
  3. (3) Find $AI : ID$ and the length $AI$. (Type $AI$ in the box.)

Uses: Angle Bisector Theorem in a Triangle, Incenter & Angle Bisectors (内心・内接円), Law of Cosines

Show hint

$BD : DC = AB : AC$. The incentre is on $AD$, and $BI$ bisects $\angle B$ in triangle $ABD$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $BD : DC = 6 : 4$, so $BD = 3$, $DC = 2$.
  2. (2) $\cos A = \dfrac{36 + 16 - 25}{2 \cdot 6 \cdot 4} = \dfrac{9}{16}$. In triangle $ABD$: $\cos B = \dfrac{36 + 25 - 16}{60} = \dfrac{3}{4}$, so $AD^2 = 36 + 9 - 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 3 \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} = 18$, $AD = 3\sqrt{2}$.
  3. (3) In triangle $ABD$, $BI$ bisects $\angle B$: $AI : ID = BA : BD = 6 : 3 = 2 : 1$.
  4. $AI = \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 3\sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2}$.

(1) $3$, $2$ (2) $\dfrac{9}{16}$, $3\sqrt{2}$ (3) $2 : 1$, $2\sqrt{2}$

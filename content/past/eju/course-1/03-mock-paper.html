Problem 1 I Q1 · Maximum and minimum

Let $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$, and let $m(a)$ be the minimum value of $f(x)$ on $a \le x \le a + 2$.

  1. (1) Find the vertex of $y = f(x)$ and the value $m(0)$.
  2. (2) Express $m(a)$ in terms of $a$ in each of the cases $a \le 1$, $1 < a < 3$ and $a \ge 3$.
  3. (3) Find all $a$ with $m(a) = 0$. (Type the larger one in the box.)

Uses: Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q, Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q

Show hint

$f(x) = (x - 3)^2 - 4$. Where is the axis $x = 3$ compared with the interval $a \le x \le a + 2$?

Show solution
  1. (1) $f(x) = (x - 3)^2 - 4$: vertex $(3, -4)$. On $0 \le x \le 2$, $f$ decreases, so $m(0) = f(2) = -3$.
  2. (2) $a + 2 \le 3$ ($a \le 1$): $m(a) = f(a + 2) = (a - 1)^2 - 4$.
  3. $1 < a < 3$: the axis is inside the interval, so $m(a) = -4$. $a \ge 3$: $m(a) = f(a) = (a - 3)^2 - 4$.
  4. (3) $a \le 1$: $(a - 1)^2 = 4$ gives $a = -1$ (or $3$, not in range). $a \ge 3$: $(a - 3)^2 = 4$ gives $a = 5$.
  5. So $a = -1$ or $a = 5$.

(1) vertex $(3, -4)$, $m(0) = -3$ (3) $a = -1, 5$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Combinations

Nine cards are numbered $1, 2, \dots, 9$. Three cards are taken out at the same time.

  1. (1) How many different sets of three cards are there?
  2. (2) Find the probability that the sum of the three numbers is even.
  3. (3) Find the probability that the largest of the three numbers is $7$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Counting Combinations, Probability of Event A, Addition Rule for Counting

Show hint

There are 5 odd and 4 even cards; a sum of three numbers is even when it uses 0 or 2 odd numbers. For (3), $7$ must be taken and the other two come from $1$–$6$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $_9\mathrm{C}_3 = 84$.
  2. (2) No odd cards: $_4\mathrm{C}_3 = 4$. Two odd and one even: $_5\mathrm{C}_2 \cdot {}_4\mathrm{C}_1 = 40$.
  3. So the probability is $\dfrac{44}{84} = \dfrac{11}{21}$.
  4. (3) Take $7$ and two of $1, \dots, 6$: $_6\mathrm{C}_2 = 15$ ways, so $\dfrac{15}{84} = \dfrac{5}{28}$.

(1) $84$ (2) $\dfrac{11}{21}$ (3) $\dfrac{5}{28}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Sets

Let $U = \{1, 2, \dots, 20\}$ be the universal set, $A$ the set of multiples of $2$ in $U$ and $B$ the set of multiples of $3$ in $U$.

  1. (1) Find $n(A \cap B)$ and $n(A \cup B)$.
  2. (2) Find $n(\overline{A} \cap B)$.
  3. (3) Find $n(\overline{A} \cap \overline{B})$. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) For $x \in U$, is “$x$ is a multiple of $6$” a necessary condition, a sufficient condition, or both, for “$x \in A$”?

Uses: Intersection and Union, De Morgan's Laws, Necessary and Sufficient Conditions

Show hint

$A \cap B$ is the set of multiples of $6$. By De Morgan's law, $\overline{A} \cap \overline{B} = \overline{A \cup B}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $n(A) = 10$, $n(B) = 6$, $A \cap B = \{6, 12, 18\}$, so $n(A \cap B) = 3$ and $n(A \cup B) = 10 + 6 - 3 = 13$.
  2. (2) $\overline{A} \cap B$ is the odd multiples of $3$: $\{3, 9, 15\}$, so $3$.
  3. (3) $n(\overline{A} \cap \overline{B}) = n(\overline{A \cup B}) = 20 - 13 = 7$.
  4. (4) Every multiple of $6$ is even, but $2$ is even and not a multiple of $6$: it is a sufficient condition but not a necessary one.

(1) $3$, $13$ (2) $3$ (3) $7$ (4) sufficient, not necessary

Problem 4 II Q2 · Discriminant

Consider $f(x) = x^2 + ax + 4$ and $g(x) = -x^2 + 2x$, where $a$ is a constant.

  1. (1) Find the range of $a$ for which the graphs of $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ have at least one common point.
  2. (2) Find the positive value of $a$ for which the two graphs touch at exactly one point.
  3. (3) For the $a$ in (2), find the $x$-coordinate of the point where they touch. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions, Quadratic Formula

Show hint

Common points come from $f(x) = g(x)$, that is $2x^2 + (a - 2)x + 4 = 0$. Use its discriminant.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f(x) - g(x) = 2x^2 + (a - 2)x + 4$. $D = (a - 2)^2 - 32 \ge 0$.
  2. So $a - 2 \le -4\sqrt{2}$ or $a - 2 \ge 4\sqrt{2}$: $a \le 2 - 4\sqrt{2}$ or $a \ge 2 + 4\sqrt{2}$.
  3. (2) $D = 0$ with $a > 0$: $a = 2 + 4\sqrt{2}$.
  4. (3) The double root is $x = -\dfrac{a - 2}{4} = -\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{4} = -\sqrt{2}$.

(1) $a \le 2 - 4\sqrt{2}$ or $a \ge 2 + 4\sqrt{2}$ (2) $a = 2 + 4\sqrt{2}$ (3) $x = -\sqrt{2}$

Problem 5 III · Linear equations in integers

Consider equations of the form $7x + 11y = c$ in integers $x$ and $y$.

  1. (1) Find one integer solution of $7x + 11y = 1$, and then all integer solutions.
  2. (2) Find all solutions of $7x + 11y = 100$ in positive integers.
  3. (3) Find how many pairs of non-negative integers $(x, y)$ satisfy $7x + 11y = 200$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Euclidean Algorithm, Integer Division

Show hint

$7 \cdot (-3) + 11 \cdot 2 = 1$. Multiply by $c$, then add $(11k, -7k)$ to get every solution.

Show solution
  1. (1) $7 \cdot (-3) + 11 \cdot 2 = 1$. All solutions: $x = -3 + 11k$, $y = 2 - 7k$ ($k$ an integer).
  2. (2) From $(1)$ times $100$: $x = -300 + 11k$, $y = 200 - 7k$. $x > 0$ needs $k \ge 28$, $y > 0$ needs $k \le 28$.
  3. So $k = 28$: $(x, y) = (8, 4)$. Check: $56 + 44 = 100$.
  4. (3) $x = -600 + 11k \ge 0$ needs $k \ge 55$; $y = 400 - 7k \ge 0$ needs $k \le 57$.
  5. $k = 55, 56, 57$ give $(5, 15), (16, 8), (27, 1)$: $3$ pairs.

(1) $x = -3 + 11k$, $y = 2 - 7k$ (2) $(8, 4)$ (3) $3$

Problem 6 IV · Cyclic quadrilaterals

Quadrilateral $ABCD$ is inscribed in a circle, with $AB = 2$, $BC = 3$, $CD = 1$ and $\angle ABC = 60^\circ$.

  1. (1) Find $AC$.
  2. (2) Find $\angle ADC$ and the length $AD$.
  3. (3) Find the area of $ABCD$. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) Find the radius of the circle.

Uses: Law of Cosines, Properties of a Cyclic Quadrilateral, Triangle Area Formulas, Law of Sines

Show hint

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$. Use the law of cosines in triangles $ABC$ and $ACD$ for the same $AC$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $AC^2 = 4 + 9 - 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cos 60^\circ = 7$, so $AC = \sqrt{7}$.
  2. (2) $\angle ADC = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$. With $AD = x$: $7 = x^2 + 1 + x$, so $(x + 3)(x - 2) = 0$ and $AD = 2$.
  3. (3) Area $= \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \sin 60^\circ + \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \sin 120^\circ = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\sqrt{3}$.
  4. (4) $2R = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{\sin 60^\circ}$, so $R = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{21}}{3}$.

(1) $\sqrt{7}$ (2) $120^\circ$, $AD = 2$ (3) $2\sqrt{3}$ (4) $\dfrac{\sqrt{21}}{3}$

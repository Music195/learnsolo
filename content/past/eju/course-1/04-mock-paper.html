Problem 1 I Q1 · Quadratics with a parameter

Consider the parabola $y = x^2 - 2ax + a^2 + 2a - 3$, where $a$ is a constant.

  1. (1) Find the vertex, and show that it always lies on the line $y = 2x - 3$.
  2. (2) Find the range of $a$ for which the parabola meets the positive part of the $x$-axis at two different points.
  3. (3) Find $a$ when the two points where the parabola meets the $x$-axis are $1$ apart. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q, Quadratic Graph and Intersection with x-axis, Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions

Show hint

$y = (x - a)^2 + 2a - 3$. Two positive roots need: the vertex below the axis, the axis to the right of $0$, and $y > 0$ at $x = 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $y = (x - a)^2 + 2a - 3$: vertex $(a, 2a - 3)$, which satisfies $y = 2x - 3$.
  2. (2) Two roots: $2a - 3 < 0$, so $a < \dfrac{3}{2}$. Axis: $a > 0$. At $x = 0$: $a^2 + 2a - 3 = (a + 3)(a - 1) > 0$, so $a > 1$ (with $a > 0$).
  3. So $1 < a < \dfrac{3}{2}$.
  4. (3) The roots are $a \pm \sqrt{3 - 2a}$, so they are $2\sqrt{3 - 2a}$ apart.
  5. $2\sqrt{3 - 2a} = 1 \iff 3 - 2a = \dfrac{1}{4} \iff a = \dfrac{11}{8}$.

(1) $(a, 2a - 3)$ (2) $1 < a < \dfrac{3}{2}$ (3) $a = \dfrac{11}{8}$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Repeated trials

A point starts at $0$ on a number line. A coin is tossed 6 times; each head moves the point $+2$ and each tail moves it $-1$.

  1. (1) Find the position after 6 tosses when there are $h$ heads.
  2. (2) Find the probability that the point is at $3$ after 6 tosses.
  3. (3) Find the probability that the point is at $6$ or more after 6 tosses. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Probability in Repeated Trials (Binomial), Counting Combinations

Show hint

The position depends only on how many heads there are, not on their order.

Show solution
  1. (1) $2h - (6 - h) = 3h - 6$.
  2. (2) $3h - 6 = 3$ gives $h = 3$: $_6\mathrm{C}_3 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^6 = \dfrac{20}{64} = \dfrac{5}{16}$.
  3. (3) $3h - 6 \ge 6$ gives $h \ge 4$: $\dfrac{_6\mathrm{C}_4 + {}_6\mathrm{C}_5 + {}_6\mathrm{C}_6}{64} = \dfrac{15 + 6 + 1}{64} = \dfrac{11}{32}$.

(1) $3h - 6$ (2) $\dfrac{5}{16}$ (3) $\dfrac{11}{32}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Radicals

Let $x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5} - 2}$. Let $a$ be the integer part of $x$ and $b = x - a$ its fractional part.

  1. (1) Rationalise the denominator of $x$.
  2. (2) Find $a$ and $b$.
  3. (3) Find the value of $b^2 + 4b$. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) Find the value of $\dfrac{1}{b} - b$.

Uses: Calculation with Radicals, Properties of Square Roots

Show hint

$2 < \sqrt{5} < 3$. For (3), $b^2 + 4b = (b + 2)^2 - 4$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $x = \dfrac{\sqrt{5} + 2}{(\sqrt{5} - 2)(\sqrt{5} + 2)} = \sqrt{5} + 2$.
  2. (2) $4 < \sqrt{5} + 2 < 5$, so $a = 4$ and $b = \sqrt{5} - 2$.
  3. (3) $b^2 + 4b = (b + 2)^2 - 4 = 5 - 4 = 1$.
  4. (4) $\dfrac{1}{b} = \sqrt{5} + 2$, so $\dfrac{1}{b} - b = 4$.

(1) $\sqrt{5} + 2$ (2) $a = 4$, $b = \sqrt{5} - 2$ (3) $1$ (4) $4$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Conditions and inequalities

Let $a$ be a constant. For real $x$ consider the conditions $p$: $x^2 - 4x + 3 \le 0$ and $q$: $|x - a| \le 2$.

  1. (1) Write the sets of $x$ satisfying $p$ and $q$ as intervals.
  2. (2) Find the range of $a$ for which $p$ is a sufficient condition for $q$.
  3. (3) Find the range of $a$ for which some $x$ satisfies both $p$ and $q$, and give the largest such $a$. (Type the largest $a$ in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Inequality Solutions, Equations and Inequalities with Absolute Value, Necessary and Sufficient Conditions

Show hint

“$p$ is sufficient for $q$” means the set for $p$ is inside the set for $q$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $p$: $(x - 1)(x - 3) \le 0$, so $1 \le x \le 3$. $q$: $a - 2 \le x \le a + 2$.
  2. (2) Need $a - 2 \le 1$ and $a + 2 \ge 3$: $1 \le a \le 3$.
  3. (3) The intervals overlap when $a - 2 \le 3$ and $a + 2 \ge 1$: $-1 \le a \le 5$.
  4. The largest $a$ is $5$ (then both hold only at $x = 3$).

(1) $1 \le x \le 3$; $a - 2 \le x \le a + 2$ (2) $1 \le a \le 3$ (3) $-1 \le a \le 5$; largest $5$

Problem 5 III · Prime factorisation

Use the prime factorisation of $720$ to answer the following.

  1. (1) Find the prime factorisation of $720$ and its number of positive divisors.
  2. (2) Find the smallest positive integer $n$ for which $720n$ is a perfect square.
  3. (3) Find the smallest positive integer $n$ for which $720n$ is a perfect cube. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Number of Divisors, Divisibility Rules

Show hint

A square has every prime exponent even; a cube has every exponent a multiple of $3$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $720 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$: $(4 + 1)(2 + 1)(1 + 1) = 30$ divisors.
  2. (2) Only the exponent of $5$ is odd, so $n = 5$ ($3600 = 60^2$).
  3. (3) Raise the exponents to $6, 3, 3$: $n = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2 = 300$.
  4. Check: $720 \cdot 300 = 216000 = 60^3$.

(1) $2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$, $30$ divisors (2) $5$ (3) $300$

Problem 6 IV · Law of sines

In triangle $ABC$, $AC = 2\sqrt{6}$, $\angle B = 45^\circ$ and $\angle C = 60^\circ$.

  1. (1) Find the radius $R$ of the circumcircle.
  2. (2) Find $AB$.
  3. (3) Find $BC$, using $\sin 75^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$.
  4. (4) Find the area of triangle $ABC$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Law of Sines, Triangle Area Formulas

Show hint

$\dfrac{AC}{\sin B} = 2R$. The third angle is $75^\circ$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $2R = \dfrac{2\sqrt{6}}{\sin 45^\circ} = 4\sqrt{3}$, so $R = 2\sqrt{3}$.
  2. (2) $AB = 2R \sin 60^\circ = 4\sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 6$.
  3. (3) $\angle A = 75^\circ$: $BC = 4\sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} = 3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6}$.
  4. (4) Area $= \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot AC \sin 75^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot 2\sqrt{6} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{6}(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2})}{2} = 9 + 3\sqrt{3}$.

(1) $2\sqrt{3}$ (2) $6$ (3) $3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6}$ (4) $9 + 3\sqrt{3}$

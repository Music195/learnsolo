Problem 1 I Q1 · Translation and reflection

Consider the parabola $C$: $y = x^2 - 4x + 5$.

  1. (1) Find the equation of the parabola obtained by reflecting $C$ in the $y$-axis.
  2. (2) Find the equation of the parabola obtained by reflecting $C$ in the origin.
  3. (3) $C$ is moved by a translation of $p$ in the $x$-direction and $q$ in the $y$-direction, and the new parabola passes through $(0, 0)$ and $(6, 0)$. Find $p$ and $q$. (Type $q$ in the box.)

Uses: Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q, Graph Shape of y = ax²

Show hint

Reflecting in the $y$-axis replaces $x$ by $-x$; in the origin, $x$ by $-x$ and $y$ by $-y$. A translation moves the vertex and keeps the shape.

Show solution
  1. (1) $y = x^2 + 4x + 5$.
  2. (2) $-y = x^2 + 4x + 5$, so $y = -x^2 - 4x - 5$.
  3. (3) The new parabola is $y = x(x - 6) = (x - 3)^2 - 9$, vertex $(3, -9)$.
  4. $C$ has vertex $(2, 1)$, so $p = 3 - 2 = 1$ and $q = -9 - 1 = -10$.

(1) $y = x^2 + 4x + 5$ (2) $y = -x^2 - 4x - 5$ (3) $p = 1$, $q = -10$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Paths on a grid

On a grid of streets, you walk from $A(0, 0)$ to $B(5, 3)$, each step moving $1$ to the right or $1$ up.

  1. (1) How many shortest paths are there from $A$ to $B$?
  2. (2) How many of them pass through $P(2, 1)$?
  3. (3) One shortest path is chosen at random. Find the probability that it passes through $P$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Permutations with Identical Objects, Probability of Event A

Show hint

A shortest path is an arrangement of 5 rights and 3 ups. Split the path at $P$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{8!}{5!\,3!} = 56$.
  2. (2) $A$ to $P$: $\dfrac{3!}{2!\,1!} = 3$. $P$ to $B$: $\dfrac{5!}{3!\,2!} = 10$. So $30$.
  3. (3) $\dfrac{30}{56} = \dfrac{15}{28}$.

(1) $56$ (2) $30$ (3) $\dfrac{15}{28}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Absolute value inequalities

Consider inequalities involving $|2x - 3|$.

  1. (1) Solve $|2x - 3| < x + 1$.
  2. (2) How many integers satisfy the inequality in (1)?
  3. (3) Find the range of the constant $a$ for which $|2x - 3| < x + a$ has no solution, and its largest value. (Type the largest value of $a$ in the box.)

Uses: Equations and Inequalities with Absolute Value, Absolute Value

Show hint

For (3), the inequality has no solution exactly when $a$ is at most the smallest value of $|2x - 3| - x$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $x \ge \dfrac{3}{2}$: $2x - 3 < x + 1 \iff x < 4$. $x < \dfrac{3}{2}$: $3 - 2x < x + 1 \iff x > \dfrac{2}{3}$.
  2. So $\dfrac{2}{3} < x < 4$.
  3. (2) The integers $1, 2, 3$: three of them.
  4. (3) $h(x) = |2x - 3| - x$ decreases until $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and increases after, so its smallest value is $h\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right) = -\dfrac{3}{2}$.
  5. $h(x) < a$ has no solution $\iff a \le -\dfrac{3}{2}$. The largest value is $-\dfrac{3}{2}$.

(1) $\dfrac{2}{3} < x < 4$ (2) $3$ (3) $a \le -\dfrac{3}{2}$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Maximum with a parameter

Let $a$ be a constant and $f(x) = -x^2 + 2ax - a + 2$. Let $M(a)$ be the maximum value of $f(x)$ on $0 \le x \le 1$.

  1. (1) Express $M(a)$ in terms of $a$ for $a < 0$, $0 \le a \le 1$ and $a > 1$.
  2. (2) Find $M\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ and $M(2)$.
  3. (3) Find the smallest value of $M(a)$ as $a$ varies. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q, Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q

Show hint

$f(x) = -(x - a)^2 + a^2 - a + 2$: the graph opens downward with axis $x = a$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $a < 0$: $M = f(0) = -a + 2$. $0 \le a \le 1$: $M = f(a) = a^2 - a + 2$. $a > 1$: $M = f(1) = a + 1$.
  2. (2) $M\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{2} + 2 = \dfrac{7}{4}$; $M(2) = 3$.
  3. (3) For $a < 0$, $M > 2$; for $a > 1$, $M > 2$; for $0 \le a \le 1$, $M = \left(a - \dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \dfrac{7}{4} \ge \dfrac{7}{4}$.
  4. So the smallest value is $\dfrac{7}{4}$, at $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

(2) $\dfrac{7}{4}$, $3$ (3) $\dfrac{7}{4}$

Problem 5 III · Integer solutions

Consider the equation $5x + 8y = 200$.

  1. (1) Find how many pairs of non-negative integers $(x, y)$ satisfy it.
  2. (2) Find how many pairs of positive integers satisfy it.
  3. (3) Among the pairs of positive integers, find the smallest value of $x + y$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Euclidean Algorithm, Divisibility Rules

Show hint

$8y = 200 - 5x$ is a multiple of $5$, so $y$ is a multiple of $5$; also $5x$ is a multiple of $8$, so $x = 8k$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $x = 8k$ and $y = 25 - 5k$; $y \ge 0$ gives $k = 0, 1, \dots, 5$: $6$ pairs.
  2. (2) Positive: $k = 1, 2, 3, 4$: $4$ pairs.
  3. (3) $x + y = 8k + 25 - 5k = 25 + 3k$, smallest at $k = 1$: $x + y = 28$ ($x = 8$, $y = 20$).

(1) $6$ (2) $4$ (3) $28$

Problem 6 IV · Solid figures

Consider a regular tetrahedron $OABC$ with every edge of length $6$.

  1. (1) Find the area of one face.
  2. (2) Let $H$ be the centroid of face $ABC$. Find $AH$ and the height $OH$.
  3. (3) Find the volume of the tetrahedron. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) Find the radius of the sphere that touches all four faces.

Uses: Triangle Area Formulas, Centroid & Medians (重心・中線), Platonic Solids

Show hint

$H$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $O$ to face $ABC$, and $AH$ is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a median of the face. For the sphere, split the tetrahedron into four pyramids with apex at its centre.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 36 = 9\sqrt{3}$.
  2. (2) A median of the face is $3\sqrt{3}$, so $AH = 2\sqrt{3}$, and $OH = \sqrt{36 - 12} = 2\sqrt{6}$.
  3. (3) $V = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 9\sqrt{3} \cdot 2\sqrt{6} = 6\sqrt{18} = 18\sqrt{2}$.
  4. (4) $V = 4 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 9\sqrt{3} \cdot r$, so $r = \dfrac{18\sqrt{2}}{12\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$.

(1) $9\sqrt{3}$ (2) $AH = 2\sqrt{3}$, $OH = 2\sqrt{6}$ (3) $18\sqrt{2}$ (4) $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$

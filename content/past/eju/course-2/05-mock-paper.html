Problem 1 I Q1 · Maximum on a moving interval

Let $f(x) = 2x^2 - 8x + 5$, and let $M(t)$ be the maximum value of $f(x)$ on $t \le x \le t + 1$.

  1. (1) Find the vertex of $y = f(x)$.
  2. (2) Express $M(t)$ in terms of $t$ for $t \le \dfrac{3}{2}$ and for $t > \dfrac{3}{2}$.
  3. (3) Find the smallest value of $M(t)$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q, Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q

Show hint

The graph opens upward, so the maximum is at the end of the interval further from the axis $x = 2$. The midpoint of the interval is $t + \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f(x) = 2(x - 2)^2 - 3$: vertex $(2, -3)$.
  2. (2) If $t + \dfrac{1}{2} \le 2$, i.e. $t \le \dfrac{3}{2}$, the left end is further: $M(t) = f(t) = 2t^2 - 8t + 5$.
  3. If $t > \dfrac{3}{2}$: $M(t) = f(t + 1) = 2t^2 - 4t - 1$.
  4. (3) $M$ decreases until $t = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and increases after, so the smallest value is $f\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right) = \dfrac{9}{2} - 12 + 5 = -\dfrac{5}{2}$.

(1) $(2, -3)$ (3) $-\dfrac{5}{2}$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Conditional probability

Box A holds 3 red balls and 1 white ball; box B holds 2 red and 2 white. A coin is tossed: heads, a ball is drawn from A; tails, from B.

  1. (1) Find the probability that the ball is red.
  2. (2) Given that the ball is red, find the probability that it came from box A. (Type this one in the box.)
  3. (3) The ball is put back and the whole process is repeated. Find the probability that both balls are white.

Uses: Multiplication Rule of Probability, Probability of Independent Trials

Show hint

$P(\text{A and red}) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4}$. Then divide by $P(\text{red})$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{5}{8}$.
  2. (2) $\dfrac{3/8}{5/8} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.
  3. (3) One round gives white with probability $1 - \dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{3}{8}$, so $\left(\dfrac{3}{8}\right)^2 = \dfrac{9}{64}$.

(1) $\dfrac{5}{8}$ (2) $\dfrac{3}{5}$ (3) $\dfrac{9}{64}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Combining sine and cosine

Consider $f(\theta) = \sin\theta + \sqrt{3}\cos\theta$.

  1. (1) Write $f(\theta)$ in the form $r\sin(\theta + \alpha)$ with $r > 0$.
  2. (2) Find the maximum and minimum of $f(\theta)$ on $0 \le \theta \le \pi$.
  3. (3) Solve $f(\theta) = 1$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$, and give the sum of the solutions. (Type the sum in the box.)

Uses: Sum of Sine and Cosine Formula, Trigonometric Equations and Inequalities

Show hint

$r = \sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Track the range of $\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f(\theta) = 2\sin\left(\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$.
  2. (2) $\dfrac{\pi}{3} \le \theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \le \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$: maximum $2$ ($\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$), minimum $2\sin\dfrac{4\pi}{3} = -\sqrt{3}$ ($\theta = \pi$).
  3. (3) $\sin\left(\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ with $\dfrac{\pi}{3} \le \theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3} < \dfrac{7\pi}{3}$: $\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{13\pi}{6}$.
  4. $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6}$; the sum is $\dfrac{7\pi}{3}$.

(1) $2\sin\left(\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$ (2) max $2$, min $-\sqrt{3}$ (3) $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6}$; sum $\dfrac{7\pi}{3}$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Circles and lines

Consider the circle $C$: $x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 2y + 1 = 0$.

  1. (1) Find the centre and radius of $C$.
  2. (2) Find $k$ for which the line $y = kx$ touches $C$.
  3. (3) Find the length of the chord that the line $y = x$ cuts from $C$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Equation of a Circle, Distance Between a Point and a Line, Circle and Line: Distance Method

Show hint

A line touches a circle when its distance from the centre equals the radius. For a chord, half its length is $\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 4$: centre $(2, 1)$, radius $2$.
  2. (2) Distance from $(2, 1)$ to $kx - y = 0$: $\dfrac{|2k - 1|}{\sqrt{k^2 + 1}} = 2 \iff 4k^2 - 4k + 1 = 4k^2 + 4 \iff k = -\dfrac{3}{4}$. (The $y$-axis also touches $C$.)
  3. (3) Distance from $(2, 1)$ to $x - y = 0$: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.
  4. Chord $= 2\sqrt{4 - \dfrac{1}{2}} = 2\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{2}} = \sqrt{14}$.

(1) centre $(2, 1)$, radius $2$ (2) $k = -\dfrac{3}{4}$ (3) $\sqrt{14}$

Problem 5 III · Extreme values with trigonometry

Consider $f(x) = x + 2\cos x$ on $0 \le x \le \pi$.

  1. (1) Find $f'(x)$ and the values of $x$ where $f'(x) = 0$.
  2. (2) Find the local maximum and local minimum values.
  3. (3) Find the minimum value of $f(x)$ on $0 \le x \le \pi$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions, Sign of the Derivative and Monotonicity, Necessary Condition for Local Extrema

Show hint

$f'(x) = 1 - 2\sin x$. Compare the local minimum with the value at the end $x = 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f'(x) = 1 - 2\sin x = 0 \iff \sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.
  2. (2) $f' > 0$ on $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$, $< 0$ on $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$, $> 0$ on $\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \pi\right)$.
  3. Local maximum $f\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \sqrt{3}$; local minimum $f\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3}$.
  4. (3) $f(0) = 2$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3} \approx 0.89 < 2$, so the minimum is $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3}$.

(1) $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ (2) max $\dfrac{\pi}{6} + \sqrt{3}$, min $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3}$ (3) $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3}$

Problem 6 IV · Integration by parts

Consider the curve $y = x\log x$ for $x > 0$.

  1. (1) Find the $x$-coordinates where the curve meets the $x$-axis.
  2. (2) Use integration by parts to find $\displaystyle\int x\log x\,dx$.
  3. (3) Find the area enclosed by the curve, the $x$-axis and the line $x = e$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Integration by Parts, Definite Integral: Integration by Parts, Area Bounded by Curve y = f(x)

Show hint

Differentiate $\log x$ and integrate $x$. For $1 \le x \le e$, $x\log x \ge 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $x\log x = 0$ with $x > 0$: $x = 1$.
  2. (2) $\displaystyle\int x\log x\,dx = \dfrac{x^2}{2}\log x - \int \dfrac{x^2}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{x}\,dx = \dfrac{x^2}{2}\log x - \dfrac{x^2}{4} + C$.
  3. (3) $\left[\dfrac{x^2}{2}\log x - \dfrac{x^2}{4}\right]_1^e = \left(\dfrac{e^2}{2} - \dfrac{e^2}{4}\right) - \left(0 - \dfrac{1}{4}\right) = \dfrac{e^2 + 1}{4}$.

(1) $x = 1$ (2) $\dfrac{x^2}{2}\log x - \dfrac{x^2}{4} + C$ (3) $\dfrac{e^2 + 1}{4}$

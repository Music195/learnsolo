Problem 1 I Q1 · Signs of roots

Consider the quadratic equation $x^2 + 2(a - 1)x + 2a^2 - 4 = 0$, where $a$ is a constant.

  1. (1) Find the range of $a$ for which it has two different real solutions.
  2. (2) Find the range of $a$ for which both solutions are positive and different.
  3. (3) Give the left end of the range in (2). (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions, Roots and Coefficients

Show hint

Two positive roots: discriminant $> 0$, sum $> 0$ and product $> 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{D}{4} = (a - 1)^2 - (2a^2 - 4) = -a^2 - 2a + 5 > 0 \iff a^2 + 2a - 5 < 0 \iff -1 - \sqrt{6} < a < -1 + \sqrt{6}$.
  2. (2) Sum $-2(a - 1) > 0$: $a < 1$. Product $2a^2 - 4 > 0$: $a < -\sqrt{2}$ or $a > \sqrt{2}$.
  3. With (1): $-1 - \sqrt{6} < a < -\sqrt{2}$ (note $-1 - \sqrt{6} \approx -3.45 < -\sqrt{2}$).
  4. (3) The left end is $-1 - \sqrt{6}$.

(1) $-1 - \sqrt{6} < a < -1 + \sqrt{6}$ (2) $-1 - \sqrt{6} < a < -\sqrt{2}$ (3) $-1 - \sqrt{6}$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Random walk on a square

A piece starts at vertex $A$ of square $ABCD$. A die is rolled; on $1$ or $2$ the piece moves to the next vertex clockwise, otherwise to the next vertex anticlockwise. This is done 4 times.

  1. (1) Find the probability that the piece is at $C$ (opposite $A$) after 2 moves.
  2. (2) Find how many clockwise moves out of 4 bring the piece back to $A$.
  3. (3) Find the probability that the piece is at $A$ after 4 moves. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Probability in Repeated Trials (Binomial), Basic Properties of Probability

Show hint

With $k$ clockwise moves out of 4, the piece moves $k - (4 - k) = 2k - 4$ places; it is at $A$ when this is a multiple of $4$.

Show solution
  1. (1) Two moves in the same direction: $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{5}{9}$.
  2. (2) $2k - 4 \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ means $k$ is even: $k = 0, 2, 4$.
  3. (3) $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^4 + {}_4\mathrm{C}_2\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^4 = \dfrac{16 + 24 + 1}{81} = \dfrac{41}{81}$.

(1) $\dfrac{5}{9}$ (2) $0, 2, 4$ (3) $\dfrac{41}{81}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Common logarithms

Use $\log_{10} 2 = 0.3010$ and $\log_{10} 3 = 0.4771$.

  1. (1) Find $\log_2 3 \cdot \log_3 8$.
  2. (2) Find $\log_{10} 6^{20}$, and how many digits $6^{20}$ has. (Type the number of digits in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the leading (first) digit of $6^{20}$.

Uses: Logarithm Change of Base, Logarithm Properties

Show hint

A number $N$ with $n - 1 \le \log_{10} N < n$ has $n$ digits. For the leading digit, compare the decimal part of $\log_{10} N$ with $\log_{10} 3$ and $\log_{10} 4$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\log_2 3 \cdot \dfrac{\log_2 8}{\log_2 3} = 3$.
  2. (2) $\log_{10} 6^{20} = 20(0.3010 + 0.4771) = 15.562$, so $10^{15} < 6^{20} < 10^{16}$: $16$ digits.
  3. (3) The decimal part is $0.562$, and $\log_{10} 3 = 0.4771 < 0.562 < 0.6020 = \log_{10} 4$.
  4. So $6^{20} = 10^{15} \times (\text{a number between 3 and 4})$: the leading digit is $3$.

(1) $3$ (2) $15.562$, $16$ digits (3) $3$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Complex plane

The complex number $z$ satisfies $|z - 2i| = 1$.

  1. (1) Describe the set of points $z$ in the complex plane.
  2. (2) Find the largest and smallest values of $|z|$.
  3. (3) Find the largest value of $|z + 2|$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Absolute Value of Complex Number, Complex Plane

Show hint

$|z - w|$ is the distance between the points $z$ and $w$. The farthest point of a circle from a point $P$ is on the line through $P$ and the centre.

Show solution
  1. (1) The circle with centre $2i$ (the point $(0, 2)$) and radius $1$.
  2. (2) The centre is $2$ from the origin: largest $|z| = 3$, smallest $|z| = 1$.
  3. (3) $|z + 2|$ is the distance from $-2$ (the point $(-2, 0)$) to $z$. The centre is $\sqrt{4 + 4} = 2\sqrt{2}$ from $-2$.
  4. Largest value $2\sqrt{2} + 1$.

(1) circle, centre $2i$, radius $1$ (2) $3$ and $1$ (3) $2\sqrt{2} + 1$

Problem 5 III · Maximum volume

A cone is inscribed in a sphere of radius $3$: its apex and the circle of its base lie on the sphere. Let $h$ be the height of the cone and $r$ the radius of its base.

  1. (1) Express $r^2$ in terms of $h$ ($0 < h < 6$).
  2. (2) Express the volume $V$ in terms of $h$, and find $\dfrac{dV}{dh}$.
  3. (3) Find the largest possible volume. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Sign of the Derivative and Monotonicity, Necessary Condition for Local Extrema

Show hint

Put the apex at the top of the sphere. The base is at distance $h - 3$ below the centre, so $r^2 + (h - 3)^2 = 9$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $r^2 = 9 - (h - 3)^2 = 6h - h^2$.
  2. (2) $V = \dfrac{\pi}{3}r^2h = \dfrac{\pi}{3}(6h^2 - h^3)$, $\dfrac{dV}{dh} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}(12h - 3h^2) = \pi h(4 - h)$.
  3. (3) $V$ increases for $0 < h < 4$ and decreases for $4 < h < 6$, so the largest volume is at $h = 4$:
  4. $V = \dfrac{\pi}{3} \cdot 8 \cdot 4 = \dfrac{32\pi}{3}$.

(1) $r^2 = 6h - h^2$ (2) $V = \dfrac{\pi}{3}(6h^2 - h^3)$ (3) $\dfrac{32\pi}{3}$

Problem 6 IV · Integrals of 1/x

Consider the curve $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x > 0$.

  1. (1) Find the area enclosed by the curve, the $x$-axis and the lines $x = 1$, $x = e$.
  2. (2) Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating that region about the $x$-axis. (Type this one in the box.)
  3. (3) The line $x = t$ divides the region between $x = 1$ and $x = e^2$ (under the curve) into two parts of equal area. Find $t$.

Uses: Indefinite Integrals of x^α, Area Bounded by Curve y = f(x), Volume of Solid of Revolution About x-axis

Show hint

$\displaystyle\int \dfrac{dx}{x} = \log x$ and $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{dx}{x^2} = -\dfrac{1}{x}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\displaystyle\int_1^e \dfrac{dx}{x} = \log e - \log 1 = 1$.
  2. (2) $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_1^e \dfrac{dx}{x^2} = \pi\left[-\dfrac{1}{x}\right]_1^e = \pi\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{e}\right) = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{e}$.
  3. (3) The whole area is $\log e^2 = 2$, so $\log t = 1$ and $t = e$.

(1) $1$ (2) $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{e}$ (3) $t = e$

Problem 1 I Q1 · Tangent lines

Consider the parabola $C: y = x^2 - 2x$ and the lines $y = mx - 4$ through the point $(0, -4)$.

  1. (1) Find the positive $m$ for which the line touches $C$, and the point of contact.
  2. (2) Find the negative $m$ for which the line touches $C$, and the point of contact.
  3. (3) Find the area of the region bounded by $C$ and the two tangent lines. (Type the area in the box.)

Uses: Discriminant and Real Solutions, Area Between Two Curves

Show hint

The line touches $C$ when $x^2 - 2x = mx - 4$ has a double root. Split the area at $x = 0$, where the two tangents meet.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 - (2 + m)x + 4 = 0$ has a double root when $(2 + m)^2 - 16 = 0$, so $m = 2$ or $m = -6$.
  2. (1) $m = 2$: $x^2 - 4x + 4 = (x - 2)^2$, contact point $(2, 0)$.
  3. (2) $m = -6$: $x^2 + 4x + 4 = (x + 2)^2$, contact point $(-2, 8)$.
  4. (3) The tangents meet at $(0, -4)$. On $-2 \le x \le 0$: $C$ minus the line $= (x + 2)^2$; on $0 \le x \le 2$: $(x - 2)^2$.
  5. Area $= \displaystyle\int_{-2}^{0} (x + 2)^2\,dx + \int_{0}^{2} (x - 2)^2\,dx = \dfrac{8}{3} + \dfrac{8}{3} = \dfrac{16}{3}$.

(1) $m = 2$, $(2, 0)$ (2) $m = -6$, $(-2, 8)$ (3) $\dfrac{16}{3}$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Probability

A bag holds 3 red balls and 2 white balls. In one turn, 2 balls are taken out at the same time and then put back. A turn scores $2$ points if the two balls are the same colour and $-1$ point otherwise.

  1. (1) Find the probability that the two balls are the same colour.
  2. (2) Four turns are played. Find the probability that the balls are the same colour in at least 3 turns.
  3. (3) Find the probability that the total score after four turns is $2$ or more. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) Find the expected total score after four turns.

Uses: Combinations, Repeated Trials, Expected Value

Show hint

If $k$ of the four turns are the same colour, the score is $2k - (4 - k) = 3k - 4$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{{}_3C_2 + {}_2C_2}{{}_5C_2} = \dfrac{3 + 1}{10} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.
  2. (2) $k = 3$ or $4$: ${}_4C_3\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^3\dfrac{3}{5} + \left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^4 = \dfrac{96 + 16}{625} = \dfrac{112}{625}$.
  3. (3) $3k - 4 \ge 2 \iff k \ge 2$. P$(k = 0) = \dfrac{81}{625}$, P$(k = 1) = 4 \cdot \dfrac{2}{5}\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^3 = \dfrac{216}{625}$.
  4. So P$(k \ge 2) = 1 - \dfrac{81 + 216}{625} = \dfrac{328}{625}$.
  5. (4) $E[k] = 4 \cdot \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{8}{5}$, so the expected score is $3 \cdot \dfrac{8}{5} - 4 = \dfrac{4}{5}$.

(1) $\dfrac{2}{5}$ (2) $\dfrac{112}{625}$ (3) $\dfrac{328}{625}$ (4) $\dfrac{4}{5}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Space vectors

O, A, B, C are vertices of a cube with edges of length 1, with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \vec{a}$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \vec{b}$, $\overrightarrow{OC} = \vec{c}$ along three edges from O. For $0 < t < 1$, let P divide segment AB in the ratio $t : (1 - t)$, and let Q be the midpoint of BC.

  1. (1) Find $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$, and express $\overrightarrow{OP}$ and $\overrightarrow{OQ}$ in terms of $\vec{a}$, $\vec{b}$, $\vec{c}$.
  2. (2) Find $\overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \overrightarrow{OQ}$, $|\overrightarrow{OP}|^2$ and $|\overrightarrow{OQ}|$, and express $\cos\angle POQ$ in terms of $t$.
  3. (3) Find $t$ such that $\angle POQ = 60^\circ$. (Type $t$ in the box.)
  4. (4) Find the range of $\cos\angle POQ$ for $0 < t < 1$.

Uses: Dot Product, Position Vector of Division Points

Show hint

$\vec{a}$, $\vec{b}$, $\vec{c}$ are perpendicular unit vectors. For (4), check that $\cos^2\angle POQ$ increases with $t$.

Show solution
  1. (1) The edges are perpendicular: $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$. $\overrightarrow{OP} = (1 - t)\vec{a} + t\vec{b}$, $\overrightarrow{OQ} = \dfrac{1}{2}\vec{b} + \dfrac{1}{2}\vec{c}$.
  2. (2) $\overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \overrightarrow{OQ} = \dfrac{t}{2}$, $|\overrightarrow{OP}|^2 = (1 - t)^2 + t^2 = 2t^2 - 2t + 1$, $|\overrightarrow{OQ}| = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
  3. $\cos\angle POQ = \dfrac{t/2}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{2t^2 - 2t + 1}} = \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2(2t^2 - 2t + 1)}}$.
  4. (3) $\cos\angle POQ = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $4t^2 = 2(2t^2 - 2t + 1)$, so $4t = 2$ and $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  5. (4) $\cos^2\angle POQ = \dfrac{t^2}{2(2t^2 - 2t + 1)}$; its derivative has the sign of $2t(1 - t) > 0$, so it increases from $0$ ($t \to 0$) to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ ($t \to 1$): $0 < \cos\angle POQ < \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

(2) $\cos\angle POQ = \dfrac{t}{\sqrt{2(2t^2 - 2t + 1)}}$ (3) $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$ (4) $0 < \cos\angle POQ < \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Complex plane

Points $A(\alpha)$, $B(\beta)$, $C(\gamma)$ on the complex plane satisfy $\dfrac{\gamma - \alpha}{\beta - \alpha} = 1 + \sqrt{3}\,i$. (Arguments are taken in $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.)

  1. (1) Write $1 + \sqrt{3}\,i$ in polar form, and describe how C is obtained from B.
  2. (2) Find $\angle ABC$.
  3. (3) Let $w = \dfrac{\gamma - \beta}{\alpha - \beta}$. Find $|w|$ and $\arg w$. (Type $\arg w$ in the box.)
  4. (4) If $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 0$, find $|\alpha| : |\beta| : |\gamma|$.

Uses: Polar Form, Rotation About a Point, Angle Between Rays

Show hint

Place A at $0$ and B at $1$ to see the shape; the conditions only fix the triangle up to size and position. For (4), $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 0$ puts the centroid at the origin.

Show solution
  1. (1) $1 + \sqrt{3}\,i = 2\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$: C is B rotated by $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ about A, with its distance from A doubled.
  2. (2) With $\alpha = 0$, $\beta = 1$: $\gamma = 1 + \sqrt{3}\,i$. Then $\gamma - \beta = \sqrt{3}\,i$ is perpendicular to $\alpha - \beta = -1$, so $\angle ABC = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
  3. (3) $w = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}\,i}{-1} = -\sqrt{3}\,i$: $|w| = \sqrt{3}$, $\arg w = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$.
  4. (4) Shift by the centroid $g = \dfrac{2 + \sqrt{3}\,i}{3}$: $\alpha = -g$, $\beta = \dfrac{1 - \sqrt{3}\,i}{3}$, $\gamma = \dfrac{1 + 2\sqrt{3}\,i}{3}$.
  5. $|\alpha|^2 = \dfrac{7}{9}$, $|\beta|^2 = \dfrac{4}{9}$, $|\gamma|^2 = \dfrac{13}{9}$, so $|\alpha| : |\beta| : |\gamma| = \sqrt{7} : 2 : \sqrt{13}$.

(2) $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ (3) $|w| = \sqrt{3}$, $\arg w = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ (4) $\sqrt{7} : 2 : \sqrt{13}$

Problem 5 III · Exponential functions

Find the minimum value of $f(x) = 27^x + 27^{-x} - 3\,(9^x + 9^{-x}) - 6\,(3^x + 3^{-x})$ and the values of $x$ where it is taken.

  1. (1) Let $t = 3^x + 3^{-x}$. Express $9^x + 9^{-x}$ and $27^x + 27^{-x}$ in terms of $t$, and find the range of $t$.
  2. (2) Write $f(x) = g(t)$ and factorise $g'(t)$.
  3. (3) Find the minimum value of $f(x)$. (Type it in the box.)
  4. (4) Find the values of $x$ where the minimum is taken.

Uses: Exponent Laws, AM-GM Inequality, Increase, Decrease and the Derivative

Show hint

$t^2 = 9^x + 2 + 9^{-x}$ and $t^3 = 27^x + 27^{-x} + 3t$. By AM-GM, $t \ge 2$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $9^x + 9^{-x} = t^2 - 2$, $27^x + 27^{-x} = t^3 - 3t$, and $t \ge 2\sqrt{3^x \cdot 3^{-x}} = 2$ (equality at $x = 0$).
  2. (2) $g(t) = t^3 - 3t - 3(t^2 - 2) - 6t = t^3 - 3t^2 - 9t + 6$, so $g'(t) = 3(t + 1)(t - 3)$.
  3. (3) For $t \ge 2$: $g$ decreases until $t = 3$ and increases after, so the minimum is $g(3) = 27 - 27 - 27 + 6 = -21$.
  4. (4) $3^x + 3^{-x} = 3$: with $u = 3^x > 0$, $u^2 - 3u + 1 = 0$, $u = \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$ (both positive).
  5. $x = \log_3 \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$.

(2) $g'(t) = 3(t + 1)(t - 3)$ (3) $-21$ (4) $x = \log_3 \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$

Problem 6 IV · Area between curves

Let $k > 0$. On $0 \le x \le \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the curves $C_1: y = \sin x$ and $C_2: y = k\cos x$ meet at $x = \alpha$. Let $S_1$ be the area bounded by $C_1$, $C_2$ and the $y$-axis, and $S_2$ the area bounded by $C_1$, $C_2$ and the line $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

  1. (1) Express $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$ in terms of $k$.
  2. (2) Show that $S_1 = \sqrt{1 + k^2} - 1$ and $S_2 = \sqrt{1 + k^2} - k$.
  3. (3) Find the value of $k$ that makes $S_1 + S_2$ smallest, and the smallest value. (Type the smallest value in the box.)

Uses: Area Between Two Curves, Integrals of Trigonometric Functions, Chain Rule

Show hint

$\sin\alpha = k\cos\alpha$ means $\tan\alpha = k$. On $0 \le x \le \alpha$ the curve $C_2$ is above $C_1$; after $\alpha$ it is below.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\tan\alpha = k$, so $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}$, $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}$.
  2. (2) $S_1 = \displaystyle\int_0^{\alpha} (k\cos x - \sin x)\,dx = k\sin\alpha + \cos\alpha - 1 = \dfrac{k^2 + 1}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}} - 1 = \sqrt{1 + k^2} - 1$.
  3. $S_2 = \displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\pi/2} (\sin x - k\cos x)\,dx = \cos\alpha + k\sin\alpha - k = \sqrt{1 + k^2} - k$.
  4. (3) $S(k) = S_1 + S_2 = 2\sqrt{1 + k^2} - 1 - k$, $S'(k) = \dfrac{2k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}} - 1 = 0 \iff 4k^2 = 1 + k^2 \iff k = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ ($S' < 0$ before, $> 0$ after).
  5. $S\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = 2 \cdot \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} - 1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3} - 1$.

(1) $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{k}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}$, $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + k^2}}$ (3) $k = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, smallest value $\sqrt{3} - 1$

$S_2 - S_1 = 1 - k$ is not constant here, but the total area has a clean minimum: the two areas trade off as the meeting point $\alpha$ moves.

Problem 1 I Q1 · Quadratics with a parameter

Let $a$ be a constant, $f(x) = x^2 - 2ax + 2a + 3$, and let $m(a)$ be the minimum value of $f(x)$ over all real $x$.

  1. (1) Express $m(a)$ in terms of $a$.
  2. (2) Find the range of $a$ for which $f(x) > 0$ for every real $x$.
  3. (3) Find the largest value of $m(a)$ and the $a$ where it is taken. (Type the largest value in the box.)

Uses: Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q, Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q, Quadratic Inequality Solutions

Show hint

Complete the square in $x$; then $m(a)$ is itself a quadratic in $a$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f(x) = (x - a)^2 - a^2 + 2a + 3$, so $m(a) = -a^2 + 2a + 3$.
  2. (2) $f(x) > 0$ for all $x$ $\iff m(a) > 0 \iff a^2 - 2a - 3 < 0 \iff -1 < a < 3$.
  3. (3) $m(a) = -(a - 1)^2 + 4$: the largest value is $4$, at $a = 1$.

(1) $m(a) = -a^2 + 2a + 3$ (2) $-1 < a < 3$ (3) $4$ at $a = 1$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Probability with three dice

Three dice are rolled.

  1. (1) Find the probability that the three numbers are all different.
  2. (2) Find the probability that the sum is $10$.
  3. (3) Find the probability that the product of the three numbers is a multiple of $4$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Counting Permutations, Probability of the Complement Event, Probability of Independent Trials

Show hint

For (3), count the complement: the product is not a multiple of $4$ when all three numbers are odd, or exactly one is $2$ or $6$ and the other two are odd.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4}{216} = \dfrac{5}{9}$.
  2. (2) Sum $10$: $(1, 3, 6), (1, 4, 5), (2, 2, 6), (2, 3, 5), (2, 4, 4), (3, 3, 4)$ give $6 + 6 + 3 + 6 + 3 + 3 = 27$ ordered outcomes: $\dfrac{27}{216} = \dfrac{1}{8}$.
  3. (3) All odd: $3^3 = 27$. Exactly one of $2, 6$ and two odd: $3 \cdot 2 \cdot 9 = 54$. (A $4$, or two even numbers, always gives a multiple of $4$.)
  4. So $P = 1 - \dfrac{81}{216} = \dfrac{135}{216} = \dfrac{5}{8}$.

(1) $\dfrac{5}{9}$ (2) $\dfrac{1}{8}$ (3) $\dfrac{5}{8}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Recurrence relations

The sequence $\{a_n\}$ is defined by $a_1 = 5$ and $a_{n+1} = 3a_n - 4$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$).

  1. (1) Find the constant $c$ for which $a_{n+1} - c = 3(a_n - c)$.
  2. (2) Find the general term $a_n$.
  3. (3) Find $S_n = a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n$, and the value of $S_4$. (Type $S_4$ in the box.)

Uses: General Term of a Geometric Sequence, Sum of Geometric Sequence, Properties of Sigma (Σ)

Show hint

$c$ satisfies $c = 3c - 4$. Then $\{a_n - c\}$ is a geometric sequence.

Show solution
  1. (1) $c = 3c - 4$, so $c = 2$.
  2. (2) $a_n - 2 = (a_1 - 2) \cdot 3^{n - 1} = 3^n$, so $a_n = 3^n + 2$.
  3. (3) $S_n = \dfrac{3(3^n - 1)}{3 - 1} + 2n = \dfrac{3^{n + 1} - 3}{2} + 2n$.
  4. $S_4 = \dfrac{243 - 3}{2} + 8 = 128$.

(1) $c = 2$ (2) $a_n = 3^n + 2$ (3) $S_n = \dfrac{3^{n+1} - 3}{2} + 2n$, $S_4 = 128$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Space vectors

In space, let $O$ be the origin and $A(2, 0, 0)$, $B(0, 2, 0)$, $C(0, 0, 2)$. Let $H$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $O$ to the plane $ABC$.

  1. (1) Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}$ and the area of triangle $ABC$.
  2. (2) Write $\overrightarrow{OH} = s\overrightarrow{OA} + t\overrightarrow{OB} + u\overrightarrow{OC}$ with $s + t + u = 1$, and use $\overrightarrow{OH} \perp \overrightarrow{AB}$, $\overrightarrow{OH} \perp \overrightarrow{AC}$ to find $H$.
  3. (3) Find the length $OH$. (Type this one in the box.)
  4. (4) Check your answer with the volume of the tetrahedron $OABC$.

Uses: Component Representation of Vector Operations, Dot Product of Vectors, Perpendicular Condition For Vectors, Position Vectors

Show hint

$\overrightarrow{OH} = (2s, 2t, 2u)$. Each dot product gives one equation.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\overrightarrow{AB} = (-2, 2, 0)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (-2, 0, 2)$. $ABC$ is equilateral with side $2\sqrt{2}$: area $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 8 = 2\sqrt{3}$.
  2. (2) $\overrightarrow{OH} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = -4s + 4t = 0$ and $\overrightarrow{OH} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC} = -4s + 4u = 0$, so $s = t = u = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $H\left(\dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}\right)$.
  3. (3) $OH = \sqrt{3 \cdot \dfrac{4}{9}} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$.
  4. (4) Volume $= \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = \dfrac{4}{3}$, and also $\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 2\sqrt{3} \cdot OH = \dfrac{4}{3}$, giving the same $OH$.

(1) area $2\sqrt{3}$ (2) $H\left(\dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{2}{3}\right)$ (3) $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$

Problem 5 III · Graph of x e^(−x)

Consider $f(x) = xe^{-x}$.

  1. (1) Find $f'(x)$ and the maximum value of $f(x)$.
  2. (2) Find $f''(x)$ and the point of inflection of $y = f(x)$.
  3. (3) Find the $x$-intercept of the tangent line at the point of inflection. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Derivatives of Logarithmic and Exponential Functions, Necessary Condition for Local Extrema, Inflection Points of y = f(x), Equation of the Tangent Line

Show hint

Product rule: $(xe^{-x})' = e^{-x} - xe^{-x}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f'(x) = (1 - x)e^{-x}$: positive for $x < 1$, negative for $x > 1$. Maximum $f(1) = \dfrac{1}{e}$.
  2. (2) $f''(x) = -e^{-x} - (1 - x)e^{-x} = (x - 2)e^{-x}$, which changes sign at $x = 2$: inflection point $\left(2, \dfrac{2}{e^2}\right)$.
  3. (3) $f'(2) = -\dfrac{1}{e^2}$, so the tangent is $y = -\dfrac{1}{e^2}(x - 2) + \dfrac{2}{e^2} = \dfrac{4 - x}{e^2}$.
  4. It meets the $x$-axis at $x = 4$.

(1) $f'(x) = (1 - x)e^{-x}$, maximum $\dfrac{1}{e}$ (2) $\left(2, \dfrac{2}{e^2}\right)$ (3) $x = 4$

Problem 6 IV · Area between curves

Consider the curves $y = \sin 2x$ and $y = \sin x$ on $0 \le x \le \pi$.

  1. (1) Find the $x$-coordinates of their common points.
  2. (2) Find the area enclosed by the two curves on $0 \le x \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
  3. (3) Find the total area enclosed by the two curves on $0 \le x \le \pi$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Area Between Two Curves, Indefinite Integrals of Trigonometric Functions, Half-Angle and Double-Angle Trigonometric Formula

Show hint

$\sin 2x = \sin x \iff \sin x(2\cos x - 1) = 0$. An antiderivative of $\sin 2x - \sin x$ is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\cos 2x + \cos x$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\sin x = 0$ or $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $x = 0, \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \pi$.
  2. (2) On $0 \le x \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\sin 2x \ge \sin x$: $\left[-\dfrac{1}{2}\cos 2x + \cos x\right]_0^{\pi/3} = \left(\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{2}\right) - \left(-\dfrac{1}{2} + 1\right) = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
  3. (3) On $\dfrac{\pi}{3} \le x \le \pi$, $\sin x \ge \sin 2x$: $\left[-\cos x + \dfrac{1}{2}\cos 2x\right]_{\pi/3}^{\pi} = \left(1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\right) - \left(-\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{4}\right) = \dfrac{9}{4}$.
  4. Total $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{9}{4} = \dfrac{5}{2}$.

(1) $0, \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \pi$ (2) $\dfrac{1}{4}$ (3) $\dfrac{5}{2}$

Problem 1 I Q1 · Tangent to the x-axis

The parabola $y = x^2 + ax + b$ touches the $x$-axis and passes through the point $(1, 4)$.

  1. (1) Express $b$ in terms of $a$ using the first condition.
  2. (2) Find all pairs $(a, b)$. (Type the negative value of $a$ in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the translation that moves one of the two parabolas onto the other.

Uses: Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions, Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q

Show hint

Touching the $x$-axis means the discriminant is $0$, i.e. the vertex is on the $x$-axis.

Show solution
  1. (1) $a^2 - 4b = 0$, so $b = \dfrac{a^2}{4}$.
  2. (2) $1 + a + \dfrac{a^2}{4} = 4 \iff a^2 + 4a - 12 = 0 \iff (a + 6)(a - 2) = 0$.
  3. $(a, b) = (2, 1)$ or $(-6, 9)$: the parabolas $y = (x + 1)^2$ and $y = (x - 3)^2$.
  4. (3) The vertices are $(-1, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$: a translation of $4$ in the $x$-direction (or $-4$ the other way).

(1) $b = \dfrac{a^2}{4}$ (2) $(2, 1)$, $(-6, 9)$ (3) $4$ in the $x$-direction

Problem 2 I Q2 · Repeated trials

A player hits a target with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$ on each shot, independently. The player takes 4 shots.

  1. (1) Find the probability of exactly 3 hits.
  2. (2) Find the probability of at least one hit.
  3. (3) Find the probability of two hits in a row at least once. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Probability in Repeated Trials (Binomial), Probability of the Complement Event

Show hint

For (3), count the sequences of 4 shots with no two hits in a row, and use the complement.

Show solution
  1. (1) $_4\mathrm{C}_3 \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^3 \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{32}{81}$.
  2. (2) $1 - \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^4 = \dfrac{80}{81}$.
  3. (3) No two hits in a row: 0 hits (1 pattern), 1 hit (4 patterns), 2 hits not adjacent (3 patterns: 1st&3rd, 1st&4th, 2nd&4th); 3 or 4 hits always have two in a row.
  4. $P(\text{none in a row}) = \dfrac{1 + 4 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 4}{81} = \dfrac{21}{81} = \dfrac{7}{27}$, so the answer is $1 - \dfrac{7}{27} = \dfrac{20}{27}$.

(1) $\dfrac{32}{81}$ (2) $\dfrac{80}{81}$ (3) $\dfrac{20}{27}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Exponents and logarithms

Solve the following.

  1. (1) $4^x - 3 \cdot 2^{x+1} + 8 = 0$.
  2. (2) $\log_2(x - 1) + \log_2(x + 1) = 3$.
  3. (3) $\log_{\frac{1}{2}}(x - 2) > -2$. How many integers satisfy it? (Type the number of integers in the box.)

Uses: Exponential Function Properties, Logarithm Properties, Logarithm Function Properties

Show hint

(1): put $t = 2^x > 0$. (3): the base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is less than $1$, so the inequality turns round. Don't forget $x - 2 > 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $t^2 - 6t + 8 = 0$, $t = 2, 4$, so $x = 1, 2$.
  2. (2) Need $x > 1$. $\log_2(x^2 - 1) = 3$, so $x^2 = 9$ and $x = 3$.
  3. (3) $-2 = \log_{\frac{1}{2}} 4$, and $\log_{\frac{1}{2}}$ is decreasing: $0 < x - 2 < 4$, so $2 < x < 6$.
  4. Integers $3, 4, 5$: three of them.

(1) $x = 1, 2$ (2) $x = 3$ (3) $2 < x < 6$; $3$ integers

Problem 4 II Q2 · Complex numbers

Consider the equation $z^3 = 8i$ in complex numbers.

  1. (1) Write $8i$ in polar form.
  2. (2) Find the three solutions in the form $a + bi$.
  3. (3) Find the area of the triangle whose vertices are the three solutions. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Polar Form of Complex Number, De Moivre's Theorem, N-th Roots of 1

Show hint

Write $z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$; De Moivre's theorem gives $r^3 = 8$ and $3\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $8i = 8\left(\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$.
  2. (2) $r = 2$, $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$: $z = \sqrt{3} + i,\ -\sqrt{3} + i,\ -2i$.
  3. (3) The triangle is equilateral with side $2\sqrt{3}$ (from $\sqrt{3} + i$ to $-\sqrt{3} + i$).
  4. Area $= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(2\sqrt{3})^2 = 3\sqrt{3}$.

(2) $\sqrt{3} + i$, $-\sqrt{3} + i$, $-2i$ (3) $3\sqrt{3}$

Problem 5 III · Graph of (log x)/x

Consider $f(x) = \dfrac{\log x}{x}$ for $x > 0$ ($\log$ is the natural logarithm).

  1. (1) Find $f'(x)$ and the maximum value of $f(x)$. (Type the maximum value in the box.)
  2. (2) Find the $x$-coordinate of the point of inflection.
  3. (3) Use the graph to decide which is larger, $3^\pi$ or $\pi^3$.

Uses: Quotient Rule for Derivatives, Derivatives of Logarithmic and Exponential Functions, Sign of the Second Derivative and Concavity

Show hint

For (3), compare $f(3)$ and $f(\pi)$, using that $f$ decreases for $x > e$; then take exponentials.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f'(x) = \dfrac{1 - \log x}{x^2}$: positive for $x < e$, negative for $x > e$. Maximum $f(e) = \dfrac{1}{e}$.
  2. (2) $f''(x) = \dfrac{2\log x - 3}{x^3}$, zero at $\log x = \dfrac{3}{2}$: $x = e^{3/2}$.
  3. (3) $e < 3 < \pi$ and $f$ decreases there, so $\dfrac{\log 3}{3} > \dfrac{\log \pi}{\pi}$.
  4. Multiply by $3\pi$: $\pi\log 3 > 3\log\pi$, i.e. $3^\pi > \pi^3$.

(1) $f'(x) = \dfrac{1 - \log x}{x^2}$, maximum $\dfrac{1}{e}$ (2) $x = e^{3/2}$ (3) $3^\pi$

Problem 6 IV · Volume of revolution

Let $D$ be the region enclosed by the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ and the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$.

  1. (1) Find the common points of the curve and the line.
  2. (2) Find the area of $D$.
  3. (3) Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating $D$ about the $x$-axis. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Area Between Two Curves, Volume of Solid of Revolution About x-axis

Show hint

When rotating, each cross-section is a washer: outer radius $\sqrt{x}$, inner radius $\dfrac{x}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\sqrt{x} = \dfrac{x}{2} \iff 4x = x^2$: $x = 0, 4$. The points are $(0, 0)$ and $(4, 2)$.
  2. (2) $\displaystyle\int_0^4 \left(\sqrt{x} - \dfrac{x}{2}\right)dx = \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 8 - 4 = \dfrac{4}{3}$.
  3. (3) $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^4 \left(x - \dfrac{x^2}{4}\right)dx = \pi\left(8 - \dfrac{16}{3}\right) = \dfrac{8\pi}{3}$.

(1) $(0, 0)$, $(4, 2)$ (2) $\dfrac{4}{3}$ (3) $\dfrac{8\pi}{3}$

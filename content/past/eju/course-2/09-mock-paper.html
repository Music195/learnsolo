Problem 1 I Q1 · Moving vertex

Let $a$ be a constant and consider the parabola $y = x^2 - 2ax + 2a^2 - 2a$.

  1. (1) Find the vertex, and the smallest value of its $y$-coordinate as $a$ varies.
  2. (2) Find the range of $a$ for which the parabola meets the $x$-axis.
  3. (3) Find the largest length of the segment that the parabola cuts from the $x$-axis. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Graph of y = a(x − p)² + q, Quadratic Graph and Intersection with x-axis, Maximum and Minimum of y = a(x − p)² + q

Show hint

The roots are $a \pm \sqrt{2a - a^2}$, so the segment has length $2\sqrt{2a - a^2}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $y = (x - a)^2 + a^2 - 2a$: vertex $(a, a^2 - 2a)$. $a^2 - 2a = (a - 1)^2 - 1 \ge -1$: smallest $-1$ at $a = 1$.
  2. (2) It meets the $x$-axis when $a^2 - 2a \le 0$: $0 \le a \le 2$.
  3. (3) Length $2\sqrt{2a - a^2} = 2\sqrt{1 - (a - 1)^2}$, largest at $a = 1$: length $2$.

(1) $(a, a^2 - 2a)$, smallest $-1$ (2) $0 \le a \le 2$ (3) $2$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Choosing a group

Three people are chosen at random from 5 men and 4 women.

  1. (1) How many different groups of three are there?
  2. (2) Find the probability that exactly two of them are women.
  3. (3) Find the probability that the group has at least one man and at least one woman. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Counting Combinations, Probability of the Complement Event

Show hint

The complement of “both are present” is “all men or all women”.

Show solution
  1. (1) $_9\mathrm{C}_3 = 84$.
  2. (2) $\dfrac{_4\mathrm{C}_2 \cdot {}_5\mathrm{C}_1}{84} = \dfrac{30}{84} = \dfrac{5}{14}$.
  3. (3) All men: $_5\mathrm{C}_3 = 10$; all women: $_4\mathrm{C}_3 = 4$.
  4. $1 - \dfrac{14}{84} = \dfrac{5}{6}$.

(1) $84$ (2) $\dfrac{5}{14}$ (3) $\dfrac{5}{6}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Trigonometric equations

Consider $g(\theta) = \cos 2\theta + 3\sin\theta$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

  1. (1) Write $g(\theta)$ in terms of $s = \sin\theta$.
  2. (2) Solve $g(\theta) = 2$.
  3. (3) Find the largest value of $g(\theta)$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Half-Angle and Double-Angle Trigonometric Formula, Trigonometric Equations and Inequalities

Show hint

$\cos 2\theta = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$, and $s$ ranges over $-1 \le s \le 1$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $g = 1 - 2s^2 + 3s = -2s^2 + 3s + 1$.
  2. (2) $-2s^2 + 3s + 1 = 2 \iff 2s^2 - 3s + 1 = 0 \iff s = \dfrac{1}{2}, 1$: $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.
  3. (3) $g = -2\left(s - \dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 + \dfrac{17}{8}$, and $s = \dfrac{3}{4}$ is in range: largest value $\dfrac{17}{8}$.

(1) $-2s^2 + 3s + 1$ (2) $\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ (3) $\dfrac{17}{8}$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Rotation in the complex plane

Let $z = 2 + i$. The points $O$, $z$ and $w$ form an equilateral triangle, with $w$ obtained by rotating $z$ about $O$ by $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

  1. (1) Write $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} + i\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ as $a + bi$.
  2. (2) Find $w$ in the form $a + bi$.
  3. (3) Find the area of the triangle. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Rotation About the Origin, Absolute Value of Complex Number

Show hint

Rotating by $\theta$ about the origin is multiplying by $\cos\theta + i\sin\theta$. The side of the triangle is $|z|$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}i$.
  2. (2) $w = (2 + i)\left(\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}i\right) = \left(1 - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + \left(\sqrt{3} + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)i$.
  3. (3) The side is $|z| = \sqrt{5}$, so the area is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 5 = \dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

(2) $\left(1 - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + \left(\sqrt{3} + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)i$ (3) $\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{4}$

Problem 5 III · Limits

Find the following limits and constants.

  1. (1) $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin 3x}{x}$.
  2. (2) $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\sqrt{n^2 + 4n} - n\right)$.
  3. (3) Find the constants $a$ and $b$ with $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2} \dfrac{x^2 + ax + b}{x - 2} = 5$. (Type $b$ in the box.)

Uses: Limit of sin x / x, Properties of Limits of Sequences 1, Properties of Limits of Functions

Show hint

(2): multiply and divide by $\sqrt{n^2 + 4n} + n$. (3): the denominator tends to $0$, so the numerator must too.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x} = 3 \cdot \dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x} \to 3$.
  2. (2) $\sqrt{n^2 + 4n} - n = \dfrac{4n}{\sqrt{n^2 + 4n} + n} = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{1 + 4/n} + 1} \to 2$.
  3. (3) The numerator must vanish at $x = 2$: $4 + 2a + b = 0$, so $b = -2a - 4$.
  4. Then $x^2 + ax - 2a - 4 = (x - 2)(x + a + 2)$, and the limit is $4 + a = 5$: $a = 1$, $b = -6$.

(1) $3$ (2) $2$ (3) $a = 1$, $b = -6$

Problem 6 IV · Volumes of revolution

Let $D$ be the region enclosed by the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 2x$.

  1. (1) Find the area of $D$.
  2. (2) Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating $D$ about the $x$-axis. (Type this one in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating $D$ about the $y$-axis.

Uses: Area Between Two Curves, Volume of Solid of Revolution About x-axis, Volume of Solid of Revolution About y-axis

Show hint

The curves meet at $x = 0$ and $x = 2$, and the line is above the parabola in between. About the $y$-axis, use $x = \dfrac{y}{2}$ and $x = \sqrt{y}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (2x - x^2)\,dx = 4 - \dfrac{8}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.
  2. (2) $\pi\displaystyle\int_0^2 (4x^2 - x^4)\,dx = \pi\left(\dfrac{32}{3} - \dfrac{32}{5}\right) = \dfrac{64\pi}{15}$.
  3. (3) For $0 \le y \le 4$, the region runs from $x = \dfrac{y}{2}$ to $x = \sqrt{y}$:
  4. $\pi\displaystyle\int_0^4 \left(y - \dfrac{y^2}{4}\right)dy = \pi\left(8 - \dfrac{16}{3}\right) = \dfrac{8\pi}{3}$.

(1) $\dfrac{4}{3}$ (2) $\dfrac{64\pi}{15}$ (3) $\dfrac{8\pi}{3}$

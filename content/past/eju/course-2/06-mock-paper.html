Problem 1 I Q1 · Parabola and line

The line $y = mx + 2$ meets the parabola $y = x^2$ at two points $P$ and $Q$.

  1. (1) Show that the line meets the parabola at two points for every $m$.
  2. (2) Let $x_1, x_2$ be the $x$-coordinates of $P$, $Q$. Express $(x_1 - x_2)^2$ in terms of $m$.
  3. (3) Find the shortest possible length of $PQ$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Equation: Discriminant and Real Solutions, Roots and Coefficients, Distance Formula

Show hint

$x^2 - mx - 2 = 0$. Since $P$ and $Q$ are on the line, $PQ = \sqrt{1 + m^2}\,|x_1 - x_2|$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $x^2 - mx - 2 = 0$ has $D = m^2 + 8 > 0$.
  2. (2) $x_1 + x_2 = m$, $x_1x_2 = -2$: $(x_1 - x_2)^2 = m^2 + 8$.
  3. (3) $PQ^2 = (1 + m^2)(m^2 + 8)$, smallest at $m = 0$: $PQ^2 = 8$.
  4. So the shortest $PQ$ is $2\sqrt{2}$.

(2) $m^2 + 8$ (3) $2\sqrt{2}$

Problem 2 I Q2 · Arrangements of letters

The seven letters S, U, C, C, E, S, S are arranged in a row at random.

  1. (1) How many different arrangements are there?
  2. (2) How many arrangements have the two C's next to each other?
  3. (3) Find the probability that no two S's are next to each other. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Permutations with Identical Objects, Counting Combinations

Show hint

For (3), arrange U, C, C, E first; then put the three S's into the gaps, at most one per gap.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{7!}{3!\,2!} = 420$.
  2. (2) Treat CC as one letter: $\dfrac{6!}{3!} = 120$.
  3. (3) U, C, C, E: $\dfrac{4!}{2!} = 12$ arrangements, with $5$ gaps; choose $3$ gaps for the S's: $_5\mathrm{C}_3 = 10$.
  4. So $120$ arrangements, and the probability is $\dfrac{120}{420} = \dfrac{2}{7}$.

(1) $420$ (2) $120$ (3) $\dfrac{2}{7}$

Problem 3 II Q1 · Sums of sequences

The sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence $\{a_n\}$ is $S_n = 2n^2 + 3n$.

  1. (1) Find the general term $a_n$.
  2. (2) Write $\dfrac{1}{a_n a_{n+1}}$ as a difference of two fractions.
  3. (3) Find $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{10} \dfrac{1}{a_k a_{k+1}}$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Sequence Sum and General Term, Properties of Sigma (Σ), General Term of an Arithmetic Sequence

Show hint

$a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ for $n \ge 2$, and $a_1 = S_1$. Then the sum telescopes.

Show solution
  1. (1) $a_1 = 5$; for $n \ge 2$, $a_n = 2n^2 + 3n - 2(n - 1)^2 - 3(n - 1) = 4n + 1$, which also gives $a_1 = 5$. So $a_n = 4n + 1$.
  2. (2) $\dfrac{1}{(4n + 1)(4n + 5)} = \dfrac{1}{4}\left(\dfrac{1}{4n + 1} - \dfrac{1}{4n + 5}\right)$.
  3. (3) The sum telescopes: $\dfrac{1}{4}\left(\dfrac{1}{5} - \dfrac{1}{45}\right) = \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{8}{45} = \dfrac{2}{45}$.

(1) $a_n = 4n + 1$ (3) $\dfrac{2}{45}$

Problem 4 II Q2 · Plane vectors

In triangle $OAB$, $|\vec{a}| = 2$, $|\vec{b}| = 3$ and $|\vec{a} - \vec{b}| = \sqrt{7}$, where $\vec{a} = \overrightarrow{OA}$, $\vec{b} = \overrightarrow{OB}$. Let $H$ be the orthocentre (the common point of the three altitudes).

  1. (1) Find $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$.
  2. (2) Write $\overrightarrow{OH} = s\vec{a} + t\vec{b}$. Using $\overrightarrow{OH} \perp \overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AH} \perp \overrightarrow{OB}$, find $s$ and $t$.
  3. (3) Find the length $OH$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Vector Dot Product Properties, Vector Perpendicularity, Vector Magnitude

Show hint

Expand $|\vec{a} - \vec{b}|^2$. The perpendicular conditions give $\overrightarrow{OH} \cdot (\vec{b} - \vec{a}) = 0$ and $(\overrightarrow{OH} - \vec{a}) \cdot \vec{b} = 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $7 = 4 - 2\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + 9$, so $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 3$.
  2. (2) $\overrightarrow{OH} \cdot \vec{b} = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 3$: $3s + 9t = 3$. $\overrightarrow{OH} \cdot \vec{a} = \overrightarrow{OH} \cdot \vec{b}$: $4s + 3t = 3s + 9t$, so $s = 6t$.
  3. Then $27t = 3$: $t = \dfrac{1}{9}$, $s = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
  4. (3) $|\overrightarrow{OH}|^2 = \dfrac{4}{9} \cdot 4 + 2 \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{9} \cdot 3 + \dfrac{1}{81} \cdot 9 = \dfrac{16 + 4 + 1}{9} = \dfrac{7}{3}$, so $OH = \dfrac{\sqrt{21}}{3}$.

(1) $3$ (2) $s = \dfrac{2}{3}$, $t = \dfrac{1}{9}$ (3) $\dfrac{\sqrt{21}}{3}$

Problem 5 III · Tangent lines to y = e^x

Consider the curve $C$: $y = e^x$.

  1. (1) Find the tangent line to $C$ that passes through the origin.
  2. (2) Find the area enclosed by $C$, that tangent line and the $y$-axis.
  3. (3) Find the shortest distance between a point on $C$ and the line $y = x - 2$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Equation of the Tangent Line, Derivatives of Logarithmic and Exponential Functions, Area Between Two Curves, Distance Between a Point and a Line

Show hint

The tangent at $(t, e^t)$ is $y = e^t(x - t) + e^t$. For (3), the closest point is where the tangent is parallel to $y = x - 2$.

Show solution
  1. (1) Through the origin: $0 = e^t(1 - t)$, so $t = 1$ and the tangent is $y = ex$.
  2. (2) $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (e^x - ex)\,dx = (e - 1) - \dfrac{e}{2} = \dfrac{e}{2} - 1$.
  3. (3) The tangent has slope $1$ where $e^x = 1$: the point $(0, 1)$.
  4. Its distance to $x - y - 2 = 0$ is $\dfrac{|0 - 1 - 2|}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

(1) $y = ex$ (2) $\dfrac{e}{2} - 1$ (3) $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$

Problem 6 IV · Functions defined by integrals

Answer the following.

  1. (1) Find the function $f(x)$ that satisfies $f(x) = x^2 + \displaystyle\int_0^1 t f(t)\,dt$.
  2. (2) Let $g(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x (t^2 - 3t + 2)\,dt$. Find $g'(x)$.
  3. (3) Find the local maximum and local minimum values of $g(x)$. (Type the local maximum value in the box.)

Uses: Definite Integrals, Definite Integral and Derivative, Necessary Condition for Local Extrema

Show hint

For (1), $\displaystyle\int_0^1 t f(t)\,dt$ is a constant $c$: write $f(x) = x^2 + c$ and solve for $c$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $c = \displaystyle\int_0^1 t(t^2 + c)\,dt = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{c}{2}$, so $c = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $f(x) = x^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. (2) $g'(x) = x^2 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)(x - 2)$.
  3. (3) $g' > 0$ for $x < 1$, $< 0$ for $1 < x < 2$, $> 0$ for $x > 2$.
  4. Local maximum $g(1) = \dfrac{1}{3} - \dfrac{3}{2} + 2 = \dfrac{5}{6}$; local minimum $g(2) = \dfrac{8}{3} - 6 + 4 = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

(1) $f(x) = x^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}$ (2) $(x - 1)(x - 2)$ (3) max $\dfrac{5}{6}$, min $\dfrac{2}{3}$

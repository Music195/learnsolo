Sample Paper: Proof, Calculus and Probability

A sample paper in the style of Japanese university entrance exams (original problems, not an official past paper). Three problems with written solutions. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Answer the following about integers $n$.

  1. (1) Prove that $n^3 - n$ is a multiple of $6$ for every integer $n$.
  2. (2) Find every integer $n$ with $n^3 - n = 336$. (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Divisibility Rules, Factor Theorem

Show hint

$n^3 - n = (n - 1)n(n + 1)$: three consecutive integers. For (2), $336 = 6 \cdot 56$; try a value, then use the factor theorem to rule out the rest.

Show solution
  1. (1) $n^3 - n = (n - 1)n(n + 1)$. Among three consecutive integers one is a multiple of $3$ and at least one is even, so the product is a multiple of $2 \cdot 3 = 6$.
  2. (2) $7^3 - 7 = 343 - 7 = 336$, so $n = 7$ works.
  3. Factor theorem: $n^3 - n - 336 = (n - 7)(n^2 + 7n + 48)$.
  4. $n^2 + 7n + 48 = 0$ has discriminant $49 - 192 < 0$, so no other real (let alone integer) solution.

(1) Proved (2) $n = 7$

Problem 2

For $0 \le a \le 1$, let $I(a) = \displaystyle\int_0^1 |x^2 - a^2|\,dx$.

  1. (1) Write $I(a)$ in terms of $a$.
  2. (2) Find the minimum value of $I(a)$ and the $a$ that gives it. (Type the minimum in the box.)

Uses: Definite Integral and Area 2, Monotonicity and Derivative Sign

Show hint

$x^2 - a^2$ changes sign at $x = a$. Split the integral at $x = a$ and remove the absolute value on each part.

Show solution
  1. (1) $I(a) = \displaystyle\int_0^a (a^2 - x^2)\,dx + \int_a^1 (x^2 - a^2)\,dx$.
  2. $= \left(a^3 - \dfrac{a^3}{3}\right) + \left(\dfrac{1 - a^3}{3} - a^2(1 - a)\right) = \dfrac{4}{3}a^3 - a^2 + \dfrac{1}{3}$.
  3. Check: $I(0) = \dfrac{1}{3} = \displaystyle\int_0^1 x^2\,dx$ ✓.
  4. (2) $I'(a) = 4a^2 - 2a = 2a(2a - 1)$: negative for $0 < a < \dfrac{1}{2}$, positive for $\dfrac{1}{2} < a < 1$.
  5. Minimum at $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $I\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{6} - \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

(1) $I(a) = \dfrac{4}{3}a^3 - a^2 + \dfrac{1}{3}$ (2) minimum $\dfrac{1}{4}$, at $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Problem 3

A piece starts at vertex A of triangle ABC. Each move, it goes to one of the other two vertices with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$ each. Let $p_n$ be the probability that it is at A after $n$ moves ($p_0 = 1$).

  1. (1) Show that $p_{n+1} = \dfrac{1}{2}(1 - p_n)$.
  2. (2) Find $p_n$.
  3. (3) Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} p_n$. (Type this one in the box.)

Uses: Independent Probability, Geometric Sequence General Term, Limit of rⁿ

Show hint

To be at A after $n + 1$ moves, the piece must be at B or C after $n$ moves (probability $1 - p_n$), then choose A. For (2), find the fixed point $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}(1 - \alpha)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) From A it can't stay at A. From B or C (probability $1 - p_n$) it moves to A with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. So $p_{n+1} = \dfrac{1}{2}(1 - p_n)$.
  2. (2) $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}(1 - \alpha)$ gives $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$, and $p_{n+1} - \dfrac{1}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}\left(p_n - \dfrac{1}{3}\right)$.
  3. So $p_n - \dfrac{1}{3} = \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^n\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{3}\right)$, i.e. $p_n = \dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3}\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$.
  4. Check: $p_1 = 0$ and $p_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, as expected.
  5. (3) $\left|-\dfrac{1}{2}\right| < 1$, so $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^n \to 0$ and $p_n \to \dfrac{1}{3}$.

(2) $p_n = \dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3}\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$ (3) $\dfrac{1}{3}$

In the long run the piece is equally likely to be at any of the three vertices, which is why the limit is $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

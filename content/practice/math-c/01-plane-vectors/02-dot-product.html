The Dot Product

Seven problems on the dot product, the angle between vectors, perpendicularity, squared lengths such as $|\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2$, and triangle areas. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

$\vec{a} = (3, -2)$ and $\vec{b} = (4, 5)$. Find $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ and $(\vec{a} + \vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} - \vec{b})$. (Type the second in the box.)

Uses: Dot Product, Dot Product Properties

Show hint

$(\vec{a} + \vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} - \vec{b}) = |\vec{a}|^2 - |\vec{b}|^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 12 - 10 = 2$.
  2. $|\vec{a}|^2 - |\vec{b}|^2 = 13 - 41 = -28$.

$2$; $-28$

Problem 2

Find the angle between $\vec{a} = (1, 3)$ and $\vec{b} = (-2, 4)$.

Uses: Dot Product, Angle Between Vectors

Show hint

$\cos\theta = \dfrac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|}$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = -2 + 12 = 10$, $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{10}$, $|\vec{b}| = \sqrt{20}$.
  2. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{200}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $\theta = 45^\circ$.

$45^\circ$

Problem 3

(1) Find $k$ so that $(k, 2)$ and $(3, k - 5)$ are perpendicular. (2) Find the unit vectors perpendicular to $(5, 12)$. (Type $k$ in the box.)

Uses: Perpendicular Vectors

Show hint

Perpendicular $\iff$ dot product $0$. A vector perpendicular to $(a, b)$ is $(b, -a)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $3k + 2(k - 5) = 0$, so $k = 2$.
  2. (2) $(12, -5)$ is perpendicular and has length $13$: $\pm\dfrac{1}{13}(12, -5)$.

(1) $k = 2$ (2) $\pm\left(\dfrac{12}{13}, -\dfrac{5}{13}\right)$

Problem 4

$|\vec{a}| = 2$, $|\vec{b}| = 3$ and $|2\vec{a} + \vec{b}| = \sqrt{13}$. Find $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ and the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$. (Type the angle in the box.)

Uses: Dot Product Properties, Angle Between Vectors

Show hint

Square: $|2\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2 = 4|\vec{a}|^2 + 4\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + |\vec{b}|^2$.

Show solution
  1. $13 = 16 + 4\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + 9$, so $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = -3$.
  2. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{-3}{2 \cdot 3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\theta = 120^\circ$.

$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = -3$; $120^\circ$

Problem 5

Find the area of triangle ABC with A$(-1, 2)$, B$(3, 1)$ and C$(1, 5)$.

Uses: Triangle Area with Vectors

Show hint

Take both vectors from A: $S = \dfrac{1}{2}|a_1b_2 - a_2b_1|$.

Show solution
  1. $\overrightarrow{AB} = (4, -1)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (2, 3)$.
  2. $S = \dfrac{1}{2}|4 \cdot 3 - (-1) \cdot 2| = \dfrac{14}{2} = 7$.

$7$

Problem 6

$\vec{a} = (2, 1)$ and $\vec{b} = (1, 3)$. Find the real $t$ that makes $|\vec{a} + t\vec{b}|$ smallest, and the smallest value. Show that then $\vec{a} + t\vec{b} \perp \vec{b}$. (Type the smallest value in the box.)

Uses: Dot Product Properties, Perpendicular Vectors

Show hint

$|\vec{a} + t\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{b}|^2t^2 + 2(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})t + |\vec{a}|^2$ is a quadratic in $t$.

Show solution
  1. $|\vec{a}|^2 = 5$, $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 5$, $|\vec{b}|^2 = 10$: $|\vec{a} + t\vec{b}|^2 = 10t^2 + 10t + 5 = 10\left(t + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \dfrac{5}{2}$.
  2. Smallest at $t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, value $\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}$.
  3. Then $\vec{a} + t\vec{b} = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, and $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}\right) \cdot (1, 3) = 0$ ✓.

$t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$; smallest value $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}$

Problem 7

In triangle OAB, $\vec{a} = \overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\vec{b} = \overrightarrow{OB}$ with $|\vec{a}| = 3$, $|\vec{b}| = 2$ and $|\vec{a} - \vec{b}| = \sqrt{7}$.

  1. (1) Find $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$ and $\angle AOB$.
  2. (2) Find the area of triangle OAB. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) H is the foot of the perpendicular from B to line OA. Write $\overrightarrow{OH}$ in terms of $\vec{a}$.
  4. (4) Find BH, and check the area using it.

Uses: Dot Product Properties, Angle Between Vectors, Triangle Area with Vectors, Perpendicular Vectors

Show hint

(3) Put $\overrightarrow{OH} = k\vec{a}$ and use $\overrightarrow{HB} \cdot \vec{a} = 0$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $7 = 9 - 2\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + 4$, so $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 3$; $\cos\angle AOB = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$.
  2. (2) $S = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{9 \cdot 4 - 3^2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{27}}{2} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
  3. (3) $(\vec{b} - k\vec{a}) \cdot \vec{a} = 3 - 9k = 0$, so $k = \dfrac{1}{3}$: $\overrightarrow{OH} = \dfrac{1}{3}\vec{a}$.
  4. (4) $OH = 1$, so $BH = \sqrt{4 - 1} = \sqrt{3}$, and $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot \sqrt{3} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$ ✓.

(1) $3$, $60^\circ$ (2) $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (3) $\dfrac{1}{3}\vec{a}$ (4) $\sqrt{3}$

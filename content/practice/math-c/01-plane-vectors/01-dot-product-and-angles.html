Dot Product and Angles

Five problems on the dot product: finding angles, perpendicular vectors, lengths and areas. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find the angle $\theta$ ($0° \le \theta \le 180°$) between $\vec{a} = (3, 1)$ and $\vec{b} = (1, 2)$.

Uses: Vector Angle

Show hint

Use $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|}$, then find the angle with that cosine.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 3 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 2 = 5$.
  2. $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{10}$, $|\vec{b}| = \sqrt{5}$, so $|\vec{a}||\vec{b}| = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$.
  3. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{5}{5\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.

$\theta = 45°$

Problem 2

Find $k$ so that $\vec{a} = (k, 3)$ and $\vec{b} = (2, -4)$ are perpendicular.

Uses: Vector Perpendicularity

Show hint

Two nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when their dot product is $0$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 2k + 3 \cdot (-4) = 2k - 12$.
  2. $2k - 12 = 0$ gives $k = 6$.
  3. Check: $\vec{a} = (6, 3) \ne \vec{0}$, so the answer is valid.

$k = 6$

Problem 3

$|\vec{a}| = 2$, $|\vec{b}| = 3$, and the angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is $120°$. Find $|\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}|$.

Uses: Vector Dot Product Properties

Show hint

Square it first: $|\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}|^2 = (\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} + 2\vec{b})$, and expand like $(x + 2y)^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \cos 120° = 6 \cdot \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = -3$.
  2. $|\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + 4\,\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + 4|\vec{b}|^2 = 4 - 12 + 36 = 28$.
  3. A length is positive, so take the positive square root: $\sqrt{28} = 2\sqrt{7}$.

$|\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}| = 2\sqrt{7}$

Problem 4

Find the area of triangle OAB, where O is the origin, A$(3, 1)$ and B$(1, 2)$.

Uses: Triangle Area with Vectors

Show hint

With $\overrightarrow{OA} = (a_1, a_2)$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = (b_1, b_2)$, the area is $\dfrac{1}{2}|a_1b_2 - a_2b_1|$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1}{2}|3 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1| = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 5 = \dfrac{5}{2}$.
  2. Check with the other formula: $|\vec{a}|^2|\vec{b}|^2 - (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})^2 = 10 \cdot 5 - 5^2 = 25$, and $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{25} = \dfrac{5}{2}$ ✓.

Area $= \dfrac{5}{2}$

Problem 5

$|\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}| = 1$ and the angle between them is $60°$. As the real number $t$ varies, find the minimum value of $|\vec{a} + t\vec{b}|$, and the $t$ that gives it.

Uses: Vector Dot Product Properties

Show hint

$|\vec{a} + t\vec{b}|^2$ is a quadratic in $t$. Complete the square to find its minimum.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot \cos 60° = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. $|\vec{a} + t\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + 2t\,\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + t^2|\vec{b}|^2 = t^2 + t + 1 = \left(t + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \dfrac{3}{4}$.
  3. This is smallest, $\dfrac{3}{4}$, at $t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The length is positive, so its minimum is $\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Minimum $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, when $t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

At the minimum, $\vec{a} + t\vec{b}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{b}$: $\left(\vec{a} - \dfrac{1}{2}\vec{b}\right) \cdot \vec{b} = \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2} = 0$. The shortest vector from the line to the tip of $\vec{a}$ meets the line at right angles.

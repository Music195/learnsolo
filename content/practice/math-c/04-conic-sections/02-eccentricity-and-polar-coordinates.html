Eccentricity and Polar Coordinates

Six problems on eccentricity and the focus-directrix definition, and on polar coordinates and polar equations. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find the eccentricity of the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$ and of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{5} = 1$. (Type the hyperbola's in the box.)

Uses: Eccentricity

Show hint

$e = \dfrac{c}{a}$, with $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ for an ellipse and $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ for a hyperbola.

Show solution
  1. Ellipse: $c = 3$, $e = \dfrac{3}{5}$ ($< 1$).
  2. Hyperbola: $c = \sqrt{9} = 3$, $e = \dfrac{3}{2}$ ($> 1$).

$\dfrac{3}{5}$; $\dfrac{3}{2}$

Problem 2

Write the polar point $\left(4, \dfrac{5\pi}{4}\right)$ in $xy$-coordinates, and the point $(-\sqrt{3}, -1)$ in polar coordinates ($0 \le \theta < 2\pi$). (Type $\theta$ for the second point in the box.)

Uses: Polar Coordinates

Show hint

$x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$. For the reverse, use the signs of both $x$ and $y$.

Show solution
  1. $\left(4\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{4}, 4\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{4}\right) = (-2\sqrt{2}, -2\sqrt{2})$.
  2. $r = 2$, $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$: $\theta = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$.

$(-2\sqrt{2}, -2\sqrt{2})$; $\left(2, \dfrac{7\pi}{6}\right)$

Problem 3

Write the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x = 0$ as a polar equation. Find $r$ for the point of the circle with $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

Uses: Polar Coordinates

Show hint

$x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ and $x = r\cos\theta$.

Show solution
  1. $r^2 = 6r\cos\theta$, so $r = 6\cos\theta$ (this includes the pole, at $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$).
  2. $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$: $r = 3$.

$r = 6\cos\theta$; $r = 3$

Problem 4

F is $(1, 0)$, and H is the foot of the perpendicular from P to the line $x = 4$. Find the locus of P with $PF : PH = 1 : 2$, name it, and find the length of its major axis.

Uses: Eccentricity

Show hint

$PF = \dfrac{1}{2}PH$; square both sides.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + y^2} = \dfrac{|x - 4|}{2}$.
  2. $4(x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2) = x^2 - 8x + 16 \iff 3x^2 + 4y^2 = 12 \iff \dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$.
  3. An ellipse ($e = \dfrac{1}{2} < 1$), major axis $4$.

$\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$, an ellipse; major axis $4$

Problem 5

Write the polar equation $r = \dfrac{6}{2 - \cos\theta}$ in $x$ and $y$, name the curve and find its eccentricity.

Uses: Polar Coordinates, Eccentricity

Show hint

$2r = 6 + r\cos\theta = 6 + x$, then square.

Show solution
  1. $2r = 6 + x$, so $4(x^2 + y^2) = (6 + x)^2$: $3x^2 - 12x + 4y^2 = 36$.
  2. $3(x - 2)^2 + 4y^2 = 48 \iff \dfrac{(x - 2)^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{12} = 1$.
  3. An ellipse with $a = 4$, $c = \sqrt{16 - 12} = 2$: $e = \dfrac{1}{2}$. (One focus is the pole.)

$\dfrac{(x - 2)^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{12} = 1$, an ellipse; $e = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Problem 6

Consider the polar equation $r = \dfrac{4}{1 + 2\cos\theta}$.

  1. (1) Write it in $x$ and $y$.
  2. (2) Name the curve and find its eccentricity. (Type the eccentricity in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the vertices.
  4. (4) Show that $PO = 2 \times$ (distance from P to the line $x = 2$) for every point P on the curve.

Uses: Polar Coordinates, Eccentricity

Show hint

$r + 2r\cos\theta = 4$ gives $r = 4 - 2x$. Square, then complete the square.

Show solution
  1. (1) $r = 4 - 2x$: $x^2 + y^2 = 16 - 16x + 4x^2$, i.e. $3x^2 - 16x - y^2 + 16 = 0$.
  2. (2) $3\left(x - \dfrac{8}{3}\right)^2 - y^2 = \dfrac{16}{3}$, i.e. $\dfrac{\left(x - \frac{8}{3}\right)^2}{\frac{16}{9}} - \dfrac{y^2}{\frac{16}{3}} = 1$: a hyperbola with $a = \dfrac{4}{3}$, $c = \sqrt{\dfrac{16}{9} + \dfrac{48}{9}} = \dfrac{8}{3}$, $e = 2$.
  3. (3) Centre $\left(\dfrac{8}{3}, 0\right)$: vertices $\left(\dfrac{4}{3}, 0\right)$ and $(4, 0)$.
  4. (4) From (1), $PO = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = |4 - 2x| = 2|x - 2|$, and $|x - 2|$ is the distance from P to $x = 2$. (So O is a focus, $x = 2$ the directrix, and $e = 2$ again.)

(1) $3x^2 - 16x - y^2 + 16 = 0$ (2) hyperbola, $e = 2$ (3) $\left(\dfrac{4}{3}, 0\right)$, $(4, 0)$

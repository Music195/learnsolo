Vectors in Space

Five problems on distances, perpendicular vectors, angles, spheres and triangle areas in space. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find the distance between A$(1, 2, 3)$ and B$(3, -1, 9)$.

Uses: Distance and Section Points

Show hint

$AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, -3, 6)$.
  2. $AB = \sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7$.

$AB = 7$

Problem 2

Find $k$ so that $\vec{a} = (2, -1, 2)$ and $\vec{b} = (1, 1, k)$ are perpendicular.

Uses: Perpendicular Condition for Vectors

Show hint

Perpendicular nonzero vectors have dot product $0$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 2 - 1 + 2k = 1 + 2k$.
  2. $1 + 2k = 0$ gives $k = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

$k = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

Problem 3

Find the angle between $\vec{a} = (1, 0, 1)$ and $\vec{b} = (1, 1, 0)$.

Uses: Dot Product of Vectors, Magnitude of Vector (3D)

Show hint

$\cos\theta = \dfrac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|}$, just as in the plane.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 1$, $|\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}| = \sqrt{2}$.
  2. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\theta = 60°$.

$\theta = 60°$

Problem 4

Find the centre and radius of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2x + 4y - 6z + 5 = 0$.

Uses: Equation of a Sphere

Show hint

Complete the square in $x$, $y$ and $z$ separately.

Show solution
  1. $(x - 1)^2 - 1 + (y + 2)^2 - 4 + (z - 3)^2 - 9 + 5 = 0$.
  2. $(x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 + (z - 3)^2 = 9$.

Centre $(1, -2, 3)$, radius $3$

Problem 5

Find the area of the triangle with vertices A$(1, 0, 0)$, B$(0, 2, 0)$ and C$(0, 0, 3)$.

Uses: Dot Product of Vectors, Magnitude of Vector (3D)

Show hint

The plane formula still works: area $= \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{|\overrightarrow{AB}|^2|\overrightarrow{AC}|^2 - (\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC})^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, 2, 0)$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 0, 3)$.
  2. $|\overrightarrow{AB}|^2 = 5$, $|\overrightarrow{AC}|^2 = 10$, $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC} = 1$.
  3. Area $= \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{5 \cdot 10 - 1^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{49} = \dfrac{7}{2}$.

Area $= \dfrac{7}{2}$

The formula comes from $\dfrac{1}{2}|\overrightarrow{AB}||\overrightarrow{AC}|\sin A$ with $\sin^2 A = 1 - \cos^2 A$, which doesn't care whether the triangle is in a plane or in space.

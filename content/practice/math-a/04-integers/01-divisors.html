Divisibility and Divisors

Six problems on divisibility rules and on the number and sum of divisors. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

The 4-digit number $52\square4$ is divisible by both $3$ and $4$. Find the digit $\square$.

Uses: Divisibility Rules

Show hint

Divisible by $4$: the last two digits form a multiple of $4$. Divisible by $3$: the digit sum is a multiple of $3$.

Show solution
  1. By $4$: $\square4$ is a multiple of $4$, so $\square \in \{0, 2, 4, 6, 8\}$.
  2. By $3$: $5 + 2 + \square + 4 = 11 + \square$ is a multiple of $3$, so $\square \in \{1, 4, 7\}$.
  3. Both: $\square = 4$ ($5244 = 12 \times 437$).

$4$

Problem 2

How many positive divisors does $1800$ have?

Uses: Number of Divisors

Show hint

Prime-factorise first: $1800 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^2$.

Show solution
  1. $(3 + 1)(2 + 1)(2 + 1) = 36$.

$36$

Problem 3

$a$ and $b$ are digits, and the 5-digit number $7a36b$ is divisible by $36$. Find all such numbers. How many are there?

Uses: Divisibility Rules

Show hint

$36 = 4 \times 9$, and $4$ and $9$ are coprime. Use the rules for $4$ and for $9$.

Show solution
  1. By $4$: $6b$ is a multiple of $4$, so $b \in \{0, 4, 8\}$.
  2. By $9$: $7 + a + 3 + 6 + b = 16 + a + b$ is a multiple of $9$, so $a + b = 2$ or $11$.
  3. $b = 0$: $a = 2$. $\;b = 4$: $a = 7$. $\;b = 8$: $a = 3$.
  4. $72360$, $77364$, $73368$: $3$ numbers.

$72360$, $77364$, $73368$

Problem 4

Find the smallest natural number with exactly $10$ positive divisors.

Uses: Number of Divisors

Show hint

$10 = 10$ or $10 = 5 \times 2$, so the number is $p^9$ or $p^4q$. Use the smallest primes, with the larger power on the smaller prime.

Show solution
  1. $p^9$: smallest $2^9 = 512$.
  2. $p^4q$: smallest $2^4 \cdot 3 = 48$.
  3. Answer: $48$.

$48$

Problem 5

$N = 2^a \cdot 3^b$ ($a, b \ge 1$) has exactly $12$ positive divisors, and their sum is $280$. Find $N$.

Uses: Number of Divisors

Show hint

$(a + 1)(b + 1) = 12$, and the sum is $(1 + 2 + \cdots + 2^a)(1 + 3 + \cdots + 3^b)$. Try each case.

Show solution
  1. $(a, b) = (1, 5)$: $3 \cdot 364 = 1092$. $\;(2, 3)$: $7 \cdot 40 = 280$ ✓. $\;(3, 2)$: $15 \cdot 13 = 195$. $\;(5, 1)$: $63 \cdot 4 = 252$.
  2. So $a = 2$, $b = 3$: $N = 4 \cdot 27 = 108$.

$N = 108$

Problem 6

Let $N = 2520$.

  1. (1) Use divisibility rules to show that $N$ is divisible by every integer from $1$ to $10$, and prime-factorise $N$.
  2. (2) How many positive divisors does $N$ have?
  3. (3) How many of them are odd?
  4. (4) How many of them are multiples of $6$? (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Divisibility Rules, Number of Divisors

Show hint

A multiple of $6$ needs at least one $2$ and at least one $3$.

Show solution
  1. (1) Last digit $0$: divisible by $2, 5, 10$. Last two digits $20$: by $4$. Last three $520 = 8 \cdot 65$: by $8$. Digit sum $9$: by $3, 9$, hence $6$. $2520 = 7 \cdot 360$: by $7$.
  2. $N = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7$.
  3. (2) $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 48$.
  4. (3) Odd: no factor $2$: $3 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 12$.
  5. (4) $2^{1, 2, 3}$, $3^{1, 2}$, $5^{0, 1}$, $7^{0, 1}$: $3 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 24$.

(1) $2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7$ (2) $48$ (3) $12$ (4) $24$

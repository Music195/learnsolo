Division, the Euclidean Algorithm and Congruences

Six problems on division with remainder, the Euclidean algorithm and congruences. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

$a$ leaves remainder $5$ and $b$ leaves remainder $7$ when divided by $9$. Find the remainder when $3a + 4b$ is divided by $9$.

Uses: Division with Remainder

Show hint

Write $a = 9k + 5$ and $b = 9l + 7$.

Show solution
  1. $3a + 4b = 27k + 15 + 36l + 28 = 9(3k + 4l) + 43$.
  2. $43 = 9 \cdot 4 + 7$: remainder $7$.

$7$

Problem 2

Use the Euclidean algorithm to find $\gcd(1001, 182)$, and then the least common multiple. (Type the gcd in the box.)

Uses: Euclidean Algorithm

Show hint

$\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$ where $r$ is the remainder of $a \div b$. Then $\mathrm{lcm} = \dfrac{ab}{\gcd}$.

Show solution
  1. $1001 = 182 \cdot 5 + 91$; $\;182 = 91 \cdot 2 + 0$. So $\gcd = 91$.
  2. $\mathrm{lcm} = \dfrac{1001 \cdot 182}{91} = 2002$.

$\gcd = 91$, $\mathrm{lcm} = 2002$

Problem 3

Find the remainder when $5^{100}$ is divided by $13$.

Uses: Congruences

Show hint

Look for a small power of $5$ that is $\equiv \pm 1 \pmod{13}$.

Show solution
  1. $5^2 = 25 \equiv -1 \pmod{13}$, so $5^4 \equiv 1$.
  2. $5^{100} = (5^4)^{25} \equiv 1$.

$1$

Problem 4

Show that $n^3 - n$ is divisible by $6$ for every integer $n$.

Uses: Division with Remainder

Show hint

$n^3 - n = (n - 1)n(n + 1)$. Look at the remainders of $n$ when divided by $2$ and by $3$.

Show solution
  1. $n^3 - n = (n - 1)n(n + 1)$, a product of three consecutive integers.
  2. Among two consecutive integers one is even, so the product is divisible by $2$.
  3. $n = 3k$, $3k + 1$ or $3k + 2$: then $n$, $n - 1$ or $n + 1$ is a multiple of $3$. So the product is divisible by $3$.
  4. $2$ and $3$ are coprime, so it is divisible by $6$.

Shown: $(n - 1)n(n + 1)$ always contains a multiple of $2$ and a multiple of $3$.

Problem 5

Find one pair of integers $(x, y)$ with $17x + 5y = 1$ using the Euclidean algorithm, then all such pairs. (Type the smallest positive $x$ in the box.)

Uses: Euclidean Algorithm, Congruences

Show hint

Run the algorithm on $17$ and $5$, then work backwards to write $1$ as a combination of $17$ and $5$.

Show solution
  1. $17 = 5 \cdot 3 + 2$, $\;5 = 2 \cdot 2 + 1$.
  2. $1 = 5 - 2 \cdot 2 = 5 - 2(17 - 5 \cdot 3) = 7 \cdot 5 - 2 \cdot 17$: $(x, y) = (-2, 7)$.
  3. All solutions: $17(x + 2) = -5(y - 7)$ with $\gcd(17, 5) = 1$, so $x = -2 + 5k$, $y = 7 - 17k$ ($k$ an integer).
  4. Smallest positive $x$: $k = 1$, $x = 3$ (then $y = -10$). Check with congruences: $17x \equiv 2x \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ gives $x \equiv 3$ ✓.

$(x, y) = (-2 + 5k, 7 - 17k)$; smallest positive $x = 3$

Problem 6

Congruences.

  1. (1) Find the remainder when $2^{50}$ is divided by $7$.
  2. (2) Find the last digit of $7^{2026}$. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) Find all integers $x$ with $0 < x < 40$, $x \equiv 2 \pmod 5$ and $x \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.

Uses: Congruences, Division with Remainder

Show hint

(2) The last digit is the remainder mod $10$; find the cycle of $7^n \bmod 10$. (3) List the numbers $\equiv 2 \pmod 5$ and test them.

Show solution
  1. (1) $2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$; $2^{50} = (2^3)^{16} \cdot 2^2 \equiv 4$.
  2. (2) $7^1 \equiv 7$, $7^2 \equiv 9$, $7^3 \equiv 3$, $7^4 \equiv 1 \pmod{10}$. $2026 = 4 \cdot 506 + 2$, so $7^{2026} \equiv 9$.
  3. (3) $x \equiv 2 \pmod 5$: $2, 7, 12, \ldots$; the first one $\equiv 3 \pmod 4$ is $7$. Both conditions together: $x \equiv 7 \pmod{20}$.
  4. $x = 7, 27$.

(1) $4$ (2) $9$ (3) $x = 7, 27$

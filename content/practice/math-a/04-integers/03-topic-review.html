Divisors, GCD and Remainders

Topic review: a mix of the whole topic, best tried after the two concept sets. Five problems on divisors, divisibility, the Euclidean algorithm and remainders. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

How many positive divisors does $72$ have?

Uses: Number of Divisors

Show hint

Factor into primes. If $N = p^a q^b$, it has $(a + 1)(b + 1)$ divisors.

Show solution
  1. $72 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2$.
  2. Each divisor is $2^i 3^j$ with $0 \le i \le 3$ and $0 \le j \le 2$: $4 \cdot 3 = 12$ choices.

$12$ divisors

Problem 2

$a$ is a digit, and the four-digit number $4a72$ is a multiple of $9$. Find $a$.

Uses: Divisibility Rules

Show hint

A number is a multiple of $9$ exactly when the sum of its digits is.

Show solution
  1. Digit sum: $4 + a + 7 + 2 = 13 + a$.
  2. $0 \le a \le 9$ gives $13 \le 13 + a \le 22$; the only multiple of $9$ is $18$, so $a = 5$.

$a = 5$ ($4572 = 9 \cdot 508$)

Problem 3

Find the greatest common divisor of $391$ and $299$.

Uses: Euclidean Algorithm

Show hint

Euclidean algorithm: $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$ where $r$ is the remainder of $a \div b$. Repeat until the remainder is $0$.

Show solution
  1. $391 = 299 \cdot 1 + 92$.
  2. $299 = 92 \cdot 3 + 23$.
  3. $92 = 23 \cdot 4 + 0$, so the GCD is $23$.

$\gcd(391, 299) = 23$ ($391 = 23 \cdot 17$, $299 = 23 \cdot 13$)

Problem 4

An integer $n$ leaves remainder $3$ when divided by $7$. Find the remainder when $n^2 + 2n$ is divided by $7$.

Uses: Congruence Rules, Integer Division

Show hint

$n \equiv 3 \pmod 7$, and you can add and multiply congruences.

Show solution
  1. $n^2 + 2n \equiv 3^2 + 2 \cdot 3 = 15 \pmod 7$.
  2. $15 = 7 \cdot 2 + 1$, so the remainder is $1$.

Remainder $1$

Problem 5

Find the remainder when $3^{100}$ is divided by $7$.

Uses: Congruence Rules

Show hint

Work out $3^1, 3^2, 3^3, \ldots \pmod 7$ until you reach $1$. Then the remainders repeat.

Show solution
  1. Mod $7$: $3^1 \equiv 3$, $3^2 \equiv 2$, $3^3 \equiv 6$, $3^4 \equiv 4$, $3^5 \equiv 5$, $3^6 \equiv 1$.
  2. $100 = 6 \cdot 16 + 4$, so $3^{100} = (3^6)^{16} \cdot 3^4 \equiv 1 \cdot 4 = 4$.

Remainder $4$

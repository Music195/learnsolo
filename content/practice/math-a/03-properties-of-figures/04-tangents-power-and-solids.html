Tangents, Power of a Point and Solids

Seven problems on equal tangent lengths, the tangent-chord angle, the power of a point, and polyhedra. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

The incircle of triangle ABC touches AB at D, BC at E and CA at F. $AB = 10$, $BC = 12$ and $CA = 8$. Find AD.

Uses: Equal Tangent Lengths

Show hint

Tangent lengths from one point are equal: $AD = AF$, $BD = BE$, $CE = CF$.

Show solution
  1. Let $AD = AF = x$. Then $BD = BE = 10 - x$ and $CF = CE = 8 - x$.
  2. $BE + CE = 12$: $18 - 2x = 12$, so $x = 3$.

$AD = 3$

Problem 2

From a point P outside a circle, a line meets the circle at A and then B, with $PA = 3$ and $AB = 9$. PT is a tangent from P. Find PT.

Uses: Power of a Point

Show hint

$PA \cdot PB = PT^2$.

Show solution
  1. $PB = 3 + 9 = 12$, so $PT^2 = 3 \cdot 12 = 36$ and $PT = 6$.

$PT = 6$

Problem 3

Triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle, and AT is the tangent at A. The angle between AT and AB, on the side away from C, is $70^\circ$, and $\angle ABC = 60^\circ$. Find $\angle ACB$ and $\angle BAC$. (Type $\angle BAC$ in the box.)

Uses: Tangent-Chord Angle

Show hint

The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle on the other side of the chord.

Show solution
  1. $\angle ACB = \angle BAT = 70^\circ$.
  2. $\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - 70^\circ = 50^\circ$.

$\angle ACB = 70^\circ$, $\angle BAC = 50^\circ$

Problem 4

A regular octahedron has $8$ triangular faces, with $4$ meeting at each corner. Find its number of edges $E$ and corners $V$, and check Euler's formula. Do the same for a regular dodecahedron ($12$ pentagons, $3$ at each corner). (Type $E$ for the octahedron in the box.)

Uses: Platonic Solids

Show hint

$E = \dfrac{pF}{2}$ and $V = \dfrac{pF}{q}$, where each face has $p$ edges and $q$ faces meet at a corner.

Show solution
  1. Octahedron: $E = \dfrac{3 \cdot 8}{2} = 12$, $V = \dfrac{3 \cdot 8}{4} = 6$; $6 - 12 + 8 = 2$ ✓.
  2. Dodecahedron: $E = \dfrac{5 \cdot 12}{2} = 30$, $V = \dfrac{5 \cdot 12}{3} = 20$; $20 - 30 + 12 = 2$ ✓.

Octahedron $E = 12$, $V = 6$; dodecahedron $E = 30$, $V = 20$

Problem 5

Tangents from P touch a circle with centre O and radius $2$ at A and B, and $\angle APB = 60^\circ$. Find PA, and $\angle ACB$ for a point C on the larger arc AB. (Type PA in the box.)

Uses: Equal Tangent Lengths, Tangent-Chord Angle

Show hint

OP bisects $\angle APB$, and $\angle OAP = 90^\circ$. For $\angle ACB$, triangle PAB is isosceles; use the tangent-chord angle.

Show solution
  1. $\angle APO = 30^\circ$ and $\angle OAP = 90^\circ$, so $PA = \dfrac{OA}{\tan 30^\circ} = 2\sqrt{3}$.
  2. $PA = PB$, so $\angle PAB = \angle PBA = \dfrac{180^\circ - 60^\circ}{2} = 60^\circ$.
  3. By the tangent-chord angle, $\angle ACB = \angle PAB = 60^\circ$.

$PA = 2\sqrt{3}$; $\angle ACB = 60^\circ$

Problem 6

Each corner of a cube is cut off so that the cut faces are triangles and each square face becomes a regular octagon (a truncated cube). Find its numbers of faces, edges and corners, and check Euler's formula. (Type the number of edges in the box.)

Uses: Platonic Solids

Show hint

Faces: $6$ octagons and $8$ triangles. Each edge is shared by $2$ faces, and $3$ faces meet at each corner.

Show solution
  1. $F = 6 + 8 = 14$.
  2. $E = \dfrac{6 \cdot 8 + 8 \cdot 3}{2} = \dfrac{72}{2} = 36$.
  3. $V = \dfrac{72}{3} = 24$ (equivalently, $8$ corners of the cube $\times$ $3$ new corners each).
  4. $V - E + F = 24 - 36 + 14 = 2$ ✓.

$F = 14$, $E = 36$, $V = 24$

Problem 7

Two circles meet at A and B. P is a point on line AB, outside segment AB. PS is a tangent to the first circle and PT a tangent to the second circle (S, T the points of contact).

  1. (1) Show that $PS = PT$.
  2. (2) If $PA = 2$ and $AB = 6$, find PS. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) A line through P meets the first circle at C and D, with $PC = 1$. Find CD.

Uses: Power of a Point

Show hint

Use the power of P with respect to each circle; line PAB is a secant of both.

Show solution
  1. (1) First circle: $PS^2 = PA \cdot PB$. Second circle: $PT^2 = PA \cdot PB$. So $PS = PT$.
  2. (2) $PB = 8$: $PS^2 = 2 \cdot 8 = 16$, $PS = 4$.
  3. (3) $PC \cdot PD = PS^2 = 16$, so $PD = 16$ and $CD = 15$.

(1) both equal $\sqrt{PA \cdot PB}$ (2) $4$ (3) $15$

So line AB is where the tangent lengths to the two circles are equal (the radical axis).

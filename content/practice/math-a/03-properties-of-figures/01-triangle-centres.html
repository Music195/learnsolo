Angle Bisectors and Triangle Centres

Six problems on the interior and exterior angle bisector theorems, the circumcenter, the incenter and the centroid. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

In triangle ABC, $AB = 10$, $AC = 6$ and $BC = 8$. The bisector of angle A meets BC at D. Find BD.

Uses: Angle Bisector Theorem

Show hint

$BD : DC = AB : AC$.

Show solution
  1. $BD : DC = 10 : 6 = 5 : 3$.
  2. $BD = 8 \cdot \dfrac{5}{8} = 5$.

$BD = 5$

Problem 2

In triangle ABC the median AL has length $12$, and G is the centroid. The area of triangle ABC is $48$. Find AG and the area of triangle ABG. (Type the area in the box.)

Uses: Centroid and Medians

Show hint

G divides each median in the ratio $2 : 1$ from the vertex. The three medians cut the triangle into three triangles GAB, GBC, GCA of equal area.

Show solution
  1. $AG = \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 12 = 8$.
  2. Area of ABG $= \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 48 = 16$.

$AG = 8$; area $16$

Problem 3

In triangle ABC, $AB = 8$, $AC = 5$ and $BC = 6$. The bisector of the exterior angle at A meets line BC at E. Find CE. Also find BD, where D is the foot of the interior bisector. (Type CE in the box.)

Uses: External Angle Bisector Theorem, Angle Bisector Theorem

Show hint

$BE : EC = AB : AC = 8 : 5$, with E outside segment BC on the side of C (because $AB > AC$).

Show solution
  1. $BE : EC = 8 : 5$ and $BE = BC + CE = 6 + CE$.
  2. $5(6 + CE) = 8CE$, so $CE = 10$.
  3. Interior: $BD = 6 \cdot \dfrac{8}{13} = \dfrac{48}{13}$.

$CE = 10$; $BD = \dfrac{48}{13}$

Problem 4

In triangle ABC, I is the incenter and O the circumcenter. $\angle BIC = 125^\circ$. Find $\angle A$ and $\angle BOC$. (Type $\angle A$ in the box.)

Uses: Incenter and Angle Bisectors, Circumcenter and Circumcircle

Show hint

$\angle BIC = 90^\circ + \dfrac{\angle A}{2}$, and $\angle BOC$ is the central angle on arc BC.

Show solution
  1. $90^\circ + \dfrac{\angle A}{2} = 125^\circ$, so $\angle A = 70^\circ$.
  2. $\angle BOC = 2\angle A = 140^\circ$ (A is acute, so O and A are on the same side of BC).

$\angle A = 70^\circ$, $\angle BOC = 140^\circ$

Problem 5

In triangle ABC, $AB = 7$, $AC = 5$ and $BC = 8$. The bisectors of the interior and exterior angles at A meet line BC at D and E. Find DE, and show that $\angle DAE = 90^\circ$.

Uses: Angle Bisector Theorem, External Angle Bisector Theorem

Show hint

Both D and E divide BC in the ratio $7 : 5$, one inside and one outside. The two bisectors split two angles that add up to $180^\circ$.

Show solution
  1. $BD = 8 \cdot \dfrac{7}{12} = \dfrac{14}{3}$, $DC = \dfrac{10}{3}$.
  2. $BE : EC = 7 : 5$ with E beyond C: $5(8 + CE) = 7CE$, so $CE = 20$.
  3. $DE = DC + CE = \dfrac{10}{3} + 20 = \dfrac{70}{3}$.
  4. The interior and exterior angles at A add up to $180^\circ$, so their halves add up to $90^\circ$: $\angle DAE = 90^\circ$.

$DE = \dfrac{70}{3}$; $\angle DAE = 90^\circ$

Problem 6

Triangle ABC has $\angle C = 90^\circ$, $CA = 5$ and $CB = 12$.

  1. (1) Find the circumradius $R$ and the position of the circumcenter O.
  2. (2) Find the inradius $r$.
  3. (3) G is the centroid. Find the distance OG. (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Circumcenter and Circumcircle, Incenter and Angle Bisectors, Centroid and Medians

Show hint

For a right triangle the circumcenter is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. For (3), CO is a median, and G lies on it.

Show solution
  1. (1) $AB = 13$; O is the midpoint of AB and $R = \dfrac{13}{2}$.
  2. (2) Area $30 = \dfrac{1}{2}r(5 + 12 + 13)$, so $r = 2$.
  3. (3) CO is the median from C, with $CO = R = \dfrac{13}{2}$. $CG : GO = 2 : 1$, so $OG = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{13}{2} = \dfrac{13}{6}$.

(1) $R = \dfrac{13}{2}$, O is the midpoint of AB (2) $r = 2$ (3) $\dfrac{13}{6}$

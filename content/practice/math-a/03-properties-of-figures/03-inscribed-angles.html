Inscribed Angles and Cyclic Quadrilaterals

Six problems on the inscribed angle theorem and its converse, and on cyclic quadrilaterals and how to recognise them. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle with $\angle ABC = 35^\circ$. Find $\angle ACB$ and $\angle CAB$. (Type $\angle CAB$ in the box.)

Uses: Inscribed Angle Theorem

Show hint

An angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

Show solution
  1. $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$ (angle on a diameter).
  2. $\angle CAB = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$.

$\angle ACB = 90^\circ$, $\angle CAB = 55^\circ$

Problem 2

ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral with $\angle A = 2\angle C$ and $\angle B = 85^\circ$. Find $\angle A$ and $\angle D$. (Type $\angle A$ in the box.)

Uses: Cyclic Quadrilaterals

Show hint

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$.

Show solution
  1. $\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ$ and $\angle A = 2\angle C$: $\angle C = 60^\circ$, $\angle A = 120^\circ$.
  2. $\angle D = 180^\circ - 85^\circ = 95^\circ$.

$\angle A = 120^\circ$, $\angle D = 95^\circ$

Problem 3

In quadrilateral ABCD (A and D on the same side of BC), $\angle BAC = \angle BDC = 35^\circ$ and $\angle ABD = 45^\circ$. Find $\angle ACD$.

Uses: Converse of the Inscribed Angle Theorem, Inscribed Angle Theorem

Show hint

Equal angles on the same side of BC mean A, B, C, D lie on one circle.

Show solution
  1. $\angle BAC = \angle BDC$ with A, D on the same side of BC, so A, B, C, D are on one circle.
  2. Then $\angle ACD$ and $\angle ABD$ stand on the same arc AD: $\angle ACD = 45^\circ$.

$45^\circ$

Problem 4

Quadrilateral ABCD has $\angle A = 75^\circ$, $\angle B = 100^\circ$ and $\angle C = 105^\circ$. Find $\angle D$, and decide whether ABCD is cyclic.

Uses: Condition for a Cyclic Quadrilateral

Show hint

The angles of a quadrilateral add up to $360^\circ$. A quadrilateral is cyclic if a pair of opposite angles adds up to $180^\circ$.

Show solution
  1. $\angle D = 360^\circ - 75^\circ - 100^\circ - 105^\circ = 80^\circ$.
  2. $\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ$, so ABCD is cyclic (and indeed $\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ$ too).

$\angle D = 80^\circ$; cyclic

Problem 5

Chords AC and BD of a circle cross at P inside the circle. The arc AB (not containing C, D) has central angle $80^\circ$, and the arc CD (not containing A, B) has central angle $40^\circ$. Find $\angle APB$.

Uses: Inscribed Angle Theorem

Show hint

Join B and C. $\angle APB$ is an exterior angle of triangle PBC.

Show solution
  1. $\angle ACB = \dfrac{80^\circ}{2} = 40^\circ$ (inscribed angle on arc AB).
  2. $\angle DBC = \dfrac{40^\circ}{2} = 20^\circ$ (inscribed angle on arc CD).
  3. $\angle APB$ is an exterior angle of triangle PBC: $40^\circ + 20^\circ = 60^\circ$.

$60^\circ$

In general, the angle between two chords is half the sum of the two arcs they cut off.

Problem 6

In acute triangle ABC, the altitudes BE (E on CA) and CF (F on AB) meet at H. $\angle A = 64^\circ$ and $\angle ABC = 70^\circ$.

  1. (1) Show that B, C, E, F lie on one circle.
  2. (2) Show that A, F, H, E lie on one circle.
  3. (3) Find $\angle BHC$. (Type it in the box.)
  4. (4) Find $\angle AEF$.

Uses: Converse of the Inscribed Angle Theorem, Condition for a Cyclic Quadrilateral, Cyclic Quadrilaterals

Show hint

(1) $\angle BEC = \angle BFC = 90^\circ$. (2) Look at $\angle AFH + \angle AEH$. (4) In the cyclic quadrilateral BCEF, an exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\angle BFC = \angle BEC = 90^\circ$, with E, F on the same side of BC: B, C, E, F are on one circle (with diameter BC).
  2. (2) $\angle AFH + \angle AEH = 90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$, so AFHE is cyclic.
  3. (3) $\angle FHE = 180^\circ - \angle A = 116^\circ$, and $\angle BHC = \angle FHE$ (vertical angles): $116^\circ$.
  4. (4) $\angle AEF$ is the exterior angle of BCEF at E, so it equals the opposite angle $\angle FBC = \angle ABC = 70^\circ$.

(3) $116^\circ$ (4) $70^\circ$

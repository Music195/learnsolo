Ceva, Menelaus and Triangle Inequalities

Six problems on Ceva's theorem, Menelaus' theorem, the triangle inequality, and the relation between sides and angles. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

In triangle ABC, AD, BE and CF meet at one point. $AF : FB = 3 : 2$ and $BD : DC = 1 : 3$. Find $CE : EA$. (Type $\dfrac{CE}{EA}$ in the box.)

Uses: Ceva's Theorem

Show hint

$\dfrac{AF}{FB} \cdot \dfrac{BD}{DC} \cdot \dfrac{CE}{EA} = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{3}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{CE}{EA} = 1$, so $\dfrac{CE}{EA} = 2$.
  2. $CE : EA = 2 : 1$.

$CE : EA = 2 : 1$

Problem 2

Two sides of a triangle are $4$ and $9$. Find the possible lengths $x$ of the third side. How many of them are whole numbers?

Uses: Triangle Inequality

Show hint

$|b - c| < a < b + c$.

Show solution
  1. $9 - 4 < x < 9 + 4$, so $5 < x < 13$.
  2. Whole numbers: $6, 7, \ldots, 12$: $7$ of them.

$5 < x < 13$; $7$ whole numbers

Problem 3

In triangle ABC, F is on AB with $AF : FB = 2 : 1$, and D is on BC with $BD : DC = 3 : 2$. AD and CF meet at P. Find $AP : PD$. (Type $\dfrac{AP}{PD}$ in the box.)

Uses: Menelaus' Theorem

Show hint

Use Menelaus on triangle ABD with the line through F, P and C.

Show solution
  1. Line FPC cuts triangle ABD: $\dfrac{AF}{FB} \cdot \dfrac{BC}{CD} \cdot \dfrac{DP}{PA} = 1$.
  2. $2 \cdot \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot \dfrac{DP}{PA} = 1$, so $\dfrac{DP}{PA} = \dfrac{1}{5}$.
  3. $AP : PD = 5 : 1$.

$AP : PD = 5 : 1$

Problem 4

(1) A triangle has sides $6$, $7$ and $9$. Which angle is largest and which is smallest? (2) In triangle ABC, $\angle A = 50^\circ$ and $\angle B = 65^\circ$. Put the sides in order of length.

Uses: Angles and Sides of a Triangle

Show hint

The larger side is opposite the larger angle.

Show solution
  1. (1) The largest angle is opposite the side $9$, the smallest opposite the side $6$.
  2. (2) $\angle C = 65^\circ$. So $\angle A < \angle B = \angle C$, and $BC < CA = AB$.

(1) largest opposite $9$, smallest opposite $6$ (2) $BC < CA = AB$

Problem 5

In triangle ABC, D is on BC with $BD : DC = 1 : 2$, and E is on CA with $CE : EA = 3 : 1$. AD and BE meet at P, and CP meets AB at F. Find $AF : FB$ and $AP : PD$. (Type $\dfrac{AF}{FB}$ in the box.)

Uses: Ceva's Theorem, Menelaus' Theorem

Show hint

Ceva gives $AF : FB$. For $AP : PD$, use Menelaus on triangle ADC with the line through B, P and E.

Show solution
  1. Ceva: $\dfrac{AF}{FB} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{1} = 1$, so $\dfrac{AF}{FB} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
  2. Menelaus on ADC with line BPE: $\dfrac{CB}{BD} \cdot \dfrac{DP}{PA} \cdot \dfrac{AE}{EC} = 1$.
  3. $3 \cdot \dfrac{DP}{PA} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = 1$, so $AP : PD = 1 : 1$.

$AF : FB = 2 : 3$; $AP : PD = 1 : 1$

Problem 6

The three lengths $2x - 1$, $x + 2$ and $8 - x$ are the sides of a triangle.

  1. (1) Find the range of $x$.
  2. (2) How many integers $x$ are possible? (Type the number in the box.)
  3. (3) For which integers $x$ is $2x - 1$ the longest side?
  4. (4) When $x = 4$, which angle of the triangle is the largest?

Uses: Triangle Inequality, Angles and Sides of a Triangle

Show hint

Each side must be less than the sum of the other two. (All three lengths must also be positive.)

Show solution
  1. (1) $(2x - 1) + (x + 2) > 8 - x \iff x > \dfrac{7}{4}$.
  2. $(x + 2) + (8 - x) > 2x - 1 \iff x < \dfrac{11}{2}$.
  3. $(2x - 1) + (8 - x) > x + 2 \iff 7 > 2$: always true. So $\dfrac{7}{4} < x < \dfrac{11}{2}$ (all lengths are then positive).
  4. (2) $x = 2, 3, 4, 5$: $4$ integers.
  5. (3) $2x - 1 > x + 2 \iff x > 3$ and $2x - 1 > 8 - x \iff x > 3$: $x = 4, 5$.
  6. (4) Sides $7$, $6$, $4$: the largest angle is opposite the side $2x - 1 = 7$.

(1) $\dfrac{7}{4} < x < \dfrac{11}{2}$ (2) $4$ (3) $x = 4, 5$ (4) the angle opposite the side $7$

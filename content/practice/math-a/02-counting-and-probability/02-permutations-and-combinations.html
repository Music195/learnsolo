Permutations and Combinations

Six problems on permutations, circular permutations, permutations with repetition, combinations and arrangements with identical objects. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

(1) How many 4-digit numbers can be made from the digits $0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ without repeating a digit? (2) In how many ways can $5$ people sit at a round table? (Type (1) in the box.)

Uses: Permutations, Circular Permutations

Show hint

(1) The first digit can't be $0$. (2) Rotations of the same seating count once: $(n - 1)!$.

Show solution
  1. (1) First digit: $5$ choices; then ${}_5\mathrm{P}_3 = 60$ for the rest. $5 \times 60 = 300$.
  2. (2) $(5 - 1)! = 24$.

(1) $300$ (2) $24$

Problem 2

From $7$ boys and $5$ girls, $3$ people are chosen. In how many ways does the group include at least one girl?

Uses: Combinations

Show hint

All groups minus the all-boy groups.

Show solution
  1. All: ${}_{12}\mathrm{C}_3 = 220$. All boys: ${}_7\mathrm{C}_3 = 35$.
  2. $220 - 35 = 185$.

$185$

Problem 3

$7$ people, including A and B, sit at a round table. In how many ways do A and B sit next to each other?

Uses: Circular Permutations

Show hint

Tie A and B together as one unit. Then arrange $6$ units in a circle, and remember A and B can swap.

Show solution
  1. $6$ units around a table: $(6 - 1)! = 120$.
  2. A and B in either order: $\times 2$, so $240$.

$240$

Problem 4

$6$ people are put into three rooms A, B, C. In how many ways can this be done if every room gets at least one person?

Uses: Permutations with Repetition

Show hint

Without the condition each person has $3$ choices. Then remove the cases with an empty room.

Show solution
  1. All: $3^6 = 729$.
  2. Exactly one given room empty: everyone in the other two rooms, but not all in one: $2^6 - 2 = 62$; there are $3$ choices of empty room: $186$.
  3. Two rooms empty: $3$ ways.
  4. $729 - 186 - 3 = 540$.

$540$

Problem 5

On a grid you walk from A$(0, 0)$ to B$(6, 4)$, moving one block right or up each time. How many shortest routes are there, and how many of them do not pass through C$(3, 2)$? (Type the second number in the box.)

Uses: Permutations with Identical Objects, Combinations

Show hint

A shortest route is an arrangement of $6$ R's and $4$ U's. Routes through C: (A to C) $\times$ (C to B).

Show solution
  1. All routes: $\dfrac{10!}{6!\,4!} = {}_{10}\mathrm{C}_4 = 210$.
  2. Through C: A to C is ${}_5\mathrm{C}_2 = 10$, C to B is ${}_5\mathrm{C}_2 = 10$: $100$.
  3. Not through C: $210 - 100 = 110$.

$210$ routes; $110$ avoid C

Problem 6

The six letters of BANANA.

  1. (1) How many different arrangements of the six letters are there?
  2. (2) In how many of them are the two N's not next to each other? (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) How many 4-letter strings can be made from the letters B, A, N if repeats are allowed? How many of them use all three letters?

Uses: Permutations with Identical Objects, Permutations, Permutations with Repetition

Show hint

(2) Count the arrangements with NN together (treat NN as one letter) and subtract. (3) Use the complement: strings that miss a letter.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{6!}{3!\,2!\,1!} = 60$.
  2. (2) NN as one block: arrange B, NN, A, A, A: $\dfrac{5!}{3!} = 20$. Not adjacent: $60 - 20 = 40$.
  3. (3) $3^4 = 81$ strings. Using at most two given letters: $2^4 = 16$, for $3$ choices of pair, but each single-letter string is counted twice: $3 \cdot 16 - 3 = 45$.
  4. Using all three: $81 - 45 = 36$.

(1) $60$ (2) $40$ (3) $81$; $36$

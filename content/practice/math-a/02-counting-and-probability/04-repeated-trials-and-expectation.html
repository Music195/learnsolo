Repeated Trials, Conditional Probability and Expectation

Six problems on repeated trials, conditional probability and the multiplication rule, and expected value. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

A fair coin is tossed $6$ times. Find the probability of exactly $4$ heads.

Uses: Repeated Trials

Show hint

${}_n\mathrm{C}_r\,p^r(1 - p)^{n - r}$.

Show solution
  1. ${}_6\mathrm{C}_4\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{15}{64}$.

$\dfrac{15}{64}$

Problem 2

There are $12$ lottery tickets and $4$ are winners. A draws one, then B draws one, without replacement. Find the probability that both win.

Uses: Multiplication Rule of Probability

Show hint

$P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P_A(B)$: after A wins, $3$ winners are left among $11$ tickets.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{4}{12} \times \dfrac{3}{11} = \dfrac{1}{11}$.

$\dfrac{1}{11}$

Problem 3

A die is rolled. If the number is odd you get $100$ times that number in yen; if it is even you get nothing. Find the expected amount.

Uses: Expected Value

Show hint

$E = x_1p_1 + x_2p_2 + \cdots$; each face has probability $\dfrac{1}{6}$.

Show solution
  1. $E = \dfrac{100 + 300 + 500 + 0 + 0 + 0}{6} = \dfrac{900}{6} = 150$ yen.

$150$ yen

Problem 4

A die is rolled $5$ times. Find the probability that a $1$ or a $2$ comes up at least $4$ times.

Uses: Repeated Trials

Show hint

Success probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$. 'At least $4$' means exactly $4$ or exactly $5$.

Show solution
  1. Exactly $4$: ${}_5\mathrm{C}_4\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^4 \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{10}{243}$.
  2. Exactly $5$: $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^5 = \dfrac{1}{243}$.
  3. Total: $\dfrac{11}{243}$.

$\dfrac{11}{243}$

Problem 5

$2\%$ of a population has a disease. A test is positive for $90\%$ of people with the disease and for $5\%$ of people without it. A person tests positive. Find the probability that they have the disease.

Uses: Multiplication Rule of Probability

Show hint

$P(\text{disease} \mid \text{positive}) = \dfrac{P(\text{disease and positive})}{P(\text{positive})}$.

Show solution
  1. $P(\text{disease and positive}) = 0.02 \times 0.9 = 0.018$.
  2. $P(\text{no disease and positive}) = 0.98 \times 0.05 = 0.049$.
  3. $P = \dfrac{0.018}{0.018 + 0.049} = \dfrac{18}{67} \approx 0.27$.

$\dfrac{18}{67}$

Only about $27\%$, because healthy people are so much more common that their false positives outnumber the true ones.

Problem 6

A and B play a series of games. A wins each game with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$, independently, and there are no draws. The first to win $3$ games wins the match.

  1. (1) Find the probability that A wins the match in exactly $3$ games, and in exactly $4$ games.
  2. (2) Find the probability that A wins the match. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the expected number of games played.

Uses: Repeated Trials, Expected Value

Show hint

To win in exactly $n$ games, A must win the $n$-th game and exactly $2$ of the first $n - 1$.

Show solution
  1. (1) In $3$: $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^3 = \dfrac{8}{27}$. In $4$: ${}_3\mathrm{C}_2\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{27}$.
  2. (2) In $5$: ${}_4\mathrm{C}_2\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{16}{81}$. Total: $\dfrac{24 + 24 + 16}{81} = \dfrac{64}{81}$.
  3. (3) $3$ games: $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^3 + \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^3 = \dfrac{9}{27}$. $4$ games: $\dfrac{8}{27} + {}_3\mathrm{C}_2\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{8}{27} + \dfrac{2}{27} = \dfrac{10}{27}$. $5$ games: $1 - \dfrac{9}{27} - \dfrac{10}{27} = \dfrac{8}{27}$.
  4. $E = 3 \cdot \dfrac{9}{27} + 4 \cdot \dfrac{10}{27} + 5 \cdot \dfrac{8}{27} = \dfrac{107}{27}$.

(1) $\dfrac{8}{27}$, $\dfrac{8}{27}$ (2) $\dfrac{64}{81}$ (3) $\dfrac{107}{27}$

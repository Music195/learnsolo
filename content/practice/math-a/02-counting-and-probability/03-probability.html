Probability

Six problems on the probability of an event, the addition rule, complementary events and independent trials. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the sum is $9$.

Uses: Probability of an Event

Show hint

There are $36$ equally likely outcomes.

Show solution
  1. Sum $9$: $(3, 6), (4, 5), (5, 4), (6, 3)$: $4$ outcomes.
  2. $\dfrac{4}{36} = \dfrac{1}{9}$.

$\dfrac{1}{9}$

Problem 2

A bag has $4$ red and $6$ white balls. Three balls are taken out together. Find the probability that exactly two are red.

Uses: Probability of an Event

Show hint

Count with combinations: choose $2$ of the red and $1$ of the white.

Show solution
  1. All: ${}_{10}\mathrm{C}_3 = 120$.
  2. Exactly two red: ${}_4\mathrm{C}_2 \times {}_6\mathrm{C}_1 = 6 \times 6 = 36$.
  3. $\dfrac{36}{120} = \dfrac{3}{10}$.

$\dfrac{3}{10}$

Problem 3

One card is drawn from $30$ cards numbered $1$ to $30$. Find the probability that its number is a multiple of $3$ or of $4$.

Uses: Properties of Probability

Show hint

$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$.

Show solution
  1. Multiples of $3$: $10$. Of $4$: $7$. Of $12$: $2$.
  2. $P = \dfrac{10 + 7 - 2}{30} = \dfrac{15}{30} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$\dfrac{1}{2}$

Problem 4

From $6$ boys and $4$ girls, $3$ people are chosen at random. Find the probability that at least one boy is chosen.

Uses: Complementary Events

Show hint

The complement of 'at least one boy' is 'all girls'.

Show solution
  1. $P(\text{all girls}) = \dfrac{{}_4\mathrm{C}_3}{{}_{10}\mathrm{C}_3} = \dfrac{4}{120} = \dfrac{1}{30}$.
  2. $P(\text{at least one boy}) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{30} = \dfrac{29}{30}$.

$\dfrac{29}{30}$

Problem 5

A, B and C each shoot once at a target and hit it with probabilities $\dfrac{2}{3}$, $\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, independently. Find the probability that at least one hits, and that exactly two hit. (Type the second in the box.)

Uses: Independent Trials, Complementary Events

Show hint

Independent: multiply. 'Exactly two' has three cases, depending on who misses.

Show solution
  1. At least one: $1 - \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{24} = \dfrac{23}{24}$.
  2. C misses: $\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{6}{24}$. B misses: $\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{2}{24}$. A misses: $\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{24}$.
  3. Exactly two: $\dfrac{11}{24}$.

$\dfrac{23}{24}$; $\dfrac{11}{24}$

Problem 6

Bag P has $3$ red and $2$ white balls; bag Q has $2$ red and $3$ white balls. One ball is drawn from each bag.

  1. (1) Find the probability that both balls are red.
  2. (2) Find the probability that the two balls are the same colour.
  3. (3) Find the probability that at least one ball is red.
  4. (4) Let $A$ be 'the ball from P is red' and $B$ 'the two balls are the same colour'. Find $P(A \cup B)$. (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Independent Trials, Properties of Probability, Complementary Events

Show hint

The two draws are independent. In (4), $A \cap B$ is 'both red'.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{6}{25}$.
  2. (2) Both white: $\dfrac{2}{5} \cdot \dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{6}{25}$. Same colour: $\dfrac{12}{25}$.
  3. (3) $1 - P(\text{both white}) = 1 - \dfrac{6}{25} = \dfrac{19}{25}$.
  4. (4) $P(A \cup B) = \dfrac{3}{5} + \dfrac{12}{25} - \dfrac{6}{25} = \dfrac{21}{25}$.

(1) $\dfrac{6}{25}$ (2) $\dfrac{12}{25}$ (3) $\dfrac{19}{25}$ (4) $\dfrac{21}{25}$

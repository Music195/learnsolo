Counting Rules

Six problems on counting with unions and complements and on the addition and multiplication rules. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

How many integers from $1$ to $200$ are divisible by $4$ or $6$?

Uses: Counting Unions and Complements

Show hint

$n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$. Divisible by both $4$ and $6$ means divisible by $12$.

Show solution
  1. Multiples of $4$: $50$. Multiples of $6$: $33$. Multiples of $12$: $16$.
  2. $50 + 33 - 16 = 67$.

$67$

Problem 2

Two dice are rolled. In how many ways is the sum $4$ or less?

Uses: Addition Rule

Show hint

Count the cases sum $= 2$, $3$, $4$ separately; they can't happen together, so add.

Show solution
  1. Sum $2$: $1$ way. Sum $3$: $2$ ways. Sum $4$: $3$ ways.
  2. $1 + 2 + 3 = 6$.

$6$

Problem 3

How many positive divisors does $360$ have? Find also their sum. (Type the number of divisors in the box.)

Uses: Multiplication Rule

Show hint

$360 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$. A divisor is $2^a3^b5^c$: choose $a$, then $b$, then $c$.

Show solution
  1. $a \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, $b \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, $c \in \{0, 1\}$: $4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24$ divisors.
  2. Sum: $(1 + 2 + 4 + 8)(1 + 3 + 9)(1 + 5) = 15 \cdot 13 \cdot 6 = 1170$.

$24$ divisors, sum $1170$

Problem 4

How many 3-digit numbers have at least one digit equal to $7$?

Uses: Multiplication Rule, Counting Unions and Complements

Show hint

Count the complement: 3-digit numbers with no $7$ at all.

Show solution
  1. All 3-digit numbers: $900$.
  2. No $7$: first digit $1$–$9$ except $7$ ($8$ ways), other digits $0$–$9$ except $7$ ($9$ ways each): $8 \cdot 9 \cdot 9 = 648$.
  3. At least one $7$: $900 - 648 = 252$.

$252$

Problem 5

How many integers from $1$ to $300$ are divisible by none of $2$, $3$ and $5$?

Uses: Counting Unions and Complements

Show hint

Find $n(A \cup B \cup C)$ with the three-set formula, then take the complement.

Show solution
  1. $n(A) = 150$, $n(B) = 100$, $n(C) = 60$; $n(A \cap B) = 50$, $n(B \cap C) = 20$, $n(C \cap A) = 30$; $n(A \cap B \cap C) = 10$.
  2. $n(A \cup B \cup C) = 310 - 100 + 10 = 220$.
  3. None: $300 - 220 = 80$.

$80$

Check: in every block of $30$ numbers, $8$ are coprime to $30$, and $300 = 10 \times 30$ gives $80$.

Problem 6

Two dice, one large and one small, are rolled.

  1. (1) In how many ways is the product a multiple of $3$?
  2. (2) In how many ways is the sum a multiple of $4$?
  3. (3) In how many ways is the sum a multiple of $4$ and the product a multiple of $3$? (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Addition Rule, Multiplication Rule, Counting Unions and Complements

Show hint

(1) Use the complement: neither die shows $3$ or $6$. (2) and (3): list the cases for sum $4$, $8$, $12$.

Show solution
  1. (1) All: $6 \times 6 = 36$. Neither die is $3$ or $6$: $4 \times 4 = 16$. So $36 - 16 = 20$.
  2. (2) Sum $4$: $3$ ways. Sum $8$: $5$ ways. Sum $12$: $1$ way. Total $9$.
  3. (3) Sum $4$: $(1, 3), (3, 1)$. Sum $8$: $(2, 6), (3, 5), (5, 3), (6, 2)$. Sum $12$: $(6, 6)$. Total $7$.

(1) $20$ (2) $9$ (3) $7$

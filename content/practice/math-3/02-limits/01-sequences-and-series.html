Limits of Sequences and Infinite Series

Seven problems on limits of sequences, the squeeze theorem, the limit of rⁿ, geometric series and infinite series. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{4n^2 - 3n}{2n^2 + 5}$.

Uses: Convergence and Divergence of Sequences, Limit Laws for Sequences

Show hint

Divide the numerator and the denominator by the highest power, $n^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{4 - \frac{3}{n}}{2 + \frac{5}{n^2}} \to \dfrac{4 - 0}{2 + 0} = 2$.

$2$

Problem 2

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{3^{n+1} - 2^n}{3^n + 2^{n+1}}$.

Uses: Limit of rⁿ, Limit Laws for Sequences

Show hint

Divide everything by $3^n$, the fastest-growing term.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{3 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n}{1 + 2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n}$.
  2. $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^n \to 0$ since $\left|\dfrac{2}{3}\right| < 1$, so the limit is $\dfrac{3}{1} = 3$.

$3$

Problem 3

Find (1) $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} n\left(\sqrt{n^2 + 1} - n\right)$ (2) $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^2\cos n}{n^3 + 1}$. (Type (1) in the box.)

Uses: Convergence and Divergence of Sequences, Squeeze Theorem

Show hint

(1) Multiply and divide by $\sqrt{n^2 + 1} + n$. (2) $-1 \le \cos n \le 1$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $n \cdot \dfrac{(n^2 + 1) - n^2}{\sqrt{n^2 + 1} + n} = \dfrac{n}{\sqrt{n^2 + 1} + n} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{n^2}} + 1} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. (2) $-\dfrac{n^2}{n^3 + 1} \le \dfrac{n^2\cos n}{n^3 + 1} \le \dfrac{n^2}{n^3 + 1}$, and both sides $\to 0$. By the squeeze theorem the limit is $0$.

(1) $\dfrac{1}{2}$ (2) $0$

Problem 4

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n + 2\sin n}{3n}$.

Uses: Squeeze Theorem, Limit Laws for Sequences

Show hint

$-2 \le 2\sin n \le 2$. Squeeze the expression between two sequences with the same limit.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{n - 2}{3n} \le \dfrac{n + 2\sin n}{3n} \le \dfrac{n + 2}{3n}$.
  2. Both sides $\to \dfrac{1}{3}$, so the limit is $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

$\dfrac{1}{3}$

Problem 5

(1) Write $0.2\dot{4}\dot{5} = 0.2454545\ldots$ as a fraction. (2) Find $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{3}{4^n} - \frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\right)$. (Type (1) in the box.)

Uses: Geometric Series, Properties of Infinite Series

Show hint

(1) $0.2 + 0.045 + 0.00045 + \cdots$: after the first term it is geometric with ratio $\dfrac{1}{100}$. (2) Split into two convergent geometric series.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{2}{10} + \dfrac{0.045}{1 - \frac{1}{100}} = \dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{45}{990} = \dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{1}{22} = \dfrac{27}{110}$.
  2. (2) $\displaystyle\sum \frac{3}{4^n} = \frac{3/4}{1 - 1/4} = 1$ and $\displaystyle\sum \frac{1}{2^{n+1}} = \frac{1/4}{1 - 1/2} = \frac{1}{2}$.
  3. Both converge, so the sum is $1 - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

(1) $\dfrac{27}{110}$ (2) $\dfrac{1}{2}$

Problem 6

For which real $x$ ($x \ne -1$) does the series $x + \dfrac{x}{1 + x} + \dfrac{x}{(1 + x)^2} + \cdots$ converge? Find its sum, and its value at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. (Type the value in the box.)

Uses: Geometric Series, Limit of rⁿ

Show hint

It is geometric with first term $x$ and ratio $\dfrac{1}{1 + x}$. Don't forget the case where the first term is $0$.

Show solution
  1. $x = 0$: every term is $0$, so it converges to $0$.
  2. $x \ne 0$: converges $\iff \left|\dfrac{1}{1 + x}\right| < 1 \iff |1 + x| > 1 \iff x < -2$ or $x > 0$.
  3. Sum: $\dfrac{x}{1 - \frac{1}{1 + x}} = \dfrac{x(1 + x)}{x} = 1 + x$.
  4. At $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$: $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

Converges for $x < -2$, $x = 0$ or $x > 0$; sum $1 + x$ ($x \ne 0$) and $0$ ($x = 0$); $\dfrac{3}{2}$

Problem 7

Infinite series.

  1. (1) Find $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2n - 1)(2n + 1)}$.
  2. (2) Find $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left\{\frac{2}{3^n} - \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^n\right\}$. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) Does $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n + 1}{3n}$ converge?

Uses: Properties of Infinite Series, Geometric Series

Show hint

(1) Split into partial fractions and look at the partial sum $S_n$. (3) Look at the limit of the terms.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{1}{(2n - 1)(2n + 1)} = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{2n - 1} - \dfrac{1}{2n + 1}\right)$, so $S_n = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{2n + 1}\right) \to \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. (2) $\displaystyle\sum \frac{2}{3^n} = \frac{2/3}{1 - 1/3} = 1$, $\displaystyle\sum \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^n = \frac{-1/2}{1 + 1/2} = -\frac{1}{3}$. Sum $= 1 + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.
  3. (3) $\dfrac{n + 1}{3n} \to \dfrac{1}{3} \ne 0$, so the series diverges.

(1) $\dfrac{1}{2}$ (2) $\dfrac{4}{3}$ (3) diverges

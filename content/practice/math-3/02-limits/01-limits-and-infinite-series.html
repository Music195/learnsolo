Limits and Infinite Series

Five problems on limits of sequences, infinite series and a trigonometric limit. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \dfrac{3n^2 + n}{n^2 - 2}$.

Uses: Properties of Limits of Sequences 1

Show hint

Divide the top and bottom by the highest power, $n^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{3 + \frac{1}{n}}{1 - \frac{2}{n^2}} \to \dfrac{3 + 0}{1 - 0} = 3$.

$3$

Problem 2

Find the sum of the infinite series $1 + \dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{9} + \dfrac{1}{27} + \cdots$.

Uses: Convergence of Geometric Series

Show hint

A geometric series with $|r| < 1$ converges to $\dfrac{a}{1 - r}$.

Show solution
  1. $a = 1$, $r = \dfrac{1}{3}$, and $|r| < 1$, so it converges.
  2. Sum $= \dfrac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

$\dfrac{3}{2}$

Problem 3

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\sqrt{n^2 + n} - n\right)$.

Uses: Properties of Limits of Sequences 2

Show hint

This is $\infty - \infty$. Multiply and divide by $\sqrt{n^2 + n} + n$.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{n^2 + n} - n = \dfrac{(n^2 + n) - n^2}{\sqrt{n^2 + n} + n} = \dfrac{n}{\sqrt{n^2 + n} + n}$.
  2. Divide by $n$: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{n}} + 1} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$\dfrac{1}{2}$

Problem 4

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{x^2}$.

Uses: Limit of sin x / x

Show hint

$1 - \cos 2x = 2\sin^2 x$. Then use $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin x}{x} = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{x^2} = \dfrac{2\sin^2 x}{x^2} = 2\left(\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2$.
  2. $\to 2 \cdot 1^2 = 2$.

$2$

Problem 5

Find the sum of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \dfrac{1}{n(n + 2)}$.

Uses: Properties of Infinite Series, Convergence of Sequences

Show hint

Split into partial fractions: $\dfrac{1}{n(n + 2)} = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n + 2}\right)$. Find the partial sum $S_n$ first, then its limit.

Show solution
  1. $S_n = \dfrac{1}{2}\left\{\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{3}\right) + \left(\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{4}\right) + \cdots + \left(\dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n + 2}\right)\right\}$.
  2. Almost everything cancels: $S_n = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{n + 1} - \dfrac{1}{n + 2}\right)$.
  3. $S_n \to \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

$\dfrac{3}{4}$

Don't split an infinite series into two separate series here: $\sum \frac{1}{n}$ and $\sum \frac{1}{n + 2}$ both diverge. Work with the partial sum.

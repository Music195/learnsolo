Definite Integrals

Seven problems on definite integrals and their properties, substitution, even and odd functions, integration by parts and differentiating an integral. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

(1) Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^4 \left(\sqrt{x} + \frac{1}{x^2}\right)dx$. (2) Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}\displaystyle\int_1^x \sqrt{t^3 + 1}\,dt$ at $x = 2$. (Type (1) in the box.)

Uses: Definite Integrals, Differentiating a Definite Integral

Show hint

(1) $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$, $\dfrac{1}{x^2} = x^{-2}$. (2) $\dfrac{d}{dx}\displaystyle\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\left[\dfrac{2}{3}x^{\frac{3}{2}} - \dfrac{1}{x}\right]_1^4 = \left(\dfrac{16}{3} - \dfrac{1}{4}\right) - \left(\dfrac{2}{3} - 1\right) = \dfrac{65}{12}$.
  2. (2) The derivative is $\sqrt{x^3 + 1}$, which is $\sqrt{9} = 3$ at $x = 2$.

(1) $\dfrac{65}{12}$ (2) $3$

Problem 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^3 |x^2 - 1|\,dx$.

Uses: Properties of Definite Integrals, Definite Integrals

Show hint

$x^2 - 1 < 0$ for $0 \le x < 1$ and $\ge 0$ for $x \ge 1$. Split the interval at $x = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (1 - x^2)\,dx = \frac{2}{3}$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_1^3 (x^2 - 1)\,dx = (9 - 3) - \left(\frac{1}{3} - 1\right) = \frac{20}{3}$.
  3. Total: $\dfrac{22}{3}$.

$\dfrac{22}{3}$

Problem 3

Evaluate (1) $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin x\cos^2 x\,dx$ (2) $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2} \sqrt{4 - x^2}\,dx$. (Type (1) in the box.)

Uses: Definite Integral by Substitution, Even and Odd Functions

Show hint

(1) $u = \cos x$; change the limits too. (2) The integrand is even; then put $x = 2\sin\theta$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $u = \cos x$, $du = -\sin x\,dx$; $x: 0 \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ gives $u: 1 \to 0$. $\displaystyle\int_1^0 u^2(-du) = \int_0^1 u^2\,du = \frac{1}{3}$.
  2. (2) Even: $2\displaystyle\int_0^2 \sqrt{4 - x^2}\,dx$. With $x = 2\sin\theta$ ($0 \le \theta \le \dfrac{\pi}{2}$): $2\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} 4\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = 8 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} = 2\pi$.
  3. Check: it is half the area of a circle of radius $2$ ✓.

(1) $\dfrac{1}{3}$ (2) $2\pi$

Problem 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2} (x^3 - 4x + |x|)\,dx$.

Uses: Even and Odd Functions, Properties of Definite Integrals

Show hint

Split into the odd part $x^3 - 4x$ and the even part $|x|$.

Show solution
  1. $x^3 - 4x$ is odd: its integral over $[-2, 2]$ is $0$.
  2. $|x|$ is even: $2\displaystyle\int_0^2 x\,dx = 2 \cdot 2 = 4$.
  3. Total: $4$.

$4$

Problem 5

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 x\sqrt{1 - x}\,dx$.

Uses: Definite Integral by Substitution

Show hint

Put $t = 1 - x$, so $x = 1 - t$ and $dx = -dt$.

Show solution
  1. $x: 0 \to 1$ gives $t: 1 \to 0$: $\displaystyle\int_1^0 (1 - t)\sqrt{t}\,(-dt) = \int_0^1 \left(t^{\frac{1}{2}} - t^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)dt$.
  2. $= \dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{4}{15}$.

$\dfrac{4}{15}$

Problem 6

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^e x^2\log x\,dx$.

Uses: Definite Integral by Parts

Show hint

Integrate $x^2$ and differentiate $\log x$.

Show solution
  1. $\left[\dfrac{x^3}{3}\log x\right]_1^e - \displaystyle\int_1^e \frac{x^3}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \frac{e^3}{3} - \left[\frac{x^3}{9}\right]_1^e$.
  2. $= \dfrac{e^3}{3} - \dfrac{e^3 - 1}{9} = \dfrac{2e^3 + 1}{9}$.

$\dfrac{2e^3 + 1}{9}$

Problem 7

Functions defined by integrals.

  1. (1) Find $f(x)$ such that $f(x) = x + \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} f(t)\sin t\,dt$.
  2. (2) $G(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x (t^2 - 3t + 2)\,dt$. Find $G'(x)$ and the local maximum value of $G$. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}\displaystyle\int_x^{2x} \cos t\,dt$.

Uses: Definite Integral by Parts, Differentiating a Definite Integral

Show hint

(1) The integral is a constant $A$: write $f(x) = x + A$ and substitute back. (3) Use both limits: $f(g(x))g'(x) - f(h(x))h'(x)$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $A = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} (t + A)\sin t\,dt = \int_0^{\pi} t\sin t\,dt + 2A$. By parts, $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} t\sin t\,dt = \Big[-t\cos t\Big]_0^{\pi} + \int_0^{\pi}\cos t\,dt = \pi$.
  2. So $A = \pi + 2A$, $A = -\pi$, and $f(x) = x - \pi$.
  3. (2) $G'(x) = x^2 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)(x - 2)$: $+$, $-$, $+$, so the local maximum is at $x = 1$: $G(1) = \dfrac{1}{3} - \dfrac{3}{2} + 2 = \dfrac{5}{6}$.
  4. (3) $\cos 2x \cdot 2 - \cos x \cdot 1 = 2\cos 2x - \cos x$.

(1) $f(x) = x - \pi$ (2) $G'(x) = x^2 - 3x + 2$; local maximum $\dfrac{5}{6}$ (3) $2\cos 2x - \cos x$

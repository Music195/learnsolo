Integrals, Areas and Volumes

Five problems on definite integrals by substitution and by parts, an area and a volume. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (e^x + 2x)\,dx$.

Uses: Integrals of Exponential Functions, Definite Integrals

Show hint

An antiderivative of $e^x$ is $e^x$.

Show solution
  1. $\left[e^x + x^2\right]_0^1 = (e + 1) - (1 + 0) = e$.

$e$

Problem 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1 2x(x^2 + 1)^3\,dx$.

Uses: Definite Integral: Substitution

Show hint

Put $u = x^2 + 1$, so $du = 2x\,dx$. Change the limits too.

Show solution
  1. $u = x^2 + 1$: $x = 0 \to u = 1$, $x = 1 \to u = 2$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_1^2 u^3\,du = \left[\dfrac{u^4}{4}\right]_1^2 = \dfrac{16 - 1}{4} = \dfrac{15}{4}$.

$\dfrac{15}{4}$

Problem 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x\sin x\,dx$.

Uses: Definite Integral: By Parts

Show hint

By parts with $u = x$ and $v' = \sin x$, so $v = -\cos x$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x\sin x\,dx = \Big[-x\cos x\Big]_0^\pi + \int_0^\pi \cos x\,dx$.
  2. $\Big[-x\cos x\Big]_0^\pi = -\pi \cdot (-1) - 0 = \pi$, and $\displaystyle\int_0^\pi \cos x\,dx = \Big[\sin x\Big]_0^\pi = 0$.

$\pi$

Problem 4

Find the area of the region enclosed by $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = x$.

Uses: Area Between Two Curves

Show hint

They meet at $x = 0$ and $x = 1$. Which is on top in between?

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{x} = x$ at $x = 0, 1$, and $\sqrt{x} \ge x$ for $0 \le x \le 1$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (\sqrt{x} - x)\,dx = \left[\dfrac{2}{3}x^{\frac{3}{2}} - \dfrac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{6}$.

Area $= \dfrac{1}{6}$

Problem 5

The region between $y = \sin x$ ($0 \le x \le \pi$) and the $x$-axis is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume of the solid.

Uses: Volume of Revolution About the x-axis

Show hint

$V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^\pi y^2\,dx$. Lower the power with $\sin^2 x = \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^\pi \sin^2 x\,dx = \pi\int_0^\pi \dfrac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}\,dx$.
  2. $= \pi\left[\dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{\sin 2x}{4}\right]_0^\pi = \pi \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

$V = \dfrac{\pi^2}{2}$

Volumes

Six problems on volumes by cross-sections and volumes of revolution about the x-axis and the y-axis. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

The region under $y = x^2$ for $0 \le x \le 1$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.

Uses: Volume of Revolution About the x-axis

Show hint

$V = \pi\displaystyle\int_a^b y^2\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^1 x^4\,dx = \frac{\pi}{5}$.

$\dfrac{\pi}{5}$

Problem 2

A solid lies between the planes $x = 0$ and $x = 2$. Its cross-section at $x$ is a square of side $2 - x$. Find its volume.

Uses: Volume by Cross-Sections

Show hint

$V = \displaystyle\int_a^b S(x)\,dx$ with $S(x) = (2 - x)^2$.

Show solution
  1. $V = \displaystyle\int_0^2 (2 - x)^2\,dx = \left[-\frac{(2 - x)^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = \frac{8}{3}$.

$\dfrac{8}{3}$ (a square pyramid: $\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 4 \cdot 2$ ✓)

Problem 3

The region bounded by $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 4$ is rotated about the $y$-axis. Find the volume.

Uses: Volume of Revolution About the y-axis

Show hint

Integrate in $y$: $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_c^d x^2\,dy$, and $x^2 = y$ on the curve.

Show solution
  1. $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^4 y\,dy = \pi \cdot 8 = 8\pi$.

$8\pi$

Problem 4

The region enclosed by $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = x^2$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.

Uses: Volume of Revolution About the x-axis

Show hint

They meet at $x = 0, 1$, and $\sqrt{x} \ge x^2$ there. Subtract the inner volume from the outer one.

Show solution
  1. $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^1 \{(\sqrt{x})^2 - (x^2)^2\}\,dx = \pi\int_0^1 (x - x^4)\,dx = \pi\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{5}\right) = \frac{3\pi}{10}$.

$\dfrac{3\pi}{10}$

$\pi\displaystyle\int (\sqrt{x} - x^2)^2\,dx$ would be wrong: square each radius first, then subtract.

Problem 5

A solid has the disk $x^2 + y^2 \le 4$ as its base. Every cross-section perpendicular to the $x$-axis is an equilateral triangle. Find its volume.

Uses: Volume by Cross-Sections

Show hint

At $x$, the side of the triangle is the chord $2\sqrt{4 - x^2}$. An equilateral triangle of side $s$ has area $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2$.

Show solution
  1. $S(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 4(4 - x^2) = \sqrt{3}(4 - x^2)$.
  2. $V = \sqrt{3}\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2} (4 - x^2)\,dx = 2\sqrt{3}\left(8 - \frac{8}{3}\right) = \frac{32\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

$\dfrac{32\sqrt{3}}{3}$

Problem 6

$D$ is the region bounded by $y = \log x$, the $x$-axis and the line $x = e$.

  1. (1) Find the area of $D$.
  2. (2) Find the volume when $D$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the volume when $D$ is rotated about the $y$-axis.

Uses: Volume of Revolution About the x-axis, Volume of Revolution About the y-axis, Area Under y = f(x)

Show hint

(2) $\displaystyle\int (\log x)^2\,dx = x(\log x)^2 - 2x\log x + 2x + C$ (by parts twice). (3) In $y$, $D$ runs from $x = e^y$ to $x = e$ for $0 \le y \le 1$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\displaystyle\int_1^e \log x\,dx = 1$.
  2. (2) $\pi\displaystyle\int_1^e (\log x)^2\,dx = \pi\Big[x(\log x)^2 - 2x\log x + 2x\Big]_1^e = \pi\{(e - 2e + 2e) - 2\} = \pi(e - 2)$.
  3. (3) $\pi\displaystyle\int_0^1 (e^2 - e^{2y})\,dy = \pi\left(e^2 - \frac{e^2 - 1}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi(e^2 + 1)}{2}$.

(1) $1$ (2) $\pi(e - 2)$ (3) $\dfrac{\pi(e^2 + 1)}{2}$

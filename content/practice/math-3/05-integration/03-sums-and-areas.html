Riemann Sums, Inequalities and Areas

Seven problems on Riemann sums, inequalities of integrals, and areas under a curve, between curves and beside the y-axis. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} \sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}$.

Uses: Riemann Sums

Show hint

$\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right) = \int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$: the limit is $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \sqrt{x}\,dx = \frac{2}{3}$.

$\dfrac{2}{3}$

Problem 2

Find the area bounded by $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$, the $x$-axis and the lines $x = 1$ and $x = e^2$.

Uses: Area Under y = f(x)

Show hint

The curve is above the $x$-axis for $x > 0$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_1^{e^2} \frac{dx}{x} = \Big[\log x\Big]_1^{e^2} = 2$.

$2$

Problem 3

Find $\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n^3}\sum_{k=1}^{n} (n + k)^2$.

Uses: Riemann Sums

Show hint

Take $\dfrac{1}{n}$ out and write the rest in terms of $\dfrac{k}{n}$: $\dfrac{(n + k)^2}{n^2} = \left(1 + \dfrac{k}{n}\right)^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1}{n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} \left(1 + \frac{k}{n}\right)^2 \to \int_0^1 (1 + x)^2\,dx = \left[\frac{(1 + x)^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = \frac{8 - 1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$.

$\dfrac{7}{3}$

Problem 4

Find the area enclosed by $y = \sin 2x$ and $y = \sin x$ for $0 \le x \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

Uses: Area Between Two Curves

Show hint

$\sin 2x = \sin x \iff \sin x(2\cos x - 1) = 0$. Which curve is on top between $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$?

Show solution
  1. They meet at $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$; in between $\cos x > \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\sin 2x > \sin x$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{3}} (\sin 2x - \sin x)\,dx = \left[-\frac{\cos 2x}{2} + \cos x\right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{3}} = \left(\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\right) - \left(-\frac{1}{2} + 1\right) = \frac{1}{4}$.

$\dfrac{1}{4}$

Problem 5

Show that $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\,dx > \frac{2}{3}$.

Uses: Inequalities of Definite Integrals

Show hint

Use $e^{u} \ge 1 + u$ (equality only at $u = 0$) with $u = -x^2$.

Show solution
  1. $e^{-x^2} \ge 1 - x^2$ for all $x$, with equality only at $x = 0$.
  2. So $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\,dx > \int_0^1 (1 - x^2)\,dx = \frac{2}{3}$ (strict, since the functions are not equal on the whole interval).

Shown using $e^{-x^2} \ge 1 - x^2$.

Problem 6

Find the area enclosed by the curve $x = y^2 - 2y$ and the line $x = y$.

Uses: Area Beside x = g(y)

Show hint

Integrate with respect to $y$: (right $x$) $-$ (left $x$).

Show solution
  1. $y^2 - 2y = y \iff y(y - 3) = 0$: they meet at $y = 0$ and $y = 3$.
  2. For $0 < y < 3$, the line $x = y$ is on the right.
  3. $\displaystyle\int_0^3 \{y - (y^2 - 2y)\}\,dy = \int_0^3 (3y - y^2)\,dy = \frac{27}{2} - 9 = \frac{9}{2}$.

$\dfrac{9}{2}$

Problem 7

The curve $C$: $y = \log x$.

  1. (1) Find the area bounded by $C$, the $x$-axis and the line $x = e$.
  2. (2) Show that the tangent $\ell$ to $C$ at $x = e$ passes through the origin.
  3. (3) Find the area bounded by $C$, $\ell$ and the $x$-axis. Do it in two ways: with $x$ and with $y$. (Type it in the box.)
  4. (4) Use $\log x \le \dfrac{x}{e}$ to show $1 < \dfrac{e^2 - 1}{2e}$.

Uses: Area Under y = f(x), Area Between Two Curves, Area Beside x = g(y), Inequalities of Definite Integrals

Show hint

$\displaystyle\int \log x\,dx = x\log x - x + C$. In $y$: the region runs from $x = ey$ (the tangent) to $x = e^y$ (the curve), for $0 \le y \le 1$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\displaystyle\int_1^e \log x\,dx = \Big[x\log x - x\Big]_1^e = 0 - (-1) = 1$.
  2. (2) Slope $\dfrac{1}{e}$ at $(e, 1)$: $y - 1 = \dfrac{1}{e}(x - e)$, i.e. $y = \dfrac{x}{e}$, which passes through $O$.
  3. (3) With $x$: triangle under $\ell$ from $0$ to $e$ has area $\dfrac{e}{2}$; subtract (1): $\dfrac{e}{2} - 1$.
  4. With $y$: $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (e^y - ey)\,dy = (e - 1) - \frac{e}{2} = \frac{e}{2} - 1$ ✓.
  5. (4) $C$ is concave down, so it lies below its tangent: $\log x \le \dfrac{x}{e}$, equal only at $x = e$. Integrating on $[1, e]$: $1 < \displaystyle\int_1^e \frac{x}{e}\,dx = \frac{e^2 - 1}{2e}$.

(1) $1$ (3) $\dfrac{e}{2} - 1$ (4) shown

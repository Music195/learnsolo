Derivatives of Powers, Trigonometric, Log and Exponential Functions

Seven problems on derivatives of xᵖ, trigonometric, logarithmic and exponential functions, logarithmic differentiation and parametric curves. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Differentiate $y = x^2\sqrt{x}$ and find $y'$ at $x = 4$.

Uses: Derivative of xᵖ

Show hint

Write it as a single power: $x^2\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{5}{2}}$.

Show solution
  1. $y' = \dfrac{5}{2}x^{\frac{3}{2}}$.
  2. At $x = 4$: $\dfrac{5}{2} \cdot 8 = 20$.

$y' = \dfrac{5}{2}x\sqrt{x}$; $20$

Problem 2

Differentiate $y = \cos^2 2x$ and find $y'$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8}$.

Uses: Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions

Show hint

Chain rule twice: $y = u^2$ with $u = \cos 2x$, and $u' = -2\sin 2x$.

Show solution
  1. $y' = 2\cos 2x \cdot (-2\sin 2x) = -4\sin 2x\cos 2x = -2\sin 4x$.
  2. At $x = \dfrac{\pi}{8}$: $-2\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = -2$.

$y' = -2\sin 4x$; $-2$

Problem 3

Differentiate $y = e^{2x}\log x$ and find $y'$ at $x = 1$.

Uses: Derivatives of Log and Exponential Functions

Show hint

Product rule, with $(e^{2x})' = 2e^{2x}$ and $(\log x)' = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

Show solution
  1. $y' = 2e^{2x}\log x + \dfrac{e^{2x}}{x}$.
  2. At $x = 1$: $0 + e^2 = e^2$.

$y' = e^{2x}\left(2\log x + \dfrac{1}{x}\right)$; $e^2$

Problem 4

Find the tangent to $y = \sqrt{2x + 1}$ at $x = 4$. (Type its $y$-intercept in the box.)

Uses: Derivative of xᵖ

Show hint

$\sqrt{2x + 1} = (2x + 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}$; use the chain rule.

Show solution
  1. $y' = \dfrac{1}{2}(2x + 1)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \cdot 2 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2x + 1}}$.
  2. At $x = 4$: $y = 3$, $y' = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
  3. $y - 3 = \dfrac{1}{3}(x - 4)$, i.e. $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + \dfrac{5}{3}$.

$y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + \dfrac{5}{3}$

Problem 5

A curve is given by $x = t + \dfrac{1}{t}$, $y = t - \dfrac{1}{t}$ ($t > 0$). Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, and the slope of the tangent at $t = 2$.

Uses: Parametric Derivatives

Show hint

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{t^2}$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 1 + \dfrac{1}{t^2}$.
  2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{t^2 + 1}{t^2 - 1}$ ($t \ne 1$).
  3. At $t = 2$: $\dfrac{5}{3}$.

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{t^2 + 1}{t^2 - 1}$; slope $\dfrac{5}{3}$

Problem 6

Differentiate $y = x^{\sin x}$ ($x > 0$) by taking logarithms, and find $y'$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Uses: Derivatives of Log and Exponential Functions, Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions

Show hint

$\log y = \sin x \log x$. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$; the left side gives $\dfrac{y'}{y}$.

Show solution
  1. $\log y = \sin x\log x$, so $\dfrac{y'}{y} = \cos x\log x + \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$.
  2. $y' = x^{\sin x}\left(\cos x\log x + \dfrac{\sin x}{x}\right)$.
  3. At $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$: $y = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the bracket is $0 + \dfrac{2}{\pi}$, so $y' = 1$.

$y' = x^{\sin x}\left(\cos x\log x + \dfrac{\sin x}{x}\right)$; $1$

Problem 7

The curve $C$ is given by $x = \cos^3 t$, $y = \sin^3 t$ ($0 < t < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$).

  1. (1) Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ in terms of $t$.
  2. (2) Find the tangent at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
  3. (3) Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. (Type it in the box.)
  4. (4) The tangent at $t$ meets the $x$-axis at A and the $y$-axis at B. Show that $AB = 1$.

Uses: Parametric Derivatives, Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions, Derivative Notation

Show hint

$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) \Big/ \dfrac{dx}{dt}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = -3\cos^2 t\sin t$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 3\sin^2 t\cos t$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{\sin t}{\cos t} = -\tan t$.
  2. (2) At $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$: point $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}, \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\right)$, slope $-1$: $x + y = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
  3. (3) $\dfrac{d}{dt}(-\tan t) = -\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 t}$, so $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \dfrac{-1/\cos^2 t}{-3\cos^2 t\sin t} = \dfrac{1}{3\cos^4 t\sin t}$. At $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$: $\dfrac{1}{3 \cdot \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = \dfrac{8}{3\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}$.
  4. (4) Tangent: $y - \sin^3 t = -\tan t\,(x - \cos^3 t)$. $y = 0$: $x = \cos^3 t + \sin^2 t\cos t = \cos t$. $x = 0$: $y = \sin^3 t + \sin t\cos^2 t = \sin t$.
  5. $AB = \sqrt{\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t} = 1$.

(1) $-\tan t$ (2) $x + y = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ (3) $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}$ (4) A$(\cos t, 0)$, B$(0, \sin t)$, so $AB = 1$

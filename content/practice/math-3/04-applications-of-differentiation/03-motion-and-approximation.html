Velocity, Acceleration and Approximation

Six problems on velocity and acceleration on a line and in the plane, and approximation with the derivative. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

A point P moves on a number line with position $x = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t$ at time $t \ge 0$. When is P at rest? Find its acceleration at $t = 2$. (Type the acceleration in the box.)

Uses: Velocity and Acceleration on a Line

Show hint

$v = \dfrac{dx}{dt}$, $\alpha = \dfrac{dv}{dt}$. At rest means $v = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $v = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 = 6(t - 1)(t - 2)$: at rest at $t = 1$ and $t = 2$.
  2. $\alpha = 12t - 18$, so at $t = 2$: $\alpha = 6$.

At rest at $t = 1, 2$; acceleration $6$

Problem 2

Use the approximation formula to estimate $\sqrt[3]{8.12}$.

Uses: Approximation Formula

Show hint

$f(a + h) \approx f(a) + f'(a)h$ with $f(x) = x^{\frac{1}{3}}$, $a = 8$, $h = 0.12$.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{3}x^{-\frac{2}{3}}$, so $f'(8) = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{12}$.
  2. $\sqrt[3]{8.12} \approx 2 + \dfrac{0.12}{12} = 2.01$.

$\approx 2.01$

Problem 3

A point P moves in the plane with $(x, y) = (t^3 - 3t, 3t^2)$. Find its speed and the size of its acceleration at $t = 2$. (Type the speed in the box.)

Uses: Velocity and Acceleration in the Plane

Show hint

$\vec{v} = \left(\dfrac{dx}{dt}, \dfrac{dy}{dt}\right)$, and the speed is $|\vec{v}|$.

Show solution
  1. $\vec{v} = (3t^2 - 3, 6t)$; at $t = 2$: $(9, 12)$, speed $\sqrt{81 + 144} = 15$.
  2. $\vec{\alpha} = (6t, 6)$; at $t = 2$: $(12, 6)$, size $\sqrt{144 + 36} = 6\sqrt{5}$.

Speed $15$, acceleration $6\sqrt{5}$

In fact the speed is $\sqrt{9(t^2 - 1)^2 + 36t^2} = 3(t^2 + 1)$ for every $t$.

Problem 4

A ball is thrown upward from a $25$ m high roof. Its height after $t$ seconds is $h = 25 + 20t - 5t^2$ m. Find its greatest height and its velocity when it hits the ground. (Type the velocity in the box.)

Uses: Velocity and Acceleration on a Line

Show hint

Highest point: $v = 0$. Hitting the ground: $h = 0$ with $t > 0$.

Show solution
  1. $v = 20 - 10t = 0$ at $t = 2$: greatest height $25 + 40 - 20 = 45$ m.
  2. $h = 0 \iff t^2 - 4t - 5 = 0 \iff t = 5$ (since $t > 0$).
  3. $v(5) = 20 - 50 = -30$ m/s (downward).

Greatest height $45$ m; velocity $-30$ m/s

Problem 5

Estimate $\tan 46^\circ$ with the approximation formula. Also: if the radius of a sphere is measured with a relative error of $0.5\%$, about what relative error does that give in its volume? (Type the estimate of $\tan 46^\circ$ in the box.)

Uses: Approximation Formula

Show hint

Work in radians: $46^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{180}$. For the volume, $\Delta V \approx V'\,\Delta r$.

Show solution
  1. $(\tan x)' = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$, which is $2$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
  2. $\tan 46^\circ \approx 1 + 2 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{180} = 1 + \dfrac{\pi}{90} \approx 1.035$.
  3. $V = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, $\Delta V \approx 4\pi r^2\,\Delta r$, so $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V} \approx 3\dfrac{\Delta r}{r} = 1.5\%$.

$\tan 46^\circ \approx 1 + \dfrac{\pi}{90} \approx 1.035$; about $1.5\%$

Problem 6

A point P moves in the plane with $(x, y) = (\cos t + t\sin t, \;\sin t - t\cos t)$ for $t > 0$.

  1. (1) Find the velocity $\vec{v}$ and the speed.
  2. (2) Find the acceleration $\vec{\alpha}$ and its size at $t = \sqrt{3}$. (Type the size in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the angle between $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{\alpha}$ at $t = \sqrt{3}$.

Uses: Velocity and Acceleration in the Plane

Show hint

Many terms cancel when you differentiate. For (3), use $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{\alpha}}{|\vec{v}||\vec{\alpha}|}$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = -\sin t + \sin t + t\cos t = t\cos t$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = \cos t - \cos t + t\sin t = t\sin t$.
  2. $\vec{v} = (t\cos t, t\sin t)$, speed $= t$.
  3. (2) $\vec{\alpha} = (\cos t - t\sin t, \sin t + t\cos t)$, $|\vec{\alpha}|^2 = 1 + t^2$. At $t = \sqrt{3}$: $|\vec{\alpha}| = 2$.
  4. (3) $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{\alpha} = t\cos^2 t - t^2\sin t\cos t + t\sin^2 t + t^2\sin t\cos t = t$.
  5. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{t}{t\sqrt{1 + t^2}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ at $t = \sqrt{3}$, so $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

(1) $(t\cos t, t\sin t)$, speed $t$ (2) $(\cos t - t\sin t, \sin t + t\cos t)$, size $2$ (3) $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$

Tangents, Normals and the Mean Value Theorem

Six problems on tangent and normal lines and the mean value theorem. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find the tangent to $y = \log x$ at the point where $x = e^2$. (Type its $y$-intercept in the box.)

Uses: Equation of the Tangent Line

Show hint

$y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a)$ with $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

Show solution
  1. $f(e^2) = 2$, $f'(e^2) = \dfrac{1}{e^2}$.
  2. $y - 2 = \dfrac{1}{e^2}(x - e^2)$, i.e. $y = \dfrac{x}{e^2} + 1$.

$y = \dfrac{1}{e^2}x + 1$

Problem 2

Find the normal to $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ at the point where $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. (Type its $y$-intercept in the box.)

Uses: Equation of the Normal Line

Show hint

The normal is perpendicular to the tangent: its slope is $-\dfrac{1}{f'(a)}$.

Show solution
  1. Point $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, 2\right)$; $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, so $f'\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = -4$.
  2. Normal slope $\dfrac{1}{4}$: $y - 2 = \dfrac{1}{4}\left(x - \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, i.e. $y = \dfrac{1}{4}x + \dfrac{15}{8}$.

$y = \dfrac{1}{4}x + \dfrac{15}{8}$

Problem 3

Find the tangent to $y = \sqrt{x}$ that passes through the point $(-4, 0)$. (Type its slope in the box.)

Uses: Equation of the Tangent Line

Show hint

$(-4, 0)$ is not on the curve. Write the tangent at $(a, \sqrt{a})$ and make it pass through $(-4, 0)$.

Show solution
  1. Tangent at $x = a$: $y - \sqrt{a} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{a}}(x - a)$.
  2. Through $(-4, 0)$: $-\sqrt{a} = \dfrac{-4 - a}{2\sqrt{a}} \iff -2a = -4 - a \iff a = 4$.
  3. Tangent: $y - 2 = \dfrac{1}{4}(x - 4)$, i.e. $y = \dfrac{1}{4}x + 1$.

$y = \dfrac{1}{4}x + 1$

Problem 4

Find the value $c$ in the mean value theorem for $f(x) = \log x$ on $1 \le x \le e$.

Uses: Mean Value Theorem

Show hint

$\dfrac{f(e) - f(1)}{e - 1} = f'(c)$ with $1 < c < e$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1 - 0}{e - 1} = \dfrac{1}{c}$, so $c = e - 1$.
  2. Check: $1 < e - 1 \approx 1.72 < e$ ✓.

$c = e - 1$

Problem 5

Use the mean value theorem to find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{2x} - e^{2\sin x}}{x - \sin x}$.

Uses: Mean Value Theorem

Show hint

Apply the mean value theorem to $f(t) = e^{2t}$ between $\sin x$ and $x$.

Show solution
  1. For $x \ne 0$: $\dfrac{e^{2x} - e^{2\sin x}}{x - \sin x} = f'(c) = 2e^{2c}$ for some $c$ between $\sin x$ and $x$.
  2. As $x \to 0$, both $\sin x$ and $x$ go to $0$, so $c \to 0$.
  3. The limit is $2e^0 = 2$.

$2$

Problem 6

The curve $C$: $y = x^3 - x$.

  1. (1) Find the tangent $\ell$ to $C$ at the point where $x = 1$.
  2. (2) Find the normal $m$ to $C$ at the same point.
  3. (3) $\ell$ meets $C$ again at the point Q. Find Q. (Type its $x$-coordinate in the box.)
  4. (4) Does $m$ meet $C$ anywhere else?

Uses: Equation of the Tangent Line, Equation of the Normal Line

Show hint

For (3) and (4), set the curve equal to the line. $x = 1$ is already a solution, so $x - 1$ is a factor.

Show solution
  1. (1) $y' = 3x^2 - 1$; at $(1, 0)$ the slope is $2$: $y = 2x - 2$.
  2. (2) Normal slope $-\dfrac{1}{2}$: $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{1}{2}$.
  3. (3) $x^3 - x = 2x - 2 \iff x^3 - 3x + 2 = 0 \iff (x - 1)^2(x + 2) = 0$. Q$(-2, -6)$.
  4. (4) $x^3 - x = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{1}{2} \iff 2x^3 - x - 1 = 0 \iff (x - 1)(2x^2 + 2x + 1) = 0$. The quadratic has $D/4 = 1 - 2 < 0$, so no other point.

(1) $y = 2x - 2$ (2) $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{1}{2}$ (3) Q$(-2, -6)$ (4) No

The tangent touches at $x = 1$, which is why $(x - 1)^2$ appears in (3).

Conic Sections — Translated and Rotated Conics

Practise translated parabolas with vertex $(h, k)$, axis rotation formulas, angle of rotation $\theta$ to eliminate the cross-product term, and the general conic discriminant $B^2 - 4AC$.

Find the $y$-coordinate of the vertex of the parabola $(y - 3)^2 = 8(x + 2)$.

Uses: Translated Parabolas with Vertex (h, k)

Hint

Compare with the standard translated form $(y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h)$. The vertex is $(h, k)$.

Solution

Comparing $(y - 3)^2 = 8(x + 2)$ with $(y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h)$:

\[ h = -2, \quad k = 3, \quad 4p = 8. \]

The vertex is $(-2, 3)$, so the $y$-coordinate is $3$.

Evaluate the discriminant $B^2 - 4AC$ for the second-degree equation $x^2 - 4xy + 4y^2 + 2x - 3y + 1 = 0$.

Uses: Rotation of Axes and General Conics

Hint

Identify $A = 1$, $B = -4$, and $C = 4$. Compute $B^2 - 4AC$.

Solution

From the general quadratic equation $Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$:

\[ A = 1, \quad B = -4, \quad C = 4. \]

Calculate the discriminant:

\[ B^2 - 4AC = (-4)^2 - 4(1)(4) = 16 - 16 = 0. \]

(Since $B^2 - 4AC = 0$, this conic represents a parabola or a degenerate case of parallel lines).

Find the $y$-coordinate of the focus of the parabola $(x - 1)^2 = 12(y - 2)$.

Uses: Translated Parabolas with Vertex (h, k)

Hint

Compare with $(x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)$. The vertex is $(h, k)$ and the focus is $(h, k + p)$.

Solution

From the equation $(x - 1)^2 = 12(y - 2)$:

\[ h = 1, \quad k = 2, \quad 4p = 12 \implies p = 3. \]

Since the parabola opens upwards along the line $x = 1$, the focus is:

\[ (h, k + p) = (1, 2 + 3) = (1, 5). \]

The $y$-coordinate of the focus is $5$.

For the conic section $x^2 + 2\sqrt{3}\,xy + 3y^2 - 8 = 0$, find the angle of rotation $\theta$ (in degrees, with $0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ$) required to eliminate the $xy$-term.

Uses: Rotation of Axes and General Conics

Hint

Use the formula $\cot 2\theta = \frac{A - C}{B}$. Identify $A = 1$, $B = 2\sqrt{3}$, and $C = 3$.

Solution

Here $A = 1$, $B = 2\sqrt{3}$, and $C = 3$:

\[ \cot 2\theta = \frac{A - C}{B} = \frac{1 - 3}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{-2}{2\sqrt{3}} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}. \]

Hence $\tan 2\theta = -\sqrt{3}$.

In the interval $0^\circ < 2\theta < 180^\circ$, this yields:

\[ 2\theta = 120^\circ \implies \theta = 60^\circ. \]

A parabola has equation $(y + 1)^2 = -8(x - 3)$. Its directrix is a vertical line $x = k$. Find the value of $k$.

Uses: Translated Parabolas with Vertex (h, k)

Hint

Compare with $(y - k)^2 = 4p(x - h)$. Here $h = 3$ and $4p = -8 \implies p = -2$. The directrix is $x = h - p$.

Solution

The vertex is $(h, k) = (3, -1)$ and $4p = -8 \implies p = -2$.

Since $p < 0$, the parabola opens to the left. The directrix is a vertical line located behind the vertex:

\[ x = h - p = 3 - (-2) = 5. \]

Thus $k = 5$.

For the conic section $x^2 + 4xy + y^2 = 6$, find the acute angle of rotation $\theta$ (in degrees) that transforms the coordinate system so that the cross-product term is removed.

Uses: Rotation of Axes and General Conics

Hint

When the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are equal ($A = C$), $\cot 2\theta = \frac{A - C}{B} = 0$.

Solution

From the equation $x^2 + 4xy + y^2 = 6$, we have $A = 1$, $B = 4$, and $C = 1$:

\[ \cot 2\theta = \frac{A - C}{B} = \frac{1 - 1}{4} = 0. \]

For $0^\circ < 2\theta < 180^\circ$, $\cot 2\theta = 0$ when:

\[ 2\theta = 90^\circ \implies \theta = 45^\circ. \]

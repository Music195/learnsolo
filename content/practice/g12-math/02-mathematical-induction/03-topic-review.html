Problem 1

The sum of powers of two satisfies $1 + 2 + 2^2 + \dots + 2^{n-1} = 2^n - 1$. Evaluate the sum when $n = 8$.

Uses: Principle of Mathematical Induction, Proofs of Summation Formulas

Show hint

Substitute $n = 8$ into $2^n - 1$.

Show solution
  1. Calculate $2^8 = 256$.
  2. Subtract $1$: $256 - 1 = 255$.

$255$

Problem 2

Using the sum of cubes identity $1^3 + 2^3 + \dots + n^3 = \left[\dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2}\right]^2$, find the sum of the cubes of the first four positive even integers: $2^3 + 4^3 + 6^3 + 8^3$.

Uses: Proofs of Summation Formulas

Show hint

Factor out $2^3 = 8$: $2^3(1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + 4^3)$.

Show solution
  1. Factor out $2^3 = 8$: $$2^3 + 4^3 + 6^3 + 8^3 = 8(1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + 4^3)$$
  2. Apply the sum of cubes formula for $n = 4$: $$1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + 4^3 = \left[\frac{4(4 + 1)}{2}\right]^2 = 10^2 = 100$$
  3. Multiply: $8 \times 100 = 800$.
  4. (Direct check: $8 + 64 + 216 + 512 = 800$ ✓.)

$800$

Problem 3

For all $n \ge 1$, the sum $2 + 5 + 8 + \dots + (3n - 1) = \dfrac{n(3n + 1)}{2}$. Calculate the sum when $n = 12$.

Uses: Proofs of Summation Formulas

Show hint

Substitute $n = 12$ into $\dfrac{n(3n + 1)}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. Substitute $n = 12$: $$S_{12} = \frac{12(3(12) + 1)}{2} = \frac{12(36 + 1)}{2} = 6 \times 37 = 222$$

$222$

Problem 4

The expression $2^{3n} - 1$ is divisible by $7$ for all positive integers $n$. Find the quotient $\dfrac{2^{3n} - 1}{7}$ when $n = 2$.

Uses: Proofs of Divisibility

Show hint

Notice $2^{3n} = (2^3)^n = 8^n$. For $n = 2$, evaluate $8^2 - 1$, then divide by $7$.

Show solution
  1. For $n = 2$: $2^{3(2)} - 1 = 2^6 - 1 = 64 - 1 = 63$.
  2. Divide by $7$: $\dfrac{63}{7} = 9$.
  3. Inductive step: Let $8^k - 1 = 7m$. Then: $$8^{k+1} - 1 = 8(8^k - 1) + 7 = 8(7m) + 7 = 7(8m + 1)$$ which proves divisibility by $7$ for all $k \ge 1$.

$9$

Problem 5

The product of any three consecutive integers $n(n + 1)(n + 2)$ is always divisible by $6$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Find the quotient $\dfrac{n(n + 1)(n + 2)}{6}$ for $n = 5$.

Uses: Proofs of Divisibility

Show hint

Substitute $n = 5$: evaluate $5 \times 6 \times 7$, then divide by $6$.

Show solution
  1. Evaluate for $n = 5$: $5(5 + 1)(5 + 2) = 5 \times 6 \times 7 = 210$.
  2. Divide by $6$: $\dfrac{210}{6} = 35$.
  3. Inductive reasoning: Among three consecutive integers, at least one is a multiple of $2$ and exactly one is a multiple of $3$, so their product is always divisible by $2 \times 3 = 6$.

$35$

Problem 6

By mathematical induction, $2^n > n^2$ for all integers $n \ge 5$. In verifying the base case $n = 5$, find the positive difference $2^5 - 5^2$.

Uses: Principle of Mathematical Induction

Show hint

Evaluate $2^5$ and $5^2$, then subtract.

Show solution
  1. Compute $2^5 = 32$.
  2. Compute $5^2 = 25$.
  3. Subtract: $32 - 25 = 7 > 0$.
  4. Inductive step: Assume $2^k > k^2$ for $k \ge 5$. Then: $$2^{k+1} = 2 \cdot 2^k > 2k^2 = k^2 + k^2 > k^2 + 2k + 1 = (k + 1)^2 \quad (\text{since } k^2 > 2k + 1 \text{ for } k \ge 5)$$ which completes the induction proof.

$7$

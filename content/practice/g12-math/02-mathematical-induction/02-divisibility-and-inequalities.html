Mathematical Induction — Divisibility and Inequalities

Six practice problems on inductive divisibility proofs ($5^n - 1$, $7^n - 1$, $3^{2n} - 1$, $n^3 + 2n$, $4^n + 15n - 1$) and inductive inequalities. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

By mathematical induction, $5^n - 1$ is divisible by $4$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Find the quotient $\dfrac{5^n - 1}{4}$ when $n = 3$.

Uses: Proofs of Divisibility

Show hint

Evaluate $5^3 - 1$, then divide by $4$.

Show solution
  1. Calculate $5^3 - 1 = 125 - 1 = 124$.
  2. Divide by $4$: $\dfrac{124}{4} = 31$.

$31$

Problem 2

The expression $7^n - 1$ is divisible by $6$ for every positive integer $n$. Find the quotient $\dfrac{7^n - 1}{6}$ when $n = 3$.

Uses: Proofs of Divisibility

Show hint

Evaluate $7^3 - 1$, then divide by $6$.

Show solution
  1. Calculate $7^3 - 1 = 343 - 1 = 342$.
  2. Divide by $6$: $\dfrac{342}{6} = 57$.
  3. Inductive step: $7^{k+1} - 1 = 7(7^k - 1) + 6 = 7(6m) + 6 = 6(7m + 1)$, which confirms divisibility for all $k$.

$57$

Problem 3

Prove by induction that $3^{2n} - 1$ is divisible by $8$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Evaluate the quotient $\dfrac{3^{2n} - 1}{8}$ when $n = 2$.

Uses: Proofs of Divisibility

Show hint

Notice that $3^{2n} = (3^2)^n = 9^n$. Write $9^{k+1} - 1 = 9(9^k - 1) + 8$.

Show solution
  1. For $n = 2$: $3^{2(2)} - 1 = 3^4 - 1 = 81 - 1 = 80$.
  2. Divide by $8$: $\dfrac{80}{8} = 10$.
  3. Inductive step: Let $3^{2k} - 1 = 8m$. Then: $$3^{2(k+1)} - 1 = 3^2 \cdot 3^{2k} - 1 = 9(8m + 1) - 1 = 72m + 8 = 8(9m + 1)$$ which is a multiple of $8$.

$10$

Problem 4

The polynomial expression $n^3 + 2n$ is divisible by $3$ for all integers $n \ge 1$. Evaluate $\dfrac{n^3 + 2n}{3}$ for $n = 4$.

Uses: Proofs of Divisibility

Show hint

Compute $4^3 + 2(4)$, then divide by $3$.

Show solution
  1. Evaluate for $n = 4$: $4^3 + 2(4) = 64 + 8 = 72$.
  2. Divide by $3$: $\dfrac{72}{3} = 24$.
  3. Inductive step: $$(k + 1)^3 + 2(k + 1) = (k^3 + 3k^2 + 3k + 1) + (2k + 2) = (k^3 + 2k) + 3(k^2 + k + 1)$$ Since $k^3 + 2k = 3m$ by hypothesis, the total is $3(m + k^2 + k + 1)$, divisible by $3$.

$24$

Problem 5

Prove that $4^n + 15n - 1$ is divisible by $9$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Find the quotient $\dfrac{4^n + 15n - 1}{9}$ when $n = 2$.

Uses: Proofs of Divisibility

Show hint

Evaluate for $n = 2$: $4^2 + 15(2) - 1$, then divide by $9$.

Show solution
  1. For $n = 2$: $4^2 + 15(2) - 1 = 16 + 30 - 1 = 45$.
  2. Divide by $9$: $\dfrac{45}{9} = 5$.
  3. Inductive step: Let $P(k) = 4^k + 15k - 1 = 9m$. Then: $$4^{k+1} + 15(k + 1) - 1 = 4(4^k) + 15k + 14 = 4(9m - 15k + 1) + 15k + 14 = 36m - 45k + 18 = 9(4m - 5k + 2)$$ which is clearly divisible by $9$.

$5$

Problem 6

Bernoulli's inequality states that $(1 + x)^n \ge 1 + nx$ for all $x > -1$ and integers $n \ge 1$. For $x = 0.5$ and $n = 3$, calculate the difference $(1 + x)^n - (1 + nx)$.

Uses: Principle of Mathematical Induction

Show hint

Evaluate $(1.5)^3$ and $1 + 3(0.5)$, then subtract.

Show solution
  1. Compute $(1 + 0.5)^3 = (1.5)^3 = 3.375$.
  2. Compute $1 + 3(0.5) = 1 + 1.5 = 2.5$.
  3. Subtract: $3.375 - 2.5 = 0.875$.
  4. (Notice $0.875 > 0$, satisfying the strict inequality for $n > 1$ and $x \neq 0$.)

$0.875$

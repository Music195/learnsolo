Application of Differentiation — Concavity, Optimization and Linearization

Practise finding inflection points and intervals of concavity, applied optimization problems, and local linear approximations (tangent line approximations).

Find the $x$-coordinate of the inflection point of the cubic curve $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 2$.

Uses: Concavity, Inflection Points and Optimization

Hint

Inflection points occur where the second derivative changes sign: set $f''(x) = 0$.

Solution

First and second derivatives:

\[ f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 \] \[ f''(x) = 6x - 12. \]

Setting $f''(x) = 0$:

\[ 6x - 12 = 0 \implies x = 2. \]

Use the tangent line approximation for $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ at $a = 4$ to estimate $\sqrt{4.2}$.

Uses: Linearization and Linear Approximation

Hint

The linearization formula is $L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$. Here $f(4) = 2$ and $f'(4) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{4}} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25$.

Solution

Compute the linearization around $a = 4$:

\[ f(4) = \sqrt{4} = 2 \] \[ f'(x) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \implies f'(4) = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25. \]

The linear approximation is:

\[ L(x) = 2 + 0.25(x - 4). \]

Evaluating at $x = 4.2$:

\[ L(4.2) = 2 + 0.25(0.2) = 2 + 0.05 = 2.05. \]

A farmer uses $40\text{ m}$ of fencing to enclose a three-sided rectangular field against a straight stone wall. What is the maximum possible area (in square meters) of the field?

Uses: Concavity, Inflection Points and Optimization

Hint

Let the two equal sides perpendicular to the wall be $x$. The length parallel to the wall is $40 - 2x$. Maximize $A(x) = x(40 - 2x)$.

Solution

Let the width be $x$ and the length be $y = 40 - 2x$. The area function is:

\[ A(x) = x(40 - 2x) = 40x - 2x^2. \]

Differentiating and setting to zero:

\[ A'(x) = 40 - 4x = 0 \implies x = 10\text{ m}. \]

The maximum area is:

\[ A(10) = 10(40 - 20) = 10(20) = 200\text{ m}^2. \]

Find the positive $x$-coordinate of the inflection point for the function $f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2$.

Uses: Concavity, Inflection Points and Optimization

Hint

Compute $f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12$. Find the positive root of $f''(x) = 0$.

Solution

Compute the derivatives:

\[ f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x \] \[ f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12 = 12(x^2 - 1). \]

Setting $f''(x) = 0$:

\[ x^2 - 1 = 0 \implies x = \pm 1. \]

The positive inflection point is $x = 1$.

Find the maximum product of two non-negative numbers whose sum is $12$.

Uses: Concavity, Inflection Points and Optimization

Hint

Let the numbers be $x$ and $12 - x$. Maximize $P(x) = x(12 - x) = 12x - x^2$.

Solution

Let $P(x) = x(12 - x) = 12x - x^2$.

Differentiating:

\[ P'(x) = 12 - 2x = 0 \implies x = 6. \]

The other number is $12 - 6 = 6$, giving maximum product:

\[ P(6) = 6 \times 6 = 36. \]

Find the linear approximation $L(0.04)$ for the function $f(x) = (1 + x)^5$ centered at $a = 0$.

Uses: Linearization and Linear Approximation

Hint

At $a = 0$, $f(0) = 1$ and $f'(0) = 5(1)^4 = 5$. Thus $L(x) = 1 + 5x$.

Solution

The tangent line approximation of $(1 + x)^n$ around $x = 0$ is $L(x) = 1 + nx$.

For $n = 5$:

\[ L(x) = 1 + 5x. \]

Evaluating at $x = 0.04$:

\[ L(0.04) = 1 + 5(0.04) = 1 + 0.20 = 1.2. \]

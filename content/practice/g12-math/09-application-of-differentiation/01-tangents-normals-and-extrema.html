Application of Differentiation — Tangents, Normals and Extrema

Practise equations and slopes of tangent and normal lines, finding critical points, intervals of increase and decrease, and local/global extrema.

Find the slope of the tangent line to the parabola $y = x^2 - 4x + 5$ at the point where $x = 3$.

Uses: Tangent Line and Normal Line

Hint

The slope of the tangent line is given by $m = f'(x)$ evaluated at $x = 3$.

Solution

Differentiating $y = x^2 - 4x + 5$:

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 4. \]

At $x = 3$:

\[ m = 2(3) - 4 = 6 - 4 = 2. \]

Find the slope of the normal line to the curve $y = x^3 - 2x$ at the point where $x = 1$.

Uses: Tangent Line and Normal Line

Hint

Compute the derivative $y'(1)$ for the tangent slope. The normal line slope is $m_{\text{norm}} = -\frac{1}{y'(1)}$.

Solution

The derivative is:

\[ y' = 3x^2 - 2. \]

At $x = 1$, the tangent slope is $m_{\text{tan}} = 3(1)^2 - 2 = 1$.

The slope of the normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line:

\[ m_{\text{norm}} = -\frac{1}{m_{\text{tan}}} = -\frac{1}{1} = -1. \]

Find the positive critical point of the cubic polynomial $f(x) = x^3 - 12x$.

Uses: Increasing, Decreasing Functions and Extrema

Hint

Critical points occur where $f'(x) = 0$. Differentiate and solve for positive $x$.

Solution

Differentiating $f(x)$:

\[ f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12. \]

Setting $f'(x) = 0$:

\[ 3x^2 - 12 = 0 \implies x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2. \]

The positive critical point is $x = 2$.

Find the local minimum value of the quadratic function $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 10$.

Uses: Increasing, Decreasing Functions and Extrema

Hint

Find the critical point by setting $f'(x) = 0$, then evaluate $f(x)$ at that point.

Solution

Differentiating and setting to zero:

\[ f'(x) = 2x - 6 = 0 \implies x = 3. \]

Since $f''(x) = 2 > 0$, this point is a local minimum. The minimum value is:

\[ f(3) = 3^2 - 6(3) + 10 = 9 - 18 + 10 = 1. \]

Find the $y$-intercept of the tangent line to the curve $y = x^2$ at the point where $x = 2$.

Uses: Tangent Line and Normal Line

Hint

Find the point $(2, 4)$ and the slope $m = y'(2) = 4$. Form the point-slope line equation $y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)$ and solve for $y$ when $x = 0$.

Solution

The point of tangency is $(2, 2^2) = (2, 4)$.

The slope of the tangent line is $m = 2(2) = 4$.

The equation of the tangent line is:

\[ y - 4 = 4(x - 2) \implies y = 4x - 8 + 4 \implies y = 4x - 4. \]

The $y$-intercept is $-4$.

Find the absolute maximum value of $f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x + 1$ on the closed interval $[0, 3]$.

Uses: Increasing, Decreasing Functions and Extrema

Hint

Check the values of $f(x)$ at the critical points inside $(0, 3)$ and at the boundary endpoints $x = 0$ and $x = 3$.

Solution

Find the critical points:

\[ f'(x) = 6x^2 - 6x - 12 = 6(x^2 - x - 2) = 6(x - 2)(x + 1) = 0. \]

The only critical point in $[0, 3]$ is $x = 2$.

Evaluate $f(x)$ at candidates:

\[ f(0) = 1 \] \[ f(2) = 2(8) - 3(4) - 12(2) + 1 = 16 - 12 - 24 + 1 = -19 \] \[ f(3) = 2(27) - 3(9) - 12(3) + 1 = 54 - 27 - 36 + 1 = -8. \]

The largest value is $f(0) = 1$.

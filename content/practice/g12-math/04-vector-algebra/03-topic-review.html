Vector Algebra

A comprehensive review of 3D vector algebra, covering vector components, dot product, cross product, angles, geometric applications, and equations of lines and planes.

If $\hat{\mathbf{u}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\mathbf{i} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\mathbf{j} + c\,\mathbf{k}$ is a unit vector in three-dimensional space, evaluate the squared coefficient $c^2 + \frac{2}{3}$.

Uses: Vectors in Three Dimensions

Hint

For any unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$, the sum of squares of its components is $\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|^2 = 1$.

Solution

Since $\hat{\mathbf{u}}$ is a unit vector:

\[ \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 + c^2 = 1 \implies \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + c^2 = 1. \]

Therefore, $c^2 + \frac{2}{3} = 1$.

For any non-zero vector $\mathbf{v}$, what is the magnitude of the cross product $\|\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v}\|$?

Uses: Vector Product and Area of Parallelogram

Hint

The cross product of any vector with itself is the zero vector, $\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$.

Solution

By the antisymmetry of the cross product, $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = -(\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{a})$. Setting $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a}$ gives $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{a} = -\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{a}$, so $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{0}$.

Its magnitude is therefore $0$.

Vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ satisfy $\|\mathbf{a}\| = 4$, $\|\mathbf{b}\| = 3$, and $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 4$. Evaluate $\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$.

Uses: Angle Between Vectors and Scalar Product

Hint

Use the distributive property of the dot product: $\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}) = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$.

Solution

Distribute the dot product:

\[ \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2 + \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} \]

Substitute $\|\mathbf{a}\| = 4$ and $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 4$:

\[ 4^2 + 4 = 16 + 4 = 20. \]

Two vectors emanating from a vertex of a triangle have cross product $\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v} = 12\mathbf{i} + 16\mathbf{j} - 12\sqrt{5}\mathbf{k}$ ... wait, let's simplify: $\|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\| = 24$. Find the area of the triangle formed by these two vectors.

Uses: Vector Product and Area of Parallelogram

Hint

The area of a triangle spanned by $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ is half the area of the corresponding parallelogram: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\|$.

Solution

The area of the triangle is given by:

\[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\| = \frac{1}{2}(24) = 12. \]

If the vectors $\mathbf{u} = \langle 2, 3, -1 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle 4, k, -2 \rangle$ are parallel (collinear), find the value of $k$.

Uses: Vectors in Three Dimensions

Hint

Two vectors are parallel if their corresponding components are proportional: $\frac{v_x}{u_x} = \frac{v_y}{u_y} = \frac{v_z}{u_z}$.

Solution

Since $\mathbf{v}$ is a scalar multiple of $\mathbf{u}$:

\[ \mathbf{v} = c\,\mathbf{u} \implies \langle 4, k, -2 \rangle = c\,\langle 2, 3, -1 \rangle. \]

Comparing components: $4 = 2c \implies c = 2$. Therefore:

\[ k = 3c = 3(2) = 6. \]

A plane contains the point $(0, 0, 0)$ and the two direction vectors $\mathbf{d}_1 = \langle 1, 0, 1 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{d}_2 = \langle 0, 1, 1 \rangle$. Its equation is $x + y - z = 0$. If the point $P(2, 3, z_0)$ lies on this plane, find $z_0 - 3$.

Uses: Vector Equations of Lines and Planes

Hint

Substitute the point $P(2, 3, z_0)$ into the plane equation $x + y - z = 0$ to find $z_0$, then compute $z_0 - 3$.

Solution

The normal vector is:

\[ \mathbf{n} = \mathbf{d}_1 \times \mathbf{d}_2 = \det \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = -\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k} \sim \langle 1, 1, -1 \rangle. \]

Since the plane passes through the origin, its equation is $x + y - z = 0$.

For the point $(2, 3, z_0)$ on this plane:

\[ 2 + 3 - z_0 = 0 \implies z_0 = 5. \]

Therefore, $z_0 - 3 = 5 - 3 = 2$.

Application of Integration — Volumes of Solids of Revolution

Practise finding volumes of solids of revolution using the disc method, washer method, slicing method, and rotations about coordinate axes.

The region bounded by the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$, the $x$-axis, and the vertical line $x = 4$ is revolved about the $x$-axis. If the volume of the resulting solid is $V$, evaluate $\frac{V}{\pi}$.

Uses: Volumes of Solids: Slicing, Disc, and Washer Methods

Hint

By the disc method, $V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx$.

Solution

Using the disc formula for rotation around the $x$-axis:

\[ V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^4 x\,dx = \pi \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = \pi \left(\frac{16}{2} - 0\right) = 8\pi. \]

Dividing by $\pi$ gives $\frac{V}{\pi} = 8$.

The triangular region under $y = 2x$ between $x = 0$ and $x = 3$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. If the volume generated is $V$, find $\frac{V}{\pi}$.

Uses: Volumes of Solids: Slicing, Disc, and Washer Methods

Hint

Use $V = \pi \int_0^3 (2x)^2\,dx = 4\pi \int_0^3 x^2\,dx$.

Solution

Apply the disc method:

\[ V = \pi \int_0^3 (2x)^2\,dx = 4\pi \int_0^3 x^2\,dx = 4\pi \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = 4\pi\left(\frac{27}{3}\right) = 36\pi. \]

Dividing by $\pi$ gives $36$.

The region bounded by $y = x^2$, the $x$-axis, and $x = 2$ is rotated around the $x$-axis to produce a solid of volume $V$. Evaluate $\frac{V}{\pi}$ as a decimal.

Uses: Volumes of Solids: Slicing, Disc, and Washer Methods

Hint

The radius of each circular disc is $R = x^2$. Compute $V = \pi \int_0^2 (x^2)^2\,dx = \pi \int_0^2 x^4\,dx$.

Solution

Integrate $x^4$ from $0$ to $2$:

\[ V = \pi \int_0^2 x^4\,dx = \pi \left[\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2 = \pi \left(\frac{32}{5}\right) = 6.4\pi. \]

Thus $\frac{V}{\pi} = 6.4$.

The region bounded by $y = 3$, $y = x$, and the $y$-axis ($x = 0$) is rotated about the $x$-axis. If the resulting volume of revolution is $V$, evaluate $\frac{V}{\pi}$.

Uses: Volumes of Solids: Slicing, Disc, and Washer Methods

Hint

Use the washer method: the outer radius is $R = 3$ and the inner radius is $r = x$. Integrate $V = \pi \int_0^3 (R^2 - r^2)\,dx$.

Solution

The boundaries meet where $x = 3$. The washer formula gives:

\[ V = \pi \int_0^3 (3^2 - x^2)\,dx = \pi \int_0^3 (9 - x^2)\,dx = \pi \left[9x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = \pi (27 - 9) = 18\pi. \]

Therefore $\frac{V}{\pi} = 18$.

The region in the first quadrant bounded by $x = \sqrt{y}$, the $y$-axis ($x = 0$), and the horizontal line $y = 4$ is rotated around the $y$-axis. Find $\frac{V}{\pi}$.

Uses: Volumes of Solids: Slicing, Disc, and Washer Methods

Hint

For rotation about the $y$-axis, integrate with respect to $y$: $V = \pi \int_0^4 [g(y)]^2\,dy = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{y})^2\,dy$.

Solution

Integrating in $y$ from $0$ to $4$:

\[ V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{y})^2\,dy = \pi \int_0^4 y\,dy = \pi \left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = \pi \left(\frac{16}{2}\right) = 8\pi. \]

Therefore $\frac{V}{\pi} = 8$.

The base of a solid is the region in the $xy$-plane bounded by the parabola $y = 1 - x^2$ and the $x$-axis. Cross-sections of the solid perpendicular to the $x$-axis are rectangles of constant height $h = 3$. Find the total volume of this solid.

Uses: Volumes of Solids: Slicing, Disc, and Washer Methods

Hint

The area of each cross-section at coordinate $x$ is $A(x) = \text{base} \times \text{height} = (1 - x^2) \times 3$. Integrate $A(x)$ from $x = -1$ to $x = 1$.

Solution

The cross-sectional area at $x$ is:

\[ A(x) = 3(1 - x^2). \]

The bounds are where $1 - x^2 = 0 \implies x = \pm 1$. By the slicing method:

\[ V = \int_{-1}^1 3(1 - x^2)\,dx = 3 \left[x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^1 = 3 \left[\left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right) - \left(-1 + \frac{1}{3}\right)\right] = 3\left(\frac{2}{3} - \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\right) = 3\left(\frac{4}{3}\right) = 4. \]

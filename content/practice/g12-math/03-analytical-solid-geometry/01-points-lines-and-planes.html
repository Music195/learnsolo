Analytical Solid Geometry — Points, Vectors and Lines

Practise three-dimensional Cartesian coordinates, distance between points, direction ratios and cosines, and symmetric and parametric equations of lines in space.

Find the distance between the two points $A(1, 2, 3)$ and $B(4, -2, 3)$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Uses: Coordinates of a Point in Space

Hint

Use the 3D distance formula: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}$.

Solution

Applying the coordinates $A(1, 2, 3)$ and $B(4, -2, 3)$:

\[ d = \sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (-2 - 2)^2 + (3 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 0} = \sqrt{25} = 5. \]

What is the perpendicular distance from the point $P(3, -4, 7)$ to the $xy$-coordinate plane?

Uses: Coordinates of a Point in Space

Hint

The equation of the $xy$-plane is $z = 0$. The perpendicular distance from $(x, y, z)$ to the $xy$-plane is simply $|z|$.

Solution

The projection of $P(3, -4, 7)$ onto the $xy$-plane is $P'(3, -4, 0)$. The perpendicular distance is:

\[ d = |z| = |7| = 7. \]

A line in space passes through the point $P(2, 0, -3)$ with direction vector $\mathbf{d} = \langle 1, 3, -2 \rangle$. Find the $y$-coordinate of the point on this line whose $x$-coordinate is $5$.

Uses: Lines in Space

Hint

The parametric equations of the line are $x = 2 + t$, $y = 3t$, $z = -3 - 2t$. First find the parameter $t$ when $x = 5$.

Solution

Write the parametric form of the line:

\[ x = 2 + t, \quad y = 0 + 3t = 3t, \quad z = -3 - 2t \]

Set $x = 5$ to solve for $t$:

\[ 5 = 2 + t \implies t = 3. \]

Substitute $t = 3$ into the equation for $y$:

\[ y = 3(3) = 9. \]

Line $L_1$ has direction vector $\mathbf{d}_1 = \langle 1, 2, 2 \rangle$ and Line $L_2$ has direction vector $\mathbf{d}_2 = \langle 2, 1, -2 \rangle$. Find the angle $\theta$ (in degrees) between the two lines.

Uses: Parallel, Skew, and Perpendicular Lines

Hint

Compute the dot product $\mathbf{d}_1 \cdot \mathbf{d}_2$. If the dot product is $0$, the lines are perpendicular.

Solution

Calculate the scalar product of the direction vectors:

\[ \mathbf{d}_1 \cdot \mathbf{d}_2 = (1)(2) + (2)(1) + (2)(-2) = 2 + 2 - 4 = 0. \]

Because $\mathbf{d}_1 \cdot \mathbf{d}_2 = 0$, the angle between the directions is:

\[ \cos\theta = \frac{0}{\|\mathbf{d}_1\|\|\mathbf{d}_2\|} = 0 \implies \theta = 90^\circ. \]

Two lines with direction ratios $\langle 2, k, -3 \rangle$ and $\langle 3, 2, 4 \rangle$ are perpendicular. Find the value of the constant $k$.

Uses: Parallel, Skew, and Perpendicular Lines

Hint

For perpendicular lines, $a_1 a_2 + b_1 b_2 + c_1 c_2 = 0$.

Solution

Two lines are perpendicular if and only if the sum of the products of their direction ratios equals zero:

\[ a_1 a_2 + b_1 b_2 + c_1 c_2 = (2)(3) + (k)(2) + (-3)(4) = 0 \] \[ 6 + 2k - 12 = 0 \implies 2k - 6 = 0 \implies 2k = 6 \implies k = 3. \]

A line has symmetric equations $\frac{x-1}{1} = \frac{y-2}{2} = \frac{z-3}{3}$. If this line intersects the horizontal plane $z = 6$ at the point $(x_0, y_0, 6)$, find the sum of coordinates $x_0 + y_0$.

Uses: Lines in Space

Hint

Set the common ratio equal to parameter $t$: $\frac{z-3}{3} = t$. Solve for $t$ when $z = 6$, then compute $x_0$ and $y_0$.

Solution

Let the common parameter be $t$:

\[ \frac{x-1}{1} = \frac{y-2}{2} = \frac{z-3}{3} = t \]

When $z = 6$:

\[ t = \frac{6 - 3}{3} = \frac{3}{3} = 1. \]

Now determine $x_0$ and $y_0$ at $t = 1$:

\[ x_0 = 1 + 1(1) = 2, \quad y_0 = 2 + 2(1) = 4. \]

Therefore:

\[ x_0 + y_0 = 2 + 4 = 6. \]

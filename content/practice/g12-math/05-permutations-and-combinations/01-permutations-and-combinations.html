Permutations and Combinations — Basic Principles

Practise the fundamental counting principles, factorial arithmetic, permutations of distinct objects (${}^nP_r$), and combinations (${}^nC_r$).

Evaluate the numerical value of $\frac{7!}{5!\,2!}$.

Uses: Counting Principles and Factorial Notation

Hint

Cancel out $5!$ from the numerator and denominator: $\frac{7!}{5!} = 7 \times 6$.

Solution

Simplify using factorial expansion:

\[ \frac{7!}{5!\,2!} = \frac{7 \times 6 \times 5!}{5! \times (2 \times 1)} = \frac{7 \times 6}{2} = \frac{42}{2} = 21. \]

Evaluate ${}^6P_3$, the number of ways to arrange $3$ distinct items selected from $6$ distinct items.

Uses: Permutations of Distinct Objects

Hint

Use the formula ${}^nP_r = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!} = n(n-1)\dots(n-r+1)$.

Solution

Compute the product of $3$ consecutive decreasing integers starting from $6$:

\[ {}^6P_3 = 6 \times 5 \times 4 = 120. \]

How many different committees of $4$ people can be chosen from a group of $8$ candidates?

Uses: Combinations and Selections

Hint

The order of selection does not matter, so use ${}^8C_4 = \frac{8!}{4!\,4!}$.

Solution

Calculate the combination ${}^8C_4$:

\[ {}^8C_4 = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6 \times 5}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{1680}{24} = 70. \]

A restaurant menu offers $3$ appetizers, $4$ main dishes, and $2$ desserts. How many different three-course meals consisting of one item from each course can a customer order?

Uses: Counting Principles and Factorial Notation

Hint

Apply the multiplication principle: if task 1 can be done in $m$ ways, task 2 in $n$ ways, and task 3 in $p$ ways, the total number of ways is $m \times n \times p$.

Solution

By the fundamental counting principle:

\[ N = 3 \times 4 \times 2 = 24. \]

How many $4$-digit numbers with no repeated digits can be formed from the digits $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ if the number must be even?

Uses: Permutations of Distinct Objects

Hint

An even number must end in an even digit ($2$, $4$, or $6$). Fill the units place first, then fill the remaining $3$ places from the remaining $5$ digits.

Solution

First place the units digit: there are $3$ choices ($2, 4,$ or $6$).

After choosing the units digit, $5$ digits remain available. The first $3$ digits (thousands, hundreds, tens) can be chosen in ${}^5P_3$ ways:

\[ {}^5P_3 = 5 \times 4 \times 3 = 60. \]

By the multiplication principle, the total count is:

\[ 60 \times 3 = 180. \]

A delegation of $5$ people is to be selected from $6$ men and $4$ women. In how many ways can the delegation be formed if it must contain exactly $3$ men and $2$ women?

Uses: Combinations and Selections

Hint

Choose $3$ men from $6$, and independently choose $2$ women from $4$. Multiply the two counts: ${}^6C_3 \times {}^4C_2$.

Solution

Calculate the independent selections:

\[ {}^6C_3 = \frac{6 \times 5 \times 4}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 20 \] \[ {}^4C_2 = \frac{4 \times 3}{2 \times 1} = 6. \]

Multiply the two combinations:

\[ \text{Total ways} = 20 \times 6 = 120. \]

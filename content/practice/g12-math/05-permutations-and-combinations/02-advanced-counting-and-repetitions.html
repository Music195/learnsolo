Find the number of distinct permutations of all the letters in the word $\text{LEVEL}$.

Uses: Advanced Counting Techniques and Repetitions

Hint

The word contains $5$ letters: $2$ L's, $2$ E's, and $1$ V. Use the repeated permutation formula: $\frac{n!}{p!\,q!}$.

Solution

Using the formula for permutations with repetition:

\[ N = \frac{5!}{2!\,2!\,1!} = \frac{120}{2 \times 2} = \frac{120}{4} = 30. \]

In how many distinct ways can $6$ people be seated around a circular conference table?

Uses: Advanced Counting Techniques and Repetitions

Hint

The number of circular arrangements of $n$ distinct items is $(n - 1)!$.

Solution

Fix one person to eliminate rotational symmetry. The remaining $5$ people can be arranged in:

\[ (6 - 1)! = 5! = 5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 120 \text{ ways}. \]

Find the number of different $6$-letter permutations that can be formed using all the letters in $\text{BANANA}$.

Uses: Advanced Counting Techniques and Repetitions

Hint

Count the frequencies of each letter: $1$ B, $3$ A's, and $2$ N's. Then divide $6!$ by the product of their factorials.

Solution

The counts are: $n = 6$, $n_B = 1$, $n_A = 3$, $n_N = 2$.

\[ N = \frac{6!}{1!\,3!\,2!} = \frac{720}{1 \times 6 \times 2} = \frac{720}{12} = 60. \]

In how many ways can $3$ distinct mathematics books and $2$ distinct physics books be placed in a row on a shelf if the $2$ physics books must be placed next to each other?

Uses: Advanced Counting Techniques and Repetitions

Hint

Tie the $2$ physics books together into $1$ single unit. Arrange the $4$ units ($3$ math + $1$ block), then multiply by the internal arrangements of the physics books ($2!$).

Solution

Treat the $2$ physics books as a single block. Together with the $3$ math books, there are $4$ units to arrange:

\[ 4! = 24 \text{ ways}. \]

Inside the physics block, the $2$ books can be arranged in:

\[ 2! = 2 \text{ ways}. \]

Total arrangements:

\[ 24 \times 2 = 48. \]

In how many ways can $4$ boys and $3$ girls stand in a line such that no two girls stand next to each other?

Uses: Advanced Counting Techniques and Repetitions

Hint

First arrange the $4$ boys in $4!$ ways. This creates $5$ available spaces (including ends). Place the $3$ girls into $3$ of these $5$ spaces using ${}^5P_3$.

Solution

First arrange the $4$ boys:

\[ 4! = 24 \text{ ways}. \]

The boys create $5$ available slots for the girls ($\_ B_1 \_ B_2 \_ B_3 \_ B_4 \_$).

The $3$ girls can be placed into $3$ of these $5$ slots in:

\[ {}^5P_3 = 5 \times 4 \times 3 = 60 \text{ ways}. \]

By the multiplication principle:

\[ \text{Total ways} = 24 \times 60 = 1440. \]

How many different necklaces can be formed by stringing $6$ distinctly coloured beads together, assuming that flipping the necklace over produces an identical arrangement?

Uses: Advanced Counting Techniques and Repetitions

Hint

Because the necklace can be turned over (clockwise and counterclockwise are identical), divide the circular permutations by $2$: $\frac{(n-1)!}{2}$.

Solution

For a necklace or keychain where reflections are indistinguishable:

\[ N = \frac{(n - 1)!}{2} = \frac{(6 - 1)!}{2} = \frac{5!}{2} = \frac{120}{2} = 60. \]

Logarithmic and Exponential Functions — Derivatives

Practise differentiation of exponential functions ($e^x, a^x$) and logarithmic functions ($\ln x, \log_a x$), applying product, quotient, and chain rules.

Let $f(x) = 5e^x$. Evaluate the derivative $f'(0)$ at $x = 0$.

Uses: Differentiation of Exponential Functions

Hint

The derivative of the natural exponential function is $\frac{d}{dx}(e^x) = e^x$.

Solution

Differentiating $f(x)$:

\[ f'(x) = 5e^x. \]

At $x = 0$:

\[ f'(0) = 5e^0 = 5(1) = 5. \]

If $f(x) = \ln(3x)$, evaluate the derivative $f'(2)$ as a decimal.

Uses: Differentiation of Logarithmic Functions

Hint

By the chain rule or log properties, $\frac{d}{dx}(\ln(kx)) = \frac{k}{kx} = \frac{1}{x}$.

Solution

Using the chain rule:

\[ f'(x) = \frac{1}{3x} \cdot 3 = \frac{1}{x}. \]

At $x = 2$:

\[ f'(2) = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5. \]

Let $f(x) = e^{x^2 - 4}$. Evaluate the derivative $f'(2)$.

Uses: Differentiation of Exponential Functions

Hint

By the chain rule, $\frac{d}{dx}(e^{u(x)}) = u'(x)e^{u(x)}$.

Solution

Apply the chain rule with $u(x) = x^2 - 4$:

\[ f'(x) = 2x\,e^{x^2 - 4}. \]

At $x = 2$:

\[ f'(2) = 2(2)\,e^{2^2 - 4} = 4\,e^0 = 4(1) = 4. \]

Let $f(x) = \ln(x^2 + 1)$. Evaluate the derivative $f'(1)$.

Uses: Differentiation of Logarithmic Functions

Hint

Use the formula $\frac{d}{dx}(\ln u) = \frac{u'}{u}$.

Solution

Applying the chain rule:

\[ f'(x) = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1}. \]

At $x = 1$:

\[ f'(1) = \frac{2(1)}{1^2 + 1} = \frac{2}{2} = 1. \]

Let $f(x) = x e^x$. Evaluate the derivative $f'(0)$.

Uses: Differentiation of Exponential Functions

Hint

Use the product rule: $\frac{d}{dx}(x e^x) = (1)e^x + x e^x = (1 + x)e^x$.

Solution

Applying the product rule:

\[ f'(x) = 1 \cdot e^x + x \cdot e^x = (1 + x)e^x. \]

At $x = 0$:

\[ f'(0) = (1 + 0)e^0 = 1 \cdot 1 = 1. \]

Find the slope of the tangent line to the curve $y = \frac{\ln x}{x}$ at the point where $x = 1$.

Uses: Differentiation of Logarithmic Functions

Hint

Apply the quotient rule to differentiate $y = \frac{\ln x}{x}$, then substitute $x = 1$.

Solution

Apply the quotient rule with $u = \ln x$ and $v = x$:

\[ y' = \frac{(1/x)(x) - (\ln x)(1)}{x^2} = \frac{1 - \ln x}{x^2}. \]

At $x = 1$, recall $\ln(1) = 0$:

\[ m = y'(1) = \frac{1 - \ln 1}{1^2} = \frac{1 - 0}{1} = 1. \]

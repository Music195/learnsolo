Camera, Eye, Microscope and Telescope

Six problems with $\dfrac1f = \dfrac1u + \dfrac1v$ (real distances positive, virtual negative), $m = -v/u$, lens power in dioptres, the compound microscope and the astronomical telescope. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A camera lens has focal length $50\ \mathrm{mm}$. How far from the film (sensor) must it be to photograph an object $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ away? (Type $v$ in mm, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: The Camera

Show hint

$\dfrac1v = \dfrac1f - \dfrac1u$, all in mm.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac1v = \dfrac1{50} - \dfrac1{2000} = \dfrac{40 - 1}{2000} = \dfrac{39}{2000}$.
  2. $v = \dfrac{2000}{39} \approx 51.3\ \mathrm{mm}$: a little more than $f$, so the lens moves out slightly to focus.

$v \approx 51.3\ \mathrm{mm}$

Problem 2

A $50\ \mathrm{mm}$ camera lens has an aperture diameter of $12.5\ \mathrm{mm}$. Find the f-number. If the diameter is halved, what happens to the f-number and the light reaching the film?

Uses: The Camera

Show hint

$N = f/D$. The light through the hole goes as its area, $\propto D^2$.

Show solution
  1. $N = \dfrac{50}{12.5} = 4$, written $f/4$.
  2. Halving $D$ gives $N = 8$ ($f/8$); the area and the light fall to $\tfrac14$.

$f/4$; halving $D$ gives $f/8$ and a quarter of the light

Problem 3

A short-sighted person cannot see clearly beyond $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$. What lens is needed so that very distant objects are seen clearly? Give its power. (Type $P$ in dioptres, with its sign.)

Uses: The Human Eye and Vision Defects

Show hint

The lens must form a virtual image of an object at infinity at the far point: $u = \infty$, $v = -2.0\ \mathrm{m}$.

Show solution
  1. $P = \dfrac1u + \dfrac1v = 0 + \dfrac{1}{-2.0} = -0.50\ \mathrm{D}$.
  2. Negative power: a diverging (concave) lens of focal length $2.0$ m.

Concave lens, $P = -0.50\ \mathrm{D}$

Problem 4

A long-sighted person has a near point of $50\ \mathrm{cm}$. What lens lets them read a book held at $25\ \mathrm{cm}$? (Type $P$ in dioptres, with its sign.)

Uses: The Human Eye and Vision Defects

Show hint

Object at $u = 0.25\ \mathrm{m}$; the lens must form a virtual image at the near point, $v = -0.50\ \mathrm{m}$.

Show solution
  1. $P = \dfrac{1}{0.25} + \dfrac{1}{-0.50} = 4.0 - 2.0 = +2.0\ \mathrm{D}$.
  2. Positive power: a converging (convex) lens of focal length $50$ cm.

Convex lens, $P = +2.0\ \mathrm{D}$

Problem 5

A compound microscope has an objective of focal length $1.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ and an eyepiece of focal length $5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$. An object is $1.2\ \mathrm{cm}$ from the objective and the final image is at the near point ($D = 25\ \mathrm{cm}$). Find the total magnification. (Type $M$ with its sign.)

Uses: The Compound Microscope

Show hint

Find $v_o$ from the lens formula, then $M = m_o \times m_e = -\dfrac{v_o}{u_o}\left(1 + \dfrac{D}{f_e}\right)$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{1}{v_o} = \dfrac11 - \dfrac1{1.2} = \dfrac{0.2}{1.2} \implies v_o = 6.0\ \mathrm{cm}$.
  2. $m_o = -\dfrac{6.0}{1.2} = -5.0$ and $m_e = 1 + \dfrac{25}{5.0} = 6.0$.
  3. $M = (-5.0)(6.0) = -30$: the final image is $30$ times larger and inverted.

$M = -30$

Problem 6

An astronomical telescope has an objective of focal length $100\ \mathrm{cm}$ and an eyepiece of $4.0\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find the magnifying power and the lens separation (i) in normal adjustment and (ii) with the final image at the near point, $25\ \mathrm{cm}$. (Type $M$ for case (ii).)

Uses: Telescopes and Binoculars

Show hint

(i) $M = f_o/f_e$, $L = f_o + f_e$. (ii) $M = \dfrac{f_o}{f_e}\left(1 + \dfrac{f_e}{D}\right)$ and $L = f_o + u_e$, with $u_e$ from the lens formula ($v_e = -25$ cm).

Show solution
  1. (i) $M = \dfrac{100}{4.0} = 25$, $L = 104\ \mathrm{cm}$.
  2. (ii) $M = 25\left(1 + \dfrac{4.0}{25}\right) = 25 \times 1.16 = 29$.
  3. $\dfrac{1}{u_e} = \dfrac{1}{4.0} - \dfrac{1}{-25} = 0.25 + 0.04 = 0.29 \implies u_e \approx 3.45\ \mathrm{cm}$, so $L \approx 103.4\ \mathrm{cm}$.

(i) $M = 25$, $L = 104$ cm   (ii) $M = 29$, $L \approx 103.4$ cm

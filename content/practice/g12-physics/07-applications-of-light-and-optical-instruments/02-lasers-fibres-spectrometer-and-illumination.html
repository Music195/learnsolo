Lasers, Optical Fibres, the Spectrometer and Illumination

Six problems on photon energy and photon rate, critical angle and numerical aperture, the prism spectrometer and illuminance $E = \dfrac{I\cos\theta}{r^2}$. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A green laser pointer has wavelength $532\ \mathrm{nm}$. Find the energy of one photon. ($h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$, $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; type $E$ in units of $10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Lasers and Stimulated Emission

Show hint

$E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$.

Show solution
  1. $E = \dfrac{1.989 \times 10^{-25}}{532 \times 10^{-9}} \approx 3.74 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$.

$E \approx 3.74 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 2

An optical fibre has a core of refractive index $1.50$ and cladding of $1.40$. Find the critical angle at the core–cladding boundary. (Type $\theta_c$ in degrees, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Fibre Optics and Endoscopy

Show hint

$\sin\theta_c = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin\theta_c = \dfrac{1.40}{1.50} = 0.9333$.
  2. $\theta_c \approx 69.0^\circ$. Rays meeting the boundary at more than this are totally internally reflected.

$\theta_c \approx 69.0^\circ$

Problem 3

How many photons per second does a $1.0\ \mathrm{mW}$ red laser of wavelength $650\ \mathrm{nm}$ give out? (Type $n$ in units of $10^{15}$ photons per second, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Lasers and Stimulated Emission

Show hint

$n = \dfrac{P\lambda}{hc}$.

Show solution
  1. $n = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 650 \times 10^{-9}}{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^8} = \dfrac{6.5 \times 10^{-10}}{1.989 \times 10^{-25}} \approx 3.27 \times 10^{15}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

$n \approx 3.27 \times 10^{15}$ photons per second

Problem 4

For the fibre with $n_1 = 1.50$ and $n_2 = 1.40$, find the numerical aperture and the largest angle to the axis at which light from air can enter and still be guided. (Type NA, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Fibre Optics and Endoscopy

Show hint

$\mathrm{NA} = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} = \sin\alpha_{\max}$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{NA} = \sqrt{1.50^2 - 1.40^2} = \sqrt{2.25 - 1.96} = \sqrt{0.29} \approx 0.539$.
  2. $\alpha_{\max} = \sin^{-1}(0.539) \approx 32.6^\circ$.

$\mathrm{NA} \approx 0.539$, $\alpha_{\max} \approx 32.6^\circ$

Problem 5

On a spectrometer a glass prism of angle $60^\circ$ gives a minimum deviation of $40^\circ$ for yellow light. Find the refractive index of the glass. (Type $n$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: The Optical Spectrometer

Show hint

$n = \dfrac{\sin\left(\frac{A + \delta_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}$.

Show solution
  1. $n = \dfrac{\sin 50^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ} = \dfrac{0.7660}{0.5000} \approx 1.53$.

$n \approx 1.53$

Problem 6

A lamp of luminous intensity $400\ \mathrm{cd}$ hangs $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ above the floor. Find the illuminance on the floor (i) directly below it and (ii) at a point $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from the spot directly below. (Type the answer to (ii) in lux, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Photometry and Illumination

Show hint

$E = \dfrac{I\cos\theta}{r^2}$, where $r$ is the distance from the lamp and $\theta$ the angle from the vertical.

Show solution
  1. (i) $E = \dfrac{400 \times 1}{2.0^2} = 100\ \mathrm{lx}$.
  2. (ii) $r = \sqrt{2.0^2 + 1.5^2} = 2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ and $\cos\theta = \dfrac{2.0}{2.5} = 0.80$.
  3. $E = \dfrac{400 \times 0.80}{2.5^2} = \dfrac{320}{6.25} = 51.2\ \mathrm{lx}$: about half, from the larger distance and the slant.

(i) $100\ \mathrm{lx}$   (ii) $51.2\ \mathrm{lx}$

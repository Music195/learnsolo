Forces That Supply the Centripetal Force

Six problems where tension, friction, gravity or the normal force on a banked road provides $F_c = mv^2/r$, including a vertical circle. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A $0.50\ \mathrm{kg}$ stone on a string moves in a horizontal circle of radius $1.2\ \mathrm{m}$ at $6.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ (ignore gravity\'s effect on the string). Find the tension. (Type $T$ in N.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

The tension is the only horizontal force, so it is the centripetal force.

Show solution
  1. $T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} = \dfrac{0.50 \times 36}{1.2} = 15\ \mathrm{N}$.

$T = 15\ \mathrm{N}$

Problem 2

A $1000\ \mathrm{kg}$ car rounds a flat curve of radius $50\ \mathrm{m}$ at $15\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What force must friction supply, and what is the smallest coefficient of friction that allows this? (Type the force in N.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

Friction is the centripetal force: $f = mv^2/r \le \mu mg$.

Show solution
  1. $f = \dfrac{1000 \times 15^2}{50} = 4500\ \mathrm{N}$.
  2. $\mu_{\min} = \dfrac{f}{mg} = \dfrac{4500}{1000 \times 9.8} \approx 0.46$.

$f = 4500\ \mathrm{N}$, $\mu \ge 0.46$

Problem 3

At what angle should a road curve of radius $40\ \mathrm{m}$ be banked so that a car at $50\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$ needs no friction? (Type the angle in degrees, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

For a frictionless banked curve $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{rg}$. Change km/h to m/s first.

Show solution
  1. $v = 50 \times \dfrac{1000}{3600} = 13.89\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{rg} = \dfrac{13.89^2}{40 \times 9.8} = 0.492$.
  3. $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.492) \approx 26.2^\circ$.

$\theta \approx 26.2^\circ$

Problem 4

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ bucket of water is whirled in a vertical circle on a rope of length $0.80\ \mathrm{m}$. What is the smallest speed at the top so that the water stays in? What is the tension at the top when the speed there is $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$? (Type the smallest speed in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

At the top both gravity and tension point down: $T + mg = mv^2/r$. The smallest speed has $T = 0$.

Show solution
  1. Smallest speed: $mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \implies v = \sqrt{gr} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 0.80} \approx 2.80\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. At $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$: $T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} - mg = \dfrac{2.0 \times 16}{0.80} - 2.0 \times 9.8 = 40 - 19.6 = 20.4\ \mathrm{N}$.

$v_{\min} \approx 2.80\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; $T = 20.4\ \mathrm{N}$ at $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 5

The same $2.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ bucket ($r = 0.80\ \mathrm{m}$) passes the bottom of the circle at $6.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the tension in the rope there. (Type $T$ in N, to 4 significant figures.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

At the bottom, tension points up (to the centre) and gravity down: $T - mg = mv^2/r$.

Show solution
  1. $T = mg + \dfrac{mv^2}{r} = 2.0 \times 9.8 + \dfrac{2.0 \times 36}{0.80} = 19.6 + 90 = 109.6\ \mathrm{N}$.
  2. The rope pulls hardest at the bottom, so that is where it is most likely to break.

$T \approx 110\ \mathrm{N}$ ($109.6\ \mathrm{N}$)

Problem 6

A plane flies a horizontal circle of radius $2.0\ \mathrm{km}$ at $720\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$. At what angle must it bank so that the lift (perpendicular to the wings) provides the centripetal force? (Type the angle in degrees, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

This is the banked-curve result again: vertical lift component $= mg$, horizontal component $= mv^2/r$, so $\tan\theta = v^2/(rg)$.

Show solution
  1. $v = 720 / 3.6 = 200\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $r = 2000\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{200^2}{2000 \times 9.8} = \dfrac{40\,000}{19\,600} = 2.04$.
  3. $\theta = \tan^{-1}(2.04) \approx 63.9^\circ$.

$\theta \approx 63.9^\circ$

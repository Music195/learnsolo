Forces in Circular Motion

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems on what supplies the centripetal force, banking, vertical circles and the centrifugal force. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A $0.20\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball on a $0.50\ \mathrm{m}$ string is whirled in a horizontal circle at $3.0$ revolutions per second. Find the tension. (Type $T$ in N, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

$\omega = 2\pi f$ and $T = mr\omega^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = 2\pi \times 3.0 = 6\pi\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $T = 0.20 \times 0.50 \times (6\pi)^2 = 3.6\pi^2 \approx 35.5\ \mathrm{N}$.

$T \approx 35.5\ \mathrm{N}$

Problem 2

A curve of radius $80\ \mathrm{m}$ is banked at $15^\circ$. At what speed can a car take it with no friction needed? (Type $v$ in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

$\tan\theta = v^2/(rg)$, so $v = \sqrt{rg\tan\theta}$.

Show solution
  1. $v = \sqrt{80 \times 9.8 \times \tan 15^\circ} = \sqrt{784 \times 0.268} = \sqrt{210} \approx 14.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ (about $52\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$).

$v \approx 14.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 3

A $1200\ \mathrm{kg}$ car goes over the top of a hump-backed bridge of radius $20\ \mathrm{m}$ at $10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the normal force from the road. At what speed would the car just lose contact? (Type the normal force in N.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

At the top the centre is below the car: $mg - N = mv^2/r$.

Show solution
  1. $N = mg - \dfrac{mv^2}{r} = 1200 \times 9.8 - \dfrac{1200 \times 100}{20} = 11\,760 - 6000 = 5760\ \mathrm{N}$.
  2. Losing contact: $N = 0 \implies v = \sqrt{gr} = \sqrt{196} = 14\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

$N = 5760\ \mathrm{N}$; contact is lost at $14\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 4

Explain, using the idea of centripetal force, why a car skids outward on an icy curve, and why passengers in the car say they are "thrown outward".

Uses: Centripetal and Centrifugal Forces

Show hint

Compare the friction needed, $mv^2/r$, with the friction available.

Show solution
  1. On ice the greatest friction $\mu mg$ is small. If $mv^2/r > \mu mg$, the road cannot give the needed inward force, so the car moves on a wider path than the curve: it skids outward.
  2. Passengers keep moving in a straight line by inertia while the car turns. Relative to the car they move outward; in the car's rotating frame this is described by a centrifugal (fictitious) force.

Not enough inward friction ($\mu mg < mv^2/r$); "thrown outward" is inertia seen from a rotating frame

Problem 5

A conical pendulum has a $0.30\ \mathrm{kg}$ bob on a $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ string making $30^\circ$ with the vertical. Find the tension, the speed of the bob and its period. (Type the period in s, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

Vertical: $T\cos\theta = mg$. Horizontal: $T\sin\theta = mv^2/r$ with $r = L\sin\theta$.

Show solution
  1. $T = \dfrac{mg}{\cos 30^\circ} = \dfrac{0.30 \times 9.8}{0.866} \approx 3.39\ \mathrm{N}$.
  2. $r = 1.0 \sin 30^\circ = 0.50\ \mathrm{m}$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{rg} \implies v = \sqrt{rg\tan 30^\circ} = \sqrt{0.50 \times 9.8 \times 0.577} \approx 1.68\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
  3. Period $= \dfrac{2\pi r}{v} = \dfrac{2\pi \times 0.50}{1.68} \approx 1.87\ \mathrm{s}$.

$T \approx 3.39\ \mathrm{N}$, $v \approx 1.68\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, period $\approx 1.87\ \mathrm{s}$

Problem 6

A pilot of mass $70\ \mathrm{kg}$ flies a vertical loop of radius $500\ \mathrm{m}$ at a constant $150\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the force of the seat on the pilot (i) at the bottom and (ii) at the top of the loop. (Type the force at the bottom in N.)

Uses: Forces Causing Centripetal Acceleration, Centripetal and Centrifugal Forces

Show hint

At the bottom the seat pushes up: $N - mg = mv^2/r$. At the top the seat pushes down (the pilot is upside down): $N + mg = mv^2/r$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{mv^2}{r} = \dfrac{70 \times 150^2}{500} = 3150\ \mathrm{N}$ and $mg = 686\ \mathrm{N}$.
  2. (i) Bottom: $N = 3150 + 686 = 3836\ \mathrm{N}$ (about 5.6 times her weight).
  3. (ii) Top: $N = 3150 - 686 = 2464\ \mathrm{N}$.

(i) $3836\ \mathrm{N}$   (ii) $2464\ \mathrm{N}$

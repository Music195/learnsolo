Sensors, Zener Regulators and Logic Gates

Six problems on potential-divider sensor circuits $V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}}\dfrac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$, Zener regulation and the outputs of logic gates (type 0 or 1 for logic answers). Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A light sensor is a $10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ fixed resistor $R_1$ in series with an LDR $R_2$ across $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$; $V_{\text{out}}$ is taken across the LDR. In the dark the LDR is $20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Find $V_{\text{out}}$. (Type it in V.)

Uses: Electronic Systems and Sensor Components

Show hint

$V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}}\dfrac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$.

Show solution
  1. $V_{\text{out}} = 9.0 \times \dfrac{20}{10 + 20} = 6.0\ \mathrm{V}$.
  2. In bright light the LDR resistance falls, so $V_{\text{out}}$ falls: the circuit can switch on a lamp when it gets dark.

$V_{\text{out}} = 6.0\ \mathrm{V}$

Problem 2

What is the output of a NAND gate when both inputs are $1$? And of a NOR gate when both inputs are $0$? (Type the NAND output.)

Uses: Digital Electronics and Logic Gates

Show hint

NAND is AND followed by NOT; NOR is OR followed by NOT.

Show solution
  1. NAND: $1 \cdot 1 = 1$, then NOT gives $Y = 0$.
  2. NOR: $0 + 0 = 0$, then NOT gives $Y = 1$.

NAND gives $0$; NOR gives $1$

Problem 3

A $6.0\ \mathrm{V}$ Zener diode regulates the supply to a load that takes $20\ \mathrm{mA}$. The input is $12\ \mathrm{V}$ and the series resistor is $100\ \Omega$. Find the current through the series resistor and through the Zener. (Type $I_Z$ in mA.)

Uses: Electronic Systems and Sensor Components

Show hint

The load voltage is held at $V_Z$, so the series resistor has $V_{\text{in}} - V_Z$ across it. $I_S = I_Z + I_{\text{load}}$.

Show solution
  1. $I_S = \dfrac{12 - 6.0}{100} = 0.060\ \mathrm{A} = 60\ \mathrm{mA}$.
  2. $I_Z = I_S - I_{\text{load}} = 60 - 20 = 40\ \mathrm{mA}$. If the load takes more, $I_Z$ falls but the output stays at $6.0$ V.

$I_S = 60\ \mathrm{mA}$, $I_Z = 40\ \mathrm{mA}$

Problem 4

A thermistor is $10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $1.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ at $100^\circ\mathrm{C}$. It is $R_1$ in a divider with a fixed $4.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor $R_2$ across $5.0\ \mathrm{V}$; $V_{\text{out}}$ is across $R_2$. Find $V_{\text{out}}$ at $100^\circ\mathrm{C}$. (Type it in V, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Electronic Systems and Sensor Components

Show hint

$V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}}\dfrac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$, with $R_1$ the thermistor.

Show solution
  1. At $100^\circ\mathrm{C}$: $V_{\text{out}} = 5.0 \times \dfrac{4.7}{1.0 + 4.7} \approx 4.12\ \mathrm{V}$.
  2. At $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$ it is only $5.0 \times \dfrac{4.7}{14.7} \approx 1.60\ \mathrm{V}$, so a rising $V_{\text{out}}$ signals "hot".

$V_{\text{out}} \approx 4.12\ \mathrm{V}$

Problem 5

For the circuit $Y = (A \cdot B) + \overline{C}$, write the truth table. For how many of the $8$ input combinations is $Y = 1$? (Type the number.)

Uses: Digital Electronics and Logic Gates

Show hint

$Y = 1$ if either $A \cdot B = 1$ or $C = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $C = 0$: $\overline{C} = 1$, so $Y = 1$ for all four rows with $C = 0$.
  2. $C = 1$: $Y = A \cdot B$, which is $1$ only for $A = B = 1$.
  3. Total: $4 + 1 = 5$ rows give $Y = 1$.

$Y = 1$ in $5$ of the $8$ rows

Problem 6

NAND gates are called "universal". Show how to make (i) a NOT gate and (ii) an OR gate using only NAND gates. How many NAND gates does the OR gate need? (Type the number.)

Uses: Digital Electronics and Logic Gates

Show hint

Joining both inputs of a NAND gives NOT. De Morgan: $A + B = \overline{\overline{A} \cdot \overline{B}}$.

Show solution
  1. (i) Join the two inputs: $Y = \overline{A \cdot A} = \overline{A}$.
  2. (ii) Make $\overline{A}$ and $\overline{B}$ with two NAND-NOTs, then NAND them: $\overline{\overline{A} \cdot \overline{B}} = A + B$.
  3. That uses $3$ NAND gates. Check $A = 1$, $B = 0$: $\overline{A} = 0$, $\overline{B} = 1$, NAND$(0, 1) = 1$ ✓.

NOT: one NAND with joined inputs; OR: $3$ NAND gates

Angular Kinematics and Linear–Angular Relations

Six problems on rpm and rad s⁻¹, the four equations of constant angular acceleration, and the links $s = r\theta$, $v = r\omega$, $a_T = r\alpha$. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A wheel starts from rest and turns with a constant angular acceleration of $4.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$. What is its angular velocity after $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$? (Type $\omega$ in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration

Show hint

Use $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t$ with $\omega_0 = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega_0 = 0$, $\alpha = 4.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$, $t = 5.0\ \mathrm{s}$.
  2. $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t = 0 + 4.0 \times 5.0 = 20\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.

$\omega = 20\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 2

A motor turns at $1200\ \mathrm{rpm}$. Express its angular velocity in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$. (You may type an answer such as 40π.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration

Show hint

One revolution is $2\pi$ rad and one minute is $60$ s.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = 1200\ \dfrac{\text{rev}}{\text{min}} \times \dfrac{2\pi\ \text{rad}}{1\ \text{rev}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{min}}{60\ \text{s}}$.
  2. $\omega = \dfrac{1200 \times 2\pi}{60} = 40\pi \approx 125.7\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.

$\omega = 40\pi \approx 126\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 3

A ceiling fan turning at $300\ \mathrm{rpm}$ is switched off and slows uniformly, making $50$ revolutions before it stops. Find (i) its angular acceleration and (ii) the time it takes to stop. (Type the time in s.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration

Show hint

Change both quantities to radians: $\omega_0$ in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ and $\theta$ in rad. Then use $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\theta$ with $\omega = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega_0 = 300 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{60} = 10\pi\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ and $\theta = 50 \times 2\pi = 100\pi\ \mathrm{rad}$.
  2. $0 = (10\pi)^2 + 2\alpha(100\pi) \implies \alpha = -\dfrac{100\pi^2}{200\pi} = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} \approx -1.57\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$.
  3. $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t \implies 0 = 10\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{2}t \implies t = 20\ \mathrm{s}$.
  4. Check: $\bar\omega t = \dfrac{10\pi}{2} \times 20 = 100\pi$ rad = 50 rev. ✓

(i) $\alpha = -\pi/2 \approx -1.57\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$   (ii) $t = 20\ \mathrm{s}$

Problem 4

A car with wheels of radius $0.30\ \mathrm{m}$ travels at $18\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ without slipping. Find the angular velocity of the wheels and the number of revolutions they make each second. (Type $\omega$ in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Relations between Linear and Angular Quantities

Show hint

For rolling without slipping the rim speed equals the car speed: $v = r\omega$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = \dfrac{v}{r} = \dfrac{18}{0.30} = 60\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. Revolutions per second: $f = \dfrac{\omega}{2\pi} = \dfrac{60}{2\pi} \approx 9.55\ \mathrm{rev\,s^{-1}}$.

$\omega = 60\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, about $9.5$ revolutions per second

Problem 5

A grindstone of radius $0.25\ \mathrm{m}$ starts from rest with $\alpha = 2.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$. After $4.0\ \mathrm{s}$, find for a point on its rim (i) the linear speed, (ii) the tangential acceleration and (iii) the distance it has travelled. (Type the distance in m.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration, Relations between Linear and Angular Quantities

Show hint

Find $\omega$ and $\theta$ first, then multiply by $r$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = \alpha t = 2.0 \times 4.0 = 8.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, so $v = r\omega = 0.25 \times 8.0 = 2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $a_T = r\alpha = 0.25 \times 2.0 = 0.50\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.
  3. $\theta = \tfrac12\alpha t^2 = \tfrac12 \times 2.0 \times 16 = 16\ \mathrm{rad}$, so $s = r\theta = 0.25 \times 16 = 4.0\ \mathrm{m}$.

(i) $2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$   (ii) $0.50\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$   (iii) $4.0\ \mathrm{m}$

Problem 6

A wheel accelerating uniformly turns through $90\ \mathrm{rad}$ in $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$, and at the end of that time its angular velocity is $40\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$. Find its angular velocity at the start of the $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$ and its angular acceleration. (Type $\omega_0$ in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration

Show hint

With constant $\alpha$ the average angular velocity is $\dfrac{\omega_0 + \omega}{2}$, and $\theta = \bar\omega t$.

Show solution
  1. $\theta = \dfrac{\omega_0 + \omega}{2}\,t \implies 90 = \dfrac{\omega_0 + 40}{2} \times 3.0$.
  2. $\omega_0 + 40 = 60 \implies \omega_0 = 20\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  3. $\alpha = \dfrac{\omega - \omega_0}{t} = \dfrac{40 - 20}{3.0} \approx 6.67\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$.

$\omega_0 = 20\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, $\alpha \approx 6.7\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$

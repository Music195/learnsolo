Rotational Motion

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems mixing angular kinematics, linear–angular relations and centripetal acceleration. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A wheel makes $2.0$ revolutions per second. What is its angular velocity in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$? (You may type 4π.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration

Show hint

$\omega = 2\pi f$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 2.0 = 4\pi \approx 12.6\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.

$\omega = 4\pi \approx 12.6\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 2

A flywheel slows uniformly from $30\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ to $10\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ in $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$. Find its angular acceleration, the angle it turns through and the number of revolutions. (Type the angle in rad.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration

Show hint

$\alpha = \dfrac{\omega - \omega_0}{t}$ and $\theta = \bar\omega t$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha = \dfrac{10 - 30}{5.0} = -4.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$.
  2. $\theta = \dfrac{30 + 10}{2} \times 5.0 = 100\ \mathrm{rad}$.
  3. Revolutions: $\dfrac{100}{2\pi} \approx 15.9$.

$\alpha = -4.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$, $\theta = 100\ \mathrm{rad} \approx 15.9$ rev

Problem 3

A rope is wound on a drum of radius $0.20\ \mathrm{m}$. A bucket on the rope falls from rest with acceleration $1.2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Find the drum\'s angular acceleration and its angular velocity after $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$, and the length of rope unwound. (Type $\omega$ in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Relations between Linear and Angular Quantities

Show hint

The rope\'s acceleration is the tangential acceleration of the drum rim: $a_T = r\alpha$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha = \dfrac{a_T}{r} = \dfrac{1.2}{0.20} = 6.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$.
  2. $\omega = \alpha t = 6.0 \times 3.0 = 18\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  3. Rope unwound $= \tfrac12 a t^2 = \tfrac12 \times 1.2 \times 9.0 = 5.4\ \mathrm{m}$ (or $r\theta = 0.20 \times 27 = 5.4$ m).

$\alpha = 6.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$, $\omega = 18\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, $5.4\ \mathrm{m}$ of rope

Problem 4

A satellite moves at $7.5 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in a circular orbit of radius $7.0 \times 10^6\ \mathrm{m}$. Find its centripetal acceleration. (Type $a_c$ in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

$a_c = v^2/r$; this acceleration is provided by gravity.

Show solution
  1. $a_c = \dfrac{(7.5 \times 10^3)^2}{7.0 \times 10^6} = \dfrac{5.625 \times 10^7}{7.0 \times 10^6} \approx 8.04\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

$a_c \approx 8.04\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$

Problem 5

An ultracentrifuge must give a centripetal acceleration of $1.0 \times 10^5\,g$ to a sample $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ from the axis. Find the angular velocity needed and the rate in rpm. (Use $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; type $\omega$ in units of $10^3\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

$a_c = r\omega^2$, so $\omega = \sqrt{a_c / r}$.

Show solution
  1. $a_c = 1.0 \times 10^5 \times 9.8 = 9.8 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, $r = 0.10\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{9.8 \times 10^5}{0.10}} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 10^6} \approx 3130\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  3. rpm $= \omega \times \dfrac{60}{2\pi} \approx 29\,900\ \mathrm{rpm}$.

$\omega \approx 3.13 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, about $29\,900$ rpm

Problem 6

A wheel of radius $0.40\ \mathrm{m}$ starts from rest with $\alpha = 0.50\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$. At $t = 4.0\ \mathrm{s}$ find, for a point on the rim, (i) $a_T$, (ii) $a_c$ and (iii) the total acceleration. (Type the total in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Rotational Motion under Constant Angular Acceleration, Relations between Linear and Angular Quantities, Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

Find $\omega$ at $4.0$ s first. $a_T$ stays constant but $a_c$ grows as $\omega^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = \alpha t = 0.50 \times 4.0 = 2.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. (i) $a_T = r\alpha = 0.40 \times 0.50 = 0.20\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.
  3. (ii) $a_c = r\omega^2 = 0.40 \times 4.0 = 1.6\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.
  4. (iii) $a = \sqrt{0.20^2 + 1.6^2} = \sqrt{2.60} \approx 1.61\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

$a_T = 0.20$, $a_c = 1.6$, $a \approx 1.61\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$

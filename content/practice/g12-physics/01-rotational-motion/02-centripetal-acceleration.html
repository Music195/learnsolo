Centripetal and Total Acceleration

Six problems on $a_c = v^2/r = r\omega^2$, combining centripetal and tangential acceleration, and comparing points at different radii. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A cyclist rides at a steady $12\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ round a curve of radius $48\ \mathrm{m}$. What is her centripetal acceleration? (Type $a_c$ in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.)

Uses: Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

$a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$.

Show solution
  1. $a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{r} = \dfrac{12^2}{48} = \dfrac{144}{48} = 3.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, directed towards the centre.

$a_c = 3.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$

Problem 2

A point on a CD is $6.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ from the centre and the CD spins at $500\ \mathrm{rpm}$. Find its centripetal acceleration. (Type $a_c$ in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ to the nearest whole number.)

Uses: Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

Change rpm to $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, then use $a_c = r\omega^2$ with $r$ in metres.

Show solution
  1. $\omega = 500 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{60} = 52.36\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $a_c = r\omega^2 = 0.060 \times (52.36)^2 = 164.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

$a_c \approx 164\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ (about 17 g)

Problem 3

The Earth turns once in $24\ \mathrm{h}$ and its radius is $6.4 \times 10^6\ \mathrm{m}$. Find the centripetal acceleration of a person standing on the equator. (Type $a_c$ in $\mathrm{mm\,s^{-2}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

$\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ with $T$ in seconds.

Show solution
  1. $T = 24 \times 3600 = 86\,400\ \mathrm{s}$, so $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{86\,400} = 7.27 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $a_c = r\omega^2 = (6.4 \times 10^6)(7.27 \times 10^{-5})^2 = 0.0338\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.
  3. That is $33.8\ \mathrm{mm\,s^{-2}}$, only about $0.3\,\%$ of $g$.

$a_c \approx 0.0338\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}} = 33.8\ \mathrm{mm\,s^{-2}}$

Problem 4

A wheel of radius $0.50\ \mathrm{m}$ is speeding up. At one instant $\omega = 4.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ and $\alpha = 6.0\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$. Find the magnitude of the total acceleration of a rim point and its angle to the radius. (Type $a$ in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.)

Uses: Relations between Linear and Angular Quantities, Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

The centripetal part $r\omega^2$ and the tangential part $r\alpha$ are at right angles.

Show solution
  1. $a_c = r\omega^2 = 0.50 \times 16 = 8.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ (towards the centre).
  2. $a_T = r\alpha = 0.50 \times 6.0 = 3.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ (along the tangent).
  3. $a = \sqrt{a_c^2 + a_T^2} = \sqrt{64 + 9} = \sqrt{73} \approx 8.54\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.
  4. $\tan\phi = \dfrac{a_T}{a_c} = \dfrac{3.0}{8.0} \implies \phi \approx 20.6^\circ$ from the inward radius.

$a \approx 8.54\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ at $20.6^\circ$ to the radius

Problem 5

Two coins sit on a turntable, one $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ and one $20\ \mathrm{cm}$ from the centre. What is the ratio $a_c(\text{outer}) : a_c(\text{inner})$? Would the answer change if instead both moved at the same speed $v$ on circles of these radii?

Uses: Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

On one turntable every point has the same $\omega$. Choose between $r\omega^2$ and $v^2/r$ accordingly.

Show solution
  1. Same $\omega$: $a_c = r\omega^2 \propto r$, so $\dfrac{a_{\text{outer}}}{a_{\text{inner}}} = \dfrac{20}{10} = 2$.
  2. Same $v$ instead: $a_c = v^2/r \propto 1/r$, so the ratio would be $\dfrac{10}{20} = \dfrac12$.

$2 : 1$ on the turntable (same $\omega$); it would be $1 : 2$ at equal speeds

Problem 6

A car starts from rest on a circular track of radius $100\ \mathrm{m}$ and speeds up with a constant tangential acceleration of $2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. After what time is its centripetal acceleration equal to its tangential acceleration? What is its total acceleration then? (Type the time in s.)

Uses: Relations between Linear and Angular Quantities, Centripetal Acceleration

Show hint

Set $\dfrac{v^2}{r} = a_T$ and use $v = a_T t$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{v^2}{r} = a_T \implies v^2 = a_T r = 2.0 \times 100 = 200 \implies v = 14.1\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $t = \dfrac{v}{a_T} = \dfrac{\sqrt{200}}{2.0} = \sqrt{50} \approx 7.07\ \mathrm{s}$.
  3. Total: $a = \sqrt{2.0^2 + 2.0^2} = 2\sqrt2 \approx 2.83\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, at $45^\circ$ to the radius.

$t = \sqrt{50} \approx 7.07\ \mathrm{s}$; $a \approx 2.83\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$

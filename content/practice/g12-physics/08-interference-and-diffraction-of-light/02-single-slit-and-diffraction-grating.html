Single-Slit Diffraction and the Diffraction Grating

Six problems on single-slit minima $a\sin\theta = m\lambda$, the width of the central maximum and the grating equation $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$ with $d = 1/N$. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Light of wavelength $500\ \mathrm{nm}$ passes through a slit $0.10\ \mathrm{mm}$ wide onto a screen $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ away. Find the width of the central bright band. (Type $W$ in mm.)

Uses: Diffraction of Light and Single-Slit Diffraction

Show hint

$W = \dfrac{2\lambda D}{a}$.

Show solution
  1. $W = \dfrac{2 \times 500 \times 10^{-9} \times 2.0}{0.10 \times 10^{-3}} = 2.0 \times 10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m} = 20\ \mathrm{mm}$.

$W = 20\ \mathrm{mm}$

Problem 2

A grating has $5000$ lines per cm. Find the slit spacing $d$ and the angle of the first-order maximum for $\lambda = 500\ \mathrm{nm}$. (Type $\theta$ in degrees, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Diffraction Grating and Optical Spectra

Show hint

$d = 1/N$, then $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$.

Show solution
  1. $d = \dfrac{1}{5000}\ \mathrm{cm} = 2.0 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. $\sin\theta = \dfrac{500 \times 10^{-9}}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.25 \implies \theta \approx 14.5^\circ$.

$d = 2.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, $\theta \approx 14.5^\circ$

Problem 3

How many orders of maxima (on each side of the centre) can be seen with a grating of spacing $2.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ and light of wavelength $600\ \mathrm{nm}$? (Type the highest order $m$.)

Uses: Diffraction Grating and Optical Spectra

Show hint

$\sin\theta$ cannot exceed $1$, so $m \le d/\lambda$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{d}{\lambda} = \dfrac{2.0 \times 10^{-6}}{600 \times 10^{-9}} = 3.33$.
  2. $m$ must be a whole number, so the highest order is $m = 3$.

Up to the third order, $m_{\max} = 3$

Problem 4

Red light ($600\ \mathrm{nm}$) falls on a slit of width $2.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$. At what angle is the first dark band? (Type $\theta$ in degrees, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Diffraction of Light and Single-Slit Diffraction

Show hint

First minimum: $a\sin\theta = \lambda$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin\theta = \dfrac{600 \times 10^{-9}}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.30 \implies \theta \approx 17.5^\circ$.
  2. A narrower slit would give a larger angle: more spreading.

$\theta \approx 17.5^\circ$

Problem 5

With a grating of $4000$ lines per cm, the second-order image of a spectral line is at $30.0^\circ$. Find the wavelength. (Type $\lambda$ in nm.)

Uses: Diffraction Grating and Optical Spectra

Show hint

$\lambda = \dfrac{d\sin\theta}{m}$ with $d = 1/N$.

Show solution
  1. $d = \dfrac{1}{4000}\ \mathrm{cm} = 2.5 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. $\lambda = \dfrac{2.5 \times 10^{-6} \times \sin 30.0^\circ}{2} = \dfrac{2.5 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.500}{2} = 6.25 \times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} = 625\ \mathrm{nm}$.

$\lambda = 625\ \mathrm{nm}$

Problem 6

White light falls on a grating with $d = 2.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$. Find the angle of the second-order red ($700\ \mathrm{nm}$) and of the third-order violet ($400\ \mathrm{nm}$). What does this tell you about the second- and third-order spectra? (Type the angle for second-order red in degrees, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Diffraction Grating and Optical Spectra

Show hint

Work out $\sin\theta = m\lambda/d$ for each and compare the angles.

Show solution
  1. Second-order red: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{2 \times 700 \times 10^{-9}}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.70 \implies \theta \approx 44.4^\circ$.
  2. Third-order violet: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{3 \times 400 \times 10^{-9}}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.60 \implies \theta \approx 36.9^\circ$.
  3. The third order starts ($36.9^\circ$) before the second order ends ($44.4^\circ$), so these spectra overlap.

$44.4^\circ$ and $36.9^\circ$: the second and third orders overlap

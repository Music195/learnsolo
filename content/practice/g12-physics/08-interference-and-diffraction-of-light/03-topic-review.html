Interference and Diffraction of Light

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems on phase difference, double-slit fringes, single-slit diffraction and the diffraction grating. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Two waves from coherent sources reach a point with a path difference of $\lambda/4$. Find their phase difference. (Type $\Delta\phi$ in rad; you may type π/2.)

Uses: Wavefronts and Huygens' Principle

Show hint

$\Delta\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x$.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \times \dfrac{\lambda}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\ \mathrm{rad}$ ($90^\circ$): neither fully bright nor dark.

$\Delta\phi = \pi/2$ rad

Problem 2

In a double-slit experiment the distance across $10$ fringe spacings is $12\ \mathrm{mm}$. The slits are $0.50\ \mathrm{mm}$ apart and the screen is $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ away. Find the wavelength. (Type $\lambda$ in nm.)

Uses: Interference of Light and Young's Double-Slit Experiment

Show hint

Measuring across many fringes and dividing gives a more accurate $\Delta y$.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta y = \dfrac{12}{10} = 1.2\ \mathrm{mm}$.
  2. $\lambda = \dfrac{\Delta y\,d}{D} = \dfrac{1.2 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.50 \times 10^{-3}}{1.0} = 6.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} = 600\ \mathrm{nm}$.

$\lambda = 600\ \mathrm{nm}$

Problem 3

Light of wavelength $600\ \mathrm{nm}$ through a single slit gives a central bright band $6.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ wide on a screen $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ away. How wide is the slit? (Type $a$ in mm.)

Uses: Diffraction of Light and Single-Slit Diffraction

Show hint

Rearrange $W = \dfrac{2\lambda D}{a}$.

Show solution
  1. $a = \dfrac{2\lambda D}{W} = \dfrac{2 \times 600 \times 10^{-9} \times 1.5}{6.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 3.0 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m} = 0.30\ \mathrm{mm}$.

$a = 0.30\ \mathrm{mm}$

Problem 4

Use Huygens' principle to explain why waves spread out after passing through a gap. Why can we hear round a doorway but not see round it?

Uses: Wavefronts and Huygens' Principle, Diffraction of Light and Single-Slit Diffraction

Show hint

Every point on a wavefront is a source of wavelets. Compare the wavelength with the gap.

Show solution
  1. Each point of the wavefront in the gap sends out wavelets in all forward directions. Near the edges nothing cancels the sideways wavelets, so the new wavefront curves round into the shadow.
  2. The spreading is large only when the gap is about as small as the wavelength. Sound wavelengths ($\sim 1$ m) are like a doorway, so sound bends round it; light wavelengths ($\sim 5 \times 10^{-7}$ m) are far smaller, so light barely spreads.

Wavelets from the gap spread sideways; diffraction is strong only when $\lambda$ is comparable to the gap

Problem 5

The sodium "D lines" have wavelengths $589.0\ \mathrm{nm}$ and $589.6\ \mathrm{nm}$. With a grating of $600$ lines per mm, find the angles of the two lines in the second order and their separation. (Type the separation in minutes of arc, to 2 significant figures; $1^\circ = 60^{\prime}$.)

Uses: Diffraction Grating and Optical Spectra

Show hint

$d = 1/600\ \mathrm{mm}$; use $\sin\theta = 2\lambda/d$ for each line, keeping enough figures.

Show solution
  1. $d = 1.6667 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. $\sin\theta_1 = \dfrac{2 \times 589.0 \times 10^{-9}}{1.6667 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.70680 \implies \theta_1 \approx 44.975^\circ$.
  3. $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{2 \times 589.6 \times 10^{-9}}{1.6667 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.70752 \implies \theta_2 \approx 45.033^\circ$.
  4. Separation $\approx 0.058^\circ \approx 3.5^{\prime}$: small but easy to see in a spectrometer. A higher order would separate them more.

$\Delta\theta \approx 3.5$ minutes of arc

Problem 6

White light ($400$ to $700\ \mathrm{nm}$) falls on a grating with $5000$ lines per cm. A screen is $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ away, parallel to the grating. How wide is the first-order spectrum on the screen? (Type the width in cm, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Diffraction Grating and Optical Spectra

Show hint

Find each angle from $d\sin\theta = \lambda$, then the position $y = D\tan\theta$ (the angles are too big for $\tan\theta \approx \sin\theta$).

Show solution
  1. $d = 2.0 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}$. Violet: $\sin\theta = 0.20$, $\theta \approx 11.5^\circ$; red: $\sin\theta = 0.35$, $\theta \approx 20.5^\circ$.
  2. $y_{\text{violet}} = 1.0 \times \tan 11.5^\circ \approx 0.204\ \mathrm{m}$; $y_{\text{red}} = 1.0 \times \tan 20.5^\circ \approx 0.374\ \mathrm{m}$.
  3. Width $\approx 0.374 - 0.204 \approx 17.0\ \mathrm{cm}$.

About $17.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ wide

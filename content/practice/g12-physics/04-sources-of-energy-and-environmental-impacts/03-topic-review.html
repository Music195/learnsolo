Sources of Energy and Environmental Impacts

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems on energy demand, solar, wind and hydro power, efficiency and the environment. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A country uses $2.0 \times 10^{16}$ Btu of energy in a year. Find its average rate of energy use. ($1\ \mathrm{Btu} = 1055\ \mathrm{J}$, $1$ year $= 3.15 \times 10^7\ \mathrm{s}$; type the power in units of $10^{11}\ \mathrm{W}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Energy Demand and Crisis

Show hint

Power = energy ÷ time, with the energy in joules.

Show solution
  1. $E = 2.0 \times 10^{16} \times 1055 = 2.11 \times 10^{19}\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $P = \dfrac{2.11 \times 10^{19}}{3.15 \times 10^7} \approx 6.70 \times 10^{11}\ \mathrm{W}$.

$P \approx 6.70 \times 10^{11}\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 2

A silicon solar cell has a band gap $E_g = 1.1\ \mathrm{eV}$. Find the longest wavelength of light that can free an electron in it. ($h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$, $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; type $\lambda$ in nm, to the nearest 10 nm.)

Uses: Renewable Energy Sources — Solar and Wind

Show hint

$\lambda_{\max} = \dfrac{hc}{E_g}$ with $E_g$ in joules.

Show solution
  1. $E_g = 1.1 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 1.76 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $\lambda_{\max} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^8}{1.76 \times 10^{-19}} \approx 1.13 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m} = 1\,130\ \mathrm{nm}$.
  3. This is in the infrared, so all visible light can be used by the cell.

$\lambda_{\max} \approx 1\,130\ \mathrm{nm}$

Problem 3

A family needs to heat $200\ \mathrm{kg}$ of water from $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $60^\circ\mathrm{C}$ each day ($c = 4200\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$). Their solar collector receives $5.0\ \mathrm{kWh}$ per $\mathrm{m^2}$ per day and is $50\,\%$ efficient. What collector area is needed? (Type the area in $\mathrm{m^2}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Renewable Energy Sources — Solar and Wind, Energy Demand and Crisis

Show hint

Heat needed $Q = mc\Delta T$, changed to kWh. Each $\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $0.50 \times 5.0 = 2.5\ \mathrm{kWh}$ of heat per day.

Show solution
  1. $Q = 200 \times 4200 \times 40 = 3.36 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{J} = \dfrac{3.36 \times 10^{7}}{3.6 \times 10^6} \approx 9.33\ \mathrm{kWh}$.
  2. Heat per $\mathrm{m^2}$: $0.50 \times 5.0 = 2.5\ \mathrm{kWh\,m^{-2}}$.
  3. Area $= \dfrac{9.33}{2.5} \approx 3.73\ \mathrm{m^2}$.

Area $\approx 3.73\ \mathrm{m^2}$

Problem 4

Give two reasons why burning fossil fuels harms the environment, and explain why hydroelectric and wind power are called renewable. Name one environmental cost of a large dam.

Uses: Environmental Impacts and Energy Transformations, Renewable Energy Sources — Hydro, Ocean, Geothermal, and Biomass

Show hint

Think about the gases produced and about whether the source gets used up.

Show solution
  1. Burning coal, oil and gas releases CO₂, a greenhouse gas that causes global warming, and SO₂ and nitrogen oxides that cause acid rain and smog; extraction also damages land.
  2. Water and wind are kept moving by the Sun's energy (evaporation and uneven heating), so they are replaced naturally and do not run out.
  3. A large dam floods land and forests, moves people, blocks fish migration and changes the river downstream.

CO₂ (global warming) and SO₂/NOₓ (acid rain); water and wind are renewed by the Sun; dams flood land and disturb rivers

Problem 5

A wind farm has $20$ turbines with blades $30\ \mathrm{m}$ long. On a day with $8.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ wind ($\rho = 1.3\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$) each converts $35\,\%$ of the wind power through its blade circle. Find the total electrical output. (Type $P$ in MW, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Renewable Energy Sources — Solar and Wind

Show hint

$P = \eta \times \tfrac12\rho A v^3$ per turbine, times 20.

Show solution
  1. $A = \pi(30)^2 = 2827\ \mathrm{m^2}$ and $v^3 = 512\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-3}}$.
  2. One turbine: $P = 0.35 \times \tfrac12 \times 1.3 \times 2827 \times 512 \approx 3.29 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{W}$.
  3. Twenty turbines: $20 \times 3.29 \times 10^{5} \approx 6.59\ \mathrm{MW}$.

$P \approx 6.59\ \mathrm{MW}$

Problem 6

A hydroelectric plant with a head of $100\ \mathrm{m}$ and an efficiency of $80\,\%$ is to give the same output as the wind farm in the last problem. What flow of water does it need? Give one advantage of each source over the other. (Type $Q$ in $\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Renewable Energy Sources — Hydro, Ocean, Geothermal, and Biomass, Renewable Energy Sources — Solar and Wind

Show hint

$Q = \dfrac{P}{\eta\rho g h}$.

Show solution
  1. $Q = \dfrac{6.59 \times 10^{6}}{0.80 \times 1000 \times 9.8 \times 100} \approx 8.40\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. Hydro gives steady power whenever it is needed (water can be stored); wind needs no dam, so no land is flooded, but its output changes with the weather.

$Q \approx 8.40\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$

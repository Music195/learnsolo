Hydro, Ocean Energy and Efficiency

Six problems on hydroelectric power $P = \eta\rho Q g h$, tidal energy, efficiency chains and fuel use in power stations. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Water flows through the turbines of a dam at $50\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$ and falls $80\ \mathrm{m}$. The plant is $80\,\%$ efficient. Find its electrical power output. (Type $P$ in MW, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Renewable Energy Sources — Hydro, Ocean, Geothermal, and Biomass

Show hint

$P = \eta\rho Q g h$.

Show solution
  1. $P = 0.80 \times 1000 \times 50 \times 9.8 \times 80 = 3.136 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{W} = 31.4\ \mathrm{MW}$.

$P \approx 31.4\ \mathrm{MW}$

Problem 2

A power station receives $500\ \mathrm{MW}$ of heat from burning fuel and delivers $175\ \mathrm{MW}$ of electrical power. Find its efficiency and the power wasted. (Type the efficiency in %.)

Uses: Environmental Impacts and Energy Transformations

Show hint

$\eta = \dfrac{\text{useful output}}{\text{total input}} \times 100\,\%$.

Show solution
  1. $\eta = \dfrac{175}{500} \times 100\,\% = 35\,\%$.
  2. Wasted: $500 - 175 = 325\ \mathrm{MW}$, mostly as heat to the cooling water and the air.

$\eta = 35\,\%$; $325\ \mathrm{MW}$ wasted

Problem 3

A hydroelectric plant must give $20\ \mathrm{MW}$ from a head of $40\ \mathrm{m}$ with an efficiency of $75\,\%$. What volume of water must flow through it each second? (Type $Q$ in $\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Renewable Energy Sources — Hydro, Ocean, Geothermal, and Biomass

Show hint

Rearrange: $\dfrac{m}{t} = \dfrac{P}{\eta g h}$, then $Q = \dfrac{m/t}{\rho}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{m}{t} = \dfrac{20 \times 10^6}{0.75 \times 9.8 \times 40} = \dfrac{20 \times 10^6}{294} \approx 68\,027\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $Q = \dfrac{68\,027}{1000} \approx 68.0\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$.

$Q \approx 68.0\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 4

A tidal basin of area $1.0\ \mathrm{km^2}$ is filled at high tide and emptied through turbines when the sea has fallen $4.0\ \mathrm{m}$. Find the potential energy released, and the average power if this takes $6.0$ hours. (Type the power in MW, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Renewable Energy Sources — Hydro, Ocean, Geothermal, and Biomass

Show hint

The trapped water has mass $\rho A h$, and its centre of mass falls $h/2$, so $E = \tfrac12\rho A g h^2$.

Show solution
  1. Mass $= \rho A h = 1000 \times 1.0 \times 10^6 \times 4.0 = 4.0 \times 10^9\ \mathrm{kg}$.
  2. $E = mg\dfrac{h}{2} = 4.0 \times 10^9 \times 9.8 \times 2.0 = 7.84 \times 10^{10}\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. $P = \dfrac{E}{t} = \dfrac{7.84 \times 10^{10}}{6.0 \times 3600} \approx 3.63\ \mathrm{MW}$.

$E = 7.84 \times 10^{10}\ \mathrm{J}$, average power $\approx 3.63\ \mathrm{MW}$

Problem 5

Electricity from a power station ($40\,\%$ efficient) passes through transmission lines ($90\,\%$ efficient) to an electric motor ($80\,\%$ efficient). What percentage of the fuel's energy finally does useful work? (Type the overall efficiency in %, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Environmental Impacts and Energy Transformations

Show hint

Efficiencies in a chain multiply.

Show solution
  1. $\eta = 0.40 \times 0.90 \times 0.80 = 0.288 = 28.8\,\%$.
  2. More than $70\,\%$ of the fuel energy ends up as waste heat along the way.

$\eta_{\text{overall}} = 28.8\,\%$

Problem 6

A coal-fired power station delivers $600\ \mathrm{MW}$ of electrical power at an efficiency of $38\,\%$. Coal releases $30\ \mathrm{MJ}$ per kg when burned. How many tonnes of coal does it burn each day? (Type the answer to the nearest 10 tonnes.)

Uses: Environmental Impacts and Energy Transformations, Non-Renewable Sources of Energy

Show hint

Input power $= \dfrac{\text{output}}{\eta}$; coal per second $= \dfrac{\text{input power}}{\text{energy per kg}}$.

Show solution
  1. Input power $= \dfrac{600}{0.38} \approx 1579\ \mathrm{MW}$.
  2. Coal per second $= \dfrac{1579 \times 10^6}{30 \times 10^6} \approx 52.6\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$.
  3. Per day: $52.6 \times 86\,400 \approx 4.55 \times 10^{6}\ \mathrm{kg} \approx 4\,550$ tonnes, which also puts thousands of tonnes of CO₂ into the air.

About $4\,550$ tonnes of coal per day

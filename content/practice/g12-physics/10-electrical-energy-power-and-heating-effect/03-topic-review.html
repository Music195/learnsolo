Problem 1

A lamp on a $220\ \mathrm{V}$ supply takes a current of $0.50\ \mathrm{A}$. What is its power? (Type $P$ in W.)

Uses: Electrical Energy and Electrical Power

Show hint

$P = VI$.

Show solution
  1. $P = 220 \times 0.50 = 110\ \mathrm{W}$.

$P = 110\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 2

How many $100\ \mathrm{W}$ lamps can be run at the same time from a $220\ \mathrm{V}$ circuit with a $5\ \mathrm{A}$ fuse? (Type the number.)

Uses: Applications of Heating Effect and Electrical Safety

Show hint

$P_{\max} = V I_{\text{fuse}}$, then divide and round down.

Show solution
  1. $P_{\max} = 220 \times 5 = 1100\ \mathrm{W}$.
  2. $N = \dfrac{1100}{100} = 11$ lamps. A twelfth lamp would make $I \approx 5.45\ \mathrm{A}$ and blow the fuse.

$11$ lamps

Problem 3

A cell has emf $2.0\ \mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $0.40\ \Omega$. What load resistance takes the most power from it, and how much power is that? (Type $P$ in W.)

Uses: Electromotive Force and Internal Resistance, Electrical Energy and Electrical Power

Show hint

Try a few values of $R$ in $P = \left(\dfrac{E}{R + r}\right)^2 R$: the power is greatest when $R = r$.

Show solution
  1. For $R = 0.20$, $0.40$, $0.80\ \Omega$: $P = 2.22$, $2.50$, $2.22\ \mathrm{W}$. The maximum is at $R = r = 0.40\ \Omega$.
  2. $I = \dfrac{2.0}{0.80} = 2.5\ \mathrm{A}$, so $P = 2.5^2 \times 0.40 = 2.5\ \mathrm{W}$.

$R = 0.40\ \Omega$; $P_{\max} = 2.5\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 4

The element of a $220\ \mathrm{V}$ kettle takes $10\ \mathrm{A}$. Find its resistance and the heat produced in $5.0$ minutes, in kilocalories. ($J = 4.2\ \mathrm{J\,cal^{-1}}$; type $H$ in kcal, to the nearest kcal.)

Uses: Joule's Law and Heating Effect of Electric Current

Show hint

$R = V/I$; $W = VIt$; $H = W/J$.

Show solution
  1. $R = \dfrac{220}{10} = 22\ \Omega$ and $P = 220 \times 10 = 2200\ \mathrm{W}$.
  2. $W = 2200 \times 300 = 6.6 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. $H = \dfrac{6.6 \times 10^5}{4.2} \approx 157\,143\ \mathrm{cal} \approx 157\ \mathrm{kcal}$.

$R = 22\ \Omega$; $H \approx 157\ \mathrm{kcal}$

Problem 5

A cell of emf $6.0\ \mathrm{V}$ (internal resistance $1.0\ \Omega$) and a cell of emf $2.0\ \mathrm{V}$ (internal resistance $1.0\ \Omega$) are joined in series opposition with a $2.0\ \Omega$ resistor. Find the current and the terminal voltage of the $6.0\ \mathrm{V}$ cell. (Type that voltage in V.)

Uses: Batteries in Series and Parallel Combinations, Electromotive Force and Internal Resistance

Show hint

$E_{\text{net}} = 6.0 - 2.0$. The $6.0$ V cell is discharging, so $V = E - Ir$.

Show solution
  1. $I = \dfrac{6.0 - 2.0}{1.0 + 1.0 + 2.0} = 1.0\ \mathrm{A}$.
  2. $V = 6.0 - 1.0 \times 1.0 = 5.0\ \mathrm{V}$.
  3. Of the $6.0\ \mathrm{W}$ the big cell supplies, $1.0$ W heats it, $2.0$ W heats the resistor, $1.0$ W heats the small cell and $2.0$ W charges it.

$I = 1.0\ \mathrm{A}$; $V = 5.0\ \mathrm{V}$

Problem 6

Two heaters rated $1000\ \mathrm{W}$, $220\ \mathrm{V}$ and $500\ \mathrm{W}$, $220\ \mathrm{V}$ are joined in series to the $220\ \mathrm{V}$ mains. Find the power taken by each and the total. Which glows hotter? (Type the total power in W, to the nearest watt.)

Uses: Electrical Energy and Electrical Power

Show hint

Find each resistance from its rating; in series the same current flows, so $P = I^2R$ is larger in the larger resistance.

Show solution
  1. $R_1 = \dfrac{220^2}{1000} = 48.4\ \Omega$, $R_2 = \dfrac{220^2}{500} = 96.8\ \Omega$; total $145.2\ \Omega$.
  2. $I = \dfrac{220}{145.2} \approx 1.52\ \mathrm{A}$.
  3. $P_1 = I^2R_1 \approx 111.1\ \mathrm{W}$, $P_2 = I^2R_2 \approx 222.2\ \mathrm{W}$, total $\approx 333\ \mathrm{W}$. The $500$ W heater takes more power in series.

$111.1\ \mathrm{W}$ and $222.2\ \mathrm{W}$, total $\approx 333\ \mathrm{W}$; the $500\ \mathrm{W}$ heater is hotter

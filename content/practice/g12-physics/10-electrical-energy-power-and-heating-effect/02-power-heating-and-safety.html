Electrical Power, Joule Heating and Safety

Six problems on $P = VI = I^2R = V^2/R$, Joule's law $H = I^2Rt/J$ with $J = 4.2\ \mathrm{J\,cal^{-1}}$, electricity bills in kWh and fuse ratings. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A heater is rated $1000\ \mathrm{W}$, $220\ \mathrm{V}$. Find its resistance and the current it draws. (Type $R$ in $\Omega$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Electrical Energy and Electrical Power

Show hint

$R = \dfrac{V^2}{P}$ and $I = \dfrac{P}{V}$.

Show solution
  1. $R = \dfrac{220^2}{1000} = \dfrac{48\,400}{1000} = 48.4\ \Omega$.
  2. $I = \dfrac{1000}{220} \approx 4.55\ \mathrm{A}$.

$R = 48.4\ \Omega$, $I \approx 4.55\ \mathrm{A}$

Problem 2

A current of $2.0\ \mathrm{A}$ flows through a $50\ \Omega$ resistor for $1.0$ minute. Find the heat produced in joules and in calories. (Type $H$ in calories, to the nearest calorie.)

Uses: Joule's Law and Heating Effect of Electric Current

Show hint

$W = I^2Rt$ in joules; $H = W/J$ with $J = 4.2\ \mathrm{J\,cal^{-1}}$.

Show solution
  1. $W = 2.0^2 \times 50 \times 60 = 12\,000\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $H = \dfrac{12\,000}{4.2} \approx 2\,857\ \mathrm{cal}$.

$W = 12\,000\ \mathrm{J} \approx 2\,857\ \mathrm{cal}$

Problem 3

A $1.5\ \mathrm{kW}$ air conditioner runs for $6$ hours a day for $30$ days. Electricity costs $50$ kyats per unit (kWh). Find the cost. (Type the cost in kyats.)

Uses: Applications of Heating Effect and Electrical Safety, Electrical Energy and Electrical Power

Show hint

Units $= $ power in kW $\times$ hours.

Show solution
  1. Energy $= 1.5\ \mathrm{kW} \times 6\ \mathrm{h} \times 30 = 270\ \mathrm{kWh} = 270$ units.
  2. Cost $= 270 \times 50 = 13\,500$ kyats.

$13\,500$ kyats

Problem 4

A $220\ \mathrm{V}$ circuit supplies a $1000\ \mathrm{W}$ kettle, a $1500\ \mathrm{W}$ iron and a $100\ \mathrm{W}$ lamp together. Find the total current. Which fuse should be used: $5\ \mathrm{A}$, $10\ \mathrm{A}$ or $13\ \mathrm{A}$? (Type the current in A, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Applications of Heating Effect and Electrical Safety

Show hint

Add the powers, then $I = P/V$. Choose the smallest fuse rated above the working current.

Show solution
  1. $P = 1000 + 1500 + 100 = 2600\ \mathrm{W}$.
  2. $I = \dfrac{2600}{220} \approx 11.8\ \mathrm{A}$, so the $13\ \mathrm{A}$ fuse is needed (a $10$ A fuse would blow).

$I \approx 11.8\ \mathrm{A}$: use the $13\ \mathrm{A}$ fuse

Problem 5

A $2.0\ \mathrm{kW}$ electric kettle holds $1.5\ \mathrm{kg}$ of water at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Assuming no heat is lost, how long does it take to bring the water to $100^\circ\mathrm{C}$? ($c = 4200\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$; type $t$ in s, to the nearest second.)

Uses: Joule's Law and Heating Effect of Electric Current, Electrical Energy and Electrical Power

Show hint

Electrical energy $Pt$ = heat gained $mc\Delta T$.

Show solution
  1. $mc\Delta T = 1.5 \times 4200 \times 75 = 472\,500\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $t = \dfrac{472\,500}{2000} \approx 236\ \mathrm{s}$ (about 4 minutes).

$t \approx 236\ \mathrm{s}$

Problem 6

A lamp rated $60\ \mathrm{W}$, $220\ \mathrm{V}$ is connected to a $110\ \mathrm{V}$ supply. Assuming its resistance does not change, what power does it take? (Type $P$ in W.)

Uses: Electrical Energy and Electrical Power

Show hint

The resistance is fixed by the rating: $R = V^2/P$. Then $P = V^2/R$ at the new voltage.

Show solution
  1. $R = \dfrac{220^2}{60} \approx 807\ \Omega$.
  2. $P = \dfrac{110^2}{807} = 15\ \mathrm{W}$. Exactly: halving $V$ divides $P$ by $2^2 = 4$, so $\dfrac{60}{4} = 15$ W.

$P = 15\ \mathrm{W}$

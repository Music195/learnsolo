EMF, Internal Resistance and Combinations of Cells

Six problems on $E = I(R + r)$, terminal voltage $V = E - Ir$, and cells joined in series, in parallel and in opposition. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A battery of emf $12\ \mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $0.50\ \Omega$ is connected to a $5.5\ \Omega$ resistor. Find the current and the terminal voltage. (Type $I$ in A.)

Uses: Electromotive Force and Internal Resistance

Show hint

$I = \dfrac{E}{R + r}$, then $V = E - Ir$.

Show solution
  1. $I = \dfrac{12}{5.5 + 0.50} = \dfrac{12}{6.0} = 2.0\ \mathrm{A}$.
  2. $V = 12 - 2.0 \times 0.50 = 11\ \mathrm{V}$ (also $IR = 2.0 \times 5.5 = 11$ V ✓).

$I = 2.0\ \mathrm{A}$, $V = 11\ \mathrm{V}$

Problem 2

A $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$ battery drives $20\ \mathrm{C}$ of charge round a circuit. How much energy does it give to the charge? (Type $W$ in J.)

Uses: Electromotive Force and Internal Resistance

Show hint

emf is energy per unit charge: $E = W/Q$.

Show solution
  1. $W = EQ = 9.0 \times 20 = 180\ \mathrm{J}$.

$W = 180\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 3

A cell reads $1.50\ \mathrm{V}$ on a high-resistance voltmeter with nothing else connected. With a $2.0\ \Omega$ resistor across it, the reading falls to $1.20\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the internal resistance. (Type $r$ in $\Omega$.)

Uses: Electromotive Force and Internal Resistance

Show hint

With no current the voltmeter reads the emf. Then $I = V/R$ and $Ir = E - V$.

Show solution
  1. $E = 1.50\ \mathrm{V}$; $I = \dfrac{1.20}{2.0} = 0.60\ \mathrm{A}$.
  2. $r = \dfrac{E - V}{I} = \dfrac{0.30}{0.60} = 0.50\ \Omega$.

$r = 0.50\ \Omega$

Problem 4

Four cells, each of emf $1.5\ \mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $0.25\ \Omega$, are joined in series and connected to a $5.0\ \Omega$ lamp. Find the current. (Type $I$ in A.)

Uses: Batteries in Series and Parallel Combinations

Show hint

In series both the emfs and the internal resistances add.

Show solution
  1. $E = 4 \times 1.5 = 6.0\ \mathrm{V}$, $r = 4 \times 0.25 = 1.0\ \Omega$.
  2. $I = \dfrac{6.0}{5.0 + 1.0} = 1.0\ \mathrm{A}$.

$I = 1.0\ \mathrm{A}$

Problem 5

Three identical cells, each $1.5\ \mathrm{V}$ with internal resistance $0.60\ \Omega$, are joined in parallel across a $1.3\ \Omega$ resistor. Find the current in the resistor and in each cell. (Type the current in the resistor in A.)

Uses: Batteries in Series and Parallel Combinations

Show hint

Identical cells in parallel: the emf stays $1.5$ V and the internal resistance becomes $r/n$.

Show solution
  1. $E = 1.5\ \mathrm{V}$, $r = \dfrac{0.60}{3} = 0.20\ \Omega$.
  2. $I = \dfrac{1.5}{1.3 + 0.20} = 1.0\ \mathrm{A}$, so each cell gives $\dfrac{1.0}{3} \approx 0.33\ \mathrm{A}$.

$1.0\ \mathrm{A}$ in the resistor, $0.33\ \mathrm{A}$ from each cell

Problem 6

A $12\ \mathrm{V}$ charger (internal resistance $0.20\ \Omega$) charges a $10\ \mathrm{V}$ battery (internal resistance $0.30\ \Omega$) through a $1.5\ \Omega$ resistor, positive to positive. Find the charging current and the voltage across the terminals of the battery being charged. (Type that voltage in V, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Batteries in Series and Parallel Combinations, Electromotive Force and Internal Resistance

Show hint

The emfs oppose: $E_{\text{net}} = E_1 - E_2$. For a battery being charged, current flows in at its positive terminal, so $V = E + Ir$.

Show solution
  1. $I = \dfrac{12 - 10}{0.20 + 0.30 + 1.5} = \dfrac{2.0}{2.0} = 1.0\ \mathrm{A}$.
  2. Battery being charged: $V = E_2 + Ir_2 = 10 + 1.0 \times 0.30 = 10.3\ \mathrm{V}$.

$I = 1.0\ \mathrm{A}$; terminal voltage $10.3\ \mathrm{V}$

Stored Energy, RC Circuits and Combinations

Six problems on $W = \tfrac12 CV^2$, charging through a resistor $V_C = V_0(1 - e^{-t/RC})$, and capacitors in series and parallel. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A $100\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is charged to $20\ \mathrm{V}$. How much energy does it store? (Type $W$ in mJ.)

Uses: Energy Stored and RC Circuits

Show hint

$W = \tfrac12 CV^2$.

Show solution
  1. $W = \tfrac12 \times 100 \times 10^{-6} \times 20^2 = 0.020\ \mathrm{J} = 20\ \mathrm{mJ}$.

$W = 20\ \mathrm{mJ}$

Problem 2

Find the total capacitance of a $6.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ and a $3.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor (i) in series and (ii) in parallel. (Type the series value in $\mu\mathrm{F}$.)

Uses: Combinations of Capacitors in Series and Parallel

Show hint

Series: $\dfrac{1}{C_s} = \dfrac{1}{C_1} + \dfrac{1}{C_2}$. Parallel: $C_p = C_1 + C_2$.

Show solution
  1. (i) $\dfrac{1}{C_s} = \dfrac16 + \dfrac13 = \dfrac12 \implies C_s = 2.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ (smaller than either).
  2. (ii) $C_p = 6.0 + 3.0 = 9.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$.

(i) $2.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$   (ii) $9.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$

Problem 3

A $2.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ and a $3.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor are joined in series across $10\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the charge on each and the voltage across each. (Type the voltage across the $2.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor in V.)

Uses: Combinations of Capacitors in Series and Parallel

Show hint

In series every capacitor carries the same charge $Q = C_s V$.

Show solution
  1. $C_s = \dfrac{2.0 \times 3.0}{2.0 + 3.0} = 1.2\ \mu\mathrm{F}$, so $Q = 1.2 \times 10 = 12\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ on each.
  2. $V_1 = \dfrac{12}{2.0} = 6.0\ \mathrm{V}$ and $V_2 = \dfrac{12}{3.0} = 4.0\ \mathrm{V}$ (they add to $10$ V ✓). The smaller capacitor takes the larger voltage.

$Q = 12\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ each; $6.0\ \mathrm{V}$ and $4.0\ \mathrm{V}$

Problem 4

An uncharged $100\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is charged from a $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$ battery through a $10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor. Find the time constant and the capacitor voltage after $1.0\ \mathrm{s}$. (Type $V_C$ in V, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Energy Stored and RC Circuits

Show hint

$\tau = RC$ and $V_C = V_0\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$.

Show solution
  1. $\tau = RC = 10 \times 10^3 \times 100 \times 10^{-6} = 1.0\ \mathrm{s}$.
  2. $V_C = 9.0\left(1 - e^{-1}\right) = 9.0 \times 0.632 \approx 5.69\ \mathrm{V}$: after one time constant the capacitor is $63\,\%$ charged.

$\tau = 1.0\ \mathrm{s}$, $V_C \approx 5.69\ \mathrm{V}$

Problem 5

A camera flash uses a $150\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor charged to $300\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the energy stored. If it is released in $1.0\ \mathrm{ms}$, what is the average power? (Type $W$ in J, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Energy Stored and RC Circuits

Show hint

$W = \tfrac12 CV^2$ and $P = W/t$.

Show solution
  1. $W = \tfrac12 \times 150 \times 10^{-6} \times 300^2 = 6.75\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $P = \dfrac{6.75}{1.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 6750\ \mathrm{W}$: a small battery could never give this power directly.

$W = 6.75\ \mathrm{J}$, $P = 6750\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 6

A $4.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is in series with a parallel pair of $2.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ and $6.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$, all across $12\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the total capacitance and the charge on the $6.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor. (Type that charge in $\mu\mathrm{C}$.)

Uses: Combinations of Capacitors in Series and Parallel

Show hint

Combine the parallel pair first, then the series. The pair shares the voltage that is left over.

Show solution
  1. Parallel pair: $2.0 + 6.0 = 8.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$. In series with $4.0$: $C = \dfrac{4.0 \times 8.0}{12} = \dfrac{8}{3} \approx 2.67\ \mu\mathrm{F}$.
  2. $Q_{\text{total}} = \dfrac83 \times 12 = 32\ \mu\mathrm{C}$; $V_{4} = \dfrac{32}{4.0} = 8.0\ \mathrm{V}$, so the pair has $12 - 8.0 = 4.0\ \mathrm{V}$.
  3. $Q_6 = 6.0 \times 4.0 = 24\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ (and $Q_2 = 8\ \mu\mathrm{C}$; $24 + 8 = 32$ ✓).

$C \approx 2.67\ \mu\mathrm{F}$; $Q = 24\ \mu\mathrm{C}$ on the $6.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$

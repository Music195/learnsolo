Capacitance, Reactance and Parallel Plates

Six problems on $C = Q/V$, capacitive reactance $X_C = \dfrac{1}{2\pi f C}$ and the parallel-plate capacitor $C = \dfrac{\kappa\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A $4.7\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is connected to a $12\ \mathrm{V}$ battery. How much charge does it store? (Type $Q$ in $\mu\mathrm{C}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Capacitance and Charge Storage

Show hint

$Q = CV$.

Show solution
  1. $Q = 4.7 \times 10^{-6} \times 12 = 5.64 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{C} = 56.4\ \mu\mathrm{C}$.

$Q = 56.4\ \mu\mathrm{C}$

Problem 2

Find the reactance of a $10\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor on the $50\ \mathrm{Hz}$ mains. What is its reactance to steady DC? (Type $X_C$ in $\Omega$, to the nearest ohm.)

Uses: Types and Applications of Capacitors

Show hint

$X_C = \dfrac{1}{2\pi f C}$; for DC, $f = 0$.

Show solution
  1. $X_C = \dfrac{1}{2\pi \times 50 \times 10 \times 10^{-6}} = \dfrac{1}{3.14 \times 10^{-3}} \approx 318\ \Omega$.
  2. For DC $f = 0$, so $X_C \to \infty$: once charged, a capacitor blocks steady current.

$X_C \approx 318\ \Omega$; infinite for DC

Problem 3

A parallel-plate capacitor has plates of area $0.020\ \mathrm{m^2}$, $1.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ apart in air. Find its capacitance. ($\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$; type $C$ in pF.)

Uses: Parallel-Plate Capacitor and Dielectrics

Show hint

$C_0 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$ with $d$ in metres; $1\ \mathrm{pF} = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F}$.

Show solution
  1. $C_0 = \dfrac{8.85 \times 10^{-12} \times 0.020}{1.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 1.77 \times 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{F} = 177\ \mathrm{pF}$.

$C_0 = 177\ \mathrm{pF}$

Problem 4

The gap of the capacitor in the last problem ($177\ \mathrm{pF}$ in air) is filled with a dielectric of $\kappa = 5.0$ while it stays connected to $12\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the new capacitance and the new charge. (Type $C$ in pF.)

Uses: Parallel-Plate Capacitor and Dielectrics, Capacitance and Charge Storage

Show hint

$C = \kappa C_0$; the battery keeps $V$ fixed, so $Q = CV$ grows.

Show solution
  1. $C = 5.0 \times 177 = 885\ \mathrm{pF}$.
  2. $Q = 885 \times 10^{-12} \times 12 \approx 1.06 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{C} = 10.6\ \mathrm{nC}$, five times the charge without the dielectric.

$C = 885\ \mathrm{pF}$, $Q \approx 10.6\ \mathrm{nC}$

Problem 5

At what frequency does a $2.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor have a reactance of $100\ \Omega$? (Type $f$ in Hz, to the nearest Hz.)

Uses: Types and Applications of Capacitors

Show hint

Rearrange: $f = \dfrac{1}{2\pi X_C C}$.

Show solution
  1. $f = \dfrac{1}{2\pi \times 100 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-6}} = \dfrac{1}{1.257 \times 10^{-3}} \approx 796\ \mathrm{Hz}$.
  2. At higher frequencies $X_C$ is smaller, so the capacitor passes high-frequency signals more easily.

$f \approx 796\ \mathrm{Hz}$

Problem 6

What plate area would an air-filled parallel-plate capacitor need to have a capacitance of $1.0\ \mathrm{F}$ with plates $0.10\ \mathrm{mm}$ apart? What does this tell you about the farad? (Type $A$ in $\mathrm{km^2}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Parallel-Plate Capacitor and Dielectrics, Capacitance and Charge Storage

Show hint

$A = \dfrac{Cd}{\varepsilon_0}$; $1\ \mathrm{km^2} = 10^6\ \mathrm{m^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $A = \dfrac{1.0 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-4}}{8.85 \times 10^{-12}} \approx 1.13 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{m^2} \approx 11.3\ \mathrm{km^2}$.
  2. About the size of a small town: one farad is a very large capacitance, which is why everyday capacitors are measured in $\mu\mathrm{F}$, nF and pF.

$A \approx 11.3\ \mathrm{km^2}$

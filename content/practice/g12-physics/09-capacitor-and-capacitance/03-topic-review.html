Capacitor and Capacitance

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems on capacitance, dielectrics, stored energy, RC charging and combinations. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A $220\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor is charged to $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the charge stored. (Type $Q$ in mC, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Capacitance and Charge Storage

Show hint

$Q = CV$; $1\ \mathrm{mC} = 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{C}$.

Show solution
  1. $Q = 220 \times 10^{-6} \times 9.0 = 1.98 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{C} = 1.98\ \mathrm{mC}$.

$Q = 1.98\ \mathrm{mC}$

Problem 2

A $10\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ capacitor charges through a $47\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor. How long does it take to reach half the battery voltage? (Type $t$ in s, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Energy Stored and RC Circuits

Show hint

Set $1 - e^{-t/RC} = \tfrac12$, so $e^{-t/RC} = \tfrac12$ and $t = RC\ln 2$.

Show solution
  1. $RC = 47 \times 10^3 \times 10 \times 10^{-6} = 0.47\ \mathrm{s}$.
  2. $t = 0.47 \times \ln 2 = 0.47 \times 0.693 \approx 0.326\ \mathrm{s}$.

$t \approx 0.326\ \mathrm{s}$

Problem 3

A capacitor is made from two sheets of foil, each $0.050\ \mathrm{m^2}$, separated by polythene $0.20\ \mathrm{mm}$ thick ($\kappa = 2.2$). Find its capacitance. ($\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$; type $C$ in nF, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Parallel-Plate Capacitor and Dielectrics

Show hint

$C = \dfrac{\kappa\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$.

Show solution
  1. $C = \dfrac{2.2 \times 8.85 \times 10^{-12} \times 0.050}{0.20 \times 10^{-3}} \approx 4.87 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{F} = 4.87\ \mathrm{nF}$.

$C \approx 4.87\ \mathrm{nF}$

Problem 4

Explain why a capacitor in a circuit blocks a steady direct current but lets an alternating current pass. Give one use of each property.

Uses: Types and Applications of Capacitors

Show hint

Think about what happens once the plates are fully charged, and about what $X_C = 1/(2\pi f C)$ does as $f$ changes.

Show solution
  1. With DC the plates charge up until the capacitor voltage equals the supply; then no more charge flows, so the steady current stops ($X_C \to \infty$ at $f = 0$).
  2. With AC the supply keeps reversing, so the plates keep charging and discharging and current flows in the wires all the time; $X_C$ falls as $f$ rises.
  3. Uses: blocking DC while passing an audio signal between amplifier stages; smoothing the output of a rectifier; tuning a radio with an LC circuit.

Charged plates stop DC; AC keeps charging and discharging them

Problem 5

A $10\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ air capacitor is charged to $100\ \mathrm{V}$ and then disconnected. Its plates are pulled apart to double the gap. Find the new capacitance, voltage and stored energy, and explain where the extra energy comes from. (Type the new energy in J.)

Uses: Parallel-Plate Capacitor and Dielectrics, Energy Stored and RC Circuits

Show hint

Disconnected means $Q$ stays the same. Doubling $d$ halves $C$.

Show solution
  1. $Q = CV = 10 \times 10^{-6} \times 100 = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{C}$ (unchanged).
  2. $C' = 5.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ and $V' = \dfrac{Q}{C'} = 200\ \mathrm{V}$.
  3. $W = \tfrac12 CV^2 = 0.050\ \mathrm{J}$ before; $W' = \tfrac12 \times 5.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 200^2 = 0.10\ \mathrm{J}$ after.
  4. The extra $0.050\ \mathrm{J}$ is the work done pulling the oppositely charged plates apart against their attraction.

$C' = 5.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$, $V' = 200\ \mathrm{V}$, $W' = 0.10\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 6

Capacitors of $2.0$, $3.0$ and $6.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$ are joined in series across $12\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the total capacitance, the voltage across each and the total energy stored. (Type the energy in $\mu\mathrm{J}$.)

Uses: Combinations of Capacitors in Series and Parallel, Energy Stored and RC Circuits

Show hint

Series: same $Q$ on each; $V_i = Q/C_i$; $W = \tfrac12 C V^2$ for the whole set.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac1C = \dfrac12 + \dfrac13 + \dfrac16 = 1 \implies C = 1.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$.
  2. $Q = 1.0 \times 12 = 12\ \mu\mathrm{C}$; $V = 6.0$, $4.0$ and $2.0\ \mathrm{V}$ (total $12$ V ✓).
  3. $W = \tfrac12 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 12^2 = 72\ \mu\mathrm{J}$.

$C = 1.0\ \mu\mathrm{F}$; $6.0$, $4.0$, $2.0\ \mathrm{V}$; $W = 72\ \mu\mathrm{J}$

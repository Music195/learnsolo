Generators, Transformers, the Grid and Safety

Six problems on the generator emf $E_0 = 2\pi f N B A$, transformers $V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p$, transmission losses $P_{\text{loss}} = I^2R$, earthing and shock currents. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A transformer steps $220\ \mathrm{V}$ down to $11\ \mathrm{V}$. Its primary coil has $2000$ turns. How many turns are on the secondary? (Type $N_s$.)

Uses: Applications: Induction Coil, AC Generator, and Transformers

Show hint

$\dfrac{N_s}{N_p} = \dfrac{V_s}{V_p}$.

Show solution
  1. $N_s = 2000 \times \dfrac{11}{220} = 2000 \times \dfrac{1}{20} = 100$ turns.

$N_s = 100$

Problem 2

An AC generator has a $100$-turn coil of area $0.050\ \mathrm{m^2}$ turning at $50$ revolutions per second in a $0.20\ \mathrm{T}$ field. Find the peak emf. (Type $E_0$ in V, to the nearest volt.)

Uses: Applications: Induction Coil, AC Generator, and Transformers

Show hint

$E_0 = NBA\omega = 2\pi f NBA$.

Show solution
  1. $E_0 = 2\pi \times 50 \times 100 \times 0.20 \times 0.050 = 100\pi \approx 314\ \mathrm{V}$.

$E_0 = 100\pi \approx 314\ \mathrm{V}$

Problem 3

A transformer takes $0.50\ \mathrm{A}$ from the $220\ \mathrm{V}$ mains and gives $8.0\ \mathrm{A}$ at $12\ \mathrm{V}$. Find its efficiency. Give two reasons why real transformers lose energy. (Type $\eta$ in %, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Applications: Induction Coil, AC Generator, and Transformers

Show hint

$\eta = \dfrac{V_sI_s}{V_pI_p} \times 100\,\%$.

Show solution
  1. $P_{\text{in}} = 220 \times 0.50 = 110\ \mathrm{W}$; $P_{\text{out}} = 12 \times 8.0 = 96\ \mathrm{W}$.
  2. $\eta = \dfrac{96}{110} \times 100\,\% \approx 87.3\,\%$.
  3. Losses: heating in the copper coils ($I^2R$), eddy currents and magnetic hysteresis in the iron core, and flux leakage.

$\eta \approx 87.3\,\%$

Problem 4

$100\ \mathrm{kW}$ is sent along lines of total resistance $5.0\ \Omega$. Find the power lost in the lines if it is sent (i) at $1000\ \mathrm{V}$ and (ii) at $10\ \mathrm{kV}$. (Type the loss for (ii) in W.)

Uses: Power Transmission and the Grid System

Show hint

$I = P/V$, then $P_{\text{loss}} = I^2 R$.

Show solution
  1. (i) $I = \dfrac{100\,000}{1000} = 100\ \mathrm{A}$, $P_{\text{loss}} = 100^2 \times 5.0 = 50\,000\ \mathrm{W}$: half the power is wasted!
  2. (ii) $I = \dfrac{100\,000}{10\,000} = 10\ \mathrm{A}$, $P_{\text{loss}} = 10^2 \times 5.0 = 500\ \mathrm{W}$, only $0.5\,\%$.
  3. Ten times the voltage cuts the loss by $10^2 = 100$ times.

(i) $50\ \mathrm{kW}$   (ii) $500\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 5

The live wire inside a metal washing machine touches its case. The case is earthed, and the earth path plus wiring has resistance $2.0\ \Omega$. Find the fault current on the $220\ \mathrm{V}$ supply and explain how this protects the user. (Type $I$ in A.)

Uses: Principles of House Wiring and Earthing

Show hint

$I_{\text{fault}} = \dfrac{V}{R_{\text{earth}} + R_{\text{wire}}}$.

Show solution
  1. $I = \dfrac{220}{2.0} = 110\ \mathrm{A}$.
  2. This is far more than the $13$ A fuse rating, so the fuse blows (or the MCB trips) at once, cutting off the live wire. The case cannot stay live, so a person touching it is safe.

$I = 110\ \mathrm{A}$: the fuse blows at once

Problem 6

A person touches a $220\ \mathrm{V}$ live wire. With dry skin the body resistance is about $100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$; with wet skin the skin resistance drops to $1.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and the inside of the body adds $500\ \Omega$. Find the current in each case and compare with the dangerous level of about $50\ \mathrm{mA}$. (Type the wet-skin current in mA, to the nearest mA.)

Uses: Electrical Hazards and Safety Precautions

Show hint

$I_{\text{body}} = \dfrac{V}{R_{\text{skin}} + R_{\text{internal}}}$.

Show solution
  1. Dry: $I \approx \dfrac{220}{100\,000} = 2.2\ \mathrm{mA}$, a painful shock but usually survivable.
  2. Wet: $I = \dfrac{220}{1000 + 500} \approx 147\ \mathrm{mA}$, about three times the dangerous level: this can stop the heart.

Dry $\approx 2.2\ \mathrm{mA}$; wet $\approx 147\ \mathrm{mA}$ (deadly)

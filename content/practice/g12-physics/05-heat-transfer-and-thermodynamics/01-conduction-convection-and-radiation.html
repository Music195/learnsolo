Conduction, Convection and Radiation

Six problems on $H = kA\,\dfrac{\Delta T}{l}$, $H = qA(T_s - T_f)$ and $H = e\sigma A T^4$, using $\sigma = 5.685 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-4}}$ as in the notes. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A glass window $1.0\ \mathrm{m} \times 1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ is $5.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ thick ($k = 0.80\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$). Its inner surface is $10\ \mathrm{K}$ warmer than its outer surface. Find the rate of heat flow through it. (Type $H$ in W.)

Uses: Heat Transfer — Conduction, Convection, and Radiation

Show hint

$H = kA\dfrac{\Delta T}{l}$ with $l$ in metres.

Show solution
  1. $A = 1.5\ \mathrm{m^2}$, $l = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. $H = 0.80 \times 1.5 \times \dfrac{10}{5.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 2400\ \mathrm{W}$.

$H = 2400\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 2

A black sphere ($e = 1$) of surface area $0.010\ \mathrm{m^2}$ is at $500\ \mathrm{K}$. At what rate does it radiate energy? ($\sigma = 5.685 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-4}}$; type $H$ in W, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Heat Transfer — Conduction, Convection, and Radiation

Show hint

$H = e\sigma A T^4$ with $T$ in kelvin.

Show solution
  1. $T^4 = 500^4 = 6.25 \times 10^{10}\ \mathrm{K^4}$.
  2. $H = 1 \times 5.685 \times 10^{-8} \times 0.010 \times 6.25 \times 10^{10} \approx 35.5\ \mathrm{W}$.

$H \approx 35.5\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 3

A heater panel of area $0.50\ \mathrm{m^2}$ loses $300\ \mathrm{W}$ by convection to air at $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The convection coefficient is $q = 12\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-1}}$. What is the temperature of the panel surface? (Type $T_s$ in °C.)

Uses: Heat Transfer — Conduction, Convection, and Radiation

Show hint

$H = qA(T_s - T_f)$, so $T_s - T_f = \dfrac{H}{qA}$.

Show solution
  1. $T_s - T_f = \dfrac{300}{12 \times 0.50} = 50\ \mathrm{K}$.
  2. $T_s = 20 + 50 = 70^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

$T_s = 70^\circ\mathrm{C}$

Problem 4

A copper rod $0.50\ \mathrm{m}$ long with cross-section $2.0\ \mathrm{cm^2}$ has one end in boiling water and the other in melting ice ($k = 385\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$). Find the rate of heat flow along it and the temperature at its middle. (Type $H$ in W, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Heat Transfer — Conduction, Convection, and Radiation

Show hint

Change $\mathrm{cm^2}$ to $\mathrm{m^2}$. In steady state the temperature falls evenly along a uniform rod.

Show solution
  1. $H = kA\dfrac{\Delta T}{l} = 385 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-4} \times \dfrac{100}{0.50} = 15.4\ \mathrm{W}$.
  2. The temperature drops uniformly from $100^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $0^\circ\mathrm{C}$, so the middle is at $50^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

$H = 15.4\ \mathrm{W}$; middle at $50^\circ\mathrm{C}$

Problem 5

A person with skin area $1.5\ \mathrm{m^2}$, emissivity $0.97$ and skin temperature $34^\circ\mathrm{C}$ stands in a room at $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Find the net rate of heat loss by radiation. ($\sigma = 5.685 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-4}}$; type $H_{\text{net}}$ in W, to the nearest watt.)

Uses: Heat Transfer — Conduction, Convection, and Radiation

Show hint

$H_{\text{net}} = e\sigma A(T^4 - T_{\text{surr}}^4)$ with both temperatures in kelvin.

Show solution
  1. $T = 307\ \mathrm{K}$, $T_{\text{surr}} = 293\ \mathrm{K}$; $T^4 - T_{\text{surr}}^4 = 8.883 \times 10^{9} - 7.370 \times 10^{9} = 1.513 \times 10^{9}\ \mathrm{K^4}$.
  2. $H_{\text{net}} = 0.97 \times 5.685 \times 10^{-8} \times 1.5 \times 1.513 \times 10^{9} \approx 125\ \mathrm{W}$, about the power of a bright light bulb.

$H_{\text{net}} \approx 125\ \mathrm{W}$

Problem 6

A wall of area $10\ \mathrm{m^2}$ is $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ of brick ($k = 0.60\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$) with $5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ of insulation ($k = 0.040\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$) on the outside. Inside is $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$, outside $0^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Find the heat flow and the temperature where brick meets insulation. (Type $H$ in W, to the nearest watt.)

Uses: Heat Transfer — Conduction, Convection, and Radiation

Show hint

The same $H$ flows through both layers, and the temperature drops add: $\Delta T_{\text{total}} = \dfrac{H}{A}\left(\dfrac{l_1}{k_1} + \dfrac{l_2}{k_2}\right)$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{l_1}{k_1} + \dfrac{l_2}{k_2} = \dfrac{0.10}{0.60} + \dfrac{0.050}{0.040} = 0.167 + 1.25 = 1.42\ \mathrm{m^2K\,W^{-1}}$.
  2. $H = \dfrac{A\,\Delta T}{1.42} = \dfrac{10 \times 20}{1.42} \approx 141\ \mathrm{W}$.
  3. Drop across the brick: $\Delta T_1 = \dfrac{H l_1}{k_1 A} = \dfrac{141 \times 0.10}{0.60 \times 10} \approx 2.35\ \mathrm{K}$, so the joint is at about $17.6^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Most of the drop is across the thin insulation.

$H \approx 141\ \mathrm{W}$; joint at about $17.6^\circ\mathrm{C}$

Laws of Thermodynamics and Heat Engines

Six problems on the first law $\Delta U = Q - W$, work in thermodynamic processes, engine efficiency and the Carnot and Otto limits. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A gas is given $500\ \mathrm{J}$ of heat and at the same time does $200\ \mathrm{J}$ of work on its surroundings. Find the change in its internal energy. (Type $\Delta U$ in J.)

Uses: Laws of Thermodynamics and Thermodynamic Processes

Show hint

First law: $\Delta U = Q - W$, with $W$ the work done by the gas.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta U = 500 - 200 = 300\ \mathrm{J}$: the internal energy rises, so the temperature rises.

$\Delta U = +300\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 2

What is the greatest possible efficiency of an engine working between reservoirs at $500\ \mathrm{K}$ and $300\ \mathrm{K}$? (Type $\eta$ in %.)

Uses: Heat Engines and the Carnot Cycle

Show hint

No engine can beat the Carnot efficiency $1 - T_C/T_H$.

Show solution
  1. $\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \dfrac{300}{500} = 0.40 = 40\,\%$.

$\eta_{\max} = 40\,\%$

Problem 3

$2.0\ \mathrm{mol}$ of a monatomic ideal gas is heated at constant volume so that its temperature rises by $50\ \mathrm{K}$. Find $W$, $\Delta U$ and $Q$. ($R = 8.3143\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$; type $Q$ in J, to the nearest joule.)

Uses: Laws of Thermodynamics and Thermodynamic Processes

Show hint

At constant volume the gas does no work. For a monatomic gas $\Delta U = \tfrac32 nR\Delta T$.

Show solution
  1. Constant volume: $W = p\Delta V = 0$.
  2. $\Delta U = \tfrac32 \times 2.0 \times 8.3143 \times 50 \approx 1247\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. $Q = \Delta U + W \approx 1247\ \mathrm{J}$: all the heat goes into internal energy.

$W = 0$, $\Delta U = Q \approx 1247\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 4

A gas at a constant pressure of $1.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$ expands from $2.0$ litres to $5.0$ litres while absorbing $800\ \mathrm{J}$ of heat. Find the work done by the gas and the change in internal energy. (Type $\Delta U$ in J.)

Uses: Laws of Thermodynamics and Thermodynamic Processes

Show hint

Isobaric work: $W = p\Delta V$, with $1\ \mathrm{L} = 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^3}$.

Show solution
  1. $W = p\Delta V = 1.0 \times 10^5 \times (5.0 - 2.0) \times 10^{-3} = 300\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $\Delta U = Q - W = 800 - 300 = 500\ \mathrm{J}$.

$W = 300\ \mathrm{J}$, $\Delta U = 500\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 5

In each cycle an engine takes in $2000\ \mathrm{J}$ from its hot reservoir and gives out $1500\ \mathrm{J}$ to the cold reservoir. Find the work per cycle and the efficiency. If the reservoirs are at $600\ \mathrm{K}$ and $300\ \mathrm{K}$, how does this compare with a Carnot engine? (Type $\eta$ in %.)

Uses: Heat Engines and the Carnot Cycle

Show hint

$W = Q_H - Q_C$ and $\eta = W/Q_H$.

Show solution
  1. $W = 2000 - 1500 = 500\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $\eta = \dfrac{500}{2000} = 25\,\%$.
  3. Carnot: $1 - \dfrac{300}{600} = 50\,\%$, so this engine reaches only half of the greatest possible efficiency.

$W = 500\ \mathrm{J}$, $\eta = 25\,\%$ (Carnot limit $50\,\%$)

Problem 6

A petrol engine works on the Otto cycle with a compression ratio of $8$. Taking $\gamma = 1.4$ for air, find its ideal efficiency. What would it be with a compression ratio of $10$? (Type $\eta$ for ratio 8 in %, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Heat Engines and the Carnot Cycle

Show hint

$\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{r_c^{\,\gamma - 1}}$.

Show solution
  1. $r_c^{\,0.4} = 8^{0.4} \approx 2.297$, so $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2.297} \approx 56.5\,\%$.
  2. With $r_c = 10$: $10^{0.4} \approx 2.512$, so $\eta \approx 60.2\,\%$. A higher compression ratio gives a more efficient engine.

$\eta \approx 56.5\,\%$ (about $60.2\,\%$ for ratio 10)

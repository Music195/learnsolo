Heat Transfer and Thermodynamics

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems mixing heat transfer, the first law of thermodynamics and heat engines. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

An ideal gas is compressed slowly at constant temperature, and $600\ \mathrm{J}$ of work is done on it. Find $\Delta U$ and the heat $Q$ that flows. Does heat flow into or out of the gas? (Type $Q$ in J, with its sign.)

Uses: Laws of Thermodynamics and Thermodynamic Processes

Show hint

For an ideal gas $U$ depends only on $T$. Work done on the gas means $W = -600\ \mathrm{J}$ in $\Delta U = Q - W$.

Show solution
  1. Isothermal: $\Delta T = 0$, so $\Delta U = 0$.
  2. $Q = \Delta U + W = 0 + (-600) = -600\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. Negative $Q$: $600\ \mathrm{J}$ of heat flows out of the gas to keep its temperature steady.

$\Delta U = 0$, $Q = -600\ \mathrm{J}$ (heat flows out)

Problem 2

A Carnot engine works between $227^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $27^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and takes in $5.0\ \mathrm{kJ}$ of heat each cycle. Find the work done and the heat rejected per cycle. (Type the work in J.)

Uses: Heat Engines and the Carnot Cycle

Show hint

Change the temperatures to kelvin first.

Show solution
  1. $T_H = 500\ \mathrm{K}$, $T_C = 300\ \mathrm{K}$, so $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{300}{500} = 0.40$.
  2. $W = \eta Q_H = 0.40 \times 5000 = 2000\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. $Q_C = Q_H - W = 3000\ \mathrm{J}$.

$W = 2000\ \mathrm{J}$, $Q_C = 3000\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 3

$2.0\ \mathrm{g}$ of water is boiled at $1.013 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$. Each gram of water ($1\ \mathrm{cm^3}$) becomes $1671\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of steam, and $L_v = 2265\ \mathrm{J\,g^{-1}}$. Find the work done by the expanding steam and the increase in internal energy. (Type $\Delta U$ in J, to the nearest joule.)

Uses: Laws of Thermodynamics and Thermodynamic Processes

Show hint

$W = p\Delta V$, $Q = mL_v$, then $\Delta U = Q - W$.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta V = 2.0 \times (1671 - 1) = 3340\ \mathrm{cm^3} = 3.34 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^3}$.
  2. $W = 1.013 \times 10^5 \times 3.34 \times 10^{-3} \approx 338\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. $Q = 2.0 \times 2265 = 4530\ \mathrm{J}$.
  4. $\Delta U = 4530 - 338 \approx 4192\ \mathrm{J}$. Most of the heat goes into pulling the molecules apart.

$W \approx 338\ \mathrm{J}$, $\Delta U \approx 4192\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 4

A cool box has walls of total area $0.80\ \mathrm{m^2}$ made of foam $2.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ thick ($k = 0.010\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$). It holds ice at $0^\circ\mathrm{C}$ on a $30^\circ\mathrm{C}$ day. Find the rate of heat flow in and the mass of ice that melts each hour. ($L_f = 3.34 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$; type the mass in g, to the nearest gram.)

Uses: Heat Transfer — Conduction, Convection, and Radiation

Show hint

$H = kA\dfrac{\Delta T}{l}$; heat in one hour $= H \times 3600$; mass melted $= \dfrac{\text{heat}}{L_f}$.

Show solution
  1. $H = 0.010 \times 0.80 \times \dfrac{30}{0.020} = 12\ \mathrm{W}$.
  2. In one hour: $12 \times 3600 = 43\,200\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. $m = \dfrac{43\,200}{3.34 \times 10^5} \approx 0.129\ \mathrm{kg} \approx 129\ \mathrm{g}$.

$H = 12\ \mathrm{W}$; about $129\ \mathrm{g}$ of ice per hour

Problem 5

A Carnot engine with its cold reservoir at $280\ \mathrm{K}$ has an efficiency of $30\,\%$. By how much must the hot reservoir temperature be raised to increase the efficiency to $40\,\%$? (Type the rise in K, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Heat Engines and the Carnot Cycle

Show hint

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_C}{T_H} \implies T_H = \dfrac{T_C}{1 - \eta}$.

Show solution
  1. At $30\,\%$: $T_H = \dfrac{280}{0.70} = 400\ \mathrm{K}$.
  2. At $40\,\%$: $T_H = \dfrac{280}{0.60} \approx 466.7\ \mathrm{K}$.
  3. Rise: $466.7 - 400 \approx 66.7\ \mathrm{K}$.

Raise $T_H$ by about $66.7\ \mathrm{K}$

Problem 6

$1.0\ \mathrm{mol}$ of a monatomic ideal gas is heated at constant pressure from $300\ \mathrm{K}$ to $400\ \mathrm{K}$. Find (i) the work done by the gas, (ii) $\Delta U$ and (iii) the heat supplied. Why is more heat needed than at constant volume? ($R = 8.3143\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$; type $Q$ in J, to the nearest joule.)

Uses: Laws of Thermodynamics and Thermodynamic Processes

Show hint

At constant pressure $W = p\Delta V = nR\Delta T$ (from $pV = nRT$).

Show solution
  1. (i) $W = nR\Delta T = 1.0 \times 8.3143 \times 100 \approx 831\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. (ii) $\Delta U = \tfrac32 nR\Delta T \approx 1247\ \mathrm{J}$.
  3. (iii) $Q = \Delta U + W \approx 2079\ \mathrm{J}$.
  4. At constant volume only $\Delta U$ is needed; at constant pressure the gas also does work pushing the surroundings back.

$W \approx 831\ \mathrm{J}$, $\Delta U \approx 1247\ \mathrm{J}$, $Q \approx 2079\ \mathrm{J}$

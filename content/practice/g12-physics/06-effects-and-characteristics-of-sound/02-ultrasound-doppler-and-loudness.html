Ultrasound, the Doppler Effect and Loudness

Six problems on $v = f\lambda$ for audible sound and ultrasound, the Doppler formula $f_o = f_s\dfrac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s}$ and sound intensity level $\beta = 10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

An ultrasound scanner works at $2.0\ \mathrm{MHz}$ and sound travels at $1540\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in soft tissue. Find the wavelength. (Type $\lambda$ in mm.)

Uses: Range of Audibility, Infrasound, and Ultrasound

Show hint

$\lambda = v/f$ with $f$ in Hz.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda = \dfrac{1540}{2.0 \times 10^6} = 7.7 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m} = 0.77\ \mathrm{mm}$. Short waves like this can show small details.

$\lambda = 0.77\ \mathrm{mm}$

Problem 2

Humans hear from about $20\ \mathrm{Hz}$ to $20\ \mathrm{kHz}$. Find the longest and shortest audible wavelengths in air ($v = 340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$). (Type the longest in m.)

Uses: Range of Audibility, Infrasound, and Ultrasound

Show hint

The lowest frequency has the longest wavelength.

Show solution
  1. Longest: $\lambda = \dfrac{340}{20} = 17\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. Shortest: $\lambda = \dfrac{340}{20\,000} = 0.017\ \mathrm{m} = 1.7\ \mathrm{cm}$.

$17\ \mathrm{m}$ down to $1.7\ \mathrm{cm}$

Problem 3

An ambulance siren has frequency $700\ \mathrm{Hz}$. The ambulance moves at $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ past a person standing at the roadside. What frequency does the person hear as it approaches, and as it moves away? ($v = 340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; type the approaching frequency in Hz, to the nearest Hz.)

Uses: The Doppler Effect

Show hint

Moving source, still observer: $f_o = f_s\dfrac{v}{v - v_s}$ approaching, $f_s\dfrac{v}{v + v_s}$ moving away.

Show solution
  1. Approaching: $f_o = 700 \times \dfrac{340}{340 - 30} = 700 \times \dfrac{340}{310} \approx 768\ \mathrm{Hz}$.
  2. Moving away: $f_o = 700 \times \dfrac{340}{370} \approx 643\ \mathrm{Hz}$.
  3. The pitch drops by about $125\ \mathrm{Hz}$ as it passes.

$\approx 768\ \mathrm{Hz}$ approaching, $\approx 643\ \mathrm{Hz}$ moving away

Problem 4

The sound intensity near a busy road is $1.0 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. Find the sound intensity level. ($I_0 = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$; type $\beta$ in dB.)

Uses: Characteristics of Musical Sound

Show hint

$\beta = 10\log_{10}\dfrac{I}{I_0}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{I}{I_0} = \dfrac{10^{-5}}{10^{-12}} = 10^7$.
  2. $\beta = 10 \times \log_{10} 10^7 = 10 \times 7 = 70\ \mathrm{dB}$.

$\beta = 70\ \mathrm{dB}$

Problem 5

The amplitude of a sound wave is doubled while its frequency stays the same. By what factor does the intensity change, and by how many decibels does the intensity level rise? (Type the rise in dB, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Characteristics of Musical Sound

Show hint

$I = 2\pi^2\rho v f^2 A^2$, so $I \propto A^2$.

Show solution
  1. Doubling $A$ multiplies $I$ by $2^2 = 4$.
  2. $\Delta\beta = 10\log_{10} 4 \approx 6.02\ \mathrm{dB}$.

$I$ becomes $4$ times larger; $\beta$ rises by about $6.02\ \mathrm{dB}$

Problem 6

A car moving at $25\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ sounds its $500\ \mathrm{Hz}$ horn while coming up behind a cyclist riding in the same direction at $5.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What frequency does the cyclist hear? ($v = 340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; type $f$ in Hz, to the nearest Hz.)

Uses: The Doppler Effect

Show hint

The source moves towards the observer (minus in the bottom), and the observer moves away from the source (minus in the top).

Show solution
  1. $f_o = f_s\dfrac{v - v_o}{v - v_s} = 500 \times \dfrac{340 - 5.0}{340 - 25} = 500 \times \dfrac{335}{315}$.
  2. $f_o \approx 532\ \mathrm{Hz}$: higher than $500$ Hz because the car is closing the gap.

$f_o \approx 532\ \mathrm{Hz}$

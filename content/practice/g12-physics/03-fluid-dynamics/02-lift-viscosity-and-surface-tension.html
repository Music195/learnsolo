Lift, Viscosity and Surface Tension

Six problems on aerodynamic lift and the Venturi meter, Stokes' law and terminal velocity, and surface tension with capillary rise. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A small sphere of radius $1.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ moves at $0.050\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ through glycerine ($\eta = 1.5\ \mathrm{Pa\,s}$). Find the viscous drag on it. (Type $F$ in mN, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Viscosity and Fluid Friction

Show hint

Stokes\' law: $F = 6\pi\eta r v$.

Show solution
  1. $F = 6\pi \times 1.5 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.050 = 1.41 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{N} = 1.41\ \mathrm{mN}$.

$F \approx 1.41\ \mathrm{mN}$

Problem 2

A wire frame holds a soap film. The movable side is $5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ long and the soap solution has $\gamma = 0.030\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$. What force does the film exert on that side? (Type $F$ in mN.)

Uses: Surface Tension and Capillarity

Show hint

A film has two surfaces, so the length that pulls is $2l$: $F = \gamma \times 2l$.

Show solution
  1. $F = 2\gamma l = 2 \times 0.030 \times 0.050 = 3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{N} = 3.0\ \mathrm{mN}$.

$F = 3.0\ \mathrm{mN}$

Problem 3

Air ($\rho = 1.2\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$) flows over the top of a wing at $280\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and under it at $250\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The wing area is $20\ \mathrm{m^2}$. Find the pressure difference and the lift. (Type the lift in kN, to the nearest kN.)

Uses: Manifestations of Bernoulli's Theorem

Show hint

$\Delta p = \tfrac12\rho(v_{\text{upper}}^2 - v_{\text{lower}}^2)$ and $F = \Delta p\,A$.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta p = \tfrac12 \times 1.2 \times (280^2 - 250^2) = 0.60 \times (78\,400 - 62\,500) = 0.60 \times 15\,900 = 9540\ \mathrm{Pa}$.
  2. $F = 9540 \times 20 = 1.908 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{N} \approx 191\ \mathrm{kN}$, upward.

$\Delta p = 9540\ \mathrm{Pa}$; lift $\approx 191\ \mathrm{kN}$

Problem 4

A Venturi meter has a wide section of area $20\ \mathrm{cm^2}$ and a throat of area $5.0\ \mathrm{cm^2}$. Water flows through it and the pressure in the throat is $6000\ \mathrm{Pa}$ lower than in the wide section. Find the speed in the wide section. (Type $v_1$ in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Manifestations of Bernoulli's Theorem, Equation of Continuity

Show hint

Continuity gives $v_2 = 4v_1$. Then Bernoulli at equal heights: $p_1 - p_2 = \tfrac12\rho(v_2^2 - v_1^2)$.

Show solution
  1. $A_1/A_2 = 20/5.0 = 4$, so $v_2 = 4v_1$.
  2. $6000 = \tfrac12 \times 1000 \times (16v_1^2 - v_1^2) = 7500\,v_1^2$.
  3. $v_1^2 = 0.80 \implies v_1 \approx 0.894\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $v_2 \approx 3.58\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

$v_1 \approx 0.894\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 5

A steel ball of radius $1.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ ($\rho_1 = 7800\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$) falls through glycerine ($\rho_2 = 1260\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $\eta = 1.5\ \mathrm{Pa\,s}$). Find its terminal velocity. (Type $v_t$ in $\mathrm{mm\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Viscosity and Fluid Friction

Show hint

At terminal velocity weight = buoyancy + Stokes drag, which gives $v_t = \dfrac{2gr^2(\rho_1 - \rho_2)}{9\eta}$.

Show solution
  1. $v_t = \dfrac{2 \times 9.8 \times (1.0 \times 10^{-3})^2 \times (7800 - 1260)}{9 \times 1.5} = \dfrac{1.28 \times 10^{-1}}{13.5}$.
  2. $v_t = 9.50 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} = 9.50\ \mathrm{mm\,s^{-1}}$.

$v_t \approx 9.50\ \mathrm{mm\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 6

Water ($\gamma = 0.072\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$, contact angle $0^\circ$) rises in a glass capillary tube of radius $0.25\ \mathrm{mm}$. Find the height of the rise. How high would it rise in a tube of half that radius? (Type $h$ for the $0.25\ \mathrm{mm}$ tube in cm, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Surface Tension and Capillarity

Show hint

Jurin\'s law: $h = \dfrac{2\gamma\cos\theta}{\rho g r}$, so $h \propto 1/r$.

Show solution
  1. $h = \dfrac{2 \times 0.072 \times \cos 0^\circ}{1000 \times 9.8 \times 0.25 \times 10^{-3}} = \dfrac{0.144}{2.45} \approx 0.0588\ \mathrm{m} = 5.88\ \mathrm{cm}$.
  2. Halving $r$ doubles $h$: about $11.8\ \mathrm{cm}$.

$h \approx 5.88\ \mathrm{cm}$; about $11.8\ \mathrm{cm}$ in the narrower tube

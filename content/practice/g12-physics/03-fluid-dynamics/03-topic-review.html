Fluid Dynamics

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems mixing continuity, Bernoulli's equation, viscosity and surface tension. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Blood flows at $30\ \mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}$ in an aorta of radius $1.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ and then through capillaries whose total cross-section is $2000\ \mathrm{cm^2}$. Find the average speed in the capillaries. (Type $v$ in $\mathrm{mm\,s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Equation of Continuity

Show hint

The total flow rate is the same: $A_1v_1 = A_2v_2$.

Show solution
  1. $A_1 = \pi(1.0)^2 = 3.14\ \mathrm{cm^2}$.
  2. $v_2 = \dfrac{A_1 v_1}{A_2} = \dfrac{3.14 \times 30}{2000} = 0.0471\ \mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}} = 0.471\ \mathrm{mm\,s^{-1}}$. The slow flow gives time for exchange with the tissues.

$v \approx 0.471\ \mathrm{mm\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 2

Water flows through a horizontal pipe at $2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ where the pressure is $1.5 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$. At a narrower part the speed is $6.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the pressure there. (Type $p$ in kPa.)

Uses: Bernoulli's Equation

Show hint

Horizontal pipe: $p_1 + \tfrac12\rho v_1^2 = p_2 + \tfrac12\rho v_2^2$.

Show solution
  1. $p_2 = p_1 - \tfrac12\rho(v_2^2 - v_1^2) = 1.5 \times 10^5 - \tfrac12(1000)(36 - 4) = 150\,000 - 16\,000 = 134\,000\ \mathrm{Pa}$.
  2. Faster flow, lower pressure: this is the Bernoulli effect.

$p_2 = 134\ \mathrm{kPa}$

Problem 3

A water tank stands on the floor with water $3.0\ \mathrm{m}$ deep. A small hole is made in its side $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ above the floor. How far from the tank does the jet hit the floor? (Type the distance in m.)

Uses: Bernoulli's Equation

Show hint

Exit speed from Torricelli with depth $2.0$ m below the surface; then the jet is a horizontal projectile falling $1.0$ m.

Show solution
  1. $v = \sqrt{2g(2.0)} = \sqrt{39.2} \approx 6.26\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, horizontal.
  2. Fall time: $1.0 = \tfrac12 g t^2 \implies t = \sqrt{2/9.8} \approx 0.452\ \mathrm{s}$.
  3. Range $x = vt \approx 6.26 \times 0.452 \approx 2.83\ \mathrm{m}$ (exactly $2\sqrt{2.0 \times 1.0} = 2\sqrt2$ m).

$x = 2\sqrt2 \approx 2.83\ \mathrm{m}$

Problem 4

Explain, using Bernoulli\'s principle, why (i) a roof can be lifted off in a strong wind and (ii) two ships moving side by side close together are pushed towards each other.

Uses: Manifestations of Bernoulli's Theorem

Show hint

Where the air or water moves faster, the pressure is lower.

Show solution
  1. (i) Fast wind over the roof lowers the pressure above it, while the still air inside the house stays at atmospheric pressure. The pressure difference times the large roof area gives a big upward force.
  2. (ii) Water between the ships is squeezed into a narrow channel and flows faster (continuity), so its pressure drops. The higher pressure on the outer sides pushes the ships together.

Faster flow means lower pressure; the higher pressure on the other side pushes

Problem 5

A tap gives a smooth stream of water with cross-section $1.2\ \mathrm{cm^2}$ and speed $0.80\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at the outlet. Find the cross-section of the stream $5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ below the tap. (Type the area in $\mathrm{cm^2}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Equation of Continuity

Show hint

The water speeds up as it falls: $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2gh$. The flow rate $Av$ stays the same, so the stream narrows.

Show solution
  1. $v^2 = 0.80^2 + 2 \times 9.8 \times 0.050 = 0.64 + 0.98 = 1.62 \implies v \approx 1.27\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $A_2 = \dfrac{A_1 v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{1.2 \times 0.80}{1.27} \approx 0.754\ \mathrm{cm^2}$.

$A_2 \approx 0.754\ \mathrm{cm^2}$

Problem 6

A glass tube of radius $0.50\ \mathrm{mm}$ is dipped into mercury ($\gamma = 0.465\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$, $\rho = 13\,600\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, contact angle $140^\circ$). Find the change in level inside the tube and say whether the mercury rises or falls. (Type $h$ in cm, with its sign, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Surface Tension and Capillarity

Show hint

Use Jurin\'s law. An angle above $90^\circ$ has a negative cosine, so $h$ comes out negative.

Show solution
  1. $h = \dfrac{2\gamma\cos\theta}{\rho g r} = \dfrac{2 \times 0.465 \times \cos 140^\circ}{13\,600 \times 9.8 \times 0.50 \times 10^{-3}} = \dfrac{0.930 \times (-0.766)}{66.64}$.
  2. $h \approx -0.0107\ \mathrm{m} = -1.07\ \mathrm{cm}$.
  3. Negative $h$: the mercury is pushed down, below the outside level, because mercury atoms attract each other more than they attract glass.

$h \approx -1.07\ \mathrm{cm}$: the mercury falls by about $1.07\ \mathrm{cm}$

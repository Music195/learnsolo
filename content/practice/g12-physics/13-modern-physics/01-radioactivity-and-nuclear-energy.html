Radioactivity and Nuclear Energy

Six problems on alpha and beta decay equations, half-life $N = N_0\left(\tfrac12\right)^{t/T_{1/2}}$, the decay constant $\lambda = 0.693/T_{1/2}$, and energy from mass defects ($1\ \mathrm{u} = 931.5\ \mathrm{MeV}$). Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Uranium-238 ($^{238}_{\ 92}\mathrm{U}$) decays by alpha emission. Write the equation and find the atomic number of the daughter nucleus. (Type $Z$.)

Uses: Radioactivity and Radioactive Decay

Show hint

An alpha particle is $^4_2\mathrm{He}$: $A$ falls by 4 and $Z$ by 2.

Show solution
  1. $^{238}_{\ 92}\mathrm{U} \to {}^{234}_{\ 90}\mathrm{Th} + {}^4_2\mathrm{He}$.
  2. Daughter: $Z = 90$ (thorium), $A = 234$.

$Z = 90$ (thorium-234)

Problem 2

A radioisotope has a half-life of $5.25$ years. How much of an $80\ \mathrm{g}$ sample is left after $21$ years? (Type the mass in g.)

Uses: Radioactivity and Radioactive Decay

Show hint

Number of half-lives $n = t/T_{1/2}$, then $N = N_0(\tfrac12)^n$.

Show solution
  1. $n = \dfrac{21}{5.25} = 4$.
  2. $m = 80 \times \left(\tfrac12\right)^4 = \dfrac{80}{16} = 5.0\ \mathrm{g}$.

$5.0\ \mathrm{g}$

Problem 3

An isotope has a half-life of $20$ minutes. Find its decay constant in $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. (Type $\lambda$ in units of $10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Radioactivity and Radioactive Decay

Show hint

$\lambda = \dfrac{0.693}{T_{1/2}}$ with $T_{1/2}$ in seconds.

Show solution
  1. $T_{1/2} = 20 \times 60 = 1200\ \mathrm{s}$.
  2. $\lambda = \dfrac{0.693}{1200} \approx 5.77 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$: each second about $0.058\,\%$ of the nuclei decay.

$\lambda \approx 5.77 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$

Problem 4

In the fusion reaction $^2_1\mathrm{H} + {}^3_1\mathrm{H} \to {}^4_2\mathrm{He} + {}^1_0\mathrm{n}$ the masses are $2.014102$, $3.016049$, $4.002603$ and $1.008665\ \mathrm{u}$. Find the energy released. ($1\ \mathrm{u} = 931.5\ \mathrm{MeV}$; type $Q$ in MeV, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Nuclear Energy and Nuclear Reactors

Show hint

$Q = \Delta m \times 931.5\ \mathrm{MeV}$, with $\Delta m$ = mass before − mass after.

Show solution
  1. Before: $2.014102 + 3.016049 = 5.030151\ \mathrm{u}$. After: $4.002603 + 1.008665 = 5.011268\ \mathrm{u}$.
  2. $\Delta m = 0.018883\ \mathrm{u}$.
  3. $Q = 0.018883 \times 931.5 \approx 17.6\ \mathrm{MeV}$.

$Q \approx 17.6\ \mathrm{MeV}$

Problem 5

$^{238}_{\ 92}\mathrm{U}$ decays through a series of alpha and beta-minus decays to stable $^{206}_{\ 82}\mathrm{Pb}$. How many alpha and how many beta decays take place? (Type the number of beta decays.)

Uses: Radioactivity and Radioactive Decay

Show hint

Only alpha decay changes $A$ (by 4). Then compare the change in $Z$ with what the alphas alone would give; each $\beta^-$ raises $Z$ by 1.

Show solution
  1. Alpha decays: $\dfrac{238 - 206}{4} = 8$.
  2. Eight alphas lower $Z$ by $16$: from $92$ to $76$. But lead has $Z = 82$, so $Z$ must rise by $6$.
  3. Beta-minus decays: $6$.

$8$ alpha and $6$ beta decays

Problem 6

A nuclear reactor produces $1000\ \mathrm{MW}$ of heat. Each fission of U-235 releases $200\ \mathrm{MeV}$. How many fissions happen each second, and what mass of U-235 is used per day? ($1\ \mathrm{eV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$, $N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{mol^{-1}}$, molar mass $0.235\ \mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$; type the mass in kg, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Nuclear Energy and Nuclear Reactors

Show hint

Fissions per second $= \dfrac{P}{E_{\text{fission}}}$; then moles $= N/N_A$ and mass $=$ moles $\times 0.235$.

Show solution
  1. $E_{\text{fission}} = 200 \times 10^6 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 3.2 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. Fissions per second $= \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^9}{3.2 \times 10^{-11}} \approx 3.12 \times 10^{19}$.
  3. Per day: $3.12 \times 10^{19} \times 86\,400 \approx 2.70 \times 10^{24}$ nuclei $= 4.48$ mol.
  4. Mass $= 4.48 \times 0.235 \approx 1.05\ \mathrm{kg}$ a day; a coal station of the same power burns thousands of tonnes.

About $3.12 \times 10^{19}$ fissions per second; about $1.05\ \mathrm{kg}$ of U-235 per day

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems on radioactive decay, nuclear energy, matter waves and special relativity. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Carbon-14 ($^{14}_{\ 6}\mathrm{C}$) decays by beta-minus emission. Write the equation and find the atomic number of the product. (Type $Z$.)

Uses: Radioactivity and Radioactive Decay

Show hint

In $\beta^-$ decay a neutron becomes a proton: $A$ stays the same and $Z$ rises by 1.

Show solution
  1. $^{14}_{\ 6}\mathrm{C} \to {}^{14}_{\ 7}\mathrm{N} + {}^{\ \ 0}_{-1}e^- + \bar\nu_e$.

$Z = 7$ (nitrogen-14)

Problem 2

The activity of a sample falls from $3200\ \mathrm{Bq}$ to $200\ \mathrm{Bq}$ in $12$ days. Find the half-life. (Type $T_{1/2}$ in days.)

Uses: Radioactivity and Radioactive Decay

Show hint

Find how many halvings take $3200$ down to $200$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{3200}{200} = 16 = 2^4$, so $4$ half-lives have passed.
  2. $T_{1/2} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3.0$ days.

$T_{1/2} = 3.0$ days

Problem 3

Find the binding energy of a helium-4 nucleus. Use atomic masses: $^1\mathrm{H} = 1.007825\ \mathrm{u}$, neutron $= 1.008665\ \mathrm{u}$, $^4\mathrm{He} = 4.002603\ \mathrm{u}$. ($1\ \mathrm{u} = 931.5\ \mathrm{MeV}$; type the binding energy in MeV, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Nuclear Energy and Nuclear Reactors

Show hint

Mass defect $= 2m_{\mathrm{H}} + 2m_n - m_{\mathrm{He}}$.

Show solution
  1. $2(1.007825) + 2(1.008665) = 2.015650 + 2.017330 = 4.032980\ \mathrm{u}$.
  2. $\Delta m = 4.032980 - 4.002603 = 0.030377\ \mathrm{u}$.
  3. $E_B = 0.030377 \times 931.5 \approx 28.3\ \mathrm{MeV}$, about $7.07\ \mathrm{MeV}$ per nucleon.

$E_B \approx 28.3\ \mathrm{MeV}$

Problem 4

A proton ($m = 1.67 \times 10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$) is accelerated from rest through $1000\ \mathrm{V}$. Find its de Broglie wavelength. ($h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$; type $\lambda$ in units of $10^{-13}\ \mathrm{m}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Wave-Particle Duality and Matter Waves

Show hint

$\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}}$.

Show solution
  1. $2mqV = 2 \times 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 1000 = 5.34 \times 10^{-43}\ \mathrm{kg^2\,m^2\,s^{-2}}$.
  2. $\sqrt{2mqV} \approx 7.31 \times 10^{-22}\ \mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{7.31 \times 10^{-22}} \approx 9.07 \times 10^{-13}\ \mathrm{m}$.

$\lambda \approx 9.07 \times 10^{-13}\ \mathrm{m}$

Problem 5

An astronaut travels at $0.80c$ to a star $4.0$ light-years away (as measured from Earth) and comes straight back. How much time passes on Earth, and how much for the astronaut? (Type the difference in years.)

Uses: Special Theory of Relativity

Show hint

Earth time $=$ distance ÷ speed. The astronaut's clock runs slow by $\gamma$.

Show solution
  1. Earth: each way $\dfrac{4.0}{0.80} = 5.0$ years, so $10$ years in all.
  2. $\gamma = \dfrac{1}{0.60}$, so the astronaut ages $\dfrac{10}{\gamma} = 10 \times 0.60 = 6.0$ years.
  3. Difference: $10 - 6.0 = 4.0$ years. The traveller comes back younger than a twin who stayed at home.

$10$ years on Earth, $6.0$ for the astronaut: $4.0$ years difference

Problem 6

Wood from an old boat has only $\tfrac18$ of the carbon-14 activity of fresh wood. The half-life of carbon-14 is $5730$ years. How old is the boat? (Type the age in years.)

Uses: Radioactivity and Radioactive Decay

Show hint

$\tfrac18 = \left(\tfrac12\right)^3$.

Show solution
  1. Three half-lives have passed.
  2. Age $= 3 \times 5730 = 17\,190$ years.

About $17\,190$ years

Matter Waves and Special Relativity

Six problems on the de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = h/mv$, electrons accelerated through a voltage, and time dilation, length contraction and relativistic energy with $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

An electron is accelerated from rest through $100\ \mathrm{V}$. Find its de Broglie wavelength. (Use $\lambda \approx \dfrac{1.227}{\sqrt V}\ \mathrm{nm}$; type $\lambda$ in nm, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Wave-Particle Duality and Matter Waves

Show hint

Put $V = 100$ into the electron formula.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda = \dfrac{1.227}{\sqrt{100}} = \dfrac{1.227}{10} \approx 0.123\ \mathrm{nm}$, about the spacing of atoms in a crystal, which is why electrons diffract from crystals.

$\lambda \approx 0.123\ \mathrm{nm}$

Problem 2

Find the Lorentz factor $\gamma$ for a spaceship moving at $0.60c$. (Type $\gamma$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Special Theory of Relativity

Show hint

$\gamma = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$.

Show solution
  1. $\gamma = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.36}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{0.64}} = \dfrac{1}{0.80} = 1.25$.

$\gamma = 1.25$

Problem 3

Muons have a lifetime of $2.2\ \mu\mathrm{s}$ when at rest. What lifetime is measured for muons moving at $0.99c$? (Type $\Delta t$ in $\mu\mathrm{s}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Special Theory of Relativity

Show hint

Time dilation: $\Delta t = \gamma\,\Delta t_0$.

Show solution
  1. $\gamma = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.99^2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{0.0199}} \approx 7.09$.
  2. $\Delta t = 7.09 \times 2.2 \approx 15.6\ \mu\mathrm{s}$. This is why muons made high in the atmosphere reach the ground.

$\Delta t \approx 15.6\ \mu\mathrm{s}$

Problem 4

A spaceship is $100\ \mathrm{m}$ long when at rest. How long is it measured to be by an observer it passes at $0.80c$? (Type $L$ in m.)

Uses: Special Theory of Relativity

Show hint

Length contraction: $L = L_0\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $L = 100\sqrt{1 - 0.64} = 100 \times 0.60 = 60\ \mathrm{m}$, measured along the direction of motion.

$L = 60\ \mathrm{m}$

Problem 5

Find the de Broglie wavelength of a $0.10\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball moving at $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Why do we never see a ball diffract? ($h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}$; type $\lambda$ in units of $10^{-34}\ \mathrm{m}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Wave-Particle Duality and Matter Waves

Show hint

$\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv}$.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{0.10 \times 30} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{3.0} = 2.21 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{m}$.
  2. This is far smaller than any gap or atom, so the wave nature of everyday objects can never be noticed.

$\lambda = 2.21 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{m}$

Problem 6

An electron (rest energy $0.511\ \mathrm{MeV}$) moves at $0.80c$. Find its total energy and its kinetic energy. Compare with $\tfrac12 m_0v^2$. (Type the kinetic energy in MeV, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Special Theory of Relativity

Show hint

$E = \gamma m_0c^2$ and $K = E - m_0c^2 = (\gamma - 1)m_0c^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\gamma = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 0.64}} = \dfrac{1}{0.60} \approx 1.667$.
  2. $E = 1.667 \times 0.511 \approx 0.852\ \mathrm{MeV}$, so $K = 0.852 - 0.511 \approx 0.341\ \mathrm{MeV}$.
  3. The classical $\tfrac12 m_0v^2 = \tfrac12 \times 0.511 \times 0.64 \approx 0.164\ \mathrm{MeV}$ is too small at this speed.

$E \approx 0.852\ \mathrm{MeV}$, $K \approx 0.341\ \mathrm{MeV}$

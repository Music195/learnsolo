Laws of Sines and Cosines, and Area

Six problems on the law of sines, the law of cosines, triangle area, the incircle and Heron's formula. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

In triangle ABC, $a = 4$, $A = 30^\circ$ and $B = 45^\circ$. Find $b$ and the circumradius $R$. (Type $b$ in the box.)

Uses: Law of Sines

Show hint

$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = 2R$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{4}{1/2} = 8 = 2R$, so $R = 4$.
  2. $b = 8\sin 45^\circ = 8 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 4\sqrt{2}$.

$b = 4\sqrt{2}$, $R = 4$

Problem 2

In triangle ABC, $b = 3$, $c = 8$ and $A = 60^\circ$. Find $a$.

Uses: Law of Cosines

Show hint

Two sides and the angle between them: law of cosines.

Show solution
  1. $a^2 = 3^2 + 8^2 - 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 8\cos 60^\circ = 9 + 64 - 24 = 49$.
  2. $a = 7$.

$a = 7$

Problem 3

A triangle has sides $3$, $5$ and $7$. Find its largest angle and say whether the triangle is acute, right or obtuse. Then find its area with Heron's formula. (Type the angle in the box.)

Uses: Law of Cosines, Heron's Formula

Show hint

The largest angle is opposite the longest side.

Show solution
  1. The largest angle $\theta$ is opposite the side $7$.
  2. $\cos\theta = \dfrac{3^2 + 5^2 - 7^2}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5} = \dfrac{-15}{30} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
  3. $\theta = 120^\circ$: an obtuse triangle.
  4. Heron: $s = \dfrac{15}{2}$, $S = \sqrt{\dfrac{15}{2} \cdot \dfrac{9}{2} \cdot \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{675}}{4} = \dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}$.
  5. Check: $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 5\sin 120^\circ = \dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}$ ✓.

$120^\circ$; obtuse; area $\dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}$

Problem 4

Find the area of triangle ABC with $a = 4$, $b = 5$ and $C = 120^\circ$.

Uses: Triangle Area Formulas, Ratios of 180° − θ

Show hint

$S = \dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, and $\sin 120^\circ = \sin 60^\circ$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin 120^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
  2. $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 5 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}$.

$5\sqrt{3}$

Problem 5

A triangle has sides $4$, $13$ and $15$. Find its area with Heron's formula, then its inradius $r$. (Type $r$ in the box.)

Uses: Heron's Formula, Incircle and Triangle Area

Show hint

$s = \dfrac{4 + 13 + 15}{2} = 16$. Then $S = \dfrac{1}{2}r(a + b + c)$ gives $r$.

Show solution
  1. $s = 16$: $S = \sqrt{16 \cdot 12 \cdot 3 \cdot 1} = \sqrt{576} = 24$.
  2. $24 = \dfrac{1}{2}r \cdot 32$, so $r = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

$S = 24$, $r = \dfrac{3}{2}$

Problem 6

In triangle ABC, $AB = 5$, $AC = 3$ and $\angle A = 120^\circ$.

  1. (1) Find BC.
  2. (2) Find the circumradius $R$.
  3. (3) Find the area of triangle ABC.
  4. (4) Find the distance from A to the line BC. (Type it in the box.)
  5. (5) Find the inradius $r$.

Uses: Law of Cosines, Law of Sines, Triangle Area Formulas, Incircle and Triangle Area

Show hint

The distance from A to BC is the height on the base BC: area $= \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot BC \cdot h$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $BC^2 = 25 + 9 - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 3\cos 120^\circ = 34 + 15 = 49$, so $BC = 7$.
  2. (2) $2R = \dfrac{7}{\sin 120^\circ} = \dfrac{14}{\sqrt{3}}$, so $R = \dfrac{7}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{7\sqrt{3}}{3}$.
  3. (3) $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 5 \cdot 3\sin 120^\circ = \dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}$.
  4. (4) $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 7 \cdot h = \dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}$, so $h = \dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{14}$.
  5. (5) $S = \dfrac{1}{2}r(5 + 3 + 7)$: $\dfrac{15}{2}r = \dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}$, so $r = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

(1) $7$ (2) $\dfrac{7\sqrt{3}}{3}$ (3) $\dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}$ (4) $\dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{14}$ (5) $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

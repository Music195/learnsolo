Trigonometric Ratios and Identities

Six problems on sine, cosine and tangent in right triangles and on the coordinate plane, the identities, and the 90° − θ and 180° − θ rules. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

In a right triangle, the side adjacent to the angle $\theta$ is $12$ and the hypotenuse is $13$. Find $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$. (Type $\tan\theta$ in the box.)

Uses: Definition of Trigonometric Ratios

Show hint

Find the opposite side with Pythagoras first.

Show solution
  1. Opposite side $= \sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
  2. $\sin\theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{12}{13}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{5}{12}$.

$\sin\theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{12}{13}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{5}{12}$

Problem 2

A $6$ m ladder leans against a wall and makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?

Uses: Definition of Trigonometric Ratios

Show hint

The ladder is the hypotenuse; the height is the side opposite the $60^\circ$ angle.

Show solution
  1. Height $= 6\sin 60^\circ = 6 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
  2. $= 3\sqrt{3}$ m (about $5.2$ m).

$3\sqrt{3}$ m

Problem 3

$\theta$ is acute and $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{3}$. Find $\sin\theta$, $\tan\theta$ and $\tan(90^\circ - \theta)$. (Type $\tan\theta$ in the box.)

Uses: Trigonometric Ratio Identities, Ratios of 90° − θ

Show hint

$\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta$, and $\sin\theta > 0$ for an acute angle.

Show solution
  1. $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{8}{9}$, so $\sin\theta = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$.
  2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} \div \dfrac{1}{3} = 2\sqrt{2}$.
  3. $\tan(90^\circ - \theta) = \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$.

$\sin\theta = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$, $\tan\theta = 2\sqrt{2}$, $\tan(90^\circ - \theta) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}$

Problem 4

Find the value of $\sin^2 25^\circ + \sin^2 65^\circ + \tan 40^\circ \tan 50^\circ + \cos 160^\circ + \cos 20^\circ$.

Uses: Ratios of 90° − θ, Ratios of 180° − θ, Trigonometric Ratio Identities

Show hint

$65^\circ = 90^\circ - 25^\circ$, $50^\circ = 90^\circ - 40^\circ$ and $160^\circ = 180^\circ - 20^\circ$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin 65^\circ = \cos 25^\circ$, so $\sin^2 25^\circ + \sin^2 65^\circ = \sin^2 25^\circ + \cos^2 25^\circ = 1$.
  2. $\tan 50^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\tan 40^\circ}$, so $\tan 40^\circ \tan 50^\circ = 1$.
  3. $\cos 160^\circ = -\cos 20^\circ$, so the last two cancel.
  4. Total: $1 + 1 + 0 = 2$.

$2$

Problem 5

Solve $2\cos^2\theta + \sin\theta - 1 = 0$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ$.

Uses: Trigonometric Ratio Identities, Ratios on the Coordinate Plane

Show hint

Replace $\cos^2\theta$ by $1 - \sin^2\theta$ to get a quadratic in $\sin\theta$. What values can $\sin\theta$ take on $0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ$?

Show solution
  1. $2(1 - \sin^2\theta) + \sin\theta - 1 = 0 \iff 2\sin^2\theta - \sin\theta - 1 = 0$.
  2. $(2\sin\theta + 1)(\sin\theta - 1) = 0$, so $\sin\theta = 1$ or $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
  3. On $0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ$, $0 \le \sin\theta \le 1$, so $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ is impossible.
  4. $\sin\theta = 1$: $\theta = 90^\circ$.

$\theta = 90^\circ$

Problem 6

$\theta$ is an obtuse angle with $\tan\theta = -2$.

  1. (1) Find $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$.
  2. (2) Find $\sin(180^\circ - \theta) + \cos(180^\circ - \theta)$.
  3. (3) Find $\dfrac{1}{1 + \sin\theta} + \dfrac{1}{1 - \sin\theta}$. (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Trigonometric Ratio Identities, Ratios of 180° − θ, Ratios on the Coordinate Plane

Show hint

Use $1 + \tan^2\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2\theta}$; for an obtuse angle $\cos\theta < 0$. In (3), put the fractions over a common denominator first.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\dfrac{1}{\cos^2\theta} = 1 + 4 = 5$, and $\cos\theta < 0$: $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$.
  2. $\sin\theta = \tan\theta\cos\theta = \dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$.
  3. (2) $\sin(180^\circ - \theta) + \cos(180^\circ - \theta) = \sin\theta - \cos\theta = \dfrac{3\sqrt{5}}{5}$.
  4. (3) $\dfrac{(1 - \sin\theta) + (1 + \sin\theta)}{1 - \sin^2\theta} = \dfrac{2}{\cos^2\theta} = 2 \cdot 5 = 10$.

(1) $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$, $\sin\theta = \dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$ (2) $\dfrac{3\sqrt{5}}{5}$ (3) $10$

Equations, Graphs and Inequalities

Six problems on the quadratic formula, the discriminant, where graphs meet the x-axis, and quadratic inequalities. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Solve $3x^2 + 4x - 2 = 0$, and give the larger solution.

Uses: Quadratic Formula

Show hint

The middle coefficient is even ($4 = 2 \cdot 2$), so use the $b'$ form: $x = \dfrac{-b' \pm \sqrt{b'^2 - ac}}{a}$.

Show solution
  1. $b' = 2$: $x = \dfrac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 6}}{3} = \dfrac{-2 \pm \sqrt{10}}{3}$.
  2. The larger one is $\dfrac{-2 + \sqrt{10}}{3}$.

$x = \dfrac{-2 \pm \sqrt{10}}{3}$; larger $\dfrac{-2 + \sqrt{10}}{3}$

Problem 2

Find the range of $k$ for which $x^2 - 6x + k = 0$ has two different real solutions, and the largest integer $k$.

Uses: Discriminant and Real Solutions

Show hint

Two different real solutions means $D > 0$, not $D \ge 0$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{D}{4} = 9 - k > 0$, so $k < 9$.
  2. The largest integer is $k = 8$.

$k < 9$; largest integer $8$

Problem 3

Find the length of the segment that the $x$-axis cuts from the graph of $y = 2x^2 - 4x - 3$.

Uses: Intersection with the x-axis, Quadratic Formula

Show hint

Solve $2x^2 - 4x - 3 = 0$; the length is the larger solution minus the smaller.

Show solution
  1. $x = \dfrac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 6}}{2} = \dfrac{2 \pm \sqrt{10}}{2}$.
  2. Length $= \dfrac{2 + \sqrt{10}}{2} - \dfrac{2 - \sqrt{10}}{2} = \sqrt{10}$.

$\sqrt{10}$

Problem 4

Find $k$ so that the line $y = 4x + k$ touches the parabola $y = x^2 + 2x$, and find the point of contact. (Type $k$ in the box.)

Uses: Intersection with the x-axis, Discriminant and Real Solutions

Show hint

Set the two equal: $x^2 + 2x = 4x + k$. 'Touches' means this equation has a repeated root.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 - 2x - k = 0$: $\dfrac{D}{4} = 1 + k = 0$, so $k = -1$.
  2. Repeated root $x = 1$; $y = 4 - 1 = 3$. Contact point $(1, 3)$.

$k = -1$; contact point $(1, 3)$

Problem 5

Solve the system $\begin{cases} x^2 - x - 6 < 0 \\ x^2 - 2x - 2 \ge 0 \end{cases}$ and find how many integers satisfy it.

Uses: Quadratic Inequalities, Quadratic Formula

Show hint

Solve each inequality, then overlap them on a number line. $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.73$.

Show solution
  1. $(x + 2)(x - 3) < 0$: $-2 < x < 3$.
  2. $x^2 - 2x - 2 = 0$ at $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{3}$, so the second is $x \le 1 - \sqrt{3}$ or $x \ge 1 + \sqrt{3}$.
  3. Overlap: $-2 < x \le 1 - \sqrt{3}$ or $1 + \sqrt{3} \le x < 3$, i.e. about $-2 < x \le -0.73$ or $2.73 \le x < 3$.
  4. The only integer is $x = -1$: $1$ integer.

$-2 < x \le 1 - \sqrt{3}$ or $1 + \sqrt{3} \le x < 3$; $1$ integer

Problem 6

We want $kx^2 + 4x + k - 3 > 0$ to hold for every real number $x$, where $k$ is a constant.

  1. (1) Show that $k = 0$ does not work.
  2. (2) For $k \ne 0$, state the two conditions on $k$ (the sign of $k$ and the discriminant).
  3. (3) Find the range of $k$, and the smallest integer $k$. (Type the smallest integer in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic Inequalities, Discriminant and Real Solutions, General Forms of Linear and Quadratic Functions

Show hint

A quadratic is positive for every $x$ when it opens upwards and never meets the $x$-axis.

Show solution
  1. (1) $k = 0$: $4x - 3 > 0$ fails at $x = 0$.
  2. (2) Need $k > 0$ and $\dfrac{D}{4} = 4 - k(k - 3) < 0$.
  3. (3) $k^2 - 3k - 4 > 0 \iff (k - 4)(k + 1) > 0 \iff k < -1$ or $k > 4$.
  4. With $k > 0$: $k > 4$. The smallest integer is $5$.

Averages and Spread

Six problems on the mean, median, mode, quartiles, interquartile range and box plots. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

On a 4-point quiz, $2$ students scored $1$, $5$ scored $2$, $8$ scored $3$ and $5$ scored $4$. Find the mean score.

Uses: Mean

Show hint

Multiply each score by how many students got it, add, and divide by the total number of students.

Show solution
  1. Number of students: $2 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 20$.
  2. Total: $1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 5 + 3 \cdot 8 + 4 \cdot 5 = 2 + 10 + 24 + 20 = 56$.
  3. Mean $= \dfrac{56}{20} = 2.8$.

$2.8$

Problem 2

Find the median and the mode of $4, 9, 7, 9, 2, 8, 9, 5$. (Type the median in the box.)

Uses: Median, Mode

Show hint

Sort first. With $8$ values, the median is the mean of the 4th and 5th.

Show solution
  1. Sorted: $2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 9, 9$.
  2. Median $= \dfrac{7 + 8}{2} = 7.5$.
  3. Mode: $9$ appears most often (3 times).

Median $7.5$, mode $9$

Problem 3

Find the quartiles, the interquartile range and the quartile deviation of $3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 13, 16, 19$. (Type the IQR in the box.)

Uses: Quartiles, Quartile Range and Deviation, Median

Show hint

$9$ values: the median is the 5th, and it is left out of both halves.

Show solution
  1. $Q_2 = 10$ (5th value).
  2. Lower half $3, 5, 6, 8$: $Q_1 = \dfrac{5 + 6}{2} = 5.5$. Upper half $11, 13, 16, 19$: $Q_3 = \dfrac{13 + 16}{2} = 14.5$.
  3. IQR $= 14.5 - 5.5 = 9$, quartile deviation $= 4.5$.

$Q_1 = 5.5$, $Q_2 = 10$, $Q_3 = 14.5$; IQR $9$, quartile deviation $4.5$

Problem 4

For the data $2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 30$, find the limits $Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ and $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$, and decide which values are outliers. (Type the upper limit in the box.)

Uses: Box Plot, Quartiles, Quartile Range and Deviation

Show hint

$8$ values: each half has $4$ values.

Show solution
  1. Lower half $2, 5, 6, 7$: $Q_1 = 5.5$. Upper half $8, 9, 10, 30$: $Q_3 = 9.5$. IQR $= 4$.
  2. Limits: $5.5 - 6 = -0.5$ and $9.5 + 6 = 15.5$.
  3. Only $30$ lies outside, so $30$ is the one outlier.

Limits $-0.5$ and $15.5$; $30$ is an outlier

Problem 5

The mean of $10$ numbers is $6$. One more number is added and the mean becomes $7$. Find the added number.

Uses: Mean

Show hint

Work with totals: total $= n \times$ mean.

Show solution
  1. Total of the $10$ numbers: $10 \times 6 = 60$.
  2. Total of the $11$ numbers: $11 \times 7 = 77$.
  3. Added number: $77 - 60 = 17$.

$17$

Problem 6

Seven data values are $4, 8, a, 12, 15, 9, 6$, and their mean is $9$.

  1. (1) Find $a$.
  2. (2) Find the median, the mode, $Q_1$, $Q_3$ and the interquartile range. (Type the IQR in the box.)
  3. (3) Using $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$, decide whether $15$ is an outlier.

Uses: Mean, Median, Mode, Quartiles, Quartile Range and Deviation, Box Plot

Show hint

Total $= 7 \times 9$. Then sort the seven values; the 4th is the median.

Show solution
  1. (1) $4 + 8 + 12 + 15 + 9 + 6 = 54$, and the total is $63$, so $a = 9$.
  2. (2) Sorted: $4, 6, 8, 9, 9, 12, 15$. Median $9$, mode $9$, $Q_1 = 6$, $Q_3 = 12$, IQR $= 6$.
  3. (3) $Q_3 + 1.5 \times 6 = 21 > 15$, so $15$ is not an outlier.

(1) $a = 9$ (2) median $9$, mode $9$, $Q_1 = 6$, $Q_3 = 12$, IQR $6$ (3) not an outlier

Variance and Correlation

Six problems on variance and standard deviation, transforming data, combining groups, covariance and the correlation coefficient. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find the variance and the standard deviation of $3, 5, 7, 9, 11$. (Type the variance in the box.)

Uses: Variance and Standard Deviation

Show hint

Find the mean, then the mean of the squared deviations.

Show solution
  1. Mean $= 7$; deviations $-4, -2, 0, 2, 4$.
  2. Variance $= \dfrac{16 + 4 + 0 + 4 + 16}{5} = 8$.
  3. Standard deviation $= \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$.

Variance $8$, standard deviation $2\sqrt{2}$

Problem 2

Use $s^2 = \overline{x^2} - (\overline{x})^2$ to find the variance of $1, 2, 4, 5$.

Uses: Variance and Standard Deviation

Show hint

Find the mean of the squares and the square of the mean.

Show solution
  1. $\overline{x} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$.
  2. $\overline{x^2} = \dfrac{1 + 4 + 16 + 25}{4} = 11.5$.
  3. $s^2 = 11.5 - 9 = 2.5$.

$2.5$

Problem 3

Data $x$ has mean $60$ and standard deviation $5$. Each value is changed to $y = -3x + 20$. Find the mean and the standard deviation of $y$. (Type the standard deviation in the box.)

Uses: Variance and Standard Deviation

Show hint

Adding a constant does not change the spread. Multiplying by $-3$ multiplies the standard deviation by $|-3|$.

Show solution
  1. Mean: $-3 \cdot 60 + 20 = -160$.
  2. Standard deviation: $|-3| \cdot 5 = 15$ (it can't be negative).

Mean $-160$, standard deviation $15$

Problem 4

Find the covariance and the correlation coefficient $r$ of the paired data $x$: $2, 4, 6, 8$ and $y$: $3, 7, 5, 9$.

Uses: Correlation Coefficient, Variance and Standard Deviation

Show hint

Make a table of deviations from the means ($\overline{x} = 5$, $\overline{y} = 6$) and their products.

Show solution
  1. $x - \overline{x}$: $-3, -1, 1, 3$; $\;y - \overline{y}$: $-3, 1, -1, 3$.
  2. $s_{xy} = \dfrac{9 - 1 - 1 + 9}{4} = 4$.
  3. $s_x^2 = \dfrac{9 + 1 + 1 + 9}{4} = 5$ and $s_y^2 = 5$.
  4. $r = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{5}\sqrt{5}} = 0.8$: a strong positive correlation.

$s_{xy} = 4$, $r = 0.8$

Problem 5

Group A has $4$ people with mean $5$ and variance $2$. Group B has $6$ people with mean $10$ and variance $3$. Find the mean and the variance of all $10$ people. (Type the variance in the box.)

Uses: Variance and Standard Deviation, Mean

Show hint

For each group, $\overline{x^2} = s^2 + (\overline{x})^2$. Combine the totals of $x$ and of $x^2$.

Show solution
  1. Mean: $\dfrac{4 \cdot 5 + 6 \cdot 10}{10} = 8$.
  2. A: $\overline{x^2} = 2 + 25 = 27$, total $108$. B: $\overline{x^2} = 3 + 100 = 103$, total $618$.
  3. All: $\overline{x^2} = \dfrac{726}{10} = 72.6$.
  4. Variance $= 72.6 - 8^2 = 8.6$.

Mean $8$, variance $8.6$

The combined variance (8.6) is much bigger than either group's, because the two group means are far apart.

Problem 6

Five students' scores on two tests are $x$: $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ and $y$: $5, 3, 4, 1, 2$ (same order).

  1. (1) Find the means $\overline{x}$ and $\overline{y}$.
  2. (2) Find the variances $s_x^2$, $s_y^2$ and the covariance $s_{xy}$.
  3. (3) Find the correlation coefficient $r$. (Type it in the box.)
  4. (4) If every $y$ score is doubled, what happens to $r$?

Uses: Correlation Coefficient, Variance and Standard Deviation, Mean

Show hint

Use the deviations from the means. For (4), think about what doubling does to $s_{xy}$ and to $s_y$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\overline{x} = 3$, $\overline{y} = 3$.
  2. (2) Deviations $x$: $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2$; $y$: $2, 0, 1, -2, -1$. $s_x^2 = 2$, $s_y^2 = \dfrac{4 + 0 + 1 + 4 + 1}{5} = 2$.
  3. $s_{xy} = \dfrac{-4 + 0 + 0 - 2 - 2}{5} = -\dfrac{8}{5}$.
  4. (3) $r = \dfrac{-8/5}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}} = -0.8$: a strong negative correlation.
  5. (4) $s_{xy}$ and $s_y$ both double, so $r$ stays $-0.8$.

(1) $3$, $3$ (2) $2$, $2$, $-\dfrac{8}{5}$ (3) $-0.8$ (4) unchanged

Protein Synthesis and Genetic Engineering

Six problems on transcription, mRNA processing, translation at the ribosome, recombinant DNA, golden rice and transgenic animals. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

The template strand of a gene reads $3\prime\text{-TAC GGA TTT ACT-}5\prime$. Write the mRNA and the amino acids it codes for (CCU = Pro, AAA = Lys). How many amino acids are in the chain? (Type the number.)

Uses: Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation

Show hint

mRNA is complementary to the template strand, with U in place of T.

Show solution
  1. mRNA: $5\prime\text{-AUG CCU AAA UGA-}3\prime$.
  2. AUG = Met (start), CCU = Pro, AAA = Lys, UGA = stop.
  3. The chain is Met–Pro–Lys: 3 amino acids (the stop codon has no amino acid).

AUG CCU AAA UGA → Met–Pro–Lys: $3$ amino acids

Problem 2

Give the anticodons of the tRNAs that pair with the mRNA codons AUG, GCU and UUC.

Uses: Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation

Show hint

The anticodon is complementary to the codon (A–U, G–C).

Show solution
  1. AUG → UAC (the initiator tRNA, carrying Met).
  2. GCU → CGA.
  3. UUC → AAG.

UAC, CGA, AAG

Problem 3

A pre-mRNA has three exons of $120$, $90$ and $150$ nucleotides separated by two introns. The exons run from the start codon to the stop codon with nothing else. Name the three changes made to pre-mRNA, and find how many amino acids the protein has. (Type the number of amino acids.)

Uses: Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation

Show hint

Only exons stay in mature mRNA. Every codon is 3 nucleotides, and the stop codon codes for nothing.

Show solution
  1. Changes: a 5′ cap is added, a 3′ poly-A tail is added, and the introns are cut out by the spliceosome (splicing).
  2. Coding length: $120 + 90 + 150 = 360$ nucleotides $= 120$ codons.
  3. One codon is the stop codon, so the protein has $120 - 1 = 119$ amino acids.

Cap, poly-A tail, splicing; $119$ amino acids

Problem 4

Describe one turn of the elongation cycle at the ribosome, naming the A, P and E sites.

Uses: Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation

Show hint

The growing chain is held in the P site.

Show solution
  1. A tRNA carrying the next amino acid enters the A site, its anticodon pairing with the codon.
  2. A peptide bond forms: the chain moves onto the amino acid of the tRNA in the A site.
  3. The mRNA moves forward one codon, so this tRNA (holding the chain) is now in the P site.
  4. The empty tRNA leaves from the E site, and the A site is free for the next tRNA.

A site: new tRNA arrives → peptide bond → chain moved to A-site tRNA → mRNA moves one codon (to P) → empty tRNA exits at E.

Problem 5

List the steps for putting a human gene into a bacterial plasmid. Why must the gene and the plasmid be cut with the same restriction enzyme?

Uses: Some Techniques in Molecular Biology

Show hint

Three tools: restriction enzyme (cuts), vector (carries), DNA ligase (joins).

Show solution
  1. Cut the wanted gene out of the human DNA with a restriction enzyme.
  2. Cut the plasmid open with the same restriction enzyme.
  3. Mix them and join the gene into the plasmid with DNA ligase, making recombinant DNA; put the plasmid into the bacterium.
  4. The same enzyme leaves matching ends on both pieces, so the ends fit together by base pairing and ligase can seal them.

Cut gene → cut plasmid with the same enzyme → join with ligase. The same enzyme gives matching ends that pair up.

Problem 6

Explain how golden rice was made and why it helps children in poor countries.

Uses: Some Techniques in Molecular Biology

Show hint

The vector was a plasmid in Agrobacterium.

Show solution
  1. Genes for making beta-carotene in the endosperm were taken from daffodils (later maize) and the bacterium Erwinia.
  2. They were put into plasmids in Agrobacterium, which infected rice embryos in a Petri dish and transferred the genes.
  3. The transgenic rice was crossed with local varieties.
  4. The orange beta-carotene is turned into vitamin A in the body. Lack of vitamin A causes blindness and weak immunity, a major cause of death in children who cannot afford a varied diet.

Beta-carotene genes (daffodil/maize + Erwinia) carried by Agrobacterium plasmids into rice; beta-carotene → vitamin A, preventing blindness and infection.

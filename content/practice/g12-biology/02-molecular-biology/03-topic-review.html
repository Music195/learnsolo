Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six problems mixing DNA structure and replication, the genetic code, protein synthesis and genetic engineering. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Give three differences between DNA and RNA.

Uses: DNA, RNA and DNA Replication

Show hint

Compare the sugar, the bases and the number of strands.

Show solution
  1. Sugar: DNA has deoxyribose; RNA has ribose.
  2. Bases: DNA has thymine; RNA has uracil instead.
  3. Strands: DNA is a double helix; RNA is single-stranded.

Deoxyribose vs ribose; T vs U; double vs single strand.

Problem 2

In a double-stranded DNA, $15\,\%$ of the bases are cytosine. What percentage are adenine? (Type the percentage.)

Uses: DNA, RNA and DNA Replication

Show hint

Purines = pyrimidines = $50\,\%$.

Show solution
  1. $\%\mathrm{G} = \%\mathrm{C} = 15\,\%$, so G + C $= 30\,\%$.
  2. A + T $= 70\,\%$, so $\%\mathrm{A} = 35\,\%$.

$35\,\%$ adenine

Problem 3

A protein has $150$ amino acids. What is the smallest number of nucleotides in the coding part of its mRNA, counting the stop codon? (Type the number.)

Uses: The Genetic Code, Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation

Show hint

Each amino acid needs one codon, and there is one stop codon.

Show solution
  1. Codons: $150 + 1 = 151$.
  2. Nucleotides: $3 \times 151 = 453$.

$453$ nucleotides

Problem 4

State the job of each in DNA replication: helicase, topoisomerase, primase, DNA polymerase III, DNA polymerase I, DNA ligase.

Uses: DNA, RNA and DNA Replication

Show hint

Follow the order of events at the replication fork.

Show solution
  1. Helicase unwinds and unzips the helix by breaking hydrogen bonds.
  2. Topoisomerase relieves the strain ahead of the fork.
  3. Primase makes a short RNA primer.
  4. DNA pol III adds nucleotides to the 3′ end, building new DNA $5\prime \to 3\prime$.
  5. DNA pol I replaces the RNA primers with DNA (and proofreads).
  6. DNA ligase joins the fragments.

Unzip; relieve strain; primer; build new strand; replace primers; join fragments.

Problem 5

Explain why one new strand is made continuously but the other is made in Okazaki fragments.

Uses: DNA, RNA and DNA Replication

Show hint

DNA polymerase III can only add nucleotides to a free 3′ end.

Show solution
  1. New DNA can only grow $5\prime \to 3\prime$, and the two template strands are antiparallel.
  2. The leading strand grows towards the fork, so it can be made continuously as the fork opens.
  3. The lagging strand must grow away from the fork, so it is made in short Okazaki fragments (about 100–200 nucleotides), each starting with its own primer.
  4. DNA pol I replaces the primers and ligase joins the fragments.

Synthesis only runs 5′→3′ on antiparallel templates: the leading strand follows the fork; the lagging strand is made backwards in fragments.

Problem 6

A company wants sheep that make a human clotting factor in their milk. Explain how this is done and why the product appears only in the milk.

Uses: Some Techniques in Molecular Biology

Show hint

The promoter decides in which cells a gene is switched on.

Show solution
  1. The human clotting factor gene is joined next to the β-lactoglobulin promoter.
  2. This DNA is put into a sheep oocyte (egg), which is fertilised and implanted in a host female.
  3. The offspring are transgenic: every cell has the gene.
  4. The β-lactoglobulin promoter works only in mammary cells, so the protein is made only there and is collected from the milk.

Gene + mammary promoter → into an oocyte → implanted → transgenic sheep; the promoter is active only in mammary cells, so the protein is in the milk.

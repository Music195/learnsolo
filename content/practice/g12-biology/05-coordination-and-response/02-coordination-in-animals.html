Nerves, Senses and Hormones

Six problems on the reflex arc, the parts of the brain, synapses, the iris reflex and accommodation, and endocrine glands. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Write the five parts of a reflex arc in order. In a withdrawal reflex, suppose the nerve path from a fingertip to the spinal cord and back to the arm muscle is $1.6\ \mathrm{m}$ long and impulses travel at $100\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. How long do the impulses spend travelling along neurones? (Type the time in ms.)

Uses: Coordination and Response in Animals: Nervous System

Show hint

$\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$.

Show solution
  1. Receptor → sensory neurone → interneurone (spinal cord) → motor neurone → effector (muscle).
  2. Time $= \dfrac{1.6}{100} = 0.016\ \mathrm{s} = 16\ \mathrm{ms}$.
  3. The whole reflex takes about $50\ \mathrm{ms}$: the rest is spent at receptors, synapses and the muscle. The brain is told afterwards.

Receptor → sensory → interneurone → motor → effector; $16\ \mathrm{ms}$

Problem 2

Which part of the brain: (a) coordinates posture and fine movements, (b) controls heart rate and breathing, (c) controls temperature, thirst and hunger, (d) is the largest part, for memory and language, (e) is the "great relay station"?

Uses: Coordination and Response in Animals: Nervous System

Show hint

Forebrain: cerebrum, thalamus, hypothalamus. Hindbrain: cerebellum, pons, medulla oblongata.

Show solution
  1. (a) Cerebellum.
  2. (b) Medulla oblongata.
  3. (c) Hypothalamus.
  4. (d) Cerebrum.
  5. (e) Thalamus.

(a) cerebellum, (b) medulla oblongata, (c) hypothalamus, (d) cerebrum, (e) thalamus

Problem 3

Describe how an impulse crosses an excitatory synapse. How does an inhibitory synapse differ?

Uses: Coordination and Response in Animals: Nervous System

Show hint

The impulse does not jump the gap: a chemical crosses it.

Show solution
  1. The impulse reaches the synaptic terminal; vesicles fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release neurotransmitter by exocytosis.
  2. The transmitter diffuses across the cleft (about $0.02\ \mu\mathrm{m}$) in about $0.5$–$1\ \mathrm{ms}$.
  3. It binds to receptor proteins on the postsynaptic membrane and opens ion channels: $\mathrm{Na^+}$ flows in and the membrane depolarises; at threshold a new impulse starts.
  4. An enzyme breaks the transmitter down. At an inhibitory synapse $\mathrm{K^+}$ flows out instead, so the membrane is hyperpolarised.

Vesicles release transmitter → diffuses → binds receptors → Na⁺ in, depolarisation; inhibitory: K⁺ out, hyperpolarisation.

Problem 4

You look up from a book to a bright sky. Describe the changes in (a) the iris muscles and pupil, (b) the ciliary muscles, suspensory ligaments and lens.

Uses: Sense Organs: The Eye

Show hint

There are two reflexes: one for brightness and one for distance.

Show solution
  1. (a) Bright light: the circular muscles contract and the radial muscles relax, so the pupil constricts.
  2. (b) Distant object: the ciliary muscles relax, the pressure in the eye pulls the suspensory ligaments tight, and the lens becomes flatter (less convex).

(a) circular contract, radial relax, pupil smaller; (b) ciliary relax, ligaments tight, lens flatter

Problem 5

Compared with looking at a distant object, the eye's lens needs about $1/d$ dioptres of extra power to focus an object at distance $d$ metres. How much extra power is needed to read a book at $25\ \mathrm{cm}$? (Type the power in dioptres.)

Uses: Sense Organs: The Eye

Show hint

Convert $25\ \mathrm{cm}$ to metres.

Show solution
  1. $d = 0.25\ \mathrm{m}$, so extra power $= \dfrac{1}{0.25} = 4\ \mathrm{D}$.
  2. The ciliary muscles contract, the ligaments go slack and the elastic lens bulges to give this extra power.

$4\ \mathrm{D}$

Problem 6

Name the gland that makes each hormone and its effect: (a) ADH, (b) glucagon, (c) thyroxine, (d) PTH, (e) epinephrine. Then give three differences between nervous and hormonal control.

Uses: The Endocrine System

Show hint

Remember: ADH and oxytocin come from the posterior pituitary.

Show solution
  1. (a) Posterior pituitary: water retention by the kidneys.
  2. (b) Pancreas: raises blood glucose (liver glycogen → glucose).
  3. (c) Thyroid: raises metabolic rate, regulates growth and development.
  4. (d) Parathyroid: raises blood calcium.
  5. (e) Adrenal medulla: fight-or-flight.
  6. Nervous: electrical impulses along neurones, fast, short-lived, local. Hormonal: chemicals in the blood, slower, longer-lasting, can act on organs all over the body.

Posterior pituitary, pancreas, thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal medulla; nerves are fast, brief, local and electrical; hormones are slow, lasting, widespread and chemical.

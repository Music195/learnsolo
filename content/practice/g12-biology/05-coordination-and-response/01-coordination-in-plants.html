Coordination and Growth in Plants

Six problems on tropisms and auxin, the five plant growth regulators, germination, photoperiodism and photomorphogenesis. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Name the response: (a) a shoot grows towards a window, (b) a root grows downwards, (c) a shoot grows upwards in the dark. Which hormone controls these bending responses?

Uses: Coordination and Response in Plants

Show hint

A tropism is named after its stimulus.

Show solution
  1. (a) Positive phototropism (towards light).
  2. (b) Positive gravitropism (towards gravity).
  3. (c) Negative gravitropism (away from gravity).
  4. All are controlled by auxin collecting on one side of the shoot or root.

(a) phototropism, (b) positive gravitropism, (c) negative gravitropism; auxin

Problem 2

Name the plant growth regulator used or responsible for each: (a) ripening bananas in a store room, (b) seedless fruits, (c) breaking seed dormancy and starting germination, (d) closing down growth in drought, (e) cell division (coconut milk is rich in it).

Uses: Plant Growth and Development

Show hint

There are three promoters (auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin) and two inhibitors (ethylene, abscisic acid).

Show solution
  1. (a) Ethylene, the only gaseous hormone, ripens fruits.
  2. (b) Auxin causes parthenocarpy (seedless fruit).
  3. (c) Gibberellin stimulates amylases and proteases in germinating seeds.
  4. (d) Abscisic acid, the stress hormone.
  5. (e) Cytokinin.

(a) ethylene, (b) auxin, (c) gibberellin, (d) abscisic acid, (e) cytokinin

Problem 3

A seedling on a windowsill is lit from one side only. Explain, step by step, why its shoot bends towards the light. Why does a root, with more auxin on its lower side, bend downwards instead?

Uses: Coordination and Response in Plants

Show hint

In the shoot, auxin makes cells elongate more; in the root, it makes them elongate less.

Show solution
  1. Auxin moves to the shaded side of the shoot tip.
  2. The cells on the shaded side elongate more than those on the lit side.
  3. The longer shaded side pushes the tip towards the light.
  4. In a horizontal root, auxin collects on the lower side, where it slows elongation; the upper side grows faster, so the root bends down.

More auxin on the shaded side → faster elongation there → bends to light. In roots auxin inhibits elongation, so the root bends towards the auxin side (down).

Problem 4

The critical day length for some plants is $12$ hours. Where the days are $14$ hours long, which of these will flower: soybean (short day plant), radish (long day plant), tomato (day neutral)? Explain using night length.

Uses: Plant Growth and Development

Show hint

Short day plants are really long night plants.

Show solution
  1. A $14$-hour day is longer than the critical period, and the night ($10$ h) is short.
  2. Soybean needs a day shorter than the critical period (a long night), so it does not flower.
  3. Radish needs a longer day (a short night), so it flowers.
  4. Tomato is day neutral, so it flowers regardless.

Radish and tomato flower; soybean does not.

Problem 5

Describe germination of a bean seed, in order. How does germination of a mango differ? What is vivipary?

Uses: Plant Growth and Development

Show hint

Look at which part elongates and where the cotyledons end up.

Show solution
  1. The seed absorbs water through the micropyle; enzymes (amylase, lipase, protease) mobilise the food store.
  2. The radicle grows first, then the hypocotyl, then the plumule.
  3. Bean: epigeal: the hypocotyl elongates fast and lifts the cotyledons above the ground.
  4. Mango: hypogeal: the epicotyl elongates faster and the cotyledons stay in the soil.
  5. Vivipary: the seed germinates inside the fruit while still on the parent (mangroves, Agave).

Water in → enzymes → radicle → hypocotyl → plumule; bean is epigeal, mango is hypogeal; vivipary is germination on the parent.

Problem 6

One seedling grows in the dark and another in light. Describe how they differ and name the process. Name the three photoreceptors involved.

Uses: Plant Growth and Development

Show hint

Light controls development as well as photosynthesis.

Show solution
  1. Dark-grown: long thin stem, undifferentiated chloroplasts (pale, no chlorophyll), small unexpanded leaves.
  2. Light-grown: light inhibits stem elongation, chloroplasts differentiate and fill with chlorophyll, leaves expand.
  3. This is photomorphogenesis: light-controlled development.
  4. Photoreceptors: phytochrome (red and far-red), cryptochrome and phototropin (blue).

Dark: tall, pale, small leaves; light: short, green, expanded leaves. Photomorphogenesis; phytochrome, cryptochrome, phototropin.

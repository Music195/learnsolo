Coordination and Response: Topic Review

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six problems mixing plant responses and growth regulators with nerves, the eye and hormones. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Compare rods and cones. What is special about the fovea and the blind spot?

Uses: Sense Organs: The Eye

Show hint

One works in dim light, the other gives colour.

Show solution
  1. Rods are sensitive in low light (night vision); cones give colour vision in bright light.
  2. The fovea has only cones and gives the clearest vision; images are normally focused there.
  3. The blind spot is where the optic nerve leaves: it has no photoreceptors, so there is no vision there.

Rods: dim light; cones: colour. Fovea: only cones, sharpest. Blind spot: no receptors.

Problem 2

Humans have $12$ pairs of cranial nerves and spinal nerves in five groups: $8$ cervical, $12$ thoracic, $5$ lumbar, $5$ sacral and $1$ coccygeal pair. How many nerves are there in total in the somatic nervous system? (Type the number of nerves.)

Uses: Coordination and Response in Animals: Nervous System

Show hint

Add the pairs first, then double.

Show solution
  1. Spinal pairs: $8 + 12 + 5 + 5 + 1 = 31$.
  2. All pairs: $12 + 31 = 43$.
  3. Nerves: $2 \times 43 = 86$.

$86$ nerves ($43$ pairs)

Problem 3

Compare the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems: what response does each give, and which transmitter does each use?

Uses: Coordination and Response in Animals: Nervous System

Show hint

Both are parts of the autonomic (involuntary) system.

Show solution
  1. Sympathetic: fight-or-flight (e.g. faster heart rate); transmitter norepinephrine.
  2. Parasympathetic: rest-and-digest (e.g. slower heart rate, more digestion); transmitter acetylcholine.

Sympathetic: fight-or-flight, norepinephrine. Parasympathetic: rest-and-digest, acetylcholine.

Problem 4

A farmer wants to (a) make cuttings form roots, (b) make grapes grow larger, (c) ripen picked mangoes evenly, (d) keep lettuce leaves green longer. Which plant growth regulator would help in each case?

Uses: Plant Growth and Development

Show hint

Auxin starts adventitious roots; gibberellin promotes fruit growth; ethylene ripens; cytokinin keeps chloroplasts developing.

Show solution
  1. (a) Auxin: starts lateral and adventitious roots.
  2. (b) Gibberellin: promotes fruit growth.
  3. (c) Ethylene: ripens fruits.
  4. (d) Cytokinin: promotes chloroplast development and delays ageing of leaves.

(a) auxin, (b) gibberellin, (c) ethylene, (d) cytokinin

Problem 5

Plants have no nervous system. Compare how a plant and a human coordinate responses, giving one similarity and two differences.

Uses: Coordination and Response in Plants, The Endocrine System

Show hint

Both use chemical messengers.

Show solution
  1. Similarity: both use chemicals made in one place and carried to another, where they cause a response (plant hormones; animal hormones).
  2. Difference 1: animals also have a nervous system with fast electrical impulses; plants rely on hormones only.
  3. Difference 2: plant responses are mostly slow growth responses (tropisms); animals can respond in milliseconds with muscle movement (reflexes).

Both use hormones; animals also use nerves, and respond much faster with muscles, while plants mostly respond by growth.

Problem 6

What is seed dormancy? Give three causes and three ways to break it. Which hormone promotes dormancy, and when is it made?

Uses: Plant Growth and Development

Show hint

A dormant seed is alive but will not germinate even in good conditions.

Show solution
  1. Seed dormancy is the internal inhibition of germination of a viable seed even in favourable conditions.
  2. Causes: a hard seed coat, an immature embryo, light-sensitive seeds, chemical inhibitors.
  3. Breaking it: scarification (scratching the coat), stratification (cold treatment), hormones (e.g. gibberellin).
  4. Abscisic acid promotes dormancy; it is the stress hormone, made in drought, saline soil, cold and frost.

Viable seed that will not germinate; hard coat, immature embryo, inhibitors; scarification, stratification, gibberellin; abscisic acid (stress).

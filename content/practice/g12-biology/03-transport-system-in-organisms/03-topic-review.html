Transport System in Organisms: Topic Review

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six problems mixing transport in plants and in animals. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Why are xylem vessels dead and hollow with no end walls? How does water move through them?

Uses: Transport System in Plants, Transpiration

Show hint

Think about what would slow the flow.

Show solution
  1. With no contents and no end walls, the vessels form long continuous tubes that do not block the flow.
  2. Water moves by mass flow, pulled up by the transpiration pull: cohesion holds the water molecules together and adhesion holds them to the vessel walls.

Open tubes let water move freely by mass flow, pulled up by transpiration.

Problem 2

Using $t \approx \dfrac{x^2}{2D}$: if oxygen takes about $250\ \mathrm{s}$ to diffuse $1\ \mathrm{mm}$, how many times longer does it take to diffuse $1\ \mathrm{cm}$? About how many hours is that? (Type how many times longer.)

Uses: Transport System in Animals

Show hint

Time grows with the square of the distance.

Show solution
  1. $1\ \mathrm{cm}$ is $10$ times farther, so it takes $10^2 = 100$ times longer.
  2. $100 \times 250\ \mathrm{s} = 25\,000\ \mathrm{s} \approx 7$ hours.

$100$ times longer, about $7$ hours

Problem 3

A leafy twig is fixed by a rubber stopper to a thistle funnel full of water, with the bottom of the funnel in a bowl of mercury. After some time, mercury rises in the funnel. Explain.

Uses: Transpiration

Show hint

What does the twig do with the water?

Show solution
  1. The leaves lose water by transpiration, which pulls water up from the funnel into the twig.
  2. Because the water column holds together (cohesion) and sticks to the walls (adhesion), it is pulled as a column.
  3. This leaves an empty space below, which is filled by rising mercury: it shows the transpiration pull.

Transpiration pulls water up the twig; mercury rises to fill the space: the transpiration pull.

Problem 4

Give two differences between arteries and veins. Name the artery that carries deoxygenated blood and the vein that carries oxygenated blood (in an adult).

Uses: Cardiovascular System in Mammals

Show hint

Compare the walls, pressure and valves.

Show solution
  1. Arteries carry blood away from the heart at high pressure; they have thick elastic and muscular walls.
  2. Veins carry blood back to the heart at low pressure; they have thinner walls with less elastic tissue and have valves.
  3. Exceptions: the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood; the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.

Arteries: away, thick walls, high pressure. Veins: back, thin walls, valves. Pulmonary artery and pulmonary vein.

Problem 5

There are about $5$ million red blood cells in each $\mathrm{mm^3}$ of blood. How many are in $5\ \mathrm{L}$ of blood? ($1\ \mathrm{L} = 10^6\ \mathrm{mm^3}$.) Why do red cells have no nucleus? (Type the number in units of $10^{13}$.)

Uses: Cardiovascular System in Mammals

Show hint

Find the volume in $\mathrm{mm^3}$ first.

Show solution
  1. $5\ \mathrm{L} = 5 \times 10^6\ \mathrm{mm^3}$.
  2. Number $= 5 \times 10^6 \times 5 \times 10^6 = 2.5 \times 10^{13}$.
  3. With no nucleus there is more room for haemoglobin, so each cell carries more oxygen.

$2.5 \times 10^{13}$ red cells

Problem 6

Give three advantages of the double circulation of mammals over the single circulation of fish. Why do birds and mammals need this?

Uses: Circulatory System

Show hint

Think about mixing, pressure and the delicate lung capillaries.

Show solution
  1. Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood cannot mix, so the tissues get as much oxygen as possible.
  2. Blood is pumped a second time after the lungs, so it reaches the body quickly at high pressure.
  3. Blood in the lungs is at low pressure, which does not damage the delicate capillaries and allows gas exchange.
  4. Birds and mammals move without the support of water and keep a constant body temperature, so their cells need plenty of oxygen and glucose.

No mixing, high pressure to the body, low pressure in the lungs; needed for active, warm-blooded life.

Transport in Plants

Six problems on surface area to volume ratio, xylem and phloem, the apoplast and symplast, transpiration and the pressure flow model of translocation. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

For a cube of side $s$, $\dfrac{A}{V} = \dfrac{6}{s}$. Find $A/V$ for cubes of side $1\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $2\ \mathrm{cm}$. What happens to the ratio as the body gets bigger? (Type $A/V$ for the $2\ \mathrm{cm}$ cube, in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Transport System in Plants

Show hint

Substitute $s$ into $6/s$.

Show solution
  1. $s = 1$: $A/V = 6\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.
  2. $s = 2$: $A/V = 3\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.
  3. Doubling the size halves the ratio: big compact bodies have little surface for their volume.

$6$ and $3\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$; the ratio falls as size grows

Problem 2

Compare a xylem vessel with a phloem sieve tube: is it living, does it have a nucleus, what does it carry, and in which direction?

Uses: Transport System in Plants, Translocation

Show hint

One is a dead hollow tube; the other is living but has no nucleus.

Show solution
  1. Xylem vessel: dead, empty, no end walls, no nucleus; carries water and mineral ions up from the roots by mass flow.
  2. Sieve tube: living, with cytoplasm but no nucleus (kept alive by a companion cell); carries sucrose and amino acids from source to sink, up or down.

Xylem: dead, water and ions, upwards. Phloem: living (no nucleus), sugars and amino acids, source to sink in either direction.

Problem 3

Describe the apoplastic and symplastic pathways across a root. Which is faster, and what happens to water in the apoplast when it reaches the endodermis?

Uses: Transport Mechanisms in Plants

Show hint

Apo- = through the walls; sym- = through the living contents.

Show solution
  1. Apoplast: through cellulose cell walls and intercellular spaces, by diffusion, without crossing a membrane. It carries most water and is faster.
  2. Symplast: through the cytoplasm and plasmodesmata, entering cells by osmosis across the membrane. It is slower.
  3. At the endodermis the Casparian strip (waxy suberin) blocks the walls, so water must enter the cytoplasm (symplast) to reach the xylem.

Apoplast (walls, faster) and symplast (cytoplasm, slower); the Casparian strip forces apoplast water into the cytoplasm.

Problem 4

Rank these days from fastest to slowest transpiration and explain: (A) hot, dry, windy afternoon; (B) cool, humid, still afternoon; (C) warm night.

Uses: Transpiration

Show hint

Transpiration rises with low humidity, wind, high temperature and light (open stomata).

Show solution
  1. A is fastest: dry air makes a steep water potential gradient, wind removes vapour near the leaf, heat speeds evaporation, and the stomata are open in daylight.
  2. B is slower: humid air gives a shallow gradient, and still air lets vapour build up near the leaf.
  3. C is slowest: at night the stomata close, so transpiration is almost zero.

A > B > C

Problem 5

In spring a stored potato tuber sprouts new shoots. Using the pressure flow theory, explain which way sucrose moves in the phloem, and why the tuber is now a source, not a sink.

Uses: Translocation

Show hint

A source releases sugar into the phloem; a sink removes it.

Show solution
  1. The tuber turns its starch back into sucrose and loads it into the sieve tubes, so it is now a source.
  2. Sugar entering the sieve tubes draws water in by osmosis, giving high turgor pressure in the tuber.
  3. At the growing shoot tips (sinks) sugar is used, water leaves by osmosis and the pressure is low.
  4. The solution flows from high to low pressure: upwards, from tuber to shoots.

Sucrose flows up from the tuber (source, high pressure) to the shoot tips (sink, low pressure).

Problem 6

A leaf is $10\ \mathrm{cm} \times 10\ \mathrm{cm} \times 0.1\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find its $A/V$, and compare it with a cube of the same volume. Why is a thin leaf a good shape? (Type the leaf's $A/V$ in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Transport System in Plants

Show hint

A box has $A = 2(lw + lh + wh)$. The cube has side $\sqrt[3]{V}$.

Show solution
  1. Leaf: $V = 10\ \mathrm{cm^3}$, $A = 2(100) + 4(10 \times 0.1) = 204\ \mathrm{cm^2}$, so $A/V = 20.4\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.
  2. Cube: side $\sqrt[3]{10} \approx 2.15\ \mathrm{cm}$, so $A/V = 6/2.15 \approx 2.78\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.
  3. The leaf has about 7 times more surface per volume: more area to collect light and $\mathrm{CO_2}$, and a short distance for gases to diffuse.

Leaf $20.4\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ vs cube about $2.78\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$

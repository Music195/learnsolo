Transport in Animals

Six problems on diffusion distances, open, single and double circulation, the mammalian heart, the cardiac cycle, blood clotting and tissue fluid. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Oxygen diffuses through water with $D \approx 2 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. Using $t \approx \dfrac{x^2}{2D}$, how long does it take to diffuse $1\ \mathrm{mm}$? (Type the time in seconds.)

Uses: Transport System in Animals

Show hint

Convert to metres: $1\ \mathrm{mm} = 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}$.

Show solution
  1. $t \approx \dfrac{(10^{-3})^2}{2 \times 2 \times 10^{-9}} = \dfrac{10^{-6}}{4 \times 10^{-9}} = 250\ \mathrm{s}$ (about 4 minutes).
  2. Far too slow to supply cells deep inside a large animal, which is why a mass transport system is needed.

About $250\ \mathrm{s}$

Problem 2

How many times does blood pass through the heart in one full circuit in (a) a fish, (b) a mammal? Name the circulation that carries blood to the lungs.

Uses: Circulatory System

Show hint

Follow one drop of blood all the way round.

Show solution
  1. (a) Fish: heart → gills → body → heart: once (single circulation).
  2. (b) Mammal: heart → lungs → heart → body → heart: twice (double circulation).
  3. The heart–lungs loop is the pulmonary circulation; the heart–body loop is the systemic circulation.

(a) once, (b) twice; pulmonary circulation

Problem 3

Trace the path of blood from the vena cava to the aorta, naming each chamber, valve and vessel.

Uses: Cardiovascular System in Mammals

Show hint

Right side to the lungs, left side to the body.

Show solution
  1. Vena cava → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle.
  2. → semilunar valve → pulmonary artery → lungs (blood is oxygenated).
  3. → pulmonary vein → left atrium → bicuspid (mitral) valve → left ventricle.
  4. → semilunar valve → aorta.

Vena cava → RA → tricuspid → RV → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → LA → bicuspid → LV → aorta

Problem 4

One cardiac cycle of an athlete during exercise takes $0.6\ \mathrm{s}$. Find the heart rate. Why is the left ventricle wall thicker than the right? (Type the heart rate in beats per minute.)

Uses: Cardiovascular System in Mammals

Show hint

$\text{heart rate} = \dfrac{60}{\text{cycle time}}$.

Show solution
  1. Heart rate $= \dfrac{60}{0.6} = 100$ beats per minute (at rest, $0.8\ \mathrm{s}$ gives 75).
  2. The right ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs, whose delicate capillaries need low pressure.
  3. The left ventricle must push blood to every part of the body, against the elastic recoil of the arteries, so it needs more muscle.

$100$ beats per minute

Problem 5

Put the events of blood clotting in order: fibrin threads trap blood cells; platelets and damaged tissue release thromboplastin; thrombin turns fibrinogen into fibrin; prothrombin is turned into thrombin. Which ion is needed?

Uses: Cardiovascular System in Mammals

Show hint

The chain starts at the wound and ends with the mesh.

Show solution
  1. Platelets and damaged tissue release thromboplastin.
  2. With calcium ions ($\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$), thromboplastin turns prothrombin into thrombin.
  3. Thrombin turns soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin.
  4. Fibrin threads form a mesh that traps blood cells: the clot.

Thromboplastin → (Ca²⁺) prothrombin to thrombin → fibrinogen to fibrin → mesh traps cells

Problem 6

In an adult, about $20\ \mathrm{L}$ of fluid leaves the blood capillaries each day and about $17\ \mathrm{L}$ returns to them by osmosis. How much lymph is formed per day, and where does it rejoin the blood? What would happen if the lymph vessels of a leg were blocked? (Type the lymph volume in litres.)

Uses: Lymphatic System

Show hint

Fluid leaving = fluid returning + lymph.

Show solution
  1. Lymph $= 20 - 17 = 3\ \mathrm{L}$ per day.
  2. It drains through lymph vessels (with valves) and lymph nodes into the thoracic duct, and rejoins the blood in the subclavian veins.
  3. If the vessels are blocked, tissue fluid builds up and the leg swells (oedema).

$3\ \mathrm{L}$ per day, back into the subclavian veins; blockage causes swelling

Problem 1

Name the branch of horticulture that deals with each of: (a) vegetables, (b) fruits and nuts, (c) grapes, (d) flowers.

Uses: Importance of Biology in Daily Life

Show hint

Horticulture comes from Latin hortus (garden) and cultura (cultivation).

Show solution
  1. (a) Vegetables: olericulture.
  2. (b) Fruits and nuts: pomology (fruticulture).
  3. (c) Grapes: viticulture.
  4. (d) Flowers: floriculture.

(a) olericulture, (b) pomology, (c) viticulture, (d) floriculture

Problem 2

What is hydroponics? Give two advantages over growing plants in soil.

Uses: Importance of Biology in Daily Life

Show hint

It is a type of hydroculture.

Show solution
  1. Hydroponics grows plants without soil: the roots stand in a mineral nutrient solution, sometimes held by inert perlite or gravel.
  2. Advantages: it uses less water, plants grow faster and the yields are higher.

Growing plants without soil in a nutrient solution; less water, faster growth, higher yield.

Problem 3

Name the plant that each drug comes from and one use: (a) aspirin, (b) quinine, (c) digitalis, (d) morphine. What is the study of drugs from natural sources called?

Uses: Importance of Biology in Daily Life

Show hint

The word comes from the Greek for "drug" + "knowledge".

Show solution
  1. (a) Aspirin (salicylic acid) from willow (Salix): pain and fever.
  2. (b) Quinine from Cinchona: malaria.
  3. (c) Digitalis from foxglove: heart disease.
  4. (d) Morphine (and codeine) from the opium poppy: severe pain.
  5. This study is pharmacognosy.

Willow, Cinchona, foxglove, opium poppy; pharmacognosy.

Problem 4

Write the stages of the silkworm life cycle in order and say at which stage silk is collected. Why are silk and wool called protein fibres, while cotton is not?

Uses: Importance of Biology in Daily Life

Show hint

Sericulture rears silkworms (Bombyx mori) on mulberry leaves.

Show solution
  1. egg → caterpillar (larva) → pupa inside a cocoon → adult moth.
  2. Silk is reeled from the cocoon that the larva spins around itself before it becomes a pupa.
  3. Silk and wool are made by animals and are protein. Cotton is a plant fibre: long sclerenchyma cells whose walls are thickened with cellulose.

Egg → larva → pupa in cocoon → moth; silk comes from the cocoon. Silk and wool are animal proteins; cotton is plant cellulose.

Problem 5

What is integrated fish farming? Name the three kinds of mariculture.

Uses: Importance of Biology in Daily Life

Show hint

Look at how the wastes of one part of the farm are used.

Show solution
  1. Integrated fish farming links sub-systems so that the waste of one becomes an input of another (for example, manure from livestock fertilises a pond and feeds the fish).
  2. Mariculture is offshore (open sea), inshore (coastal waters) or onshore (seawater tanks on land).

Wastes of one sub-system feed another; mariculture: offshore, inshore, onshore.

Problem 6

Put each food into its food group: rice, egg, groundnut oil, oranges, fish. Then name the microbe that turns milk into curd and the one that turns grape juice into wine. Why do we say that only plants make food?

Uses: Importance of Biology in Daily Life

Show hint

The groups are energy foods, body-building food, protective food and luxury food.

Show solution
  1. Energy foods (carbohydrates and fats): rice, groundnut oil.
  2. Body-building food (protein): egg, fish.
  3. Protective food (vitamins and minerals): oranges.
  4. Lactobacillus makes curd; yeast ferments grapes into wine.
  5. Only plants make food from simple substances by photosynthesis; all animal food comes from plants directly or through other animals.

Energy: rice, oil. Body-building: egg, fish. Protective: oranges. Lactobacillus (curd) and yeast (wine). Only plants photosynthesise.

The Five Themes of Biology

Six problems on the levels of organization, cells, gene expression, energy flow and chemical cycling, feedback, and evolution by natural selection. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Put these levels in order from the smallest to the largest: tissue, molecule, organism, cell, organ system, organelle, organ.

Uses: Themes of Biology

Show hint

Start from the chemical level. The cell is the lowest level that can carry out all the activities of life.

Show solution
  1. Molecules make up organelles, and organelles work together inside a cell.
  2. Similar cells form a tissue; tissues form an organ; organs form an organ system; organ systems form an organism.

molecule → organelle → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism

Problem 2

Give two differences between a prokaryotic cell and a eukaryotic cell, and name one example of each.

Uses: Themes of Biology

Show hint

Think about where the DNA is kept and whether the organelles have membranes.

Show solution
  1. A eukaryotic cell keeps its DNA in a membrane-enclosed nucleus; a prokaryotic cell has no nucleus.
  2. A eukaryotic cell has membrane-enclosed organelles (for example mitochondria); a prokaryotic cell has none, and it is also much smaller.
  3. Examples: a bacterium is prokaryotic; a plant, animal or fungus cell is eukaryotic.

Eukaryotic: nucleus and membrane organelles, larger (e.g. a leaf cell). Prokaryotic: no nucleus, no membrane organelles, smaller (e.g. a bacterium).

Problem 3

After a rice meal, blood glucose rises. Explain how negative feedback brings it back down, and say how positive feedback is different.

Uses: Themes of Biology

Show hint

In negative feedback the product of a process slows down that same process.

Show solution
  1. High blood glucose makes the pancreas release insulin.
  2. Insulin makes liver and muscle cells take up glucose, so blood glucose falls.
  3. As glucose falls, less insulin is released: the result switches off its own cause, so conditions stay steady.
  4. In positive feedback the product speeds up its own production, so the change grows instead of being reversed.

High glucose → insulin → glucose taken up by liver and muscle → glucose falls → less insulin (negative feedback). Positive feedback would make the change bigger.

Problem 4

In a simple food chain, grass stores $10\,000\ \mathrm{kJ}$ of chemical energy. Suppose only $10\,\%$ of the energy at each level passes to the next. How much reaches a snake that eats frogs that eat grasshoppers that eat the grass? Where does the rest go? (Type the energy in kJ.)

Uses: Themes of Biology

Show hint

Count the steps: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake is three transfers.

Show solution
  1. Grasshoppers: $10\,000 \times 0.10 = 1000\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  2. Frogs: $1000 \times 0.10 = 100\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  3. Snake: $100 \times 0.10 = 10\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  4. The rest is lost as heat from respiration (and in waste): energy flows one way and is not recycled, while chemicals do cycle back through decomposers.

$10\ \mathrm{kJ}$; the rest is lost as heat

Problem 5

A beetle population has $60$ green and $40$ brown beetles on brown soil. Each generation, birds leave alive only $50\,\%$ of the green beetles but $90\,\%$ of the brown ones, and the survivors breed in the same ratio. What percentage of the survivors are brown after one generation? (Type the percentage, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Themes of Biology

Show hint

Find the survivors of each colour, then the brown share of the total.

Show solution
  1. Green survivors: $60 \times 0.5 = 30$; brown survivors: $40 \times 0.9 = 36$.
  2. Brown share: $\dfrac{36}{30 + 36} \times 100\,\% \approx 54.5\,\%$ (it was $40\,\%$).
  3. A heritable trait that helps survival becomes more common: this is natural selection, and the population becomes adapted to its environment.

About $54.5\,\%$ brown

Problem 6

Sort these into the three domains of life: Escherichia coli, a mushroom, a microbe living in a boiling hot spring, a mango tree, an amoeba, a human. Which two domains are prokaryotic?

Uses: Themes of Biology

Show hint

The three domains are Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya. Eukarya includes protists and the kingdoms Plantae, Fungi and Animalia.

Show solution
  1. Bacteria: E. coli.
  2. Archaea: the hot-spring microbe (many archaea live in extreme places).
  3. Eukarya: mushroom (Fungi), mango tree (Plantae), amoeba (a protist), human (Animalia).
  4. Bacteria and Archaea are prokaryotic; Eukarya are eukaryotic.

Bacteria: E. coli. Archaea: hot-spring microbe. Eukarya: mushroom, mango tree, amoeba, human. Bacteria and Archaea are the prokaryotes.

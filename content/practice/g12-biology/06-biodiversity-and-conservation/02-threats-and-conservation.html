Threats to Biodiversity and Conservation

Six problems on the major threats (habitat loss, overexploitation, invasive species), in situ and ex situ conservation, seed banks and Myanmar's protected areas. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

List the five major threats to biodiversity. Which is the most significant cause of biodiversity loss globally?

Uses: Threats to Biodiversity

Show hint

One of them is about where organisms live.

Show solution
  1. Habitat loss and degradation; climate change; excessive use of fertilisers and pollution; overexploitation; invasive alien species.
  2. Habitat loss is the most significant cause globally.

Habitat loss, climate change, pollution, overexploitation, invasive species; habitat loss is the biggest.

Problem 2

A fish stock grows by $rN\left(1 - \dfrac{N}{K}\right)$ tonnes a year, with $r = 0.5$ per year and $K = 1000\ \mathrm{t}$. The largest catch that can be taken every year forever is $\dfrac{rK}{4}$. Find it. What happens if fishers take more? (Type the catch in tonnes.)

Uses: Threats to Biodiversity

Show hint

Substitute into $rK/4$.

Show solution
  1. Largest sustainable catch $= \dfrac{0.5 \times 1000}{4} = 125\ \mathrm{t}$ per year (when the stock is kept at $K/2 = 500\ \mathrm{t}$).
  2. If more is caught, fish are removed faster than the population can replace itself, so the stock falls each year and collapses: overfishing.

$125\ \mathrm{t}$ per year; more than this makes the stock collapse

Problem 3

Water hyacinth covers $0.1\,\%$ of a lake and doubles its area every $2$ weeks. What share does it cover after $10$ weeks? About how many weeks until it covers the whole lake? Why are invasive species so dangerous? (Type the number of weeks to the nearest week.)

Uses: Threats to Biodiversity

Show hint

Area $= 0.001 \times 2^{t/2}$. Solve $2^{t/2} = 1000$.

Show solution
  1. After $10$ weeks: $0.001 \times 2^5 = 0.032$, only $3.2\,\%$.
  2. Whole lake: $2^{t/2} = 1000$, so $t = 2\log_2 1000 \approx 19.9$ weeks, about $20$.
  3. It looks harmless for a long time, then covers everything quickly: native species are shaded out, and invaders with no natural enemies (like golden apple snails too) push out endemic species.

$3.2\,\%$ after 10 weeks; whole lake in about $20$ weeks

Problem 4

Sort into in situ and ex situ conservation: a national park, a seed bank, a zoo with captive breeding, a wildlife sanctuary, a botanical garden, a community forest. Why is in situ usually best?

Uses: Conservation

Show hint

In situ = in the natural habitat.

Show solution
  1. In situ: national park, wildlife sanctuary, community forest.
  2. Ex situ: seed bank, zoo, botanical garden.
  3. In situ is best because the habitat gives all the "life support systems", and species keep adapting naturally.

In situ: park, sanctuary, community forest. Ex situ: seed bank, zoo, botanical garden.

Problem 5

About $25\,\%$ of the world's $242\,000$ flowering plant species could disappear within $50$ years. How many species is that? How are seeds stored in a seed bank, and why? (Type the number of species.)

Uses: Conservation

Show hint

$25\,\% = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

Show solution
  1. $0.25 \times 242\,000 = 60\,500$ species.
  2. Seeds are dried and stored at $-14\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in the dark, so their metabolism slows and they do not germinate.
  3. They are tested for germination at intervals and grown into plants to collect fresh seed.

$60\,500$ species; dried, $-14\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, dark

Problem 6

Match each place to its category of protected area: (a) Lampi Island, (b) Hlawga Park, (c) Tanintharyi Nature Reserve, (d) Alaungdaw Kathapa. What is a community forest?

Uses: Conservation

Show hint

The categories include scientific reserve, national park, marine national park, nature reserve and wildlife sanctuary.

Show solution
  1. (a) Lampi Island: marine national park.
  2. (b) Hlawga Park: wildlife sanctuary (no capturing, killing or poaching).
  3. (c) Tanintharyi Nature Reserve: scientific reserve (study, research and education).
  4. (d) Alaungdaw Kathapa: national park (no settlement or harvesting; visitors allowed).
  5. A community forest (since the early 1990s) is managed sustainably by the local community itself, giving them income while conserving biodiversity.

(a) marine national park, (b) wildlife sanctuary, (c) scientific reserve, (d) national park

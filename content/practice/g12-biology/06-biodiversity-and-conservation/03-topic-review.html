Biodiversity and Conservation: Topic Review

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six problems mixing biodiversity, Myanmar's forests and species, threats and conservation. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Classify each reason for conservation as narrowly utilitarian, broadly utilitarian or ethical: (a) quinine comes from the Cinchona tree, (b) bees pollinate crops, (c) every species has the right to live, (d) forests control floods.

Uses: Conservation

Show hint

Narrow: products; broad: ecosystem services; ethical: intrinsic value.

Show solution
  1. (a) Narrowly utilitarian: a useful product (a drug).
  2. (b) Broadly utilitarian: an ecosystem service.
  3. (c) Ethical: intrinsic value.
  4. (d) Broadly utilitarian: an ecosystem service.

(a) narrow, (b) broad, (c) ethical, (d) broad

Problem 2

A $100\ \mathrm{km^2}$ forest is cut by roads into $4$ equal patches. One tiger needs about $40\ \mathrm{km^2}$ of forest. Before the roads, how many tigers could the forest hold? After? What is this threat called? (Type the number of tigers after the roads are built.)

Uses: Threats to Biodiversity

Show hint

Find the area of each patch.

Show solution
  1. Before: $100 / 40 = 2.5$, so about $2$ tigers.
  2. After: each patch is $25\ \mathrm{km^2}$, smaller than one territory, so no patch can hold a tiger: $0$.
  3. This is habitat fragmentation: most of the forest is still there, but no single piece is big enough for large species.

$2$ before, $0$ after; habitat fragmentation

Problem 3

If $60\,500$ flowering plant species are lost over $50$ years, on average how many species are lost each year? Give three ways that saved genes in a seed bank help plant breeders. (Type the number per year.)

Uses: Conservation

Show hint

Divide by the number of years.

Show solution
  1. $60\,500 / 50 = 1210$ species per year.
  2. Saved genes help breeders improve resistance to diseases and insects, drought or flood tolerance, and yield and nutrition.

$1210$ species per year

Problem 4

What is genetic pollution? How can it happen, and why is it a lasting problem?

Uses: Threats to Biodiversity

Show hint

It involves genetically engineered organisms.

Show solution
  1. Genetic pollution is undesirable gene flow from genetically engineered (GE) organisms into wild populations.
  2. It happens when wind, rain, birds, bees and insects carry altered pollen to nearby fields and wild relatives.
  3. Once released, GE organisms cannot be recalled.

Unwanted gene flow from GE organisms into wild plants via pollen; it cannot be undone.

Problem 5

Name three techniques used in captive breeding and three problems with it.

Uses: Conservation

Show hint

Captive breeding is ex situ, usually a last resort.

Show solution
  1. Techniques: artificial insemination, embryo transfer to a surrogate mother, cryogenics (frozen eggs, sperm and embryos), keeping a pedigree to prevent inbreeding.
  2. Problems: not enough space or money for every endangered species; the right breeding conditions are hard to provide; the threats in the wild may continue; captive-bred animals may struggle in the wild; reintroduction is slow, expensive and may fail.

Artificial insemination, embryo transfer, cryogenics; space and cost, breeding conditions, poor survival after release.

Problem 6

Explain why ecosystems with high species diversity tend to be more stable, and give four benefits that a healthy, biodiverse ecosystem provides to people.

Uses: Biodiversity

Show hint

Each species has a niche, a role to play.

Show solution
  1. With many species, each food or role is shared by several, so if one species declines, others can take its place; the ecosystem resists change better.
  2. Benefits: higher productivity; protection of fresh water; soil formation and protection; nutrient recycling; breakdown of pollutants; climate stability; faster recovery from disasters; more food and medicines.

Many species share roles, so the loss of one is buffered; e.g. clean water, soil protection, nutrient recycling, food and medicines.

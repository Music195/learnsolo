Biodiversity and Biodiversity in Myanmar

Six problems on the three levels of biodiversity, species richness and diversity, Myanmar's forests and its threatened species. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Name the level of biodiversity shown by each: (a) many varieties of rice, (b) the number of fish species on a coral reef, (c) a country with mangroves, rainforests and alpine meadows, (d) differently coloured shells of the snail Cepaea nemoralis.

Uses: Biodiversity

Show hint

The three levels are genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.

Show solution
  1. (a) Genetic diversity: variety of genes within one species.
  2. (b) Species diversity.
  3. (c) Ecosystem diversity: variety of habitats.
  4. (d) Genetic diversity.

(a) genetic, (b) species, (c) ecosystem, (d) genetic

Problem 2

A pond has $4$ species of fish with $25$ of each. Find Simpson's diversity index $D = 1 - \sum p_i^2$. (Type $D$.)

Uses: Biodiversity

Show hint

Each $p_i = 25/100$.

Show solution
  1. $p_i = 0.25$ for every species, so $p_i^2 = 0.0625$.
  2. $\sum p_i^2 = 4 \times 0.0625 = 0.25$.
  3. $D = 1 - 0.25 = 0.75$: a $75\,\%$ chance that two fish picked at random are different species.

$D = 0.75$

Problem 3

A second pond also has $4$ species and $24$ fish, but in numbers $18$, $2$, $2$, $2$. Find $D$. Both ponds have the same species richness: which has higher species diversity, and why? (Type $D$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Biodiversity

Show hint

$p_1 = 18/24 = 0.75$, the others $2/24$.

Show solution
  1. $\sum p_i^2 = 0.75^2 + 3\left(\dfrac{2}{24}\right)^2 = 0.5625 + 0.0208 = 0.583$.
  2. $D = 1 - 0.583 \approx 0.417$.
  3. Richness is 4 in both, but this pond is dominated by one species, so its diversity is lower ($0.417 < 0.75$). Diversity depends on both richness and evenness.

$D \approx 0.417$; the first (even) pond is more diverse

Problem 4

Myanmar has a land area of $676\,577\ \mathrm{km^2}$ and a total forest area of $290\,410\ \mathrm{km^2}$. What percentage of the land is forest? Is all of it closed forest? (Type the percentage, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Biodiversity in Myanmar

Show hint

$\dfrac{\text{forest}}{\text{land}} \times 100\,\%$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{290\,410}{676\,577} \times 100\,\% \approx 42.9\,\%$ (about $43\,\%$).
  2. No: only about half of it is closed forest; the other half is open or degraded.

About $42.9\,\%$; only half is closed forest

Problem 5

Hill and temperate evergreen forest makes up $25\,\%$ of Myanmar's $290\,410\ \mathrm{km^2}$ of forest. Find its area. Which forest type is the largest, and what share does it have? (Type the area in thousands of $\mathrm{km^2}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Biodiversity in Myanmar

Show hint

$\text{area} = \text{share} \times 290\,410\ \mathrm{km^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $0.25 \times 290\,410 = 72\,602.5\ \mathrm{km^2} \approx 72.6$ thousand $\mathrm{km^2}$.
  2. The largest type is mixed deciduous forest, $38\,\%$ (about $110\,356\ \mathrm{km^2}$).

About $72\,600\ \mathrm{km^2}$; mixed deciduous ($38\,\%$) is largest

Problem 6

Myanmar has about $250$ mammal species and $370$ reptile species; $39$ mammals and $21$ reptiles are globally threatened. Find the percentage of mammals that are threatened, and compare with reptiles. Name one critically endangered plant and one critically endangered animal of Myanmar. (Type the mammal percentage, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Biodiversity in Myanmar

Show hint

Threatened share $= \dfrac{\text{threatened}}{\text{total}} \times 100\,\%$.

Show solution
  1. Mammals: $\dfrac{39}{250} \times 100\,\% = 15.6\,\%$.
  2. Reptiles: $\dfrac{21}{370} \times 100\,\% \approx 5.7\,\%$: mammals are almost three times as likely to be threatened.
  3. Critically endangered: the Black Orchid (Paphiopedilum wardii) and the Burmese Star Tortoise (Geochelone platynota).

Mammals $15.6\,\%$, reptiles about $5.7\,\%$

Problem 1

Sort into infectious and non-infectious diseases: cholera, diabetes, measles, Alzheimer's disease, TB, cancer.

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens and pass from host to host.

Show solution
  1. Infectious: cholera (Vibrio cholerae), measles (Morbillivirus), TB (Mycobacterium).
  2. Non-infectious: diabetes, Alzheimer's disease, cancer.

Infectious: cholera, measles, TB. Non-infectious: diabetes, Alzheimer's, cancer.

Problem 3

Describe how an atheroma forms in an artery. Then explain the difference between angina and a heart attack.

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

It starts with damage to the inner lining (endothelium).

Show solution
  1. High blood pressure damages the endothelium; white blood cells (macrophages) move in and, with lipids, form fatty streaks.
  2. More cells, lipids and connective tissue build up and harden into a fibrous plaque (atheroma), which narrows the lumen.
  3. Angina: plaques narrow the coronary arteries, so during exercise the heart muscle gets too little oxygen and respires anaerobically, giving chest pain.
  4. Heart attack: a clot completely blocks a coronary artery, so part of the heart muscle gets no oxygen and dies.

Damaged lining → fatty streak → plaque. Angina = reduced flow (pain on exercise); heart attack = full blockage (muscle dies).

Problem 4

A man has mass $85\ \mathrm{kg}$ and height $1.70\ \mathrm{m}$. Find his BMI using $\text{BMI} = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{height}^2}$. Is it in the healthy range of $18.5$–$25\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}$? What mass would give a BMI of $25$? (Type his BMI, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

Square the height first.

Show solution
  1. $\text{BMI} = \dfrac{85}{1.70^2} = \dfrac{85}{2.89} \approx 29.4\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}$: above the healthy range.
  2. For BMI $25$: mass $= 25 \times 2.89 \approx 72.2\ \mathrm{kg}$, so he would need to lose about $13\ \mathrm{kg}$.
  3. Obesity is a modifiable risk factor, linked to high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes.

BMI $\approx 29.4$ (too high); $72.2\ \mathrm{kg}$ for BMI $25$

Problem 5

Name the pathogen and the vector of malaria. Put the stages of its life cycle in order, starting from the mosquito bite.

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

The mosquito carries the pathogen but does not cause the disease.

Show solution
  1. Pathogen: the protist Plasmodium (e.g. P. falciparum). Vector: the female Anopheles mosquito.
  2. Bite → parasites go to the liver cells and multiply → they invade red blood cells and multiply → some form gametes.
  3. A mosquito takes up the gametes with blood → they fuse in the mosquito gut → new parasites move to the salivary glands, ready for the next bite.

Plasmodium (pathogen), female Anopheles (vector); bite → liver → red cells → gametes → mosquito gut → salivary glands.

Problem 6

Give three ways to reduce the number of mosquitoes, saying which stage each one attacks. Why did the WHO eradication programme of the 1950s fail?

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

Mosquito larvae and pupae live in still water.

Show solution
  1. Spread oil on water: it suffocates the larvae and pupae.
  2. Drain marshes: removes breeding places.
  3. Stock water with larva-eating fish (Gambusia affinis) or spray Bacillus thuringiensis: kills larvae (biological control).
  4. It failed because Plasmodium became resistant to the drugs and mosquitoes became resistant to the insecticides DDT and dieldrin.

Oil, draining, larva-eating fish / Bacillus thuringiensis (all attack larvae); failure was due to drug and insecticide resistance.

Diseases in Plants and Animals: Topic Review

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six problems mixing plant diseases, cardiovascular disease and malaria. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Define endemic, epidemic and pandemic.

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

They describe how widely and suddenly a disease appears.

Show solution
  1. Endemic: always present in a population.
  2. Epidemic: a sudden rise in the number of cases.
  3. Pandemic: a rise in cases across a continent or the whole world (e.g. Covid).

Always present; sudden rise; worldwide rise.

Problem 2

Three people have blood pressures of $118/76$, $132/84$ and $152/96\ \mathrm{mmHg}$ (each measured regularly). Which are healthy and which have hypertension? Give two treatments for hypertension.

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

Healthy is about $120/80$; regularly above $140/90$ is high blood pressure.

Show solution
  1. $118/76$: healthy (about $120/80$).
  2. $132/84$: above ideal but not yet above $140/90$, so not hypertension; a warning to change lifestyle.
  3. $152/96$: regularly above $140/90$, so hypertension.
  4. Antihypertensive drugs such as beta blockers and ACE inhibitors lower blood pressure.

First healthy, second raised, third hypertension; beta blockers, ACE inhibitors.

Problem 3

What is a nematode, and how can a plant-parasitic nematode be recognised? Name one example and what it does.

Uses: Plant Diseases

Show hint

Look at its mouthparts.

Show solution
  1. Nematodes are tiny, soft-bodied, non-segmented roundworms.
  2. Plant parasites have a stylet, a piercing mouthpart: the key sign.
  3. Example: root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne), which make knots (galls) on roots.

Unsegmented roundworm; stylet; Meloidogyne (root-knot).

Problem 4

Compare a stroke and deep vein thrombosis (DVT): where does each happen, what causes it, and give one symptom or risk.

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

One is in the brain; the other is in a deep vein, usually in the leg.

Show solution
  1. Stroke: the blood supply to part of the brain is cut off by a blockage (clot, atheroma) or a burst vessel. Symptoms: slurred speech, confusion, paralysis on one side.
  2. DVT: a blood clot in a deep vein, usually in the legs, caused e.g. by long inactivity such as long-haul flights; the risk rises with age.

Stroke: brain, blockage or bleeding, e.g. slurred speech. DVT: leg vein clot after long inactivity.

Problem 5

A girl is $1.60\ \mathrm{m}$ tall. Find the range of masses that give a healthy BMI of $18.5$–$25\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}$. (Type the maximum healthy mass in kg.)

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

$\text{mass} = \text{BMI} \times \text{height}^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\text{height}^2 = 1.60^2 = 2.56\ \mathrm{m^2}$.
  2. Lowest: $18.5 \times 2.56 \approx 47.4\ \mathrm{kg}$; highest: $25 \times 2.56 = 64\ \mathrm{kg}$.

About $47.4$ to $64\ \mathrm{kg}$

Problem 6

Explain how each treatment reduces the risk of a heart attack: (a) statins, (b) beta blockers, (c) aspirin, (d) warfarin.

Uses: Animal Diseases: Cardiovascular Disease and Malaria

Show hint

Some act on cholesterol, some on blood pressure and some on clotting.

Show solution
  1. (a) Statins lower blood cholesterol and improve the LDL : HDL balance, so less plaque forms.
  2. (b) Beta blockers are antihypertensives: lower blood pressure means less damage to artery linings.
  3. (c) Aspirin is a platelet inhibitor, so clots are less likely to form on plaques.
  4. (d) Warfarin is an anticoagulant: it reduces clotting, so a clot is less likely to block a coronary artery.

Less cholesterol; lower blood pressure; fewer platelet clots; less clotting.

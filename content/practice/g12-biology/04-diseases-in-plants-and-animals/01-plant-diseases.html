Plant Diseases

Six problems on biotic and abiotic diseases, the disease triangle, signs and symptoms, the groups of plant pathogens and how diseases are controlled. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Say whether each is a biotic or an abiotic disease: (a) frost injury on many kinds of plant in one garden, (b) rust on wheat, (c) salt damage near the coast, (d) crown gall on a fruit tree.

Uses: Plant Diseases

Show hint

Biotic diseases are caused by living pathogens and spread; abiotic ones are caused by conditions.

Show solution
  1. (a) Abiotic: frost damages many species at once and does not spread.
  2. (b) Biotic: rust is caused by the fungus Puccinia.
  3. (c) Abiotic: salt is a non-living condition.
  4. (d) Biotic: crown gall is caused by the bacterium Agrobacterium.

(a) abiotic, (b) biotic, (c) abiotic, (d) biotic

Problem 2

Sort into signs and symptoms: mildew, chlorosis, bacterial ooze, wilting, nematode cysts, mosaic pattern.

Uses: Plant Diseases

Show hint

A sign is the pathogen itself; a symptom is the plant's reaction.

Show solution
  1. Signs (the pathogen): mildew, bacterial ooze, nematode cysts.
  2. Symptoms (the plant's reaction): chlorosis, wilting, mosaic pattern.

Signs: mildew, ooze, cysts. Symptoms: chlorosis, wilting, mosaic.

Problem 3

A farmer's rice suffers from bacterial blight (Xanthomonas) every wet season. Using the disease triangle, suggest one way to attack each side of the triangle.

Uses: Plant Diseases

Show hint

Disease needs a susceptible host, a pathogen and a favourable environment. Remove any one side and it cannot develop.

Show solution
  1. Host: grow a resistant rice variety.
  2. Pathogen: remove and destroy infected plants and use clean seed, so there is less of the bacterium (or use a bactericide).
  3. Environment: bacteria like warm, humid conditions and spread by rain splash, so improve drainage and spacing so the plants dry quickly.

Resistant variety (host); remove sources / bactericide (pathogen); drier, well-spaced crop (environment).

Problem 4

About $85\,\%$ of plant diseases are caused by fungi or fungus-like organisms. In a survey of $240$ different plant diseases, how many would you expect to be fungal? Name two fungal diseases and their causes. (Type the number.)

Uses: Plant Diseases

Show hint

Multiply the total by the fraction.

Show solution
  1. $0.85 \times 240 = 204$ diseases.
  2. Examples: rust of cereals (Puccinia), root rot (Fusarium), soft rot of fruits (Rhizopus).

$204$; e.g. rust (Puccinia) and root rot (Fusarium)

Problem 5

Tomato leaves show light green and yellow patches mixed with normal green, and the plants are stunted. What kind of pathogen is likely? Why would a fungicide not help, and what should the grower do?

Uses: Plant Diseases

Show hint

Which pathogens cause mosaics, and how do they enter plants?

Show solution
  1. A mosaic with stunting points to a virus (e.g. tomato spotted wilt).
  2. Fungicides act on fungi, not on viruses, which are particles inside the plant's cells.
  3. Viruses are systemic, so infected plants must be removed (rogued) and destroyed.
  4. Viruses are often carried by insects, so controlling the insect vectors stops the spread.

A virus; chemicals do not cure it; rogue infected plants and control insect vectors.

Problem 6

Classify each control method as biological, physical or chemical: (a) treating seeds with hot water, (b) adding a fungus that parasitises a root pathogen, (c) spraying a nematicide on soil, (d) a net barrier between insects and the crop, (e) soil bacteria that release antibiotics against a pathogen.

Uses: Plant Diseases

Show hint

Biological control uses living organisms; physical control uses temperature, moisture, irradiation and barriers; chemical control uses pesticides.

Show solution
  1. (a) Physical: high temperature.
  2. (b) Biological: an antagonist that parasitises the pathogen.
  3. (c) Chemical: a nematicide.
  4. (d) Physical: a barrier between pathogen (or vector) and host.
  5. (e) Biological: an antagonist that secretes antibiotics.

(a) physical, (b) biological, (c) chemical, (d) physical, (e) biological

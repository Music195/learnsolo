Radians and Trigonometric Formulas

Topic review: a mix of the whole topic, best tried after the three concept sets. Five problems on radians, sectors, the addition and double-angle formulas, and combining sine and cosine. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Convert $150°$ to radians.

Uses: Radians and Degrees

Show hint

$180° = \pi$ radians, so multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$.

Show solution
  1. $150 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

$\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

Problem 2

A sector has radius $6$ and central angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Find its arc length and area.

Uses: Arc Length and Sector Area

Show hint

Arc length $l = r\theta$, area $S = \dfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$. Type the area in the box.

Show solution
  1. $l = 6 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{3} = 2\pi$.
  2. $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 36 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{3} = 6\pi$.

Arc length $2\pi$, area $6\pi$

Problem 3

Find the exact value of $\sin 75°$.

Uses: Addition and Subtraction Formulas

Show hint

$75° = 45° + 30°$, and $\sin(\alpha + \beta) = \sin\alpha\cos\beta + \cos\alpha\sin\beta$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin 75° = \sin 45°\cos 30° + \cos 45°\sin 30°$.
  2. $= \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$.

$\sin 75° = \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$

Problem 4

$0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{5}$. Find $\sin 2\alpha$.

Uses: Half-Angle and Double-Angle Formulas

Show hint

$\sin 2\alpha = 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$. Find $\cos\alpha$ first; its sign comes from the range of $\alpha$.

Show solution
  1. $\cos\alpha > 0$ in this range, so $\cos\alpha = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{9}{25}} = \dfrac{4}{5}$.
  2. $\sin 2\alpha = 2 \cdot \dfrac{3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{24}{25}$.

$\sin 2\alpha = \dfrac{24}{25}$

Problem 5

Find the maximum and minimum values of $y = \sin\theta + \sqrt{3}\cos\theta$ for $0 \le \theta \le \pi$.

Uses: Sum of Sine and Cosine Formula, Graphs of Trigonometric Functions

Show hint

Combine into one sine: $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\sin(\theta + \alpha)$. Then find the range of $\theta + \alpha$. Type the minimum in the box.

Show solution
  1. $\sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$, and $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ give $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$: $y = 2\sin\left(\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$.
  2. $0 \le \theta \le \pi$ means $\dfrac{\pi}{3} \le \theta + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \le \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.
  3. On this range $\sin$ is largest ($1$) at $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and smallest ($-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$) at $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.
  4. Maximum $2$ at $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$; minimum $-\sqrt{3}$ at $\theta = \pi$.

Maximum $2$ ($\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$), minimum $-\sqrt{3}$ ($\theta = \pi$)

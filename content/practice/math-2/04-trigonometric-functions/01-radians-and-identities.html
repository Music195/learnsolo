Radians, Sectors and Identities

Six problems on radians, general angles, arc length and sector area, and the identities for any angle. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Convert $210^\circ$ to radians, and $\dfrac{5}{12}\pi$ to degrees. (Type the degrees in the box.)

Uses: Radians and Degrees

Show hint

$180^\circ = \pi$ rad.

Show solution
  1. $210^\circ = 210 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{7}{6}\pi$.
  2. $\dfrac{5}{12}\pi = \dfrac{5}{12} \cdot 180^\circ = 75^\circ$.

$\dfrac{7}{6}\pi$; $75^\circ$

Problem 2

A sector has radius $10$ and arc length $6\pi$. Find its central angle and its area. (Type the area in the box.)

Uses: Arc Length and Sector Area

Show hint

$l = r\theta$ and $S = \dfrac{1}{2}lr$.

Show solution
  1. $\theta = \dfrac{l}{r} = \dfrac{6\pi}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}\pi$.
  2. $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 6\pi \cdot 10 = 30\pi$.

$\theta = \dfrac{3}{5}\pi$, $S = 30\pi$

Problem 3

$\cos\theta = -\dfrac{5}{13}$ and $\pi < \theta < \dfrac{3}{2}\pi$. Find $\sin\theta$ and $\tan\theta$. (Type $\tan\theta$ in the box.)

Uses: Trigonometric Identities

Show hint

In the third quadrant, $\sin\theta < 0$ and $\cos\theta < 0$, so $\tan\theta > 0$.

Show solution
  1. $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \dfrac{25}{169} = \dfrac{144}{169}$, and $\sin\theta < 0$: $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{12}{13}$.
  2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{-12/13}{-5/13} = \dfrac{12}{5}$.

$\sin\theta = -\dfrac{12}{13}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{12}{5}$

Problem 4

Write $-\dfrac{17}{4}\pi$ in the form $\alpha + 2n\pi$ ($0 \le \alpha < 2\pi$, $n$ an integer), and say which quadrant it lies in. (Type $\alpha$ in the box.)

Uses: Radians and Degrees

Show hint

Add multiples of $2\pi = \dfrac{8}{4}\pi$ until the angle is between $0$ and $2\pi$.

Show solution
  1. $-\dfrac{17}{4}\pi + 3 \cdot 2\pi = -\dfrac{17}{4}\pi + \dfrac{24}{4}\pi = \dfrac{7}{4}\pi$.
  2. So $-\dfrac{17}{4}\pi = \dfrac{7}{4}\pi + 2 \cdot (-3)\pi$: $\alpha = \dfrac{7}{4}\pi$, $n = -3$.
  3. $\dfrac{3}{2}\pi < \dfrac{7}{4}\pi < 2\pi$: fourth quadrant.

$\alpha = \dfrac{7}{4}\pi$ ($n = -3$); fourth quadrant

Problem 5

A sector has area $9$. Find the smallest possible perimeter, and the radius and central angle that give it.

Uses: Arc Length and Sector Area

Show hint

$\dfrac{1}{2}rl = 9$ gives $l = \dfrac{18}{r}$. The perimeter is $2r + l$; use AM-GM (Math II, Expressions and Proof).

Show solution
  1. $l = \dfrac{18}{r}$, so the perimeter is $2r + \dfrac{18}{r}$.
  2. AM-GM: $2r + \dfrac{18}{r} \ge 2\sqrt{36} = 12$, with equality when $2r = \dfrac{18}{r}$, i.e. $r = 3$.
  3. Then $l = 6$ and $\theta = \dfrac{l}{r} = 2$ rad.

Smallest perimeter $12$, at $r = 3$, $\theta = 2$

Problem 6

$0 < \theta < \pi$ and $\sin\theta - \cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

  1. (1) Find $\sin\theta\cos\theta$.
  2. (2) Find $\sin^3\theta - \cos^3\theta$. (Type it in the box.)
  3. (3) Find $\sin\theta + \cos\theta$.
  4. (4) Find $\tan\theta + \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}$.

Uses: Trigonometric Identities

Show hint

Square the given equation. For (3), first decide the sign of $\sin\theta + \cos\theta$: what does $\sin\theta\cos\theta > 0$ tell you about the quadrant?

Show solution
  1. (1) $1 - 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{9}$, so $\sin\theta\cos\theta = \dfrac{4}{9}$.
  2. (2) $\sin^3\theta - \cos^3\theta = (\sin\theta - \cos\theta)(1 + \sin\theta\cos\theta) = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{13}{9} = \dfrac{13}{27}$.
  3. (3) $(\sin\theta + \cos\theta)^2 = 1 + \dfrac{8}{9} = \dfrac{17}{9}$. $\sin\theta\cos\theta > 0$ with $0 < \theta < \pi$ means $0 < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the sum is positive: $\dfrac{\sqrt{17}}{3}$.
  4. (4) $\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} + \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.

(1) $\dfrac{4}{9}$ (2) $\dfrac{13}{27}$ (3) $\dfrac{\sqrt{17}}{3}$ (4) $\dfrac{9}{4}$

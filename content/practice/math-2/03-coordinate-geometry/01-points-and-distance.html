Points, Distance and Centroid

Six problems on dividing a segment on a number line and in the plane, the distance formula and the centroid. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

On a number line, $A(-4)$ and $B(8)$. Find the distance $AB$, and the points dividing $AB$ internally and externally in the ratio $1 : 3$. (Type the external point in the box.)

Uses: Section Formula on a Number Line

Show hint

Internal: $\dfrac{na + mb}{m + n}$. External: $\dfrac{-na + mb}{m - n}$, with $m : n = 1 : 3$.

Show solution
  1. $AB = |8 - (-4)| = 12$.
  2. Internal: $\dfrac{3 \cdot (-4) + 1 \cdot 8}{4} = \dfrac{-4}{4} = -1$.
  3. External: $\dfrac{-3 \cdot (-4) + 1 \cdot 8}{1 - 3} = \dfrac{20}{-2} = -10$ (on the side of $A$, since $m < n$).

$AB = 12$; internal $-1$; external $-10$

Problem 2

Find the distance between $A(2, -1)$ and $B(-4, 7)$, and the midpoint of $AB$. (Type $AB$ in the box.)

Uses: Distance Formula

Show hint

$AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.

Show solution
  1. $AB = \sqrt{(-6)^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$.
  2. Midpoint: $\left(\dfrac{2 - 4}{2}, \dfrac{-1 + 7}{2}\right) = (-1, 3)$.

$AB = 10$, midpoint $(-1, 3)$

Problem 3

$A(-1, 4)$ and $B(5, -2)$. Find the point $P$ dividing $AB$ internally in the ratio $1 : 2$, and the point $Q$ dividing $AB$ externally in the ratio $3 : 1$. (Type the $x$-coordinate of $Q$ in the box.)

Uses: Section Formula in the Plane

Show hint

Use the section formula on the $x$- and $y$-coordinates separately. For external division, use $-n$ in place of $n$.

Show solution
  1. $P = \left(\dfrac{2 \cdot (-1) + 1 \cdot 5}{3}, \dfrac{2 \cdot 4 + 1 \cdot (-2)}{3}\right) = (1, 2)$.
  2. $Q = \left(\dfrac{-1 \cdot (-1) + 3 \cdot 5}{3 - 1}, \dfrac{-1 \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot (-2)}{3 - 1}\right) = (8, -5)$.

$P(1, 2)$, $Q(8, -5)$

Problem 4

The centroid of triangle ABC is $G(2, 1)$, with $A(-1, 3)$ and $B(4, -2)$. Find $C$. (Type its $x$-coordinate in the box.)

Uses: Centroid Coordinates

Show hint

$\dfrac{x_A + x_B + x_C}{3} = 2$ and $\dfrac{y_A + y_B + y_C}{3} = 1$.

Show solution
  1. $-1 + 4 + x_C = 6$, so $x_C = 3$.
  2. $3 - 2 + y_C = 3$, so $y_C = 2$.
  3. $C(3, 2)$.

$C(3, 2)$

Problem 5

On a number line, $A(a)$ and $B(b)$. The point dividing $AB$ internally in the ratio $2 : 1$ is $3$, and the point dividing $AB$ externally in the ratio $2 : 1$ is $11$. Find $a$ and $b$. (Type $a$ in the box.)

Uses: Section Formula on a Number Line

Show hint

Write both section formulas with $m = 2$, $n = 1$ and solve the two equations.

Show solution
  1. Internal: $\dfrac{a + 2b}{3} = 3$, so $a + 2b = 9$.
  2. External: $\dfrac{-a + 2b}{2 - 1} = 11$, so $-a + 2b = 11$.
  3. Adding: $4b = 20$, $b = 5$; then $a = -1$.

$a = -1$, $b = 5$

Problem 6

$A(-2, 1)$, $B(4, 3)$ and $C(3, 6)$.

  1. (1) Find $AB^2$, $BC^2$ and $CA^2$, and show that triangle ABC has a right angle.
  2. (2) Find the centre and the radius of the circle through A, B and C. (Type the radius in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the centroid G.
  4. (4) M is the midpoint of AC. Show that G divides BM internally in the ratio $2 : 1$.

Uses: Distance Formula, Section Formula in the Plane, Centroid Coordinates

Show hint

In (2), the hypotenuse of a right triangle is a diameter of its circumcircle.

Show solution
  1. (1) $AB^2 = 36 + 4 = 40$, $BC^2 = 1 + 9 = 10$, $CA^2 = 25 + 25 = 50 = AB^2 + BC^2$: right angle at B.
  2. (2) AC is a diameter: centre $M\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{7}{2}\right)$, radius $\dfrac{\sqrt{50}}{2} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
  3. (3) $G\left(\dfrac{-2 + 4 + 3}{3}, \dfrac{1 + 3 + 6}{3}\right) = \left(\dfrac{5}{3}, \dfrac{10}{3}\right)$.
  4. (4) The point dividing BM in $2 : 1$ is $\left(\dfrac{1 \cdot 4 + 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}}{3}, \dfrac{1 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot \frac{7}{2}}{3}\right) = \left(\dfrac{5}{3}, \dfrac{10}{3}\right) = G$ ✓.

(1) $40$, $10$, $50$; right angle at B (2) centre $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{7}{2}\right)$, radius $\dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}$ (3) $\left(\dfrac{5}{3}, \dfrac{10}{3}\right)$

Inequalities and Regions

Six problems on regions given by linear and circle inequalities, and on largest and smallest values over a region. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

How many of the points $(1, 1)$, $(3, 0)$, $(0, 3)$, $(2, 2)$ lie in the region $y > 2x - 3$, $x + y < 4$?

Uses: Linear Inequalities and Regions

Show hint

Substitute each point into both inequalities. A point on a boundary line does not count, because the inequalities are strict.

Show solution
  1. $(1, 1)$: $1 > -1$ ✓, $2 < 4$ ✓. In.
  2. $(3, 0)$: $0 > 3$ ✗. Out.
  3. $(0, 3)$: $3 > -3$ ✓, $3 < 4$ ✓. In.
  4. $(2, 2)$: $2 > 1$ ✓, but $4 < 4$ ✗ (on the line). Out.
  5. $2$ points.

$2$: $(1, 1)$ and $(0, 3)$

Problem 2

Decide whether $A(2, 1)$ and $O(0, 0)$ lie in the region $x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y < 4$.

Uses: Circle and Regions

Show hint

Complete the square to see the circle, then compare each point's distance from the centre with the radius.

Show solution
  1. $(x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 < 9$: inside the circle with centre $(1, -2)$ and radius $3$.
  2. $A$: $1^2 + 3^2 = 10 > 9$, outside.
  3. $O$: $1^2 + 2^2 = 5 < 9$, inside.

$A$ is not in the region; $O$ is.

Problem 3

Find the area of the region $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$, $x + 2y \le 6$, $2x + y \le 6$.

Uses: Linear Inequalities and Regions

Show hint

Draw the four boundary lines. The region is a quadrilateral; find where $x + 2y = 6$ and $2x + y = 6$ cross.

Show solution
  1. Corners: $(0, 0)$, $(3, 0)$, $(0, 3)$, and $x + 2y = 6$, $2x + y = 6$ meet at $(2, 2)$.
  2. Split along the diagonal from $(0, 0)$ to $(2, 2)$: two triangles, each $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 3$.
  3. Area $= 6$.

$6$

Problem 4

Describe the region $x^2 + y^2 \le 4$, $y \ge |x|$, and find its area.

Uses: Circle and Regions, Linear Inequalities and Regions

Show hint

$y \ge |x|$ means $y \ge x$ and $y \ge -x$: the part above both lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$.

Show solution
  1. The lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$ meet at a right angle at the origin.
  2. The region is the sector of the disk of radius $2$ between them, above the origin: a quarter of the disk.
  3. Area $= \dfrac{1}{4}\pi \cdot 2^2 = \pi$.

A quarter disk; area $\pi$

Problem 5

$x, y$ satisfy $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$, $x + 2y \le 10$, $3x + y \le 15$. Find the largest value of $2x + 3y$ and where it occurs.

Uses: Linear Inequalities and Regions

Show hint

Draw the region and the lines $2x + 3y = k$. The largest $k$ is reached at a corner of the region.

Show solution
  1. Corners: $(0, 0)$, $(5, 0)$, $(0, 5)$, and $x + 2y = 10$, $3x + y = 15$ meet at $(4, 3)$.
  2. $2x + 3y$ at the corners: $0$, $10$, $15$, $17$.
  3. Largest: $17$ at $(4, 3)$.

$17$ at $(x, y) = (4, 3)$

Why a corner? The slope of $2x + 3y = k$ is $-\dfrac{2}{3}$, between the slopes $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-3$ of the two edges that meet at $(4, 3)$.

Problem 6

$D$ is the region $x^2 + y^2 \le 5$, $y \ge 0$.

  1. (1) Describe $D$.
  2. (2) Find the largest value of $x + 2y$ on $D$, and the point where it occurs. (Type the value in the box.)
  3. (3) Find the smallest value of $x + 2y$ on $D$.

Uses: Circle and Regions, Linear Inequalities and Regions, Circle and Line: Distance Method

Show hint

Move the line $x + 2y = k$. The largest $k$ is where it touches the arc; for the smallest, check where the line last leaves the region.

Show solution
  1. (1) The upper half of the disk with centre $O$ and radius $\sqrt{5}$, including the boundary.
  2. (2) $x + 2y = k$ touches the circle when $\dfrac{|k|}{\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{5}$, i.e. $k = \pm 5$. For $k = 5$ the touching point is $(1, 2)$, which has $y \ge 0$. Largest $5$.
  3. (3) The touching point for $k = -5$ is $(-1, -2)$, not in $D$. Moving the line down, it last meets $D$ at the corner $(-\sqrt{5}, 0)$: smallest $-\sqrt{5}$.

(1) upper half-disk of radius $\sqrt{5}$ (2) $5$ at $(1, 2)$ (3) $-\sqrt{5}$ at $(-\sqrt{5}, 0)$

Roots and Coefficients

Six problems on the relations between roots and coefficients, factorising with complex numbers, and building an equation from its roots. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

$\alpha, \beta$ are the roots of $x^2 + 4x - 2 = 0$. Find $\alpha^2\beta + \alpha\beta^2$.

Uses: Roots and Coefficients

Show hint

Factor out $\alpha\beta$ first.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = -4$, $\alpha\beta = -2$.
  2. $\alpha^2\beta + \alpha\beta^2 = \alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta) = (-2)(-4) = 8$.

$8$

Problem 2

Factorise $x^2 - 6x + 10$ using complex numbers, and $2x^2 + 3x - 1$ using real numbers.

Uses: Quadratic Factorization

Show hint

Solve each equation $= 0$ with the quadratic formula; then $ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)$. Don't forget the $a$ in front.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 - 6x + 10 = 0$: $x = 3 \pm i$, so $x^2 - 6x + 10 = (x - 3 - i)(x - 3 + i)$.
  2. $2x^2 + 3x - 1 = 0$: $x = \dfrac{-3 \pm \sqrt{17}}{4}$.
  3. $2x^2 + 3x - 1 = 2\left(x - \dfrac{-3 + \sqrt{17}}{4}\right)\left(x - \dfrac{-3 - \sqrt{17}}{4}\right)$.

$(x - 3 - i)(x - 3 + i)$; $2\left(x + \dfrac{3 - \sqrt{17}}{4}\right)\left(x + \dfrac{3 + \sqrt{17}}{4}\right)$

Problem 3

Find the quadratic equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$ whose roots are $1 + \sqrt{2}\,i$ and $1 - \sqrt{2}\,i$. (Type $q$ in the box.)

Uses: Quadratic From Roots

Show hint

$x^2 - (\text{sum})x + (\text{product}) = 0$.

Show solution
  1. Sum: $2$. Product: $1^2 - (\sqrt{2}\,i)^2 = 1 + 2 = 3$.
  2. $x^2 - 2x + 3 = 0$, so $q = 3$.

$x^2 - 2x + 3 = 0$

Problem 4

$\alpha, \beta$ are the roots of $x^2 - 2x + 4 = 0$. Find $(\alpha - \beta)^2$ and $\alpha^3 + \beta^3$. (Type $\alpha^3 + \beta^3$ in the box.)

Uses: Roots and Coefficients

Show hint

$(\alpha - \beta)^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 4\alpha\beta$ and $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = (\alpha + \beta)^3 - 3\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta)$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = 2$, $\alpha\beta = 4$.
  2. $(\alpha - \beta)^2 = 4 - 16 = -12$ (negative, because $\alpha, \beta$ are imaginary).
  3. $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = 8 - 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = -16$.

$(\alpha - \beta)^2 = -12$, $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = -16$

Problem 5

$\alpha, \beta$ are the roots of $2x^2 - 3x + 4 = 0$. Find a quadratic equation with integer coefficients whose roots are $\alpha + \dfrac{1}{\beta}$ and $\beta + \dfrac{1}{\alpha}$. (Type the product of the new roots in the box.)

Uses: Roots and Coefficients, Quadratic From Roots

Show hint

Find the sum and the product of the new roots using only $\alpha + \beta$ and $\alpha\beta$.

Show solution
  1. $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{3}{2}$, $\alpha\beta = 2$.
  2. Sum: $(\alpha + \beta) + \dfrac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta} = \dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.
  3. Product: $\alpha\beta + 1 + 1 + \dfrac{1}{\alpha\beta} = 2 + 2 + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{9}{2}$.
  4. $x^2 - \dfrac{9}{4}x + \dfrac{9}{2} = 0$, i.e. $4x^2 - 9x + 18 = 0$.

$4x^2 - 9x + 18 = 0$

Problem 6

The equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$ has roots $\alpha, \beta$, and the equation $x^2 + qx + p = 0$ has roots $\alpha + 1, \beta + 1$.

  1. (1) Write $\alpha + \beta$ and $\alpha\beta$ in terms of $p, q$.
  2. (2) Find $p$ and $q$. (Type $q$ in the box.)
  3. (3) Factorise $x^2 + px + q$ using real numbers.

Uses: Roots and Coefficients, Quadratic From Roots, Quadratic Factorization

Show hint

Use roots and coefficients for both equations: $(\alpha + 1) + (\beta + 1) = -q$ and $(\alpha + 1)(\beta + 1) = p$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $\alpha + \beta = -p$, $\alpha\beta = q$.
  2. (2) $(\alpha + \beta) + 2 = -q$ gives $-p + 2 = -q$, so $q = p - 2$.
  3. $\alpha\beta + (\alpha + \beta) + 1 = p$ gives $q - p + 1 = p$, so $q = 2p - 1$.
  4. $p - 2 = 2p - 1$: $p = -1$, $q = -3$.
  5. (3) $x^2 - x - 3 = 0$ has roots $\dfrac{1 \pm \sqrt{13}}{2}$, so $x^2 - x - 3 = \left(x - \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{13}}{2}\right)\left(x - \dfrac{1 - \sqrt{13}}{2}\right)$.

(1) $-p$, $q$ (2) $p = -1$, $q = -3$ (3) $\left(x - \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{13}}{2}\right)\left(x - \dfrac{1 - \sqrt{13}}{2}\right)$

Integrals and Areas

Six problems on indefinite and definite integrals and on areas above and below the x-axis and between curves. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find $F(x)$ with $F'(x) = (x - 1)(3x + 1)$ and $F(2) = 5$. (Type $F(0)$ in the box.)

Uses: Indefinite Integral of xⁿ

Show hint

Expand, integrate term by term, then use $F(2) = 5$ to find $C$.

Show solution
  1. $F'(x) = 3x^2 - 2x - 1$, so $F(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x + C$.
  2. $F(2) = 8 - 4 - 2 + C = 5$, so $C = 3$.
  3. $F(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x + 3$, $F(0) = 3$.

$F(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x + 3$

Problem 2

Calculate $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (x^2 + 2x)\,dx$.

Uses: Definite Integral and Area 1

Show hint

$\Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b = F(b) - F(a)$ with $F(x) = \dfrac{x^3}{3} + x^2$.

Show solution
  1. $\left[\dfrac{x^3}{3} + x^2\right]_{-1}^{2} = \left(\dfrac{8}{3} + 4\right) - \left(-\dfrac{1}{3} + 1\right) = \dfrac{20}{3} - \dfrac{2}{3} = 6$.

$6$

Problem 3

Find the area enclosed by $y = -x^2 + 4x$ and the $x$-axis.

Uses: Definite Integral and Area 1

Show hint

The curve crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 0$ and $x = 4$ and is above it in between.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_0^4 (-x^2 + 4x)\,dx = \left[-\dfrac{x^3}{3} + 2x^2\right]_0^4 = -\dfrac{64}{3} + 32 = \dfrac{32}{3}$.

$\dfrac{32}{3}$

The $\dfrac{1}{6}$ formula gives the same: $\dfrac{|-1| \cdot (4 - 0)^3}{6} = \dfrac{32}{3}$.

Problem 4

Find the area between $y = x^2 - 4$, the $x$-axis and the lines $x = 0$ and $x = 3$.

Uses: Definite Integral and Area 2

Show hint

The curve is below the $x$-axis for $0 \le x < 2$ and above it for $2 < x \le 3$. Split the integral at $x = 2$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (x^2 - 4)\,dx = \dfrac{8}{3} - 8 = -\dfrac{16}{3}$, so that part has area $\dfrac{16}{3}$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_2^3 (x^2 - 4)\,dx = (9 - 12) - \left(\dfrac{8}{3} - 8\right) = \dfrac{7}{3}$.
  3. Area $= \dfrac{16}{3} + \dfrac{7}{3} = \dfrac{23}{3}$.

$\dfrac{23}{3}$

$\displaystyle\int_0^3 (x^2 - 4)\,dx = -3$ is not the area: the part below the axis counts as negative.

Problem 5

Find the area enclosed by $y = x^2 - 2x$ and $y = -x^2 + 4$.

Uses: Area Between Two Curves

Show hint

Find where they meet, then integrate (upper $-$ lower). The $\dfrac{1}{6}$ formula saves work.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 - 2x = -x^2 + 4 \iff 2x^2 - 2x - 4 = 0 \iff (x + 1)(x - 2) = 0$.
  2. Between $x = -1$ and $2$, $-x^2 + 4$ is on top: area $= \displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (-2x^2 + 2x + 4)\,dx$.
  3. $= -2\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (x + 1)(x - 2)\,dx = -2 \cdot \left(-\dfrac{3^3}{6}\right) = 9$.

$9$

Problem 6

A curve $C$: $y = f(x)$ passes through the origin, and $f'(x) = 2x - 2$.

  1. (1) Find $f(x)$.
  2. (2) Find the tangent $\ell$ to $C$ at the point where $x = 3$.
  3. (3) Find the area enclosed by $C$ and the $x$-axis.
  4. (4) Find the area enclosed by $C$, $\ell$ and the $y$-axis. (Type it in the box.)

Uses: Indefinite Integral of xⁿ, Tangent Line Formula, Definite Integral and Area 2, Area Between Two Curves

Show hint

For (4), the curve is above its tangent, and $f(x) - (\text{tangent})$ turns out to be a perfect square.

Show solution
  1. (1) $f(x) = x^2 - 2x + C$, and $f(0) = 0$ gives $C = 0$: $f(x) = x^2 - 2x$.
  2. (2) $f(3) = 3$, $f'(3) = 4$: $y - 3 = 4(x - 3)$, i.e. $y = 4x - 9$.
  3. (3) $C$ meets the axis at $x = 0, 2$ and is below it between: $-\displaystyle\int_0^2 (x^2 - 2x)\,dx = -\left(\dfrac{8}{3} - 4\right) = \dfrac{4}{3}$.
  4. (4) $\displaystyle\int_0^3 \{(x^2 - 2x) - (4x - 9)\}\,dx = \int_0^3 (x - 3)^2\,dx = \left[\dfrac{(x - 3)^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = 9$.

(1) $x^2 - 2x$ (2) $y = 4x - 9$ (3) $\dfrac{4}{3}$ (4) $9$

Derivatives and Areas

Topic review: a mix of the whole topic, best tried after the two concept sets. Five problems on derivatives, tangent lines, local maxima and areas. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

$f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$. Find $f'(2)$.

Uses: Power Function Derivative

Show hint

$(x^n)' = nx^{n-1}$, and a constant differentiates to $0$.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 6x$.
  2. $f'(2) = 24 - 12 = 12$.

$f'(2) = 12$

Problem 2

Find the tangent line to $y = x^2 - 3x$ at the point where $x = 1$.

Uses: Tangent Line Formula

Show hint

The tangent at $(a, f(a))$ is $y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a)$.

Show solution
  1. $f(1) = 1 - 3 = -2$, and $f'(x) = 2x - 3$ gives $f'(1) = -1$.
  2. $y - (-2) = -1(x - 1)$, so $y = -x - 1$.

$y = -x - 1$

Problem 3

Find the local maximum value of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 1$.

Uses: Monotonicity and Derivative Sign

Show hint

Solve $f'(x) = 0$ and make a table of signs of $f'(x)$. A local maximum is where $f'$ changes from $+$ to $-$.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x + 1)(x - 1)$, zero at $x = \pm 1$.
  2. $f'(x) > 0$ for $x < -1$, $< 0$ for $-1 < x < 1$, $> 0$ for $x > 1$.
  3. Local maximum at $x = -1$: $f(-1) = -1 + 3 + 1 = 3$. (Local minimum at $x = 1$: $f(1) = -1$.)

Local maximum $3$, at $x = -1$

Problem 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (3x^2 - 2x + 1)\,dx$.

Uses: Power Function Indefinite Integral, Definite Integral and Area 1

Show hint

Find an antiderivative $F(x)$, then compute $F(2) - F(0)$.

Show solution
  1. $F(x) = x^3 - x^2 + x$.
  2. $F(2) - F(0) = (8 - 4 + 2) - 0 = 6$.

$6$

Problem 5

Find the area of the region enclosed by $y = x^2$ and $y = x + 2$.

Uses: Definite Integral and Area 3

Show hint

Find where they meet, then integrate (upper) − (lower) between those $x$ values.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 = x + 2 \iff (x - 2)(x + 1) = 0$, so they meet at $x = -1$ and $x = 2$.
  2. Between them, the line is above the parabola.
  3. $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (x + 2 - x^2)\,dx = \left[\dfrac{x^2}{2} + 2x - \dfrac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{2} = \dfrac{10}{3} - \left(-\dfrac{7}{6}\right) = \dfrac{9}{2}$.

Area $= \dfrac{9}{2}$

Shortcut: $\displaystyle\int_\alpha^\beta -(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)\,dx = \dfrac{(\beta - \alpha)^3}{6}$, so the area is $\dfrac{3^3}{6} = \dfrac{9}{2}$ at once.

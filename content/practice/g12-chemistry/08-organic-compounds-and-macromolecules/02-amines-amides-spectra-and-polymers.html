Amines, Amides, IR Spectra and Polymers

Six problems on classifying amines, amide hydrolysis, reading IR wavenumbers $\bar\nu = 1/\lambda$, and the masses of addition and condensation polymers. ($\mathrm{C} = 12$, $\mathrm{H} = 1$, $\mathrm{O} = 16$, $\mathrm{N} = 14$, $\mathrm{Cl} = 35.5$.) Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

An IR absorption occurs at a wavelength of $5.80\ \mu\mathrm{m}$. Find its wavenumber in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and name the bond likely responsible. (Type $\bar\nu$ in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, to the nearest whole number.)

Uses: Identifying Functional Groups: Tests and IR Spectra

Show hint

$\bar\nu = \dfrac{1}{\lambda}$ with $\lambda$ in cm: $1\ \mu\mathrm{m} = 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm}$.

Show solution
  1. $\lambda = 5.80 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm}$, so $\bar\nu = \dfrac{1}{5.80 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 1724\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.
  2. This lies in the $1680$–$1750\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ range of the C=O bond (an aldehyde, ketone, acid or ester).

$\bar\nu \approx 1724\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$: C=O

Problem 2

A poly(ethene) chain is made from $2000$ ethene molecules. Find its molar mass. (Type $M$ in $\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Polymers and Plastics

Show hint

Addition polymer: nothing is lost, so $M = n \times M(\text{monomer})$; $M(\mathrm{C_2H_4}) = 28$.

Show solution
  1. $M = 2000 \times 28 = 56\,000\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$.

$56\,000\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 3

A condensation polymer chain is built from $100$ monomer molecules joined end to end in a line. How many water molecules are released? (Type the number.)

Uses: Polymers and Plastics

Show hint

Each new link between two monomers releases one water molecule.

Show solution
  1. $100$ monomers in a line are joined by $99$ links.
  2. So $99$ water molecules are given off; the chain mass is the total of the monomers minus $99 \times 18$.

$99$ water molecules

Problem 4

Classify $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2NH_2}$, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH}$ and $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3N}$ as primary, secondary or tertiary amines. How many N–H bonds does propan-1-amine have, and why does it boil higher than trimethylamine, which has the same formula? (Type the number of N–H bonds.)

Uses: Amines and Amides

Show hint

Count the carbon groups on the nitrogen.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2NH_2}$: primary ($1$ C on N); $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH}$: secondary; $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3N}$: tertiary.
  2. Propan-1-amine has $2$ N–H bonds and forms hydrogen bonds between molecules; trimethylamine has none, so it boils much lower (about $48^\circ\mathrm{C}$ against $3^\circ\mathrm{C}$).

$2$ N–H bonds; hydrogen bonding raises its boiling point

Problem 5

$5.9\ \mathrm{g}$ of ethanamide, $\mathrm{CH_3CONH_2}$, is boiled with dilute hydrochloric acid. Write the equation and find the mass of ethanoic acid formed. (Type the mass in g.)

Uses: Amines and Amides

Show hint

Acid hydrolysis breaks the C–N bond; $M(\mathrm{CH_3CONH_2}) = 59$, $M(\mathrm{CH_3COOH}) = 60$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{CH_3CONH_2 + H_2O + HCl \to CH_3COOH + NH_4Cl}$.
  2. $n = \dfrac{5.9}{59} = 0.10\ \mathrm{mol}$, so ethanoic acid $= 0.10 \times 60 = 6.0\ \mathrm{g}$.

$6.0\ \mathrm{g}$ of ethanoic acid

Problem 6

A sample of PVC, made from chloroethene ($\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCl}$), has an average molar mass of $125\,000\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. How many monomer units are in an average chain? Why is PVC hard to dispose of safely? (Type the number.)

Uses: Polymers and Plastics

Show hint

$M(\mathrm{C_2H_3Cl}) = 2(12) + 3(1) + 35.5$.

Show solution
  1. $M(\text{monomer}) = 62.5\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$.
  2. $n = \dfrac{125\,000}{62.5} = 2000$ units.
  3. It is not biodegradable, and burning it gives HCl gas and toxic chlorinated compounds such as dioxins.

$2000$ monomer units

Electron Arrangement and Ionic Bonding

Six problems on filling shells and orbitals, valence electrons, electronegativity difference and writing formulas of ionic compounds. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

What is the greatest number of electrons that the third shell ($n = 3$) can hold? (Type the number.)

Uses: Basic Concepts to Understand Chemical Bonding

Show hint

Shell $n$ holds at most $2n^2$ electrons.

Show solution
  1. $2n^2 = 2 \times 3^2 = 18$: $2$ in $3s$, $6$ in $3p$ and $10$ in $3d$.

$18$ electrons

Problem 2

Write the electron configuration of calcium ($Z = 20$). How many valence electrons does it have, and what ion does it form? (Type the number of valence electrons.)

Uses: Basic Concepts to Understand Chemical Bonding, Ionic Bonding

Show hint

Fill in the order $1s \to 2s \to 2p \to 3s \to 3p \to 4s$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{Ca}: 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6\,4s^2$ (shells $2, 8, 8, 2$).
  2. It has $2$ valence electrons. Losing them gives $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ with the argon configuration.

$2$ valence electrons; forms $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$

Problem 3

Using electronegativities $\mathrm{Na} = 0.9$ and $\mathrm{Cl} = 3.0$, find $\Delta\mathrm{EN}$ for sodium chloride and decide whether the bond is ionic or covalent. (Type $\Delta\mathrm{EN}$.)

Uses: Ionic Bonding

Show hint

$\Delta\mathrm{EN} > 1.8$ means ionic.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta\mathrm{EN} = 3.0 - 0.9 = 2.1$.
  2. $2.1 > 1.8$, so the bond is ionic: sodium gives its $3s$ electron to chlorine, forming $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{Cl^-}$.

$\Delta\mathrm{EN} = 2.1$: ionic

Problem 4

Aluminium forms $\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ and oxygen forms $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$. Write the formula of aluminium oxide. How many oxide ions are in one formula unit? (Type the number.)

Uses: Ionic Bonding

Show hint

The total charge must be zero. Use the lowest common multiple of $3$ and $2$.

Show solution
  1. LCM of $3$ and $2$ is $6$: $2\,\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ give $+6$ and $3\,\mathrm{O^{2-}}$ give $-6$.
  2. Formula: $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$, with $3$ oxide ions.

$\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$: $3$ oxide ions

Problem 5

Write the orbital (box) diagram for nitrogen ($Z = 7$). How many unpaired electrons does it have? (Type the number.)

Uses: Basic Concepts to Understand Chemical Bonding

Show hint

Hund's rule: electrons go into separate $p$ orbitals with parallel spins before pairing.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{N}: 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^3$.
  2. The three $2p$ electrons sit one in each $2p$ orbital ($\uparrow\ \uparrow\ \uparrow$), so there are $3$ unpaired electrons. This is why nitrogen forms three bonds, as in $\mathrm{NH_3}$.

$3$ unpaired electrons

Problem 6

Magnesium reacts with nitrogen to form magnesium nitride. Write its formula and find how many electrons are transferred in forming one formula unit. Which noble gas configuration does each ion have? (Type the number of electrons.)

Uses: Ionic Bonding, Basic Concepts to Understand Chemical Bonding

Show hint

Mg ($2, 8, 2$) loses 2 electrons; N ($2, 5$) gains 3.

Show solution
  1. Ions: $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{N^{3-}}$. LCM of $2$ and $3$ is $6$, so the formula is $\mathrm{Mg_3N_2}$.
  2. Three Mg atoms each lose $2$ electrons: $6$ electrons, all taken by the two N atoms ($2 \times 3 = 6$ ✓).
  3. Both $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{N^{3-}}$ have the neon configuration $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6$.

$\mathrm{Mg_3N_2}$: $6$ electrons transferred; both ions like neon

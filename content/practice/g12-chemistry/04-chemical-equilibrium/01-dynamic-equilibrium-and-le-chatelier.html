Dynamic Equilibrium and Le Chatelier's Principle

Six problems predicting how an equilibrium shifts when concentration, pressure or temperature changes, or a catalyst is added. Answer $1$ for right, $-1$ for left and $0$ for no shift. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

For $\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)}$, $\Delta H = -92\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, which way does the equilibrium shift when the pressure is increased? (Type $1$ for a shift to the right, $-1$ for a shift to the left, $0$ for no shift.)

Uses: Le Chatelier's Principle

Show hint

Higher pressure favours the side with fewer gas molecules.

Show solution
  1. Left: $1 + 3 = 4$ molecules; right: $2$ molecules.
  2. The system reduces the pressure by making fewer molecules: it shifts right, giving more ammonia.

Right ($1$): more $\mathrm{NH_3}$

Problem 2

For the same reaction ($\Delta H = -92\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$), which way does the equilibrium shift when the temperature is raised? (Type $1$ for a shift to the right, $-1$ for a shift to the left, $0$ for no shift.)

Uses: Le Chatelier's Principle

Show hint

Heating favours the endothermic direction.

Show solution
  1. The forward reaction is exothermic, so the reverse (left) direction is endothermic.
  2. Raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium left: less ammonia at equilibrium (though it is reached faster).

Left ($-1$)

Problem 3

For $\mathrm{H_2(g) + I_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2HI(g)}$, what happens to the position of equilibrium when the pressure is doubled? (Type $1$ for a shift to the right, $-1$ for a shift to the left, $0$ for no shift.)

Uses: Le Chatelier's Principle

Show hint

Count the gas molecules on each side.

Show solution
  1. $2$ molecules on each side, so changing the pressure cannot favour either side.
  2. No shift; all concentrations double, but their ratio $K_c$ is unchanged. (The purple colour gets darker only because $\mathrm{I_2}$ is squeezed.)

No shift ($0$)

Problem 4

An iron catalyst is added to the Haber process mixture at equilibrium. How does the position of equilibrium change, and what does the catalyst do? (Type $1$ for a shift to the right, $-1$ for a shift to the left, $0$ for no shift.)

Uses: Le Chatelier's Principle, Reversible Reactions and Dynamic Equilibrium

Show hint

A catalyst speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally.

Show solution
  1. Both rates rise by the same factor, so the position does not move.
  2. The catalyst only makes equilibrium be reached sooner.

No shift ($0$); equilibrium is reached faster

Problem 5

In $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}(aq) + SCN^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons FeSCN^{2+}(aq)}$ the product is blood red. A few drops of $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ solution are added. Which way does the equilibrium shift and what is seen? (Type $1$ for a shift to the right, $-1$ for a shift to the left, $0$ for no shift.)

Uses: Le Chatelier's Principle

Show hint

Adding a reactant: the system uses some of it up.

Show solution
  1. The equilibrium shifts right to remove some of the added $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$.
  2. More $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ forms, so the red colour deepens.

Right ($1$): deeper red

Problem 6

$\mathrm{N_2O_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO_2(g)}$, $\Delta H = +58\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ is colourless and $\mathrm{NO_2}$ is brown. A sealed tube of the mixture is put in ice water. Which way does the equilibrium shift, and what colour change is seen? Explain why the reactions do not stop at equilibrium. (Type $1$ for a shift to the right, $-1$ for a shift to the left, $0$ for no shift.)

Uses: Le Chatelier's Principle, Reversible Reactions and Dynamic Equilibrium

Show hint

Cooling favours the exothermic direction.

Show solution
  1. Forward is endothermic, so the reverse (left) is exothermic: cooling shifts the equilibrium left.
  2. Less brown $\mathrm{NO_2}$: the gas becomes paler.
  3. The equilibrium is dynamic: both reactions keep going at equal rates, so the amounts stay constant while molecules keep changing.

Left ($-1$): the brown colour fades

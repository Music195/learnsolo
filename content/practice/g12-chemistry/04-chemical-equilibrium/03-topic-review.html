Chemical Equilibrium

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems on dynamic equilibrium, Le Chatelier's principle and equilibrium constants. Answer shift questions with $1$ (right), $-1$ (left) or $0$ (no shift). Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

For $\mathrm{2SO_2 + O_2 \rightleftharpoons 2SO_3}$ at equilibrium, $[\mathrm{SO_2}] = 0.20$, $[\mathrm{O_2}] = 0.10$ and $[\mathrm{SO_3}] = 0.40\ \mathrm{M}$. Calculate $K_c$. (Type $K_c$.)

Uses: The Equilibrium Constant

Show hint

$K_c = \dfrac{[\mathrm{SO_3}]^2}{[\mathrm{SO_2}]^2[\mathrm{O_2}]}$.

Show solution
  1. $K_c = \dfrac{0.40^2}{0.20^2 \times 0.10} = \dfrac{0.16}{0.0040} = 40$.

$K_c = 40$

Problem 2

In the Contact process, $\mathrm{2SO_2 + O_2 \rightleftharpoons 2SO_3}$, extra air is pumped in. Which way does the equilibrium shift, and why is this useful? (Type $1$ for a shift to the right, $-1$ for a shift to the left, $0$ for no shift.)

Uses: Le Chatelier's Principle

Show hint

Air adds a reactant, oxygen.

Show solution
  1. Adding $\mathrm{O_2}$ shifts the equilibrium right to use some of it up.
  2. More $\mathrm{SO_3}$ is made from the expensive $\mathrm{SO_2}$, while air costs nothing.

Right ($1$)

Problem 3

For $\mathrm{H_2 + I_2 \rightleftharpoons 2HI}$, $K_c = 54.3$. What is $K_c$ for the reverse reaction $\mathrm{2HI \rightleftharpoons H_2 + I_2}$ at the same temperature? (Type it in units of $10^{-2}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: The Equilibrium Constant

Show hint

Writing the reaction backwards turns the expression upside down.

Show solution
  1. $K_c(\text{reverse}) = \dfrac{[\mathrm{H_2}][\mathrm{I_2}]}{[\mathrm{HI}]^2} = \dfrac{1}{54.3} \approx 0.0184 = 1.84 \times 10^{-2}$.

$K_c \approx 1.84 \times 10^{-2}$

Problem 4

Explain what is meant by a dynamic equilibrium, using a saturated salt solution with undissolved salt at the bottom as the example.

Uses: Reversible Reactions and Dynamic Equilibrium

Show hint

What is still happening when nothing seems to change?

Show solution
  1. Salt keeps dissolving from the crystals while dissolved ions keep crystallising back out, at exactly the same rate.
  2. So the amount of solid and the concentration stay constant, although particles keep moving between the two. This needs a closed system at constant temperature.

Forward and reverse processes continue at equal rates, so amounts stay constant

Problem 5

$1.0\ \mathrm{mol}$ of $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ is placed in a $1.0\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ flask: $\mathrm{N_2O_4 \rightleftharpoons 2NO_2}$, $K_c = 0.36$. Find $[\mathrm{NO_2}]$ at equilibrium. (Type it in M, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: The Equilibrium Constant

Show hint

If $x$ mol of $\mathrm{N_2O_4}$ reacts, $2x$ mol of $\mathrm{NO_2}$ forms. Solve the quadratic.

Show solution
  1. $K_c = \dfrac{(2x)^2}{1.0 - x} = 0.36 \implies 4x^2 + 0.36x - 0.36 = 0$.
  2. $x = \dfrac{-0.36 + \sqrt{0.36^2 + 4 \times 4 \times 0.36}}{2 \times 4} \approx 0.258$ (the negative root is impossible).
  3. $[\mathrm{NO_2}] = 2x \approx 0.517\ \mathrm{M}$; check $\dfrac{0.517^2}{1 - 0.258} \approx 0.36$ ✓.

$[\mathrm{NO_2}] \approx 0.517\ \mathrm{M}$

Problem 6

In a Haber process mixture at $500^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $[\mathrm{N_2}] = 1.0$, $[\mathrm{H_2}] = 3.0$ and $[\mathrm{NH_3}] = 0.50\ \mathrm{M}$ at equilibrium. Calculate $K_c$. Explain why industry uses about $450^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $200$ atm with an iron catalyst. (Type $K_c$ in units of $10^{-3}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: The Equilibrium Constant, Le Chatelier's Principle

Show hint

$K_c = \dfrac{[\mathrm{NH_3}]^2}{[\mathrm{N_2}][\mathrm{H_2}]^3}$. Then weigh yield against rate.

Show solution
  1. $K_c = \dfrac{0.50^2}{1.0 \times 3.0^3} = \dfrac{0.25}{27} \approx 0.00926 = 9.26 \times 10^{-3}$.
  2. The forward reaction is exothermic, so a low temperature gives more ammonia, but too slowly; $450^\circ\mathrm{C}$ with a catalyst is a compromise giving a fair yield quickly.
  3. High pressure favours the side with fewer molecules (more $\mathrm{NH_3}$) and speeds the reaction, but very high pressure is costly and dangerous, so about $200$ atm is used.

$K_c \approx 9.26 \times 10^{-3}$; temperature and pressure are compromises between yield, rate and cost

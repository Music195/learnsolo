Energy Changes in Chemical Reactions

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems on exothermic and endothermic changes, calorimetry, Hess's law and bond enthalpies. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

The total enthalpy of the reactants of a reaction is $400\ \mathrm{kJ}$ and of the products is $250\ \mathrm{kJ}$. Find $\Delta H$ and say whether the reaction is exothermic. (Type $\Delta H$ in kJ.)

Uses: Enthalpy Changes and Calorimetry, Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions

Show hint

$\Delta H = H_{\text{products}} - H_{\text{reactants}}$.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta H = 250 - 400 = -150\ \mathrm{kJ}$: negative, so exothermic; $150\ \mathrm{kJ}$ goes to the surroundings.

$\Delta H = -150\ \mathrm{kJ}$: exothermic

Problem 2

$25\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $2.0\ \mathrm{M}$ HCl and $25\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $2.0\ \mathrm{M}$ NaOH, both at $25.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$, are mixed and reach $38.5^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Find the enthalpy of neutralisation. (Take $50\ \mathrm{g}$ of solution; type $\Delta H$ in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Enthalpy Changes and Calorimetry

Show hint

$q = mc\Delta T$; moles of water $=$ moles of HCl.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta T = 13.5^\circ\mathrm{C}$, so $q = 50 \times 4.18 \times 13.5 = 2821.5\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $n = 2.0 \times 0.025 = 0.050\ \mathrm{mol}$.
  3. $\Delta H = -\dfrac{2821.5}{0.050}\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} \approx -56.4\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$\Delta H \approx -56.4\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 3

Find $\Delta H^\circ$ for the hydrogenation $\mathrm{C_2H_4 + H_2 \to C_2H_6}$ from $\Delta H_f^\circ(\mathrm{C_2H_4}) = +52$ and $\Delta H_f^\circ(\mathrm{C_2H_6}) = -85\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. (Type it in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

$\Delta H_f^\circ$ of an element ($\mathrm{H_2}$) is zero.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta H^\circ = -85 - (52 + 0) = -137\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$\Delta H^\circ = -137\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 4

Estimate $\Delta H$ for the same reaction, $\mathrm{C_2H_4 + H_2 \to C_2H_6}$, from bond enthalpies: $\mathrm{C{=}C} = 612$, $\mathrm{H{-}H} = 436$, $\mathrm{C{-}C} = 347$, $\mathrm{C{-}H} = 410\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. (Type it in kJ.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

The four C–H bonds of ethene are unchanged. Break C=C and H–H; form C–C and two new C–H.

Show solution
  1. Broken: $612 + 436 = 1048\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  2. Formed: $347 + 2(410) = 1167\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  3. $\Delta H \approx 1048 - 1167 = -119\ \mathrm{kJ}$, close to $-137$ but not equal, because bond enthalpies are averages.

$\Delta H \approx -119\ \mathrm{kJ}$

Problem 5

Propane burns with $\Delta H_c = -2220\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Using $\Delta H_c$(C) $= -394$ and $\Delta H_c(\mathrm{H_2}) = -286\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, find $\Delta H_f^\circ$ of propane, $\mathrm{3C + 4H_2 \to C_3H_8}$. (Type it in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

$\Delta H_r^\circ = \sum\Delta H_c(\text{reactants}) - \sum\Delta H_c(\text{products})$.

Show solution
  1. Reactants: $3(-394) + 4(-286) = -1182 - 1144 = -2326$.
  2. $\Delta H_f^\circ = -2326 - (-2220) = -106\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$\Delta H_f^\circ = -106\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 6

A gas stove burns propane ($\Delta H_c = -2220\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, $M = 44\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$). What mass of propane gives $1.0\ \mathrm{MJ}$ of heat? Draw the energy profile and label $\Delta H$. (Type the mass in g, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies, Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions

Show hint

$n = \dfrac{\text{heat}}{|\Delta H_c|}$, then $m = nM$.

Show solution
  1. $n = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{kJ}}{2220\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}} \approx 0.450\ \mathrm{mol}$.
  2. $m = 0.450 \times 44 \approx 19.8\ \mathrm{g}$.
  3. Profile: products drawn $2220\ \mathrm{kJ}$ below the reactants per mole, with a hump (the activation energy) between them.

About $19.8\ \mathrm{g}$ of propane

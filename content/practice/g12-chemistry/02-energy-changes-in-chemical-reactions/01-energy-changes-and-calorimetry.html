Energy Changes and Calorimetry

Six problems on bond breaking and making, $q = mc\Delta T$ with $c = 4.18\ \mathrm{J\,g^{-1}\,{}^\circ C^{-1}}$, and enthalpy changes $\Delta H = \pm q/n$ from calorimeter data. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

In a reaction, $2500\ \mathrm{kJ}$ is absorbed in breaking bonds and $3100\ \mathrm{kJ}$ is released in making new bonds. Find the net energy change. Is the reaction exothermic or endothermic? (Type $\Delta H$ in kJ, with its sign.)

Uses: Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions

Show hint

Net change $=$ energy in $-$ energy out.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta H = 2500 - 3100 = -600\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  2. More energy is released than absorbed: exothermic, and the surroundings get warmer.

$\Delta H = -600\ \mathrm{kJ}$: exothermic

Problem 2

How much heat is needed to warm $50\ \mathrm{g}$ of water by $6.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$? ($c = 4.18\ \mathrm{J\,g^{-1}\,{}^\circ C^{-1}}$; type $q$ in J.)

Uses: Enthalpy Changes and Calorimetry

Show hint

$q = mc\Delta T$.

Show solution
  1. $q = 50 \times 4.18 \times 6.0 = 1254\ \mathrm{J}$.

$q = 1254\ \mathrm{J}$

Problem 3

$50\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $1.0\ \mathrm{M}$ HCl is mixed with $50\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $1.0\ \mathrm{M}$ NaOH, and the temperature rises by $6.8^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Taking the solution as $100\ \mathrm{g}$ of water, find the enthalpy of neutralisation. (Type $\Delta H$ in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, with its sign, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Enthalpy Changes and Calorimetry

Show hint

Heat released $q = mc\Delta T$; moles of water formed $=$ moles of HCl; the reaction is exothermic so $\Delta H$ is negative.

Show solution
  1. $q = 100 \times 4.18 \times 6.8 = 2842.4\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $n = 1.0 \times \dfrac{50}{1000} = 0.050\ \mathrm{mol}$ of water formed.
  3. $\Delta H = -\dfrac{2842.4}{0.050} = -56\,848\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} \approx -56.8\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$\Delta H \approx -56.8\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 4

Burning $0.46\ \mathrm{g}$ of ethanol ($M = 46\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$) under a can of $200\ \mathrm{g}$ of water raises the water temperature by $12.0^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Find the enthalpy of combustion from this experiment. Why is it smaller than the data-book value of $-1367\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$? (Type $\Delta H$ in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, to the nearest kJ, with its sign.)

Uses: Enthalpy Changes and Calorimetry

Show hint

Heat to the water $= mc\Delta T$; moles of ethanol $= 0.46/46$.

Show solution
  1. $q = 200 \times 4.18 \times 12.0 = 10032\ \mathrm{J}$.
  2. $n = \dfrac{0.46}{46} = 0.010\ \mathrm{mol}$.
  3. $\Delta H = -\dfrac{10032}{0.010}\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} \approx -1003\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
  4. Much heat escapes to the air and warms the can, and combustion may be incomplete, so the measured value is too small.

$\Delta H \approx -1003\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ (heat losses make it smaller)

Problem 5

When $8.0\ \mathrm{g}$ of ammonium nitrate ($M = 80\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$) dissolves in $100\ \mathrm{g}$ of water, the temperature falls by $5.6^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Find $\Delta H$ of solution. (Type $\Delta H$ in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, with its sign, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Enthalpy Changes and Calorimetry

Show hint

The water loses heat to the dissolving salt, so the process is endothermic and $\Delta H$ is positive.

Show solution
  1. $q = 100 \times 4.18 \times 5.6 = 2340.8\ \mathrm{J}$ absorbed by the dissolving salt.
  2. $n = \dfrac{8.0}{80} = 0.10\ \mathrm{mol}$.
  3. $\Delta H = +\dfrac{2340.8}{0.10} = 23\,408\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} \approx +23.4\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. This is how cold packs work.

$\Delta H \approx +23.4\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ (endothermic)

Problem 6

What mass of methane must burn to heat $1.5\ \mathrm{kg}$ of water from $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $100^\circ\mathrm{C}$, if no heat is lost? ($\Delta H_c(\mathrm{CH_4}) = -891\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, $M = 16\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$; type the mass in g, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Enthalpy Changes and Calorimetry

Show hint

Find the heat needed, then the moles of methane that give it.

Show solution
  1. $q = 1500 \times 4.18 \times 80 = 501\,600\ \mathrm{J} = 501.6\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  2. $n = \dfrac{501.6}{891} \approx 0.5630\ \mathrm{mol}$.
  3. $m = 0.5630 \times 16 \approx 9.01\ \mathrm{g}$.

About $9.01\ \mathrm{g}$ of methane

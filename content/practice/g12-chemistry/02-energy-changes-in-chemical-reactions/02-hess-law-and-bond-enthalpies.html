Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Six problems on Hess's law cycles, $\Delta H_r^\circ$ from enthalpies of formation and of combustion, and estimates from average bond enthalpies. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Given $\mathrm{C(s) + O_2(g) \to CO_2(g)}$, $\Delta H = -394\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ and $\mathrm{CO(g) + \tfrac12 O_2(g) \to CO_2(g)}$, $\Delta H = -283\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, find $\Delta H$ for $\mathrm{C(s) + \tfrac12 O_2(g) \to CO(g)}$. (Type it in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

Route I (C straight to $\mathrm{CO_2}$) $=$ Route II (C to CO, then CO to $\mathrm{CO_2}$).

Show solution
  1. $-394 = \Delta H + (-283)$.
  2. $\Delta H = -394 + 283 = -111\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. This cannot be measured directly, because burning carbon always makes some $\mathrm{CO_2}$.

$\Delta H = -111\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 2

Find $\Delta H^\circ$ for $\mathrm{CH_4 + 2O_2 \to CO_2 + 2H_2O(l)}$ from $\Delta H_f^\circ$: $\mathrm{CH_4} = -75$, $\mathrm{CO_2} = -394$, $\mathrm{H_2O(l)} = -286\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. (Type it in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

$\Delta H_r^\circ = \sum\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{reactants})$; $\Delta H_f^\circ$ of $\mathrm{O_2}$ is zero.

Show solution
  1. Products: $-394 + 2(-286) = -966$. Reactants: $-75 + 0 = -75$.
  2. $\Delta H^\circ = -966 - (-75) = -891\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$\Delta H^\circ = -891\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 3

Use bond enthalpies ($\mathrm{H{-}H} = 436$, $\mathrm{Cl{-}Cl} = 242$, $\mathrm{H{-}Cl} = 428\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$) to estimate $\Delta H$ for $\mathrm{H_2 + Cl_2 \to 2HCl}$. (Type it in kJ.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

$\Delta H \approx \sum E(\text{broken}) - \sum E(\text{formed})$.

Show solution
  1. Broken: $436 + 242 = 678\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  2. Formed: $2 \times 428 = 856\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  3. $\Delta H \approx 678 - 856 = -178\ \mathrm{kJ}$: exothermic.

$\Delta H \approx -178\ \mathrm{kJ}$

Problem 4

Estimate $\Delta H$ for $\mathrm{CH_4 + Cl_2 \to CH_3Cl + HCl}$ ($\mathrm{C{-}H} = 410$, $\mathrm{Cl{-}Cl} = 242$, $\mathrm{C{-}Cl} = 326$, $\mathrm{H{-}Cl} = 428\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$). (Type it in kJ.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

Only one C–H bond is broken; the other three stay unchanged and can be left out.

Show solution
  1. Broken: one C–H and one Cl–Cl: $410 + 242 = 652\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  2. Formed: one C–Cl and one H–Cl: $326 + 428 = 754\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  3. $\Delta H \approx 652 - 754 = -102\ \mathrm{kJ}$.

$\Delta H \approx -102\ \mathrm{kJ}$

Problem 5

The enthalpy of combustion of ethanol is $-1367\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Using $\Delta H_c$(C) $= -394$ and $\Delta H_c(\mathrm{H_2}) = -286\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, find the enthalpy of formation of ethanol, $\mathrm{2C + 3H_2 + \tfrac12 O_2 \to C_2H_5OH}$. (Type it in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

$\Delta H_r^\circ = \sum\Delta H_c^\circ(\text{reactants}) - \sum\Delta H_c^\circ(\text{products})$.

Show solution
  1. Reactants: $2(-394) + 3(-286) = -788 - 858 = -1646$.
  2. $\Delta H_f^\circ = -1646 - (-1367) = -279\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$\Delta H_f^\circ = -279\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 6

Estimate $\Delta H$ for $\mathrm{CH_4 + 2O_2 \to CO_2 + 2H_2O}$ from bond enthalpies ($\mathrm{C{-}H} = 410$, $\mathrm{O{=}O} = 496$, $\mathrm{C{=}O} = 805$, $\mathrm{O{-}H} = 465\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$). Why does it differ from the value $-891\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ found from enthalpies of formation? (Type it in kJ.)

Uses: Hess's Law and Bond Enthalpies

Show hint

Break 4 C–H and 2 O=O; form 2 C=O and 4 O–H.

Show solution
  1. Broken: $4(410) + 2(496) = 1640 + 992 = 2632\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  2. Formed: $2(805) + 4(465) = 1610 + 1860 = 3470\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  3. $\Delta H \approx 2632 - 3470 = -838\ \mathrm{kJ}$.
  4. Bond enthalpies are averages over many compounds, and they apply to gases, so they give water as steam; condensing the water releases more heat, so the data value is more negative.

$\Delta H \approx -838\ \mathrm{kJ}$ (average bond values, gaseous water)

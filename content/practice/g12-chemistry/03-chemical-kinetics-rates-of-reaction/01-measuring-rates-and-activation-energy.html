Measuring Rates and Activation Energy

Six problems on average rates from volumes, masses and concentrations, rates linked by the equation's coefficients, and reading $E_a$ and $\Delta H$ from an energy profile. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Marble chips react with acid and $60\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ is collected in the first $30\ \mathrm{s}$. Find the average rate. (Type it in $\mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Rate $=$ change in amount ÷ time.

Show solution
  1. Rate $= \dfrac{60}{30} = 2.0\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.

$2.0\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 2

For $\mathrm{2N_2O_5 \to 4NO_2 + O_2}$, oxygen forms at $0.010\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$. How fast does $\mathrm{NO_2}$ form, and how fast is $\mathrm{N_2O_5}$ used up? (Type the rate of $\mathrm{NO_2}$ formation.)

Uses: Rate of Reaction

Show hint

The rates are in the ratio of the coefficients, $2 : 4 : 1$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{NO_2}$: $4 \times 0.010 = 0.040\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $\mathrm{N_2O_5}$: $2 \times 0.010 = 0.020\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$ used up.

$\mathrm{NO_2}$ forms at $0.040$; $\mathrm{N_2O_5}$ is used at $0.020\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 3

The concentration of a reactant falls from $0.80\ \mathrm{M}$ to $0.50\ \mathrm{M}$ in $60\ \mathrm{s}$. Find the average rate of reaction. (Type it in units of $10^{-3}\ \mathrm{M\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Rate $= -\dfrac{\Delta[\text{reactant}]}{\Delta t}$; the minus sign makes the rate positive.

Show solution
  1. Rate $= -\dfrac{0.50 - 0.80}{60} = \dfrac{0.30}{60} = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{M\,s^{-1}}$.

$5.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{M\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 4

On an energy profile the reactants are at $50\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, the activated complex at $180\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ and the products at $20\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Find $E_a$ for the forward reaction, $\Delta H$, and $E_a$ for the reverse reaction. (Type the forward $E_a$ in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Collision Theory and Activation Energy

Show hint

$E_a = E(\text{activated complex}) - E(\text{reactants})$; $\Delta H = E(\text{products}) - E(\text{reactants})$.

Show solution
  1. $E_a(\text{forward}) = 180 - 50 = 130\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
  2. $\Delta H = 20 - 50 = -30\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ (exothermic).
  3. $E_a(\text{reverse}) = 180 - 20 = 160\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$E_a = 130$, $\Delta H = -30$, reverse $E_a = 160\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$

Problem 5

A catalyst lowers the peak of the profile in the last problem from $180$ to $120\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Find the new forward $E_a$. Does $\Delta H$ change? (Type the new $E_a$ in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Collision Theory and Activation Energy

Show hint

A catalyst gives a new route with a lower peak; the reactant and product levels stay where they are.

Show solution
  1. $E_a = 120 - 50 = 70\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
  2. $\Delta H$ is still $-30\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. A larger fraction of collisions now has $E \ge E_a$, so the reaction is faster.

$E_a = 70\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$; $\Delta H$ unchanged

Problem 6

Marble reacts with hydrochloric acid: $\mathrm{CaCO_3 + 2HCl \to CaCl_2 + H_2O + CO_2}$. The flask loses $0.88\ \mathrm{g}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in the first $2.0$ minutes. Find the average rate of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ formation and of HCl use, in mol per minute. ($M(\mathrm{CO_2}) = 44$; type the HCl rate.)

Uses: Rate of Reaction

Show hint

The mass lost is the $\mathrm{CO_2}$ that escapes. Then use the $2 : 1$ ratio of HCl to $\mathrm{CO_2}$.

Show solution
  1. $n(\mathrm{CO_2}) = \dfrac{0.88}{44} = 0.020\ \mathrm{mol}$ in $2.0$ min, so $0.010\ \mathrm{mol\,min^{-1}}$.
  2. HCl is used twice as fast: $0.020\ \mathrm{mol\,min^{-1}}$.

$\mathrm{CO_2}$: $0.010$; HCl: $0.020\ \mathrm{mol\,min^{-1}}$

Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Six problems on temperature (about twice as fast per $10^\circ\mathrm{C}$), surface area, concentration, pressure and catalysts, explained with collision theory. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

A reaction goes about twice as fast for every $10^\circ\mathrm{C}$ rise. By what factor does the rate increase when the temperature goes from $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $50^\circ\mathrm{C}$? (Type the factor.)

Uses: Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Count the $10^\circ\mathrm{C}$ steps.

Show solution
  1. $30^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is three steps: $2^3 = 8$ times faster.

About $8$ times faster

Problem 2

A $2\ \mathrm{cm}$ cube of marble is cut into $1\ \mathrm{cm}$ cubes. By what factor does the total surface area increase? (Type the factor.)

Uses: Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Find the surface area before and after; the volume does not change.

Show solution
  1. Before: $6 \times 2^2 = 24\ \mathrm{cm^2}$.
  2. After: $8$ cubes $\times 6 \times 1^2 = 48\ \mathrm{cm^2}$.
  3. Factor $= 2$: twice as many particles are exposed to the acid, so the initial rate roughly doubles.

$2$ times the surface area

Problem 3

A reaction takes $80\ \mathrm{s}$ at $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Using the rule of thumb, how long would it take at $40^\circ\mathrm{C}$? (Type the time in s.)

Uses: Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Faster rate, shorter time: time $\propto 1/\text{rate}$.

Show solution
  1. $40 - 20 = 20^\circ\mathrm{C}$: two doublings, so the rate is $4$ times larger.
  2. Time $= \dfrac{80}{4} = 20\ \mathrm{s}$.

About $20\ \mathrm{s}$

Problem 4

Use collision theory to explain why (i) a catalyst and (ii) raising the temperature both increase the rate, and why only (ii) changes the number of collisions per second.

Uses: Collision Theory and Activation Energy, Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Rate $\propto$ (collisions per second) $\times$ (fraction with $E \ge E_a$) $\times$ (fraction correctly oriented).

Show solution
  1. (i) A catalyst gives an alternative route with lower $E_a$, so a larger fraction of the same collisions have enough energy. It does not make the particles move faster.
  2. (ii) At a higher temperature particles move faster: they collide a little more often, and, much more important, a much larger fraction of collisions has $E \ge E_a$.

Catalyst lowers $E_a$; heating raises particle energy (and slightly the collision rate)

Problem 5

A $1\ \mathrm{cm}$ cube of zinc is cut into cubes of side $1\ \mathrm{mm}$. By what factor does its surface area increase? (Type the factor.)

Uses: Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

How many small cubes are there, and what is the area of each?

Show solution
  1. Number of cubes $= 10^3 = 1000$, each with area $6 \times (0.1)^2 = 0.06\ \mathrm{cm^2}$.
  2. Total $= 1000 \times 0.06 = 60\ \mathrm{cm^2}$, compared with $6\ \mathrm{cm^2}$: a factor of $10$. Powders can react explosively for this reason (flour or coal dust).

$10$ times the surface area

Problem 6

A reaction at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$ must be made $16$ times faster by heating alone. Using the rule of thumb, to what temperature must it be heated? (Type the temperature in °C.)

Uses: Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

$16 = 2^n$; each doubling needs $10^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

Show solution
  1. $2^4 = 16$, so four doublings are needed.
  2. $T = 25 + 4 \times 10 = 65^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

About $65^\circ\mathrm{C}$

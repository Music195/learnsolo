Problem 1

$45\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of hydrogen is collected in $15\ \mathrm{s}$ when magnesium reacts with acid. Find the average rate. (Type it in $\mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Rate $=$ volume ÷ time.

Show solution
  1. Rate $= \dfrac{45}{15} = 3.0\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.

$3.0\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 2

Hydrogen peroxide decomposes: $\mathrm{2H_2O_2 \to 2H_2O + O_2}$. Oxygen forms at $1.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,s^{-1}}$. How fast is $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ used up? (Type the rate in units of $10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Two moles of $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ are used for each mole of $\mathrm{O_2}$.

Show solution
  1. Rate of use $= 2 \times 1.5 \times 10^{-3} = 3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,s^{-1}}$.

$3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,s^{-1}}$

Problem 3

For an endothermic reaction the reactants are at $40$, the activated complex at $140$ and the products at $100\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Find $\Delta H$ and the activation energies of the forward and reverse reactions. (Type the reverse $E_a$ in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Collision Theory and Activation Energy

Show hint

Measure each $E_a$ from its own starting level up to the peak.

Show solution
  1. $\Delta H = 100 - 40 = +60\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
  2. $E_a(\text{forward}) = 140 - 40 = 100$; $E_a(\text{reverse}) = 140 - 100 = 40\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.
  3. Check: $E_a(\text{f}) - E_a(\text{r}) = \Delta H = 60$ ✓.

$\Delta H = +60$; $E_a = 100$ forward, $40$ reverse

Problem 4

Explain why (i) food keeps longer in a refrigerator and (ii) iron wool burns brightly in oxygen while an iron nail does not.

Uses: Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction, Collision Theory and Activation Energy

Show hint

Which factor is changed in each case?

Show solution
  1. (i) At low temperature fewer collisions in the food have energy $\ge E_a$, so the reactions that spoil food (and the growth of microbes) are slowed.
  2. (ii) Iron wool has a huge surface area and pure oxygen is more concentrated than air, so there are many more collisions per second.

(i) lower temperature, fewer successful collisions (ii) larger surface area and oxygen concentration

Problem 5

A reaction takes $120\ \mathrm{s}$ at $30^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Estimate the time at $60^\circ\mathrm{C}$. (Type the time in s.)

Uses: Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Three $10^\circ\mathrm{C}$ steps; the time is divided by the same factor that multiplies the rate.

Show solution
  1. Rate factor $= 2^3 = 8$.
  2. Time $= \dfrac{120}{8} = 15\ \mathrm{s}$.

About $15\ \mathrm{s}$

Problem 6

The volume of gas from a reaction is: $0\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ at $0\ \mathrm{s}$, $24$ at $10\ \mathrm{s}$, $40$ at $20\ \mathrm{s}$, $50$ at $30\ \mathrm{s}$. Find the average rate in the first $10\ \mathrm{s}$ and between $20$ and $30\ \mathrm{s}$, and explain why the rate falls. (Type the first rate in $\mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.)

Uses: Rate of Reaction, Factors Affecting the Rate of Reaction

Show hint

Average rate $= \Delta V / \Delta t$ over each interval.

Show solution
  1. $0$–$10\ \mathrm{s}$: $\dfrac{24}{10} = 2.4\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.
  2. $20$–$30\ \mathrm{s}$: $\dfrac{50 - 40}{10} = 1.0\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.
  3. As reactants are used up their concentration falls, so there are fewer collisions per second and the rate falls; the graph levels off when a reactant runs out.

$2.4$ then $1.0\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$: falling concentration means fewer collisions

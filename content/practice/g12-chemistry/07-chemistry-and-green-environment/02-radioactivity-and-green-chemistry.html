Radioactive Substances and Green Chemistry

Six problems on alpha, beta and gamma radiation, half-life, shielding, and green processes such as making bioethanol by fermentation ($180\ \mathrm{g} \to 92\ \mathrm{g} + 88\ \mathrm{g}$; one mole of gas $= 24\ \mathrm{dm^3}$). Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

What mass of ethanol can be made by fermenting $360\ \mathrm{g}$ of glucose? $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6 \to 2C_2H_5OH + 2CO_2}$. (Type the mass in g.)

Uses: Green Chemistry in Action

Show hint

$180\ \mathrm{g}$ of glucose gives $92\ \mathrm{g}$ of ethanol.

Show solution
  1. $360\ \mathrm{g} = 2$ mol glucose, giving $4$ mol ethanol.
  2. Mass $= 4 \times 46 = 184\ \mathrm{g}$.

$184\ \mathrm{g}$ of ethanol

Problem 2

Radium-226 ($^{226}_{\ 88}\mathrm{Ra}$) gives off an alpha particle. What is the mass number of the product (radon)? Which material would stop the alpha particle? (Type the mass number.)

Uses: Radioactive Substances

Show hint

An alpha particle is a helium nucleus, $^4_2\mathrm{He}$.

Show solution
  1. $^{226}_{\ 88}\mathrm{Ra} \to {}^{222}_{\ 86}\mathrm{Rn} + {}^4_2\mathrm{He}$: mass number $222$.
  2. A sheet of paper (or the outer skin) stops alpha particles; the danger is from breathing in radon gas.

$222$; stopped by paper

Problem 3

Radon-222 has a half-life of $3.8$ days. What percentage of a sample is left after $15.2$ days? (Type the percentage, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Radioactive Substances

Show hint

Count the half-lives.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{15.2}{3.8} = 4$ half-lives.
  2. Fraction left $= \left(\tfrac12\right)^4 = \tfrac{1}{16} = 6.25\,\%$.

$6.25\,\%$

Problem 4

Green chemists use the atom economy: the mass of the wanted product as a percentage of the total mass of products. Find the atom economy of fermentation for making ethanol. (Type it in %, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Green Chemistry in Action

Show hint

From one mole of glucose: $92\ \mathrm{g}$ of ethanol and $88\ \mathrm{g}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$.

Show solution
  1. Atom economy $= \dfrac{92}{92 + 88} \times 100\,\% = \dfrac{92}{180} \times 100\,\% \approx 51.1\,\%$.
  2. The rest is $\mathrm{CO_2}$, which plants took from the air, so the process is close to carbon neutral and can be captured for drinks.

About $51.1\,\%$

Problem 5

$450\ \mathrm{g}$ of glucose is fermented. What volume of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ is given off at room temperature and pressure, and what mass of ethanol forms? ($1$ mol of gas $= 24\ \mathrm{dm^3}$; type the volume in $\mathrm{dm^3}$.)

Uses: Green Chemistry in Action

Show hint

Moles of glucose $= 450/180$; each mole gives $2$ mol $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $2$ mol ethanol.

Show solution
  1. $n(\text{glucose}) = \dfrac{450}{180} = 2.5\ \mathrm{mol}$.
  2. $n(\mathrm{CO_2}) = 5.0\ \mathrm{mol}$, so $V = 5.0 \times 24 = 120\ \mathrm{dm^3}$.
  3. Ethanol: $5.0 \times 46 = 230\ \mathrm{g}$.

$120\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ and $230\ \mathrm{g}$ of ethanol

Problem 6

Gamma radiation is halved by every $3.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ of lead. What thickness of lead cuts it to $\tfrac18$? What thickness cuts it to $1\,\%$? (Type the thickness for $1\,\%$ in cm, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Radioactive Substances

Show hint

$\left(\tfrac12\right)^n = 0.01 \implies n = \dfrac{\log 100}{\log 2}$ halvings.

Show solution
  1. $\tfrac18 = \left(\tfrac12\right)^3$: $3 \times 3.0 = 9.0\ \mathrm{cm}$.
  2. For $1\,\%$: $n = \dfrac{2}{0.301} \approx 6.64$ halvings, so thickness $\approx 6.64 \times 3.0 \approx 19.9\ \mathrm{cm}$. Gamma is never stopped completely, only weakened.

$9.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ for $\tfrac18$; about $19.9\ \mathrm{cm}$ for $1\,\%$

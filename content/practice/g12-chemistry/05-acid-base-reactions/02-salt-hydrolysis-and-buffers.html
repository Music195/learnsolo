Salt Hydrolysis and Buffer Solutions

Six problems on $K_aK_b = K_w$, the pH of salt solutions, and buffers with $\mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a + \log\dfrac{[\mathrm{A^-}]}{[\mathrm{HA}]}$ (ethanoic acid: $K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$). Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Ethanoic acid has $K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$. Find $K_b$ of its conjugate base, the ethanoate ion. (Type $K_b$ in units of $10^{-10}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Salt Hydrolysis

Show hint

For a conjugate pair $K_aK_b = K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$.

Show solution
  1. $K_b = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{1.8 \times 10^{-5}} \approx 5.56 \times 10^{-10}$.
  2. The ethanoate ion is a weak base: the stronger the acid, the weaker its conjugate base.

$K_b \approx 5.56 \times 10^{-10}$

Problem 2

Is a solution of sodium chloride acidic, neutral or basic at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$? What is its pH? (Type the pH.)

Uses: Salt Hydrolysis

Show hint

Which acid and base was the salt made from?

Show solution
  1. NaCl comes from a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH). Neither ion reacts with water, so there is no hydrolysis.
  2. The solution is neutral: $\mathrm{pH} = 7$.

Neutral, $\mathrm{pH} = 7$

Problem 3

Find the pH of $0.10\ \mathrm{M}$ sodium ethanoate at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$. (Type the pH to 2 decimal places.)

Uses: Salt Hydrolysis

Show hint

The ethanoate ion hydrolyses: $[\mathrm{OH^-}] \approx \sqrt{\dfrac{K_w}{K_a}c}$.

Show solution
  1. $[\mathrm{OH^-}] \approx \sqrt{5.56 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.10} \approx 7.45 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{M}$.
  2. $\mathrm{pOH} \approx 5.13$, so $\mathrm{pH} \approx 14 - 5.13 = 8.87$: basic.

$\mathrm{pH} \approx 8.87$ (basic)

Problem 4

Find the pH of $0.10\ \mathrm{M}$ ammonium chloride at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$ ($K_b(\mathrm{NH_3}) = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$). (Type the pH to 2 decimal places.)

Uses: Salt Hydrolysis

Show hint

$\mathrm{NH_4^+}$ is a weak acid with $K_a = K_w/K_b$, so $[\mathrm{H^+}] \approx \sqrt{\dfrac{K_w}{K_b}c}$.

Show solution
  1. $K_a(\mathrm{NH_4^+}) = \dfrac{10^{-14}}{1.8 \times 10^{-5}} = 5.56 \times 10^{-10}$.
  2. $[\mathrm{H^+}] \approx \sqrt{5.56 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.10} \approx 7.45 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{M}$, so $\mathrm{pH} \approx 5.13$: acidic.

$\mathrm{pH} \approx 5.13$ (acidic)

Problem 5

A buffer contains $0.10\ \mathrm{M}$ ethanoic acid and $0.20\ \mathrm{M}$ sodium ethanoate. Find its pH. (Type the pH to 2 decimal places.)

Uses: Buffer Solutions

Show hint

$\mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a + \log\dfrac{[\mathrm{A^-}]}{[\mathrm{HA}]}$, with $\mathrm{p}K_a = -\log(1.8 \times 10^{-5})$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{p}K_a = 4.745$.
  2. $\mathrm{pH} = 4.745 + \log\dfrac{0.20}{0.10} = 4.745 + 0.301 \approx 5.05$.

$\mathrm{pH} \approx 5.05$

Problem 6

$0.010\ \mathrm{mol}$ of HCl is added to $1.0\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ of a buffer containing $0.10\ \mathrm{mol}$ each of ethanoic acid and sodium ethanoate. Find the new pH, and compare with adding the same HCl to $1.0\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ of pure water. (Type the buffer's new pH to 2 decimal places.)

Uses: Buffer Solutions

Show hint

The added $\mathrm{H^+}$ reacts with ethanoate: $\mathrm{CH_3COO^- + H^+ \to CH_3COOH}$.

Show solution
  1. After reaction: $\mathrm{CH_3COO^-} = 0.10 - 0.010 = 0.09\ \mathrm{mol}$; $\mathrm{CH_3COOH} = 0.10 + 0.010 = 0.11\ \mathrm{mol}$.
  2. $\mathrm{pH} = 4.745 + \log\dfrac{0.09}{0.11} = 4.745 - 0.087 \approx 4.66$: it falls by only $0.09$.
  3. In pure water: $[\mathrm{H^+}] = 0.010\ \mathrm{M}$, so the pH drops from $7$ to $2$.

Buffer: $\mathrm{pH} \approx 4.66$; water: $\mathrm{pH} = 2$

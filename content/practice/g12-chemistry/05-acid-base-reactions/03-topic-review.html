Acid-Base Reactions

Topic review: a mix of the whole chapter, best attempted after completing the concept sets. Six exam-style problems across acid-base theories, pH, weak acids, salt hydrolysis and buffers. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Rain water has pH $4.5$. Find $[\mathrm{H^+}]$. (Type it in units of $10^{-5}\ \mathrm{M}$, to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Ionic Product of Water, pH and pOH

Show hint

$[\mathrm{H^+}] = 10^{-\mathrm{pH}}$.

Show solution
  1. $[\mathrm{H^+}] = 10^{-4.5} \approx 3.16 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{M}$, about $300$ times more acidic than pure water.

$[\mathrm{H^+}] \approx 3.16 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{M}$

Problem 2

At $40^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $K_w = 2.9 \times 10^{-14}$. Find the pH of pure water at this temperature. Is the water acidic? (Type the pH to 2 decimal places.)

Uses: Ionic Product of Water, pH and pOH

Show hint

In pure water $[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}] = \sqrt{K_w}$.

Show solution
  1. $[\mathrm{H^+}] = \sqrt{2.9 \times 10^{-14}} \approx 1.70 \times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{M}$.
  2. $\mathrm{pH} \approx 6.77$. It is still neutral, because $[\mathrm{H^+}] = [\mathrm{OH^-}]$; neutral pH is only $7$ at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

$\mathrm{pH} \approx 6.77$, still neutral

Problem 3

Find the pH of $0.10\ \mathrm{M}$ hydrofluoric acid ($K_a = 7.2 \times 10^{-4}$). (Type the pH to 2 decimal places.)

Uses: Strengths of Acids and Bases: Ka and Kb

Show hint

$[\mathrm{H^+}] \approx \sqrt{K_a c}$.

Show solution
  1. $[\mathrm{H^+}] \approx \sqrt{7.2 \times 10^{-4} \times 0.10} = \sqrt{7.2 \times 10^{-5}} \approx 8.49 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{M}$.
  2. $\mathrm{pH} \approx 2.07$. HF is weak, but stronger than ethanoic acid, so its pH is lower.

$\mathrm{pH} \approx 2.07$

Problem 4

In $\mathrm{BF_3 + NH_3 \to F_3B{-}NH_3}$, which substance is the Lewis acid and which the Lewis base? Why can this not be described by the Brønsted–Lowry theory?

Uses: Theories of Acids and Bases

Show hint

A Lewis base donates an electron pair; a Lewis acid accepts one.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{NH_3}$ donates its lone pair: it is the Lewis base. $\mathrm{BF_3}$ (boron has only six outer electrons) accepts the pair: it is the Lewis acid.
  2. No proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) is transferred, so Brønsted–Lowry theory does not apply; the Lewis theory is wider.

$\mathrm{BF_3}$ is the Lewis acid, $\mathrm{NH_3}$ the Lewis base; no proton moves

Problem 5

What ratio $[\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}] : [\mathrm{CH_3COOH}]$ gives a buffer of pH $5.00$? ($K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$; type the ratio to 3 significant figures.)

Uses: Buffer Solutions

Show hint

$\log\dfrac{[\mathrm{A^-}]}{[\mathrm{HA}]} = \mathrm{pH} - \mathrm{p}K_a$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{p}K_a = 4.745$, so $\log(\text{ratio}) = 5.00 - 4.745 = 0.255$.
  2. Ratio $= 10^{0.255} \approx 1.80$.

$[\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}] : [\mathrm{CH_3COOH}] \approx 1.80 : 1$

Problem 6

$25\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $0.10\ \mathrm{M}$ ethanoic acid is exactly neutralised by $25\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $0.10\ \mathrm{M}$ NaOH. Find the pH at this equivalence point, and say why it is not $7$. (Type the pH to 2 decimal places.)

Uses: Salt Hydrolysis, Strengths of Acids and Bases: Ka and Kb

Show hint

All the acid becomes sodium ethanoate, now in $50\ \mathrm{cm^3}$.

Show solution
  1. $n = 0.10 \times 0.025 = 2.5 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol}$ in $0.050\ \mathrm{dm^3}$: $[\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}] = 0.050\ \mathrm{M}$.
  2. $[\mathrm{OH^-}] \approx \sqrt{5.56 \times 10^{-10} \times 0.050} \approx 5.27 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{M}$, so $\mathrm{pOH} \approx 5.28$ and $\mathrm{pH} \approx 8.72$.
  3. The ethanoate ion hydrolyses and makes $\mathrm{OH^-}$, so the equivalence point of a weak acid with a strong base is basic (phenolphthalein is the right indicator).

$\mathrm{pH} \approx 8.72$ (basic, from hydrolysis of ethanoate)

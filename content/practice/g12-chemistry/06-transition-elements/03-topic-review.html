Problem 1

How many $3d$ electrons does $\mathrm{Co^{2+}}$ ($Z = 27$) have? (Type the number.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations

Show hint

$Z - 18 - n$.

Show solution
  1. $27 - 18 - 2 = 7$: $\mathrm{Co^{2+}}$ is $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^7$, with $10 - 7 = 3$ unpaired electrons.

$7$ ($[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^7$)

Problem 2

How many unpaired electrons does $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ ($Z = 25$) have, and would its compounds be attracted by a magnet? (Type the number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

Fill five $3d$ orbitals singly first.

Show solution
  1. $25 - 18 - 2 = 5$ electrons, one in each $3d$ orbital: $5$ unpaired.
  2. Yes, strongly paramagnetic.

$5$ unpaired; strongly paramagnetic

Problem 3

Yellow chromate, $\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}$, turns into orange dichromate, $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}$, when acid is added. Find the oxidation state of Cr in each. Is this a redox change? (Type the oxidation number in $\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}$.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

The charges must add to the ion's charge.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}$: $x + 4(-2) = -2 \implies x = +6$.
  2. $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}$: $2x + 7(-2) = -2 \implies x = +6$.
  3. The oxidation state does not change, so it is not redox: $\mathrm{2CrO_4^{2-} + 2H^+ \rightleftharpoons Cr_2O_7^{2-} + H_2O}$ is an acid–base change.

$+6$ in both: not a redox change

Problem 4

Give two reasons why transition metals and their compounds are good catalysts, with an industrial example of each.

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series, Uses of the 3d Series Transition Metals

Show hint

Think about oxidation states and about the metal surface.

Show solution
  1. Variable oxidation states let them pass electrons or oxygen back and forth: $\mathrm{V_2O_5}$ in the Contact process.
  2. Their surfaces adsorb reacting molecules using $d$ orbitals, weakening bonds and bringing molecules together: iron in the Haber process, nickel in the hydrogenation of vegetable oils.

Variable oxidation states ($\mathrm{V_2O_5}$) and surface adsorption (Fe, Ni)

Problem 5

Copper ($Z = 29$) is $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10}\,4s^1$. How many unpaired electrons does a copper atom have? Why is copper still counted as a transition element? (Type the number.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations

Show hint

Look at both subshells. Then think about the $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ion.

Show solution
  1. $3d^{10}$ is full; the single $4s$ electron is unpaired: $1$ unpaired electron.
  2. Copper forms $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$, $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^9$, with a partly filled $d$ subshell, so it meets the definition.

$1$ unpaired; $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ($3d^9$) has a partly filled $d$ subshell

Problem 6

Iron(II) salts are pale green and slowly turn yellow-brown in air as they become iron(III). Find the number of unpaired electrons in $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ and in $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$, and explain why $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ is the more stable ion. (Type the number for $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations, Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

$\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$: $3d^6$; $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$: $3d^5$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$: $26 - 18 - 2 = 6$, unpaired $= 10 - 6 = 4$.
  2. $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$: $26 - 18 - 3 = 5$, all $5$ unpaired.
  3. Losing one more electron gives the half-filled $3d^5$ subshell, which is especially stable, so air oxidises $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ to $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$.

$\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$: $4$; $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$: $5$ (stable half-filled $3d^5$)

Electronic Configurations and Unpaired Electrons

Six problems on $3d$ and $4s$ configurations of atoms and ions (3d electrons in $\mathrm{M}^{n+} = Z - 18 - n$), the Cr and Cu exceptions, and counting unpaired electrons by Hund's rule. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Write the electron configuration of iron ($Z = 26$) using $[\mathrm{Ar}]$. How many $3d$ electrons does it have? (Type the number.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations

Show hint

After $[\mathrm{Ar}]$ ($18$ electrons), $4s$ fills before $3d$.

Show solution
  1. $26 - 18 = 8$ electrons beyond argon: $4s^2$ and $3d^6$.
  2. $\mathrm{Fe}: [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6\,4s^2$, with $6$ electrons in $3d$.

$[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6\,4s^2$: six $3d$ electrons

Problem 2

How many $3d$ electrons does the $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ ion have? Write its configuration. (Type the number.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations

Show hint

Ions lose their $4s$ electrons first. Use $Z - 18 - n$.

Show solution
  1. $26 - 18 - 3 = 5$.
  2. $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}: [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5$ (both $4s$ electrons and one $3d$ electron removed).

$[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5$: five $3d$ electrons

Problem 3

Chromium ($Z = 24$) has the configuration $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5\,4s^1$, not $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^4\,4s^2$. Explain why, and find the number of unpaired electrons in a chromium atom. (Type the number.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations

Show hint

A half-filled $3d$ subshell is especially stable.

Show solution
  1. Moving one $4s$ electron into $3d$ gives a half-filled $3d^5$ set, which has lower energy.
  2. All five $3d$ electrons are unpaired (Hund's rule), and so is the $4s^1$ electron: $6$ unpaired electrons.

$6$ unpaired electrons ($3d^5\,4s^1$)

Problem 4

How many unpaired electrons does $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ($Z = 29$) have? Why are copper(II) compounds coloured? (Type the number.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations, Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

$3d$ electrons $= Z - 18 - n$; with more than $5$, unpaired $= 10 - d$.

Show solution
  1. $29 - 18 - 2 = 9$: $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ is $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^9$.
  2. Unpaired $= 10 - 9 = 1$.
  3. The $3d$ subshell is partly filled, so an electron can jump between split $d$ levels by absorbing visible light: the ion is coloured (blue in water).

$1$ unpaired electron; partly filled $3d$ gives colour

Problem 5

How many unpaired electrons does $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ ($Z = 30$) have? Explain why zinc compounds are white and why zinc is often not counted as a transition element. (Type the number.)

Uses: The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations, Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

Find the $3d$ count first.

Show solution
  1. $30 - 18 - 2 = 10$: $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ is $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10}$, so $0$ unpaired electrons.
  2. A full $3d$ subshell allows no $d$–$d$ jumps, so no visible light is absorbed: white compounds, and no paramagnetism.
  3. A transition element must form at least one ion with a partly filled $d$ subshell; zinc's only ion is $3d^{10}$.

$0$ unpaired: full $3d^{10}$

Problem 6

Which of $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ ($Z = 25$), $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ ($26$), $\mathrm{Co^{2+}}$ ($27$) and $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}$ ($28$) is the most strongly attracted by a magnet? Give its number of unpaired electrons. (Type the number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series, The 3d Series and Electronic Configurations

Show hint

Paramagnetism grows with the number of unpaired electrons.

Show solution
  1. $3d$ counts: $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}\ 5$, $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\ 6$, $\mathrm{Co^{2+}}\ 7$, $\mathrm{Ni^{2+}}\ 8$.
  2. Unpaired: $5$, $10 - 6 = 4$, $10 - 7 = 3$, $10 - 8 = 2$.
  3. $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ ($3d^5$, all unpaired) is the most paramagnetic.

$\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$, with $5$ unpaired electrons

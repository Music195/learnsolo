Oxidation States, Colour, Magnetism and Uses

Six problems on finding oxidation states in compounds and ions, linking colour and magnetism to $3d$ electrons, and choosing metals for their uses. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full step-by-step solution.

Problem 1

Find the oxidation state of manganese in potassium manganate(VII), $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$. (Type the oxidation number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

K is $+1$, each O is $-2$, and the total is zero.

Show solution
  1. $+1 + x + 4(-2) = 0 \implies x = +7$.
  2. $+7$ is manganese's highest state: it has used all seven of its $3d$ and $4s$ electrons.

$+7$

Problem 2

Find the oxidation state of chromium in potassium dichromate, $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$. (Type the oxidation number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

Two Cr atoms share the charge left over.

Show solution
  1. $2(+1) + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 \implies 2x = 12 \implies x = +6$.

$+6$

Problem 3

Vanadium(V) oxide is the catalyst in the Contact process. Find the oxidation state of vanadium in $\mathrm{V_2O_5}$, and explain why variable oxidation states help a metal act as a catalyst. (Type the oxidation number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series, Uses of the 3d Series Transition Metals

Show hint

Total charge $= 0$.

Show solution
  1. $2x + 5(-2) = 0 \implies x = +5$.
  2. $\mathrm{V_2O_5}$ gives oxygen to $\mathrm{SO_2}$ (becoming $\mathrm{V^{4+}}$) and is then re-oxidised by $\mathrm{O_2}$: switching between states gives an easier route with lower $E_a$.

$+5$; it can switch states to pass on oxygen

Problem 4

$\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$ compounds are colourless but $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$ compounds are purple. How many $3d$ electrons does $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$ ($Z = 22$) have, and why does this make the difference? (Type the number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

Use $Z - 18 - n$ for both ions.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$: $21 - 18 - 3 = 0$; $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$: $22 - 18 - 3 = 1$.
  2. With no $d$ electrons, $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$ cannot absorb visible light by a $d$–$d$ jump. The single $3d$ electron of $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$ absorbs some visible light, and we see the remaining colour.

$\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$ has $1$ $3d$ electron, so it absorbs visible light

Problem 5

Find the oxidation state of iron in the complex ion $[\mathrm{Fe(CN)_6}]^{3-}$. (Type the oxidation number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series

Show hint

Each cyanide ligand is $\mathrm{CN^-}$; the charges must add to $-3$.

Show solution
  1. $x + 6(-1) = -3 \implies x = +3$.

$+3$

Problem 6

Copper(I) compounds such as $\mathrm{Cu_2O}$ are usually white or colourless in solution, while copper(II) compounds are blue or green. How many $3d$ electrons does $\mathrm{Cu^+}$ have? Explain the difference, and give one use of copper that depends on a different property. (Type the number.)

Uses: Characteristic Properties of the 3d Series, Uses of the 3d Series Transition Metals

Show hint

$29 - 18 - 1$.

Show solution
  1. $\mathrm{Cu^+}$: $29 - 18 - 1 = 10$, a full $3d^{10}$ subshell, so no $d$–$d$ jumps and no colour. $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ($3d^9$) has a gap to jump into.
  2. Copper is used for electrical wiring because it is an excellent conductor and easily drawn into wires.

$10$ ($3d^{10}$, no colour); copper wire for its conductivity

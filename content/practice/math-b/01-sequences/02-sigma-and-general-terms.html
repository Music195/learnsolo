Sigma, Difference Sequences and General Terms

Seven problems on the sum formulas, the properties of Σ, difference sequences, and finding a general term from the sum. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

Find $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{12} (k^2 + 2k)$.

Uses: Sum Formulas for Natural Numbers, Properties of Sigma

Show hint

Split the sum: $\sum k^2 + 2\sum k$.

Show solution
  1. $\sum_{k=1}^{12} k^2 = \dfrac{12 \cdot 13 \cdot 25}{6} = 650$.
  2. $2\sum_{k=1}^{12} k = 12 \cdot 13 = 156$.
  3. Total $806$.

$806$

Problem 2

The sum of the first $n$ terms of a sequence is $S_n = 2n^2 - 3n$. Find $a_n$ and $a_{15}$. (Type $a_{15}$ in the box.)

Uses: General Term from the Sum

Show hint

$a_1 = S_1$, and $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ for $n \ge 2$.

Show solution
  1. $a_1 = S_1 = -1$.
  2. For $n \ge 2$: $a_n = (2n^2 - 3n) - \{2(n - 1)^2 - 3(n - 1)\} = 4n - 5$.
  3. $n = 1$ gives $-1 = a_1$ ✓, so $a_n = 4n - 5$ for all $n$, and $a_{15} = 55$.

$a_n = 4n - 5$; $a_{15} = 55$

Problem 3

Find $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} k(k + 1)$ as a factorised expression in $n$, then its value for $n = 10$.

Uses: Sum Formulas for Natural Numbers, Properties of Sigma

Show hint

$k(k + 1) = k^2 + k$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{n(n + 1)(2n + 1)}{6} + \dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2} = \dfrac{n(n + 1)}{6}(2n + 1 + 3) = \dfrac{n(n + 1)(n + 2)}{3}$.
  2. $n = 10$: $\dfrac{10 \cdot 11 \cdot 12}{3} = 440$.

$\dfrac{n(n + 1)(n + 2)}{3}$; $440$

Problem 4

Find the general term of $3, 4, 7, 12, 19, \ldots$ and $a_{20}$. (Type $a_{20}$ in the box.)

Uses: Difference Sequences, Sum Formulas for Natural Numbers

Show hint

The differences are $1, 3, 5, 7, \ldots$, i.e. $b_k = 2k - 1$.

Show solution
  1. For $n \ge 2$: $a_n = 3 + \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (2k - 1) = 3 + (n - 1)^2$.
  2. $n = 1$ gives $3$ ✓. So $a_n = n^2 - 2n + 4$.
  3. $a_{20} = 3 + 19^2 = 364$.

$a_n = n^2 - 2n + 4$; $a_{20} = 364$

Problem 5

Find $11^3 + 12^3 + \cdots + 20^3$.

Uses: Sum Formulas for Natural Numbers, Properties of Sigma

Show hint

$\displaystyle\sum_{k=11}^{20} k^3 = \sum_{k=1}^{20} k^3 - \sum_{k=1}^{10} k^3$.

Show solution
  1. $\sum_{k=1}^{20} k^3 = 210^2 = 44100$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{10} k^3 = 55^2 = 3025$.
  2. $44100 - 3025 = 41075$.

$41075$

Problem 6

(1) The sum of the first $n$ terms of $\{a_n\}$ is $S_n = 3^n - 2$. Find $a_n$. (2) For which constant $c$ is the sequence with $S_n = 3^n + c$ geometric from the first term? (Type $c$ in the box.)

Uses: General Term from the Sum

Show hint

Find $a_1 = S_1$ separately, then check whether the formula for $n \ge 2$ also gives it.

Show solution
  1. (1) $a_1 = 3 - 2 = 1$. For $n \ge 2$: $a_n = 3^n - 3^{n-1} = 2 \cdot 3^{n-1}$, which would give $2$ at $n = 1$, not $1$.
  2. So $a_1 = 1$ and $a_n = 2 \cdot 3^{n-1}$ ($n \ge 2$).
  3. (2) For $n \ge 2$ still $a_n = 2 \cdot 3^{n-1}$; we need $a_1 = 3 + c = 2$, so $c = -1$.

(1) $a_1 = 1$, $a_n = 2 \cdot 3^{n-1}$ ($n \ge 2$) (2) $c = -1$

Problem 7

Consider the sequence $1, 5, 13, 25, 41, \ldots$

  1. (1) Find the general term $a_n$ using the difference sequence.
  2. (2) Find $S_n = a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n$ as a factorised expression.
  3. (3) Find $a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_{10}$. (Type it in the box.)
  4. (4) Check your answer to (2) by computing $S_n - S_{n-1}$.

Uses: Difference Sequences, Sum Formulas for Natural Numbers, Properties of Sigma, General Term from the Sum

Show hint

The differences are $4, 8, 12, 16, \ldots$, i.e. $b_k = 4k$.

Show solution
  1. (1) $a_n = 1 + \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} 4k = 1 + 2n(n - 1) = 2n^2 - 2n + 1$ ($n = 1$ gives $1$ ✓).
  2. (2) $S_n = 2 \cdot \dfrac{n(n + 1)(2n + 1)}{6} - 2 \cdot \dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2} + n = \dfrac{n\{(n + 1)(2n + 1) - 3(n + 1) + 3\}}{3} = \dfrac{n(2n^2 + 1)}{3}$.
  3. (3) $S_{10} = \dfrac{10 \cdot 201}{3} = 670$.
  4. (4) $S_n - S_{n-1} = \dfrac{2n^3 + n - 2(n - 1)^3 - (n - 1)}{3} = \dfrac{6n^2 - 6n + 3}{3} = 2n^2 - 2n + 1$ ✓.

(1) $a_n = 2n^2 - 2n + 1$ (2) $S_n = \dfrac{n(2n^2 + 1)}{3}$ (3) $670$

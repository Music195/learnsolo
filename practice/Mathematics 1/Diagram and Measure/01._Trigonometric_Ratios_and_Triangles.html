Trigonometric Ratios and Triangles

Five problems on trigonometric ratios, the laws of sines and cosines, and triangle areas. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

$\theta$ is acute and $\sin\theta = \dfrac{3}{5}$. Find $\tan\theta$.

Uses: Trigonometric Ratio Identities

Show hint

Find $\cos\theta$ from $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$, then $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$.

Show solution
  1. $\cos^2\theta = 1 - \dfrac{9}{25} = \dfrac{16}{25}$. $\theta$ is acute, so $\cos\theta = \dfrac{4}{5}$.
  2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{3/5}{4/5} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{3}{4}$

Problem 2

In triangle ABC, $a = 2\sqrt{2}$, $A = 45°$ and $B = 60°$. Find $b$.

Uses: Law of Sines

Show hint

You know a side and its opposite angle, plus another angle: use $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}/2} = 4$.
  2. $b = 4\sin 60° = 4 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\sqrt{3}$.

$b = 2\sqrt{3}$

Problem 3

In triangle ABC, $b = 3$, $c = 5$ and $A = 120°$. Find $a$.

Uses: Law of Cosines, Trigonometric Ratios of 180°−θ

Show hint

Two sides and the angle between them: $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$. Remember $\cos 120° = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. $a^2 = 9 + 25 - 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = 34 + 15 = 49$.
  2. $a > 0$, so $a = 7$.

$a = 7$

Problem 4

Find the area of the triangle with sides $5$, $7$ and $8$.

Uses: Heron's Formula for Triangle Area

Show hint

Heron's formula: with $s = \dfrac{a + b + c}{2}$, the area is $\sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$.

Show solution
  1. $s = \dfrac{5 + 7 + 8}{2} = 10$.
  2. Area $= \sqrt{10 \cdot 5 \cdot 3 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{300} = 10\sqrt{3}$.

Area $= 10\sqrt{3}$

With the area you also get the incircle radius: $S = rs$ gives $r = \dfrac{10\sqrt{3}}{10} = \sqrt{3}$.

Problem 5

$0° \le \theta \le 180°$ and $\sin\theta + \cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Find $\sin\theta - \cos\theta$.

Uses: Trigonometric Ratio Identities, Trigonometric Ratios (Coordinate Plane)

Show hint

Square the given equation to find $\sin\theta\cos\theta$. Then find $(\sin\theta - \cos\theta)^2$, and decide its sign.

Show solution
  1. Squaring: $1 + 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{4}$, so $\sin\theta\cos\theta = -\dfrac{3}{8}$.
  2. $(\sin\theta - \cos\theta)^2 = 1 - 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = 1 + \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{7}{4}$.
  3. $\sin\theta \ge 0$ for $0° \le \theta \le 180°$, and $\sin\theta\cos\theta < 0$, so $\sin\theta > 0 > \cos\theta$. Hence $\sin\theta - \cos\theta > 0$.
  4. $\sin\theta - \cos\theta = \sqrt{\dfrac{7}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$.

$\sin\theta - \cos\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}$

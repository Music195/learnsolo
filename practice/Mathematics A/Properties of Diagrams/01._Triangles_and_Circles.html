Triangles and Circles

Five problems on angle bisectors, intersecting chords, Ceva, tangent lengths and Menelaus. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

In triangle ABC, $AB = 6$, $AC = 4$ and $BC = 5$. The bisector of angle A meets BC at D. Find $BD$.

Uses: Angle Bisector Theorem

Show hint

The bisector divides the opposite side in the ratio of the other two sides: $BD : DC = AB : AC$.

Show solution
  1. $BD : DC = 6 : 4 = 3 : 2$.
  2. $BD = 5 \cdot \dfrac{3}{5} = 3$.

$BD = 3$

Problem 2

Chords AB and CD of a circle cross at P. $PA = 4$, $PB = 6$ and $PC = 3$. Find $PD$.

Uses: Power of a Point

Show hint

Power of a point: $PA \cdot PB = PC \cdot PD$.

Show solution
  1. $4 \cdot 6 = 3 \cdot PD$, so $PD = 8$.

$PD = 8$

Problem 3

In triangle ABC, D is on BC with $BD : DC = 2 : 3$, and E is on CA with $CE : EA = 3 : 4$. AD and BE meet at P, and CP meets AB at F. Find $AF : FB$.

Uses: Ceva's Theorem

Show hint

Ceva: $\dfrac{AF}{FB} \cdot \dfrac{BD}{DC} \cdot \dfrac{CE}{EA} = 1$. Go round the triangle in order. Type $\dfrac{AF}{FB}$.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{AF}{FB} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} = 1$.
  2. $\dfrac{AF}{FB} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$, so $\dfrac{AF}{FB} = 2$.

$AF : FB = 2 : 1$

Problem 4

Triangle ABC has $AB = 8$, $BC = 7$ and $CA = 9$. Its incircle touches AB at D. Find $AD$.

Uses: Circle Tangent Length, Incenter & Angle Bisectors

Show hint

The two tangents from a point to a circle are equal. Call the tangent lengths from A, B, C $x$, $y$, $z$.

Show solution
  1. $x + y = 8$, $y + z = 7$, $z + x = 9$.
  2. Adding: $2(x + y + z) = 24$, so $x + y + z = 12$.
  3. $AD = x = 12 - (y + z) = 12 - 7 = 5$.

$AD = 5$

Problem 5

In triangle ABC, D is on AB with $AD : DB = 1 : 2$, and E is the midpoint of AC. BE and CD meet at P. Find $BP : PE$.

Uses: Menelaus' Theorem

Show hint

Use Menelaus on triangle ABE, cut by the line through D, P and C. Type $\dfrac{BP}{PE}$.

Show solution
  1. The line DPC cuts AB at D, BE at P, and the extension of EA at C.
  2. Menelaus: $\dfrac{AD}{DB} \cdot \dfrac{BP}{PE} \cdot \dfrac{EC}{CA} = 1$.
  3. $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{BP}{PE} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$, so $\dfrac{BP}{PE} = 4$.

$BP : PE = 4 : 1$

Check with vectors: with A as origin, $\vec{d} = \dfrac{1}{3}\vec{b}$ and $\vec{e} = \dfrac{1}{2}\vec{c}$. Solving for P on both lines gives $\vec{p} = \dfrac{1}{5}\vec{b} + \dfrac{2}{5}\vec{c} = \dfrac{1}{5}\vec{b} + \dfrac{4}{5}\vec{e}$, so P is $\dfrac{4}{5}$ of the way from B to E ✓.

Sequences and Sums

Five problems on arithmetic and geometric sequences, sigma sums, and finding general terms. Try each one first. Open the hint if you are stuck, then compare with the full solution.

Problem 1

An arithmetic sequence has $a_3 = 7$ and $a_8 = 22$. Find $a_{20}$.

Uses: Arithmetic Sequence General Term

Show hint

$a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)d$. From $a_3$ to $a_8$ is $5$ steps of $d$.

Show solution
  1. $5d = 22 - 7 = 15$, so $d = 3$, and $a_1 = 7 - 2 \cdot 3 = 1$.
  2. $a_{20} = 1 + 19 \cdot 3 = 58$.

$a_{20} = 58$

Problem 2

Find the sum of the first $6$ terms of the geometric sequence $3, 6, 12, \ldots$.

Uses: Geometric Sequence Sum

Show hint

$S_n = \dfrac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$ for $r \ne 1$.

Show solution
  1. $a = 3$, $r = 2$: $S_6 = \dfrac{3(2^6 - 1)}{2 - 1} = 3 \cdot 63 = 189$.

$S_6 = 189$

Problem 3

Find $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{10} (2k^2 - k)$.

Uses: Sum of Natural Numbers Formulas, Sigma Properties

Show hint

Split it: $2\sum k^2 - \sum k$, with $\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 = \dfrac{n(n + 1)(2n + 1)}{6}$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{n} k = \dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2}$.

Show solution
  1. $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{10} k^2 = \dfrac{10 \cdot 11 \cdot 21}{6} = 385$ and $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{10} k = 55$.
  2. $2 \cdot 385 - 55 = 715$.

$715$

Problem 4

Find the general term of $1, 2, 5, 10, 17, \ldots$, and then $a_{10}$.

Uses: Difference Sequence and General Term

Show hint

Look at the differences between terms. For $n \ge 2$, $a_n = a_1 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} b_k$.

Show solution
  1. Differences: $1, 3, 5, 7, \ldots$, so $b_k = 2k - 1$.
  2. For $n \ge 2$: $a_n = 1 + \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} (2k - 1) = 1 + (n - 1)^2$.
  3. $n = 1$ gives $1$ too, so $a_n = (n - 1)^2 + 1$ for all $n$, and $a_{10} = 82$.

$a_n = (n - 1)^2 + 1$, $a_{10} = 82$

Problem 5

The sum of the first $n$ terms of a sequence is $S_n = n^2 + 2n + 1$. Find the general term $a_n$.

Uses: Sequence Sum and General Term

Show hint

$a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ works only for $n \ge 2$. Find $a_1 = S_1$ separately and compare.

Show solution
  1. $a_1 = S_1 = 4$.
  2. For $n \ge 2$: $a_n = (n^2 + 2n + 1) - \{(n - 1)^2 + 2(n - 1) + 1\} = 2n + 1$.
  3. $2n + 1$ at $n = 1$ is $3 \ne 4$, so the formula does not cover $n = 1$.

$a_1 = 4$, and $a_n = 2n + 1$ for $n \ge 2$

The formula works for $n = 1$ exactly when $S_n$ has no constant term. Here the $+1$ in $S_n$ all goes into $a_1$.
